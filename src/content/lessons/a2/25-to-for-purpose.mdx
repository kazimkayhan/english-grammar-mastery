## Why? to + verb

To say **why** a person does something, put **to + verb** after the action. This is called the *infinitive of purpose*. It answers the question **Why…?**

<FormTable caption="to + verb for purpose">

| Action | to + verb (the reason) |
|---|---|
| I came to Kabul | **to study**. |
| I went to the bank | **to get** some money. |
| She's learning English | **to get** a better job. |
| We got up early | **to catch** the first train. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="برای درس خواندن به کابل آمدم.">I came to Kabul <mark>to study</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="به بانک رفتم تا کمی پول بگیرم.">I went to the bank <mark>to get</mark> some money.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="انگلیسی یاد می‌گیرد تا کار بهتری پیدا کند.">She's learning English <mark>to get</mark> a better job.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="زود بیدار شدیم تا به اولین بس برسیم.">We got up early <mark>to catch</mark> the first bus.</Example>

- The verb after **to** is always the **base form**: *to buy*, *to see*, never ~~to bought~~ or ~~to buying~~.
- Often there are **two** *to*s in one sentence. The first is a preposition before a place, the second gives the reason: *I went **to** the shop **to** buy milk.*
- Two reasons? Join them with **and**. You can drop the second *to*: *I went to the market to buy fruit and (to) see my friend.*

### Answering Why?

A short answer to *Why…?* can be just **To + verb**. This is very common in speech.

<Example context="work" fa="«چرا زنگ زدی؟» «برای اینکه درباره‌ی جلسه بپرسم.»">“Why did you call?” “<mark>To ask</mark> about the meeting.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«هدف سفرتان چیست؟» «برای دیدن خانواده‌ام.»">“What's the purpose of your trip?” “<mark>To visit</mark> my family.”</Example>

### Questions and negatives

The question or the negative goes on the **main verb**. The purpose part (*to + verb*) does not change.

<Example context="travel" fa="برای کار به دبی رفتی؟"><mark>Did you go</mark> to Dubai to work?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="نیامدم اینجا که بخوابم! آمدم که کار کنم.">I <mark>didn't come</mark> here to sleep! I came here to work.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="چرا به کتابخانه رفتی؟"><mark>Why did</mark> you go to the library?</Example>

And when the purpose itself is negative (you do something because you **don't** want something)? At this level, the easy way is **because I didn't want to…**: *I left early because I didn't want to miss the bus.* A bare *not to* (*I left early not to miss the bus*) sounds less natural. More formal patterns (*in order not to, so as not to*) are in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 30, *Purpose, reason & result*</LessonRef>.

### in order to

**in order to** means the same as *to*. It is more formal, and you see it more in writing: *We use cookies **in order to** improve our website.* In everyday speech, plain **to** is normal.

### Purpose to or verb + to?

Don't mix two different *to*s:

- *I **want to** study.* Here *to* belongs to the verb **want** (*want, decide, hope, need* + to). That is <LessonRef>A2 lesson 24, *verb + to or verb + -ing? (basics)*</LessonRef>.
- *I **came** here **to study**.* Here *to study* gives the **reason** for coming.

A quick test: purpose *to* can become **in order to**. *I came here in order to study* ✓. ~~I want in order to study~~ ✗.

## for + noun

**for** can also give a purpose, but only before a **noun**. It names the thing you go to get, have or do.

<Example context="social" fa="بعد از شام رفتیم قدم بزنیم.">After dinner we went out <mark>for a walk</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="بین راه برای ناهار ایستادیم.">We stopped in Ghazni <mark>for lunch</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="جمعه برای شام بیا خانه‌ی ما.">Come to our house <mark>for dinner</mark> on Friday.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="فردا برای معاینه پیش دکتر می‌روم.">I'm going to the doctor tomorrow <mark>for a check-up</mark>.</Example>

Many purposes can be said both ways: with **to + verb** or with **for + noun**.

<FormTable caption="to + verb or for + noun">

| to + verb | for + noun |
|---|---|
| We went out **to have** dinner. | We went out **for dinner**. |
| Let's go **to have** a coffee. | Let's go **for a coffee**. |
| I'm saving **to buy** a new phone. | I'm saving **for a new phone**. |

</FormTable>

Some words are both a noun and a verb: *walk, swim, run, drink, drive*. After **for**, they are nouns, so they need **a**: *go **for a** walk, go **for a** swim, go **for a** run, go **for a** drive*. (Compare *go swimming*, *go shopping*: go + -ing is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 24, *verb + to or verb + -ing? (basics)*</LessonRef>.)

## for + -ing: what a thing is for

To say what a **thing** is used for (its job), use **for + -ing**.

<Example context="home" fa="این چاقو برای بریدن نان است.">This knife is <mark>for cutting</mark> bread.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="این برس برای تمیز کردن کفش است.">This brush is <mark>for cleaning</mark> shoes.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="این قیچی برای بریدن کاغذ است، نه مو!">These scissors are <mark>for cutting</mark> paper, not hair!</Example>

You can also put it after a noun: *a machine **for making** coffee*, *a bag **for carrying** a laptop*.

### What's it for?

To ask about a thing's job, put **for** at the **end** of the question. Answer with *It's for + -ing* (or *for + noun*).

<Example context="work" fa="«این دکمه برای چیست؟» «برای خاموش کردن دستگاه.»">“What's this button <mark>for</mark>?” “It's <mark>for turning</mark> the machine off.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«این کیف برای چیست؟» «برای لپ‌تاپ.»">“What's this bag <mark>for</mark>?” “It's <mark>for</mark> a laptop.”</Example>

### Thing or person?

**for + -ing** is about a **thing**. When a **person** is the subject (when you say why *someone* does something), use **to + verb**.

- A thermometer is **for measuring** temperature. (a thing's job)
- The nurse used a thermometer **to measure** my temperature. (why a person did it)
- ~~I went to the shop for buying bread.~~ → I went to the shop **to buy** bread.

Don't confuse *be used for -ing* (*It's used for cutting*) with **used to** for past habits (*I used to live in Bamyan*). That is <LessonRef>A2 lesson 10, *used to*</LessonRef>.

## The three patterns together

<FormTable caption="to + verb, for + noun, for + -ing">

| Pattern | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to + verb** | why a person does something | I went to the bank **to get** money. |
| **for + noun** | the thing you go to get or do | I went out **for a walk**. |
| **for + -ing** | what a thing is used for | a knife **for cutting** bread |
| ✗ **for (to) + verb** | never | ~~I came here for (to) study.~~ |

</FormTable>

<MemoryHook title="People to, things for">
**People** have reasons: they do things **to** + verb (*I came to study*). **Things** have jobs: they are **for** + -ing (*a knife for cutting*). And a **noun** you go out to get takes **for** (*for a walk, for lunch*).
</MemoryHook>
