## Two clauses, one sentence

A **clause** is a group of words with a subject and a verb. Words like **when, before, after, while, because** and **although** join two clauses into one sentence. They tell us about **time**, **reason** or **contrast**.

<FormTable caption="Linking words for clauses">

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | at that time | **When** I got home, I made dinner. |
| **before** | earlier than | I called my mum **before** I ate. |
| **after** | later than | **After** we had dinner, we watched a film. |
| **while** | during the same time | I listen to podcasts **while** I drive. |
| **because** | the reason | I went to bed early **because** I was tired. |
| **so** | the result | I was tired, **so** I went to bed early. |
| **although** | a surprising contrast | **Although** it was early, I fell asleep. |

</FormTable>

After each word comes a **subject + verb**: *when **I got** home*, *because **I was** tired*. In writing, a clause with *when, before, after, while, because* or *although* can't stand alone as a sentence. ~~When I got home.~~ needs another part: *When I got home, **I made dinner**.*

### Order and comma

Most of these clauses can come **first** or **second**. The meaning is the same.

<FormTable caption="Order and comma">

| Order | Example | Comma? |
|---|---|---|
| clause first | **When I got home,** I had a shower. | yes, after the clause |
| clause second | I had a shower **when I got home**. | usually no |

</FormTable>

**so** is different: the *so*-clause always comes **second**, usually with a comma before it: *I was tired, **so** I went to bed.*

## Time: when, before, after, while

### when

**when** = at that time. Use it for one moment or for a period of your life.

<Example context="home" fa="وقتی به خانه رسیدم، دوش گرفتم."><mark>When</mark> I got home, I had a shower.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="وقتی بچه بودم، در بامیان زندگی می‌کردم."><mark>When</mark> I was a child, I lived in Bamyan.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="وقتی سرما می‌خورم، چای با عسل می‌نوشم."><mark>When</mark> I have a cold, I drink tea with honey.</Example>

### before and after

**before** = earlier than the other action. **after** = later than the other action. They show the **order** of two actions.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "point", at: -0.7, label: "we had dinner" }, { type: "point", at: -0.3, label: "we watched a film" }]} caption="After we had dinner, we watched a film. = Before we watched a film, we had dinner." />

<Example context="home" fa="قبل از اینکه بخوابم، دندان‌هایم را مسواک می‌زنم.">I brush my teeth <mark>before</mark> I go to bed.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="بعد از اینکه شام خوردیم، فیلم دیدیم."><mark>After</mark> we had dinner, we watched a film.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="قبل از اینکه از دفتر بروم، در را قفل کردم.">I locked the door <mark>before</mark> I left the office.</Example>

**Use the normal tense.** For the past, the verb after *before* and *after* is in the **past**: *before I **left***, *after we **had** dinner*. (After *after*, you may also see the past perfect: *after I **had finished***. Both are correct, because *after* already shows the order.) For the future, use the **present**: *before you **leave***. That rule is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 27, *First conditional & future time clauses*</LessonRef>.

**before / after + noun** also works: *before lunch, after class* (<LessonRef>A2 lesson 45, *by, with, about; between, among; above, over*</LessonRef>). *before / after + -ing* (*after finishing work*) comes in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 25, *Participle clauses*</LessonRef>.

**after alone?** In a story, *After, we went to the cinema* is wrong. *after* needs a noun or a clause. Alone, say **after that** or **afterwards**: *We had dinner. **After that**, we went to the cinema.* (More in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 46, *Sequencing: first, then, after that, finally*</LessonRef>.)

### while

**while** = during the same time. Both actions happen together. The *while*-clause is often the longer action.

<Example context="travel" fa="وقتی رانندگی می‌کنم، پادکست گوش می‌دهم.">I listen to podcasts <mark>while</mark> I drive to work.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="وقتی داشتم آشپزی می‌کردم، تلفن زنگ زد."><mark>While</mark> I was cooking, the phone rang.</Example>

*when* and *while* with the past continuous (*While I was cooking, the phone rang*) are in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 8, *Past continuous; when & while*</LessonRef>. More time words (*as soon as, until, since, by the time*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 32, *Time & place clauses: as soon as, until, wherever*</LessonRef>.

## Reason and result: because, so

You met these in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 33, *and, but, or, because, so, also*</LessonRef>. Here is one step more.

**because** + the **reason**. It answers *Why?* The because-clause can come first or second.

<Example context="health" fa="امروز خانه ماندم چون حالم خوب نبود.">I stayed at home today <mark>because</mark> I felt sick.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="چون اتوبوس دیر کرد، به جلسه نرسیدم."><mark>Because</mark> the bus was late, I missed the meeting.</Example>

**so** + the **result**. The reason comes first.

<Example context="shopping" fa="فروشگاه بسته بود، برای همین آنلاین خریدم.">The shop was closed, <mark>so</mark> I ordered it online.</Example>

<FormTable caption="Same story, two ways">

| Word | Order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | result + **because** + reason | I missed the meeting **because** the bus was late. |
| **because** (first) | **Because** + reason, result | **Because** the bus was late, I missed the meeting. |
| **so** | reason, **so** + result | The bus was late, **so** I missed the meeting. |
| ✗ both | never | ~~Because the bus was late, so I missed the meeting.~~ |

</FormTable>

- In speech, a short answer to *Why?* can be just the because-clause: “*Why are you late?*” “*Because the bus was late.*” In writing, join it to the main clause.
- *because* needs a subject + verb. With a noun alone, say **because of**: *because of the traffic* (<LessonRef>B2 lesson 43, *Preposition or conjunction? during/while, despite/although*</LessonRef>). Other reason words (*as, since*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 30, *Purpose, reason & result*</LessonRef>.

## Contrast: although

**although** joins two ideas that don't usually go together. The second idea is a **surprise** after the first. It means the same as *but*, but the order is different.

<Example context="shopping" fa="با اینکه ماشین قدیمی است، هنوز خوب کار می‌کند."><mark>Although</mark> the car is old, it still runs well.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="با اینکه باران می‌آمد، رفتیم قدم بزنیم."><mark>Although</mark> it was raining, we went for a walk.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="با اینکه خسته بود، تا دیروقت کار کرد.">She worked late <mark>although</mark> she was very tired.</Example>

<FormTable caption="although or but">

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **although** + surprise fact, main clause | **Although** it was late, we went out. |
| main clause + **although** + surprise fact | We went out **although** it was late. |
| fact, **but** + surprise | It was late, **but** we went out. |
| ✗ although + but | ~~Although it was late, but we went out.~~ |

</FormTable>

- **Never use although and but together.** One linking word is enough.
- **although + subject + verb**: *although the traffic **was** bad*, not ~~although the traffic~~. With a noun, use *despite*, in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 31, *Contrast: although, however, despite*</LessonRef>. That lesson also has *though* and *even though*.

- **because** = چون (دلیل) · **so** = برای همین، پس (نتیجه)

<MemoryHook title="One joint per pair">
Two clauses need **one joint**: *when, before, after, while, because, so* or *although*. Two joints in one sentence (*although … but*, *because … so*) is one too many.
</MemoryHook>
