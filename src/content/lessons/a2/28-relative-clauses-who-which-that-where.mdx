## Which one? Relative clauses

A **relative clause** is a small sentence inside a bigger one. It comes **after a noun** and tells you **which** person, thing or place you mean. It starts with **who, which, that** or **where**.

<Example context="home" fa="مردی که همسایه‌ی ماست پزشک است.">The man <mark>who lives next door</mark> is a doctor.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="گوشی‌ای که خریدم کار نمی‌کند.">The phone <mark>that I bought</mark> doesn't work.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="این همان کافه‌ای است که در آن با هم آشنا شدیم.">This is the café <mark>where we met</mark>.</Example>

Without the clause, the listener asks *Which man? Which phone? Which café?* The clause answers that question. That's why we call it a **defining** relative clause: it defines (identifies) the noun. These clauses have **no commas**.

## who, which, that

<FormTable caption="Relative pronouns">

| Word | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people | a friend **who** speaks Chinese |
| **which** | things and animals | the bus **which** goes to the airport |
| **that** | people or things | the woman **that** called · the bus **that** goes to the airport |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="دوستی دارم که چینی صحبت می‌کند.">I have a friend <mark>who</mark> speaks Chinese.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="این اتوبوسی است که به فرودگاه می‌رود؟">Is this the bus <mark>which</mark> goes to the airport?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="زنی که زنگ زد اسمش را نگفت.">The woman <mark>that</mark> called didn't leave her name.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="گربه‌ای که در باغ ماست مال همسایه است.">The cat <mark>which</mark> lives in our garden belongs to our neighbor.</Example>

- In everyday speech, **that** is very common, especially for things: *the bus **that** goes to the airport*.
- For people, **who** is the usual choice. Never use **which** for people: ~~a friend which speaks Chinese~~.

## Two kinds: subject or object

The relative word can be the **subject** or the **object** of the small clause.

<FormTable caption="Subject or object?">

| | Two sentences | One sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **subject** | I met a woman. **She** works for Afghanistan Air. | I met a woman **who works** for Afghanistan Air. |
| **subject** | The bus is late. **It** goes to the airport. | The bus **that goes** to the airport is late. |
| **object** | The phone doesn't work. I bought **it**. | The phone **that I bought** doesn't work. |
| **object** | I like the man. You met **him**. | I like the man **who you met**. |

</FormTable>

- **Subject:** *who / which / that* + **verb**: *a woman **who works**…*
- **Object:** *who / which / that* + **another subject** + verb: *the phone **that I bought***.

In the object type, people often leave out the relative word: *the phone I bought*. That is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 29, *Defining & non-defining relative clauses*</LessonRef>. For now, always keep it.

### No extra pronoun

The relative word **replaces** *he, she, it, them* (or *him, her*). So don't say them again.

<Example context="home" fa="مردی که همسایه‌ی ماست عموی من است." note="Not: who he lives">The man <mark>who lives</mark> next door is my uncle.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کتابی که دیروز خریدم خیلی جالب است." note="Not: that I bought it">The book <mark>that I bought</mark> yesterday is really interesting.</Example>

### The verb agrees with the noun

In a subject clause, the verb agrees with the noun before *who / which / that*.

<Example context="home" fa="مردی که همسایه‌ی ماست خیلی مهربان است." note="one man → lives">The man who <mark>lives</mark> next door is very kind.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="آدم‌هایی که همسایه‌ی ما هستند خیلی مهربان‌اند." note="people → live">The people who <mark>live</mark> next door are very kind.</Example>

## where: places

Use **where** after a place noun when something **happens in** that place. *where* means *there* or *in it*.

<Example context="travel" fa="این هتلی است که در آن اقامت داشتیم.">This is the hotel <mark>where</mark> we stayed. (We stayed there.)</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="این بیمارستانی است که در آن به دنیا آمدم.">That's the hospital <mark>where</mark> I was born. (I was born there.)</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کتابخانه جایی است که می‌توانی کتاب امانت بگیری.">A library is a place <mark>where</mark> you can borrow books.</Example>

**where or which / that?** Ask: does the verb need an object?

- *We **met** there.* → the café **where** we met.
- *We **visited** it.* (visit + object) → the museum **that** (or **which**) we visited, not ~~the museum where we visited~~.

## Where the clause goes

The relative clause goes **right after** the noun it describes. Sometimes that is in the **middle** of the sentence, before the main verb.

<Example context="work" fa="زنی که در دفتر ما کار می‌کند اهل هرات است.">The woman <mark>who works in our office</mark> is from Herat.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کفش‌هایی که خریدی خیلی قشنگ‌اند.">The shoes <mark>that you bought</mark> are really nice.</Example>

Don't add a pronoun before the main verb: *The woman who works in our office **is** from Herat*, not ~~…office she is from Herat~~.

## In questions, negatives and definitions

Relative clauses work in questions and negatives too. The clause itself can also be negative.

<Example context="work" fa="کسی را می‌شناسی که چینی بلد باشد؟">Do you know anyone <mark>who speaks</mark> Chinese?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="گوشی‌ای می‌خواهم که خیلی گران نباشد.">I need a phone <mark>that doesn't cost</mark> too much.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="غذای مخصوص برای کسانی که گوشت نمی‌خورند داریم.">We have dishes for people <mark>who don't eat</mark> meat.</Example>

They are very useful for **definitions**: *A dentist is a person **who** looks after your teeth. A fridge is a machine **that** keeps food cold.*

**جمله‌ی موصولی (relative clause): کدام یکی؟**

جمله‌ی موصولی بعد از اسم می‌آید و می‌گوید منظورمان **کدام** آدم، چیز یا جاست. در فارسی با «که» می‌سازیم: «مردی **که** همسایه‌ی ماست». در انگلیسی چند کلمه داریم:

- **who** برای آدم: the man who lives next door (مردی که همسایه‌ی ماست)
- **which** برای چیز و حیوان: the bus which goes to the airport (اتوبوسی که به فرودگاه می‌رود)
- **that** برای هر دو: the phone that I bought (گوشی‌ای که خریدم)
- **where** برای مکان: the café where we met (کافه‌ای که در آن آشنا شدیم)

**مهم‌ترین اشتباه فارسی‌زبان‌ها: تکرار ضمیر.** در فارسی می‌گوییم «کتابی که دیروز خریدم**ش**» یا «مردی که دیدم**ش**». در انگلیسی that یا who خودش جای آن اسم را می‌گیرد، پس ضمیر را تکرار **نمی‌کنیم**:

- the book that I bought ✓ (نه the book that I bought it ✗)
- the man who lives next door ✓ (نه the man who he lives ✗)

**«که» برای همه، اما در انگلیسی نه:** چون در فارسی همیشه «که» می‌گوییم، فارسی‌زبان‌ها گاهی which را برای آدم به کار می‌برند: a friend which speaks Chinese ✗. برای آدم who یا that بگویید.

**where یا which؟** where یعنی «در آنجا». اگر کاری **در** آن مکان انجام می‌شود، where: the hotel where we stayed (هتلی که در آن ماندیم). اما اگر خود مکان مفعول فعل است (موزه را **دیدیم**)، that یا which: the museum that we visited.

**جای جمله‌ی موصولی:** درست بعد از اسم، حتی وسط جمله: The phone that I bought doesn't work. (گوشی‌ای که خریدم کار نمی‌کند.) بعد از آن ضمیر اضافه نیاورید: The woman who works here she is… ✗.

**مطابقت فعل:** فعل با اسم قبل از who هماهنگ است: the man who **lives** / the people who **live**.

**ویرگول ندارد:** این جمله‌ها اسم را مشخص می‌کنند و ویرگول نمی‌گیرند. جمله‌های موصولی با ویرگول، whose و when در درس ۲۹ سطح B1 می‌آیند.

<MemoryHook title="The pronoun moves in">
When you join two sentences, **who, which** or **that** moves in and takes the place of *he, she, it* or *them*. The old pronoun moves **out**: *The phone doesn't work. I bought ~~it~~.* → *The phone **that** I bought doesn't work.*
</MemoryHook>
