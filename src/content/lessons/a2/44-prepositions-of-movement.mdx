## Position or movement?

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 31 (Prepositions of place)</LessonRef> you learned **where** things are: *in the box, on the table, at the bank*. In this lesson, things **move**. They go from one place to another.

Many prepositions come in pairs: one for **position** (no movement) and one for **movement**.

<FormTable caption="Position or movement?">

| Position (where it is) | Movement (where it goes) |
|---|---|
| I'm **at** the bank. | I'm going **to** the bank. |
| The cat is **in** the house. | The cat ran **into** the house. |
| The book is **on** the table. | The book fell **onto** the floor. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="ساعت هشت به محل کارم می‌روم.">I go <mark>to</mark> work at eight.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="از کابل تا مزار شریف با ماشین رفتیم.">We drove <mark>from</mark> Kabul <mark>to</mark> Mazar-i-Sharif.</Example>

**to** shows the place you go to. **from** shows where you start. Use them together for a whole trip: *from* A *to* B.

## into and out of; onto and off

<FormTable caption="Moving in and out, on and off">

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **into** | to the inside of | She walked **into** the kitchen. |
| **out of** | from the inside to the outside | He took his phone **out of** his bag. |
| **onto** | to a position on a surface | The cat jumped **onto** the sofa. |
| **off** | away from a surface | A cup fell **off** the table. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="بچه‌ها دویدند توی باغ.">The children ran <mark>into</mark> the garden.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً لپ‌تاپت را از کیفت بیرون بیاور.">Please take your laptop <mark>out of</mark> your bag.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او از دوچرخه افتاد و دستش شکست.">He fell <mark>off</mark> his bike and broke his arm.</Example>

After verbs like *put, jump* and *fall*, people often use **in** and **on** for movement too: *Put the milk **in** the fridge.* *The cat jumped **on** the table.* Both are correct. **into** and **onto** make the movement clearer.

### Getting in and out of transport

<FormTable caption="Transport: getting in and out">

| Car, taxi | Bus, train, plane, bike |
|---|---|
| get **in** / get **into** the car | get **on** the bus |
| get **out of** the taxi | get **off** the train |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="در ایستگاه بعدی پیاده می‌شوم.">I'll get <mark>off</mark> at the next stop.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="سوار تاکسی شد و رفت.">She got <mark>into</mark> a taxi and left.</Example>

Where you **are** during the trip (*in a car, on a bus*) and how you travel (*by bus*) are in <LessonRef>lesson 45 (by, with, about; between, among; above, over)</LessonRef>.

## across, through, over, along, past

In Persian, we often say «از … رد شدن» for all of these. English uses a different word for each **shape** of movement.

<FormTable caption="Crossing and passing">

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **across** | from one side to the other of a flat area | Walk **across** the road. · swim **across** the river |
| **through** | into one end and out of the other, inside something | drive **through** a tunnel · walk **through** the park · push **through** the crowd |
| **over** | up and across something high, or from one side to the other above it | climb **over** the wall · jump **over** the fence |
| **along** | following a line: a road, a river, a beach | walk **along** the river |
| **past** | up to something and beyond it, without stopping | walk **past** the bank |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="از پل رد شوید و موزه سمت چپتان است.">Go <mark>across</mark> the bridge. The museum is on your left.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="قطار از یک تونل طولانی رد شد.">The train went <mark>through</mark> a long tunnel.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="سگ از روی دیوار پرید.">The dog jumped <mark>over</mark> the wall.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="بعد از شام کنار ساحل قدم زدیم.">After dinner, we walked <mark>along</mark> the beach.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="از جلوی بانک رد شوید. نانوایی بعد از آن است.">Walk <mark>past</mark> the bank. The bakery is just after it.</Example>

**across or through?** Think about the shape. A road or a river is **flat**, so you go **across** it. A tunnel, a forest, a door or a crowd is **all around you**, so you go **through** it. (For a bridge, you can say *across* or *over*.)

## up, down, around, towards

<FormTable caption="Direction">

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **up** | to a higher place | walk **up** the hill · go **up** the stairs |
| **down** | to a lower place; also *along* a street | run **down** the stairs · walk **down** the street |
| **around** | in a circle, or to different parts of a place | run **around** the park · show you **around** the city |
| **towards** | in the direction of (maybe you don't arrive) | walk **towards** the door |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="بچه‌ها از پله‌ها دویدند پایین.">The kids came running <mark>down</mark> the stairs.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="هر صبح دور دریاچه می‌دوم.">I run <mark>around</mark> the lake every morning.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="طوفان به سمت ساحل حرکت می‌کند.">The storm is moving <mark>towards</mark> the coast.</Example>

**to or towards?** *She walked **to** the door* = she got there. *She walked **towards** the door* = she went in that direction. Maybe she stopped before it.

<UkNote>American English usually says **toward**; British English usually says **towards**. Both are correct. British speakers also often say **round** (*walk round the park*) where Americans say **around**.</UkNote>

## arrive in or arrive at?

**arrive** never takes *to*. Use **in** or **at**, like for position:

<FormTable caption="arrive">

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **arrive in** + city, country | We arrived **in** London at 2 a.m. · They arrived **in** Afghanistan last week. |
| **arrive at** + building, place | We arrived **at** the airport at 10:30. · She arrived **at** the party late. |
| **arrive** + home, here, there (no preposition) | He arrived **home** at midnight. · Call me when you arrive **there**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="ساعت ۷ صبح به قندهار رسیدیم.">We arrived <mark>in</mark> Kandahar at 7 a.m.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او همیشه دیر به جلسه می‌رسد.">He always arrives <mark>at</mark> meetings late.</Example>

In everyday speech, people often say **get to** instead of *arrive*: *What time did you **get to** work?* But **get home**, **get here**, **get there**: no *to*.

## go home, come home, get home

After **go, come, get** and **arrive**, **home** has **no preposition**. When you don't move, say **at home**.

<Example context="home" fa="ساعت شش می‌روم خانه.">I go <mark>home</mark> at six.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="کی می‌آیی خانه؟">What time are you coming <mark>home</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="امروز تمام روز خانه بودم.">I was <mark>at home</mark> all day today.</Example>

Say **go back home**, not ~~go back to home~~.

## Words that need no preposition

These words already show a place or a direction. Don't put *to* or *in* before them.

<FormTable caption="Adverbs of place and direction">

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **here** / **there** | Come **here**! · I've never been **there**. |
| **home** | Let's go **home**. |
| **abroad** (= to or in another country) | She often goes **abroad** for work. · He studied **abroad**. |
| **upstairs** / **downstairs** | I went **upstairs** and had a shower. |
| **inside** / **outside** | It's cold. Let's go **inside**. |
| **back** | I'll be **back** in five minutes. · Go **back** to bed. |
| **away** | Go **away**! · She's **away** this week. |
| **downtown** (American English) | I have to go **downtown** later. |

</FormTable>

**here** and **there** also say if someone is present: *Is Zahra **there**?* (on the phone) · *Mr. Karimi isn't **here** today. He's sick.*

<Example context="travel" fa="هیچ‌وقت خارج از کشور زندگی نکرده‌ام.">I've never lived <mark>abroad</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="رئیس امروز اینجا نیست.">The boss isn't <mark>here</mark> today.</Example>

## Verbs that need a place

Some verbs are not complete without a **place** after them. The place answers the question **Where?**

<FormTable caption="Verbs + place">

| Verb | Not complete | Complete |
|---|---|---|
| **live** (= have your home) | ~~I live.~~ | I **live in** Herat. · Does Paul still **live here**? |
| **put** | ~~I put the keys.~~ | I **put** the keys **on the table**. |
| **keep** (= store) | ~~We keep the sugar.~~ | We **keep** the sugar **in the cupboard**. |
| **stay** (as a guest) | | We **stayed at** a small hotel. · I'm **staying with** friends. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="پاسپورتت را کجا نگه می‌داری؟">“Where do you <mark>keep</mark> your passport?” “In the drawer.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="لطفاً تخم‌مرغ‌ها را بگذار توی یخچال.">Please <mark>put</mark> the eggs in the fridge.</Example>

**stay** can stand alone when the place is clear: *Please stay!* (= here) · *How long are you staying?* But **live**, **put** and **keep** (= store) always need a place.

## Giving directions

All these words come together when you give directions:

<Example context="travel" fa="مستقیم در این خیابان بروید، از کنار پارک رد شوید و از پل عبور کنید. ایستگاه سمت راستتان است.">Go <mark>along</mark> this street, walk <mark>past</mark> the park and go <mark>across</mark> the bridge. The station is on your right.</Example>

The verb **enter** takes no preposition: *She entered the room* (not ~~entered to~~ or ~~entered into the room~~). More verbs like this are in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 42 (Prepositional phrases & zero-preposition traps)</LessonRef>. Movement words used alone after a verb, with no noun after them (*She ran **in**. He walked **off**.*), come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 14 (Phrasal & prepositional verbs)</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**حرکت یا مکان؟** در درس ۳۱ سطح A1 یاد گرفتید که چیزها **کجا هستند** (in، on، at). در این درس چیزها **حرکت می‌کنند**. برای حرکت از حرف‌های اضافه‌ی دیگری استفاده می‌کنیم:

- at ← **to** (به): I'm at the bank. (در بانک هستم.) ← I'm going to the bank. (به بانک می‌روم.)
- in ← **into** (به داخلِ، توی): She's in the kitchen. ← She walked into the kitchen. (رفت توی آشپزخانه.)
- on ← **onto** (روی، با حرکت): The cat jumped onto the sofa. (گربه پرید روی مبل.)

**جفت‌ها:** into ↔ **out of** (بیرون از): She ran out of the house. (از خانه دوید بیرون.) · onto ↔ **off** (از روی): He fell off his bike. (از دوچرخه افتاد.)

**سوار و پیاده شدن:** برای ماشین و تاکسی: get in / get into (سوار شدن) و get out of (پیاده شدن). برای اتوبوس، قطار، هواپیما و دوچرخه: get on (سوار شدن) و get off (پیاده شدن).

**«از … رد شدن»:** در فارسی برای همه‌ی این حرکت‌ها می‌گوییم «از … رد شدن» یا «از … گذشتن». اما در انگلیسی شکل حرکت مهم است:

- **across** = از این طرف به آن طرفِ یک سطح صاف: walk across the road (از خیابان رد شدن)
- **through** = از داخلِ چیزی، از یک سر وارد و از سر دیگر خارج: go through the tunnel (از تونل رد شدن)، walk through the park (از وسط پارک رد شدن)
- **over** = از روی چیزی بلند: jump over the wall (از روی دیوار پریدن)
- **along** = در طولِ یک خط: walk along the river (کنار رودخانه قدم زدن، در طول آن)
- **past** = از کنار یا جلوی چیزی رد شدن بدون توقف: walk past the bank (از جلوی بانک رد شدن)

**جهت:** up (بالا)، down (پایین)، around (دورِ)، towards (به سمتِ). to یعنی به مقصد می‌رسیم؛ towards یعنی فقط به آن سمت می‌رویم.

**arrive (رسیدن):** در فارسی می‌گوییم «به کابل رسیدیم»، برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند arrive to Kabul. ✗ اما بعد از arrive هیچ‌وقت to نمی‌آید:

- **arrive in** + شهر یا کشور: We arrived in Kabul. (به کابل رسیدیم.)
- **arrive at** + ساختمان یا مکان: We arrived at the airport. (به فرودگاه رسیدیم.)
- arrive home / here / there بدون حرف اضافه.

**home:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «به خانه رفتم»، اما در انگلیسی بعد از go، come، get و arrive قبل از home حرف اضافه نمی‌آید: I went home. (نه I went to home.) وقتی حرکت نیست: I'm at home. (خانه هستم.)

**بدون حرف اضافه:** here (اینجا)، there (آنجا)، abroad (خارج از کشور)، upstairs (طبقه‌ی بالا)، downstairs (طبقه‌ی پایین)، inside (داخل)، outside (بیرون)، back (برگشت)، away (دور): go abroad (نه go to abroad)، come here (نه come to here).

**برعکسِ این اشتباه:** در فارسی محاوره‌ای گاهی «به» را نمی‌گوییم: «می‌رم مدرسه»، «رفتم دانشگاه». اما در انگلیسی برای رفتن به یک مکان باید to بیاید: I go to school. (نه I go school.) فقط home و کلمه‌های بالا بدون to هستند.

**فعل‌هایی که مکان لازم دارند:** live (زندگی کردن)، put (گذاشتن) و keep (نگه داشتن چیزی در جایی) بدون مکان کامل نیستند: I live in Herat. · Put it on the table. (آن را روی میز بگذار.) · We keep the sugar in the cupboard. (شکر را در کابینت نگه می‌داریم.) stay هم معمولاً با مکان می‌آید: We stayed at a hotel. (در یک هتل ماندیم.)

**enter:** «وارد اتاق شد» = She entered the room. (نه entered to the room.)

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="I go to home at six." right="I go home at six." why="home after go, come, get needs no preposition." />
<WrongRight wrong="We arrived to Kabul at midnight." right="We arrived in Kabul at midnight." why="arrive in + city; arrive at + building. Never arrive to." />
<WrongRight wrong="He went to abroad last year." right="He went abroad last year." why="No preposition before abroad, here, there, upstairs." />
<WrongRight wrong="I go university by bus." right="I go to university by bus." why="Movement to a place needs to." />
<WrongRight wrong="Come to here, please." right="Come here, please." why="here and there take no preposition." />
<WrongRight wrong="We walked across the tunnel." right="We walked through the tunnel." why="Inside something, in one end and out the other → through." />
<WrongRight wrong="I got out of the bus." right="I got off the bus." why="bus, train, plane → get on / get off; car, taxi → get in / get out of." />
<WrongRight wrong="She lives." right="She lives in Kandahar." why="live (= have your home) needs a place." />

<MemoryHook title="Shape decides">
Picture the movement. **Flat** road → **across**. **Tube** or crowd → **through**. **Wall** → **over**. **Line** → **along**. **Going by** → **past**. And **home, here, there, abroad**: they are already places, so no *to*.
</MemoryHook>
