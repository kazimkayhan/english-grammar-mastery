## all

**all** is for a **group**: every person or thing in it. It goes with **plural** or **uncountable** nouns.

<FormTable caption="all">

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **all** + plural noun | people or things in general | **All** children like games. |
| **all the / all my / all these** + noun | a particular group | **All the** students in my class are from Afghanistan. |
| **all of the** + noun | the same as *all the* | **All of the** students have gone home. |
| **all of** + pronoun | | **all of us** · **all of them** · **all of it** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="همه‌ی دانشجوهای کلاس من افغان‌اند."><mark>All the</mark> students in my class are from Afghanistan.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="همه‌ی دوستانم را به جشن تولدم دعوت کردم.">I invited <mark>all my</mark> friends to my birthday party.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="همه‌ی ما این پروژه را دوست داریم."><mark>All of us</mark> like this project.</Example>

**The verb** follows the noun: *All the students **are** here* (plural), but *All the money **is** in the bank* (uncountable).

**With a pronoun, you need *of***: ***all of** them*, not ~~all them~~. Or put **all** after the subject:

- after the subject, before the main verb: ***They all** speak English.*
- after *be*: ***We're all** tired.*

<Example context="home" fa="همه‌مان خسته‌ایم. بیایید زود بخوابیم.">We're <mark>all</mark> tired. Let's go to bed early.</Example>

**all or everybody?** To mean "all the people", say **everybody** or **everyone**, not *all* alone: ***Everybody** stood up* (not ~~All stood up~~). ***Everyone** liked the movie* or ***They all** liked the movie* (not ~~All people liked the movie~~). *everybody* takes a singular verb. That is <LessonRef>lesson 32 (some, any, no; something, anyone, nowhere)</LessonRef>.

## both

**both** means **the two** (people or things) together. It always has a **plural** verb.

<FormTable caption="both">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **both** + plural noun | **Both** hands · **Both** women were tall. |
| **both (of) the / my / these** + noun | **Both (of) my** parents are teachers. |
| **both of** + pronoun | **both of us** · **both of them** |
| subject + **both** + verb | **We both** like tea. · **They're both** doctors. |
| **both** alone | “Tea or coffee?” “I like **both**.” |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="پدر و مادرم هر دو معلم‌اند."><mark>Both</mark> my parents are teachers.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="دو خواهر دارم. هر دوی آن‌ها پزشک‌اند.">I have two sisters. <mark>Both of them</mark> are doctors.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ما هر دو در یک شرکت کار می‌کنیم.">We <mark>both</mark> work for the same company.</Example>

**both** comes **before** *the* and *my*: *both the girls*, never ~~the both girls~~.

**Negative?** Don't use *both … not*. Use **neither**: ***Neither** of them is here.* That is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 40 (each, every, either, neither, both, all, whole, none)</LessonRef>. *both … and* (*both Zahra and Ahmad*) is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 41 (Paired conjunctions: both … and, either … or)</LessonRef>.

## every and each

**every** and **each** go with a **singular** countable noun and a **singular** verb, even though they talk about a whole group.

<Example context="school" fa="هر دانشجو یک کمد دارد."><mark>Every student has</mark> a locker.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هر اتاق هتل تلویزیون دارد."><mark>Each room has</mark> a TV and a small fridge.</Example>

Compare *every* and *all*. They talk about the same group, but the grammar is different:

- ***Every student has** a book.* (singular)
- ***All the students have** a book.* (plural)

**every or each?** Often both are possible. But there is a small difference:

- **every** = the whole group together, with no exceptions: ***Every** child needs love.*
- **each** = the members **one by one**, separately: *The teacher gave **each** student a certificate.*
- For **two** things, use **each**, not *every*: *She had a bag in **each hand**.* (not ~~every hand~~)

<Example context="news" fa="هر کودکی حق دارد به مکتب برود."><mark>Every</mark> child has the right to go to school.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="معلم با تک‌تک دانشجوها حرف زد.">The teacher spoke to <mark>each</mark> student after the test.</Example>

Two more patterns:

- **every** + number + plural noun, for how often: *Take this medicine **every four hours**. The bus comes **every ten minutes**.*
- **each** at the end, meaning "for one": *The tickets cost $15 **each**.*

After *every* and *each*, we often use **they / their** when the person could be a man or a woman: ***Every child** has **their** own room.* *each of* and *every one of* come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 40 (each, every, either, neither, both, all, whole, none)</LessonRef>.

## every day or all day?

These look similar, but they answer different questions.

<FormTable caption="every day or all day?">

| | every day | all day |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | on **all** the days (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday…) | the **whole** of one day, from morning to night |
| Question | **How often?** | **How long?** |
| Example | I drink coffee **every day**. | I worked **all day**. I'm so tired. |

</FormTable>

The same pattern works with other time words:

- *every morning / all morning · every night / all night · every week / all week · every summer / all summer*

<Example context="health" fa="هر صبح می‌دوم.">I go running <mark>every morning</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="تمام صبح باران بارید.">It rained <mark>all morning</mark>, so we stayed home.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="تمام هفته سرم شلوغ بود.">I was busy <mark>all week</mark>. I didn't have time for lunch.</Example>

**every day or everyday?**

- **every day** (two words) = each day: *I see him **every day**.*
- **everyday** (one word) is an adjective before a noun. It means **ordinary, usual**: ***everyday** English, **everyday** life*.

*the whole day* (= all day) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 40 (each, every, either, neither, both, all, whole, none)</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**all (همه):** با اسم جمع یا غیرشمردنی می‌آید:
- All children like games. (همه‌ی بچه‌ها بازی دوست دارند، به‌طور کلی)
- All the students in my class… (همه‌ی دانشجوهای کلاس من، گروه مشخص)
- با ضمیر of لازم است: all of us (همه‌ی ما)، all of them (همه‌ی آن‌ها). یا بعد از فاعل: We're all tired. (همه‌مان خسته‌ایم.)

برای «همه» به معنی «همه‌ی آدم‌ها» از everybody استفاده کنید، نه all تنها: Everybody stood up. (همه بلند شدند.)

**both (هر دو):** فقط برای **دو** نفر یا دو چیز: both my parents (پدر و مادرم هر دو)، both of them (هر دوی آن‌ها)، They're both doctors. (هر دو پزشک‌اند.) نگویید the both. برای منفی از neither استفاده می‌کنیم.

**every و each (هر):** در فارسی «هر» با اسم مفرد می‌آید: «هر دانشجو». در انگلیسی هم همین‌طور است: every student، each student، و فعل هم مفرد است: Every student **has** a book. اشتباه رایج این است که «همه‌ی دانشجویان» (جمع) با «هر» قاطی شود و بگوییم every students. یادتان باشد:
- every / each + اسم **مفرد** + فعل مفرد: Every student has…
- all + اسم **جمع** + فعل جمع: All the students have…

**every یا each؟** every یعنی «همه، بدون استثنا» و each یعنی «تک‌تک، یکی‌یکی». برای **دو** چیز فقط each: in each hand (در هر دست)، نه every hand.

**every day یا all day؟**
- every day = «هر روز» (چند وقت یک بار؟): I drink coffee every day.
- all day = «تمام روز / کل روز» (چه مدت؟): I worked all day.

همچنین every morning (هر صبح) و all morning (تمام صبح). دقت کنید: everyday (سرهم) صفت است و یعنی «روزمره»: everyday English (انگلیسی روزمره).

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="One by one, or all together">
**every** and **each** look at **one** member at a time, so they take **one** (singular) noun: *every student, each room*. **all** looks at the **whole** group: *all the students*. And **both**? Exactly **two**.
</MemoryHook>
