## The big picture

The first question is always the same as in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 15 (Countable & uncountable nouns; some, any, how much, how many)</LessonRef>: **can you count the noun?** Then choose the word.

<FormTable caption="Quantity words with the two kinds of noun">

| Meaning | + plural noun (countable) | + uncountable noun |
|---|---|---|
| a big number / amount | **a lot of** people · **many** people | **a lot of** time · **much** time |
| a small number / amount | **a few** people | **a little** time |
| more than you want | **too many** people | **too much** time |
| an extra number / amount | **more** people | **more** time |
| question | **How many** people? | **How much** time? |

</FormTable>

Only two words work with **both** kinds of noun: **a lot of** (or **lots of**) and **more**.

## much and many

**many** goes with **plural** nouns. **much** goes with **uncountable** nouns. Both mean "a big number" or "a big amount".

We use them mainly in **negatives** and **questions**.

<Example context="travel" fa="شب‌ها بس زیادی نیست.">There aren't <mark>many</mark> buses at night.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="امروز وقت زیادی ندارم.">I don't have <mark>much</mark> time today.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="آدم‌های زیادی در مهمانی بودند؟">Were there <mark>many</mark> people at the party?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در بزرگراه ترافیک زیاد است؟">Is there <mark>much</mark> traffic on the highway?</Example>

Questions with **How many** and **How much** work the same way: *How many brothers do you have? How much sugar do you take?*

### In positive sentences, say a lot of

Why not *much* in a positive sentence? In everyday English, **much** sounds very formal there, almost strange. **many** sounds formal too. It is fine in writing and in the news, but in speech we say **a lot of** or **lots of**.

<Example context="home" fa="امروز تکلیف زیادی داریم.">We have <mark>a lot of</mark> homework today. (not ~~much homework~~)</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="امروزه خیلی‌ها از خانه کار می‌کنند.">Today <mark>many</mark> people work from home. (written English, the news)</Example>

Two exceptions: after **too** and **so**, *much* and *many* are normal in positive sentences: *You work **too much**. There are **so many** people here!* (*so* for emphasis is <LessonRef>lesson 4 (Exclamations: What a…! How…!)</LessonRef>.)

### No noun? Use much or many alone

When the noun is clear, *much* and *many* can stand alone, often in short answers.

<Example context="shopping" fa="«پول داری؟» «زیاد نه.»">“Do you have any money?” “Not <mark>much</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«عکس گرفتی؟» «زیاد نه.»">“Did you take any photos?” “Not <mark>many</mark>.”</Example>

## a lot of and lots of

**a lot of** and **lots of** mean the same: a big number or a big amount. **lots of** is a little more informal. They are easy because they work with **both** kinds of noun, and in **every** kind of sentence: positive, negative and question.

<Example context="home" fa="کتاب‌های زیادی دارد.">She has <mark>a lot of</mark> books.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="آب زیاد بنوش.">Drink <mark>lots of</mark> water.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="تکلیف زیاد داری؟">Do you have <mark>a lot of</mark> homework?</Example>

**The verb agrees with the noun**, not with *lot*:

- plural noun → plural verb: *A lot of people **are** waiting. Lots of shops **were** closed.*
- uncountable noun → singular verb: *A lot of money **is** spent on food. Lots of time **was** lost.*

<Example context="news" fa="آدم‌های زیادی در صف منتظرند."><mark>A lot of</mark> people <mark>are</mark> waiting in line.</Example>

**No noun after it? Drop *of*.** *“How many emails did you get?” “**A lot.**” · I learned **a lot**.* (*a lot* after a verb, as in *I like it a lot*, is <LessonRef>lesson 40 (too, enough, very)</LessonRef>.)

Watch the small words: it is **a lot of** (never ~~a lots of~~), and **of** is needed before a noun (never ~~a lot people~~).

## a few and a little

**a few** = a small number. It goes with **plural** nouns.
**a little** = a small amount. It goes with **uncountable** nouns.

Both have a **positive** feeling: "some, not a lot, but enough".

<FormTable caption="a few and a little">

| | Noun | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **a few** | plural (countable) | a few eggs · a few minutes · a few friends |
| **a little** | uncountable | a little milk · a little time · a little money |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="چند تا تخم‌مرغ در یخچال هست.">There are <mark>a few</mark> eggs in the fridge.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="چند دقیقه وقت داری؟">Do you have <mark>a few</mark> minutes?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="کمی شیر برای قهوه‌ام لازم دارم.">I need <mark>a little</mark> milk for my coffee.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کمی فرانسوی بلدم.">I speak <mark>a little</mark> French.</Example>

You can add **only** or **just**: *There are **only a few** seats left. Just **a little** sugar, please.*

**a bit of** means the same as *a little*. It is informal: *I need **a bit of** help.* **a little** and **a bit** also go before adjectives: *I'm **a little** tired. It's **a bit** cold.*

<UkNote>
British speakers use **a bit of** very often in conversation: *a bit of cake, a bit of help*. In American English, **a little** is more common.
</UkNote>

**few** and **little** without *a* have a different, negative meaning (*Few people came.* = almost nobody). That is <LessonRef>B2 lesson 36 (Quantifiers 3: few or a few?)</LessonRef>. Other quantity words (*plenty of, quite a few, a great deal of*) are in the same lesson.

## many of, a few of, a lot of + the, my, them

Do you mean a **particular** group (the students in my class, my friends, these photos)? Then put **of** after the quantity word, and then **the, my, this, these** or a pronoun (**them, us, it**).

<Example context="school" fa="خیلی از دانشجوهای کلاسم هراتی‌اند."><mark>Many of the</mark> students in my class are from Herat.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="بعضی از دوستانم در کانادا زندگی می‌کنند."><mark>A few of my</mark> friends live in Canada.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="خیلی از آن‌ها خوب‌اند.">I took fifty photos. <mark>A lot of them</mark> are really good.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="کمی از شیر ریخت."><mark>A little of the</mark> milk got spilled.</Example>

Don't put **of** straight before a noun: *many of ~~students~~* is wrong. Say **many students** (people in general) or **many of the students** (a particular group).

## too much and too many

**too much** and **too many** mean **more than you want or need**. They describe a **problem**. Compare:

- *There's **a lot of** food.* (a big amount, no problem)
- *There's **too much** food. We can't eat it all.* (a problem)

<FormTable caption="too much and too many">

| | Noun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **too many** | plural | There are **too many** cars downtown. |
| **too much** | uncountable | There's **too much** sugar in this tea. |
| **too much** (alone) | after a verb | You worry **too much**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="news" fa="موترها در شهر بیش از حد زیادند.">There are <mark>too many</mark> cars in the city. The air is terrible.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="بیش از حد قهوه می‌خورم.">I drink <mark>too much</mark> coffee. I can't sleep.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="بیش از حد کار می‌کنی. استراحت کن!">You work <mark>too much</mark>. Take a break!</Example>

Two traps:

- Before an **adjective**, use **too** alone, not *too much*: *This bag is **too expensive**.* (not ~~too much expensive~~). More on *too* + adjective in <LessonRef>lesson 40 (too, enough, very)</LessonRef>.
- **too much** is never "very much". To say you like something strongly, say *I like it **very much*** or *I like it **a lot***, not ~~I like it too much~~.

## more

**more** means an **extra** number or amount. It works with **both** kinds of noun.

<Example context="work" fa="به وقت بیشتری نیاز دارم.">I need <mark>more</mark> time.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هر سال دانشجوهای بیشتری آنلاین درس می‌خوانند.">Every year, <mark>more</mark> students study online.</Example>

Put a small word or a number **before** *more*:

<FormTable caption="more with other words">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **some more** (positive, offers) | Would you like **some more** tea? |
| **any more** (negative, question) | Are there **any more** sandwiches? |
| **no more** | I have **no more** questions. |
| number + **more** | We need **two more** chairs. · I have **one more** question. |
| **a few more / a little more** | Can I have **a little more** time? |
| **more of** + the / my / it / them | Have **more of** the cake. · I want to see **more of** it. |
| **more** alone | Tell me **more**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="دو تا صندلی دیگر لازم داریم.">We need <mark>two more</mark> chairs for the guests.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="فقط یک سؤال دیگر.">Just <mark>one more</mark> question, please.</Example>

The number comes **first**: **two more** chairs, not ~~more two chairs~~. And put **of** before *the, my, it*: **more of the** cake, not ~~more the cake~~.

*more* also makes comparatives (*more expensive*), in <LessonRef>lesson 37 (Comparatives)</LessonRef>. The opposite words, *less* and *fewer*, are in <LessonRef>lesson 38 (Superlatives)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>B1 lesson 45 (Comparatives 2: much better, more and more, the … the)</LessonRef>.

**خلاصه:** در انگلیسی برای «زیاد»، «کم» و «بیشتر» باید اول ببینیم اسم **شمردنی** است یا **غیرشمردنی**.

- **many** + اسم جمع: many friends (دوستان زیاد)
- **much** + اسم غیرشمردنی: much time (وقت زیاد)
- **a lot of / lots of** + هر دو نوع: a lot of friends، a lot of time

**نکته‌ی مهم:** much و many بیشتر در جمله‌ی **منفی** و **سؤالی** می‌آیند: I don't have much time. (وقت زیادی ندارم.) / Were there many people? (آدم زیادی بود؟) در جمله‌ی **مثبت** معمولاً a lot of می‌گوییم: We have a lot of time، نه We have much time. (much در جمله‌ی مثبت خیلی رسمی و عجیب به نظر می‌رسد.) بعد از too و so مشکلی نیست: too much، so many.

**تفاوت با فارسی:** در فارسی «زیاد» و «خیلی» را برای همه‌ی اسم‌ها به کار می‌بریم: «دوستان زیاد»، «پول زیاد». در انگلیسی باید انتخاب کنیم. همچنین «خیلی کار دارم» را نباید کلمه‌به‌کلمه ترجمه کنیم (I have very work): درستش I have a lot of work است.

**فعل با اسم هماهنگ می‌شود:** A lot of people **are** here. (اسم جمع ← فعل جمع) / A lot of money **is** spent. (اسم غیرشمردنی ← فعل مفرد). اگر بعدش اسمی نیاید، of را نمی‌گوییم: I learned a lot.

**a few و a little (کمی، چند تا):**
- **a few** + اسم جمع = «چند تا»: a few eggs (چند تا تخم‌مرغ)
- **a little** + اسم غیرشمردنی = «کمی / یه کم»: a little milk (کمی شیر)

در فارسی محاوره‌ای «یه کم» را برای همه‌چیز می‌گوییم («یه کم سیب»)، اما در انگلیسی a little فقط با اسم غیرشمردنی می‌آید: a few apples، نه a little apples. هر دو معنی مثبت دارند: «کم، ولی کافی». a bit of هم یعنی a little و غیررسمی است.

**many of / a few of:** وقتی منظور گروه مشخصی است، مثل فارسی «خیلی از دوستانم»، بعد از of باید the، my یا ضمیر بیاید: many of my friends، a few of them. نگویید many of students؛ بگویید many students یا many of the students.

**too much / too many = بیش از حد:** در فارسی «خیلی» گاهی یعنی «زیاد» و گاهی یعنی «بیش از حد». در انگلیسی این دو فرق دارند:
- a lot of = زیاد (بدون مشکل): There's a lot of food.
- too much / too many = بیش از حد (مشکل دارد): There's too much food. We can't eat it all.

too many + اسم جمع (too many cars)، too much + اسم غیرشمردنی (too much noise). قبل از صفت فقط too می‌آید: too expensive، نه too much expensive. «خیلی دوستت دارم» یا «خیلی ممنون» را با too much نگویید: I like it very much / a lot، Thank you very much.

**more (بیشتر / دیگر):** با هر دو نوع اسم: more time (وقت بیشتر)، more chairs. برای «دو تا صندلی دیگر» عدد **قبل از** more می‌آید: two more chairs (نه more two chairs). همچنین: some more tea (کمی چای دیگر)، any more questions?، no more questions، one more question (یک سؤال دیگر).

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="We have much time." right="We have a lot of time." why="In positive sentences, a lot of is natural. much sounds very formal." />
<WrongRight wrong="I have very work today." right="I have a lot of work today." why="very goes with adjectives (very busy). For a big amount, use a lot of." />
<WrongRight wrong="There aren't much buses at night." right="There aren't many buses at night." why="buses is plural → many." />
<WrongRight wrong="I need a few water." right="I need a little water." why="water is uncountable → a little." />
<WrongRight wrong="There are a lot people here." right="There are a lot of people here." why="Before a noun, a lot needs of." />
<WrongRight wrong="This hotel is too much expensive." right="This hotel is too expensive." why="too + adjective. too much + noun: too much money." />
<WrongRight wrong="Thank you too much!" right="Thank you very much!" why="too much means more than is good. very much and so much are the strong, polite forms." />
<WrongRight wrong="Can I have more two coffees?" right="Can I have two more coffees?" why="The number comes before more." />

<MemoryHook title="Count it or pour it?">
If you can **count** it (eggs, cars, friends): **many**, **a few**, **too many**. If you can only **pour** or **measure** it (milk, time, money): **much**, **a little**, **too much**. Not sure, or a positive sentence? **a lot of** fits both.
</MemoryHook>
