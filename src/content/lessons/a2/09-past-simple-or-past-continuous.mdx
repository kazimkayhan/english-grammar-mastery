## The two past tenses side by side

You know both forms: the past simple (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 26</LessonRef>, <LessonRef>A1 lesson 27</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 7</LessonRef>) and the past continuous (<LessonRef>lesson 8 (Past continuous; when & while)</LessonRef>). This lesson is about **choosing** between them.

<FormTable caption="Past simple or past continuous?">

| | Past simple | Past continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Form | worked · went | was / were + -ing |
| How we see the action | **complete**, from start to end | **in progress**, in the middle |
| In a story | the **events**: what happened | the **background**: what was happening |
| Example | I **wrote** an email. (finished) | I **was writing** an email. (maybe not finished) |

</FormTable>

It is the same idea as in the present (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 23</LessonRef>): the **simple** form sees the whole action; the **continuous** form sees an action **in progress**.

<FormTable caption="Simple and continuous: present and past">

| | Simple | Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Present | I **work** every day. (habit) | I**'m working** now. (in progress) |
| Past | I **worked** yesterday. (complete) | I **was working** at 8 p.m. (in progress) |

</FormTable>

## Finished or in progress?

The past simple usually tells us the action was **complete**. The past continuous only tells us it was **in progress**. Maybe it finished, maybe it didn't.

<Example context="work" fa="دیروز گزارش را نوشتم. (تمامش کردم.)">I <mark>wrote</mark> the report yesterday. (It's finished.)</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="داشتم گزارش را می‌نوشتم که برق رفت. (تمامش نکردم.)">I <mark>was writing</mark> the report when the power <mark>went</mark> off. (I didn't finish it.)</Example>

Repeated actions are complete actions, so they use the **past simple**: *I **called** her three times*, not ~~I was calling her three times~~.

## Interrupted actions: long + short

The most common pattern puts the two tenses together. A **long** action was in progress (past continuous), and a **short** event happened in the middle of it (past simple). Often the short event **interrupts** the long one.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "wave", from: -0.85, to: -0.2, label: "I was walking home" }, { type: "point", at: -0.5, label: "it started to rain" }]} caption="The long action (continuous) was in progress when the short event (simple) happened." />

<FormTable caption="Long action + short event">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| past continuous + **when** + past simple | I **was walking** home **when** it **started** to rain. |
| **While** + past continuous, + past simple | **While** I **was cooking**, the smoke alarm **went** off. |
| **When** + past simple, + past continuous | **When** I **arrived**, they **were having** dinner. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="داشتیم در کوهستان رانندگی می‌کردیم که ماشین ناگهان خاموش شد.">We <mark>were driving</mark> through the mountains <mark>when</mark> the car suddenly <mark>stopped</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="وقتی داشت اسکی می‌کرد، پایش شکست.">She <mark>broke</mark> her leg <mark>while</mark> she <mark>was skiing</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="داشتم دوش می‌گرفتم که زنگ در را زدند.">I <mark>was having</mark> a shower <mark>when</mark> the doorbell <mark>rang</mark>.</Example>

Remember from <LessonRef>lesson 8</LessonRef>: **while** goes with the long action, **when** usually goes with the short event. When the *when / while* part comes first, put a comma after it.

## when: the tense changes the meaning

After a **when** clause, the past simple and the past continuous tell two different stories.

<Example context="social" fa="وقتی رسیدم، داشتند شام می‌خوردند. (قبلاً شروع کرده بودند.)">When I arrived, they <mark>were having</mark> dinner. (They started before I arrived.)</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="وقتی رسیدم، شام خوردند. (اول من رسیدم، بعد شام خوردند.)">When I arrived, they <mark>had</mark> dinner. (First I arrived. Then they had dinner.)</Example>

<Timeline items={[{ type: "wave", from: -0.85, to: -0.3, label: "they were having dinner" }, { type: "point", at: -0.6, label: "I arrived" }]} caption="When I arrived, they were having dinner: the dinner was already in progress." />

**when + past simple + past simple** = one action, then the next (see <LessonRef>lesson 7</LessonRef>). **when + past simple + past continuous** = the second action was already in progress.

## Same time or one after another?

- Two **long** actions at the **same time**: past continuous + past continuous. *While I **was cooking**, my son **was doing** his homework.*
- Actions **one after another**: past simple + past simple. *She **opened** the door, **saw** the mess and **screamed**.*

<Example context="home" fa="من آشپزی می‌کردم و پسرم تکلیفش را انجام می‌داد.">While I <mark>was cooking</mark>, my son <mark>was doing</mark> his homework.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="در را باز کرد، آن به‌هم‌ریختگی را دید و جیغ زد.">She <mark>opened</mark> the door, <mark>saw</mark> the mess and <mark>screamed</mark>.</Example>

**State verbs** (*be, know, want, like, need, have* = own) stay in the past simple, even in the background: *I **was** tired and I **didn't know** anyone*, not ~~I wasn't knowing anyone~~.

## Telling a story in the past

A good short story has three parts. Each part has its favorite tense.

<FormTable caption="How to build a story">

| Part | What you say | Tense | Useful words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The scene** | when, where, the weather, who was there, what was happening | **past continuous** (actions in progress) and past simple of **be** and state verbs | One day… · Last summer… · It was a cold night. |
| **2. The events** | what happened, in order | **past simple** | Suddenly… · Then… · When… · A few minutes later… |
| **3. The ending** | how it finished, how people felt | **past simple** | In the end… · Finally… · I felt… |

</FormTable>

Read this short story. The **background** is in the past continuous; the **events** are in the past simple.

<Example context="travel" fa="یک روز سرد در زمستان پارسال بود. با دوستم داشتیم از کابل به رشت رانندگی می‌کردیم. برف می‌بارید و هیچ‌کس در جاده نبود. ناگهان ماشین خاموش شد. سعی کردیم دوباره روشنش کنیم، ولی روشن نشد. چند دقیقه بعد، یک کشاورز با تراکتورش آمد. ماشین را تا روستا کشید. در آخر، شب را در خانه‌ی او ماندیم و آش خوشمزه‌ای خوردیم.">It was a cold day last winter. My friend and I <mark>were driving</mark> from Kabul to Rasht. It <mark>was snowing</mark>, and nobody was on the road. Suddenly the car <mark>stopped</mark>. We <mark>tried</mark> to start it again, but it didn't start. A few minutes later, a farmer <mark>came</mark> with his tractor. He <mark>pulled</mark> our car to the village. In the end, we <mark>stayed</mark> the night at his house and <mark>had</mark> a delicious soup.</Example>

- **Scene:** *It was a cold day last winter. We were driving… It was snowing… nobody was on the road.*
- **Events:** *the car stopped · we tried · a farmer came · he pulled our car.* Each verb moves the story forward.
- **Ending:** *In the end, we stayed… and had…*

Some tips for good stories:

- **Start with a time phrase**: *One day, Last summer, Two years ago, It was a Friday evening.*
- **Use suddenly** to start the big event. It almost always goes with the **past simple**: *Suddenly the lights **went** out.*
- **Keep the events in order.** If you need to go back to something earlier, English uses the past perfect (*I realized I **had left** my phone at home*). That is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 9 (Past perfect)</LessonRef>. All the story tenses together come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 3 (Narrative tenses & past perfect continuous)</LessonRef>.
- **More linking words** for the order of events (*first, after that, finally*) are in <LessonRef>lesson 46 (Sequencing: first, then, after that, finally)</LessonRef>.

**گذشته‌ی ساده یا گذشته‌ی استمراری؟** هر دو درباره‌ی گذشته‌اند، اما کار را از دو زاویه می‌بینند:

- **past simple** (گذشته‌ی ساده) کار را **کامل** می‌بیند، از اول تا آخر: I wrote the report. (گزارش را نوشتم = تمام شد.)
- **past continuous** (گذشته‌ی استمراری) کار را **در جریان** می‌بیند، وسط کار: I was writing the report. (داشتم گزارش را می‌نوشتم = شاید تمام نشد.)

**کار طولانی + اتفاق کوتاه:** این رایج‌ترین ترکیب است. کار طولانی با past continuous و اتفاق کوتاهی که وسط آن پیش آمد با past simple:

- I was walking home when it started to rain. (داشتم به خانه می‌رفتم که باران گرفت.)
- While I was cooking, the smoke alarm went off. (وقتی داشتم آشپزی می‌کردم، دزدگیر دود به صدا درآمد.)

**خبر خوب برای فارسی‌زبان‌ها:** فارسی هم دقیقاً همین تفاوت را دارد:

- «وقتی رسیدم، **داشتند شام می‌خوردند**» ← When I arrived, they were having dinner. (شام را قبلاً شروع کرده بودند.)
- «وقتی رسیدم، **شام خوردند**» ← When I arrived, they had dinner. (اول من رسیدم، بعد شام خوردند.)

پس هر جا در فارسی «داشتم ... می‌کردم» می‌گویید، در انگلیسی معمولاً past continuous لازم است. اشتباه رایج این است که برای کار در جریان، گذشته‌ی ساده به کار ببریم: I walked home when it started to rain یعنی «اول باران گرفت، بعد پیاده به خانه رفتم»، نه «داشتم می‌رفتم که باران گرفت».

**کارهای تکراری و پشت سر هم:** با past simple: I called her three times. (سه بار به او زنگ زدم.) / She opened the door and screamed. (در را باز کرد و جیغ زد.) در فارسی گاهی «زنگ می‌زدم» می‌گوییم، اما در انگلیسی برای کار کامل و تکراری I was calling نمی‌گوییم.

**فعل‌های حالت** (be، know، want، like) حتی در پس‌زمینه‌ی داستان ing نمی‌گیرند: I didn't know anyone. (هیچ‌کس را نمی‌شناختم.) با اینکه در فارسی «نمی‌شناختم» استمراری است.

**تعریف کردن داستان:** یک داستان کوتاه سه بخش دارد:

- **صحنه:** زمان، مکان، هوا و کارهایی که در جریان بود ← past continuous: It was snowing. We were driving to Rasht.
- **اتفاق‌ها:** به ترتیب زمان ← past simple: Suddenly the car stopped. A farmer came.
- **پایان:** In the end, we stayed at his house.

کلمه‌های مفید: One day (یک روز)، suddenly (ناگهان)، then (بعد)، a few minutes later (چند دقیقه بعد)، in the end (در آخر). بعد از suddenly تقریباً همیشه past simple می‌آید.

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="I walked home when it started to rain." right="I was walking home when it started to rain." why="The walk was in progress when the rain started: past continuous for the long action." />
<WrongRight wrong="While I was sleeping, the phone was ringing." right="While I was sleeping, the phone rang." why="The phone call is a short event that interrupts: past simple." />
<WrongRight wrong="I was calling you five times!" right="I called you five times!" why="Repeated, complete actions: past simple." />
<WrongRight wrong="Suddenly the lights were going out." right="Suddenly the lights went out." why="suddenly introduces an event: past simple." />
<WrongRight wrong="I wasn't knowing anyone at the party." right="I didn't know anyone at the party." why="know is a state verb: past simple, even in the background." />
<WrongRight wrong="I was reading the whole book on the plane. It was great!" right="I read the whole book on the plane. It was great!" why="A complete, finished action: past simple." />

<MemoryHook title="The stage and the actors">
A story is a play. The **past continuous** builds the **stage**: the weather, the place, what people were doing. The **past simple** is the **actors moving**: each verb is one step forward in the story. *It was raining* (stage). *A man walked in* (action!).
</MemoryHook>
