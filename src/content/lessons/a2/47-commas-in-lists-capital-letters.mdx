## Commas in lists

When you write a list of **three or more** things, put a **comma** between the items. Before the **last** item, use **and** (or **or** in a question or a choice).

<FormTable caption="Commas in lists">

| Items | Example |
|---|---|
| two | I bought **eggs and milk**. (no comma) |
| three | I bought **eggs, milk and bread**. |
| four | I bought **eggs, milk, bread and rice**. |
| a choice | Do you want **tea, coffee or juice**? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="shopping" fa="تخم‌مرغ، شیر، نان و برنج خریدم.">I bought <mark>eggs, milk, bread and rice</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در سفرمان هرات، مزار شریف و یزد را دیدیم.">On our trip we saw <mark>Herat, Mazar-i-Sharif and Yazd</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="چای، قهوه یا آبمیوه میل دارید؟">Would you like <mark>tea, coffee or juice</mark>?</Example>

### Use and only once

In English, **and** comes only **once**, before the last item. Don't repeat it between every item:

- ~~We visited Herat and Mazar-i-Sharif and Yazd.~~
- We visited **Herat, Mazar-i-Sharif and Yazd**.

### A comma before and?

Some writers put a comma before the final **and** as well: *eggs, milk, **and** bread.* This comma is common in **American** English and less common in **British** English. **Both are correct.** Choose one style and use it every time. (More about this comma, often called the *Oxford comma*, in <LessonRef>C2 lesson 28</LessonRef>, Expert punctuation.)

<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً گزارش، فاکتورها و قرارداد را بفرستید.">Please send the report, the invoices<mark>, and</mark> the contract.</Example>

### Lists of actions and descriptions

A list can be **verbs** or whole **actions**, too:

<Example context="home" fa="در را باز کرد، چراغ را روشن کرد و نشست.">She <mark>opened the door, turned on the light and sat down</mark>.</Example>

It can also be **adjectives** after a verb like *be*:

<Example context="travel" fa="هتل تمیز، آرام و ارزان بود.">The hotel was <mark>clean, quiet and cheap</mark>.</Example>

Before a noun, we often put a comma between two adjectives of the same kind: *a **long, hot** summer.* The order of adjectives (*a big old house*, with no comma) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 47</LessonRef>, Adjective order (first steps).

## Capital letters: days, months, languages, nationalities and titles

You know from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 2</LessonRef> (Capital letters, periods & question marks) that sentences, **I** and names take capitals. These words take a capital letter **everywhere** in a sentence, too:

<FormTable caption="More words with capitals">

| Word type | Examples |
|---|---|
| days and months | **M**onday, **F**riday, **J**une, **D**ecember |
| holidays | **N**owruz, **R**amadan, **C**hristmas, **N**ew **Y**ear's **D**ay |
| languages | **P**ersian, **E**nglish, **A**rabic, **T**urkish |
| nationalities (nouns and adjectives) | **I**ranian, **C**anadian, an **I**talian restaurant |
| titles before a name | **M**r. **K**arimi, **M**s. **R**ahimi, **D**r. **A**hmadi, **P**rofessor **L**ee |
| titles of books and films | *The **L**ord of the **R**ings*, *A **S**eparation* |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="کلاس فرانسه‌مان سه‌شنبه‌ها و پنجشنبه‌هاست.">Our <mark>French</mark> class is on <mark>Tuesdays</mark> and <mark>Thursdays</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="در نوروز به خانه‌ی پدربزرگ و مادربزرگم می‌رویم.">We visit my grandparents at <mark>Nowruz</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="یک فروشگاه ژاپنی تازه در خیابان ما باز شده.">A new <mark>Japanese</mark> store opened on our street.</Example>

Nationality words keep their capital even as an **adjective** before a noun: *Afghan food, an English teacher, Chinese tea.*

In the **titles of books and films**, use a capital for the **first word** and the **important words**. Small words in the middle (*the, of, a, in*) usually stay small: *The Lord **of the** Rings.*

### Small letters

- **Seasons**: *spring, summer, fall, winter.*
- A **job** or **family word** without a name: *my **doctor**, our **teacher**, my **mom**.* But: ***Dr.** Ahmadi* (title + name).
- When you use **Mom** or **Dad** instead of a name, give it a capital: *Can I go out, **Mom**?* But: *my **mom** is a nurse.*

<Example context="health" fa="دکترم گفت بیشتر بخوابم. دکتر احمدی خیلی مهربان است.">My <mark>doctor</mark> told me to sleep more. <mark>Dr.</mark> Ahmadi is very kind.</Example>

<UkNote>British English usually writes titles with **no period**: **Mr** Karimi, **Mrs** Hosseini, **Dr** Ahmadi. American English uses a period: **Mr.**, **Mrs.**, **Ms.**, **Dr.**</UkNote>

## Nationality words

For every country there is a **nationality adjective**, and often a **language** with the same or a similar word. All of these take **capital** letters.

<FormTable caption="Countries, nationalities and languages">

| Country | Nationality (adjective) | A person | Main language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Afghanistan | **Afghan** | an Afghan | **Persian** (Farsi) |
| Turkey | **Turkish** | a Turk | Turkish |
| Iraq | **Iraqi** | an Iraqi | Arabic |
| Germany | **German** | a German | German |
| China | **Chinese** | a Chinese person | Chinese |
| Japan | **Japanese** | a Japanese person | Japanese |
| Spain | **Spanish** | a Spaniard | Spanish |
| Britain / the UK | **British** | a British person | English |
| the Netherlands | **Dutch** | a Dutch person | Dutch |
| Switzerland | **Swiss** | a Swiss person | German, French, Italian… |

</FormTable>

### I'm Afghan: use the adjective

To say someone's nationality, the normal way is **be + the adjective**:

<Example context="travel" fa="«اهل کجایی؟» «افغان‌ام. اهل بامیانم.»">“Where are you from?” “I'm <mark>Afghan</mark>. I'm from Bamyan.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="همکار جدیدمان سوئیسی است و آلمانی و فرانسوی صحبت می‌کند.">Our new colleague is <mark>Swiss</mark>. She speaks <mark>German</mark> and <mark>French</mark>.</Example>

- The **country** is not the nationality: *I'm **Afghan***, not ~~I'm Afghanistan~~.
- Some nationality words are **only adjectives**. Don't put *a* before them: *She's **Dutch***, not ~~She's a Dutch~~. *He's **Chinese***, or *He's a **Chinese man***.
- A language has **no the** and usually no *language*: *I speak **Persian***, not ~~I speak the Persian language~~.

### Afghan or Persian?

- **Afghan** = the nationality (a person from Afghanistan, or something from Afghanistan today): *Afghan students, the Afghan team.*
- **Persian** = the **language** (also called **Farsi**) and things from Persian culture and history: *Persian poetry, a Persian carpet, the Persian Empire.*

<Example context="school" fa="من افغان‌ام و زبان مادری‌ام فارسی است.">I'm <mark>Afghan</mark>, and my first language is <mark>Persian</mark>.</Example>

Talking about a whole nation (*the Dutch, the Swiss, the Chinese*) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 37</LessonRef>, Articles 2: places, institutions, the rich.

## Speaking to someone: names and commas

When you speak **to** a person and use their name or title, that word is separated from the rest of the sentence with a **comma** (two commas in the middle).

<FormTable caption="Commas with names">

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| at the start | **John,** come here. |
| at the end | Come here**, John.** |
| in the middle | I think**, Zahra,** you're right. |
| with a greeting | Good morning**, everyone.** · Hi**, Karim.** |
| with a title | Thank you**, Mr. Smith.** · Is it serious**, Doctor?** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="احمد، شام آماده است!"><mark>Ahmad,</mark> dinner's ready!</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ممنون از کمکتان، خانم رحیمی.">Thanks for your help<mark>, Ms. Rahimi</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="ببخشید آقای کریمی، می‌شود سؤالی بپرسم؟">Excuse me<mark>, Mr. Karimi,</mark> can I ask a question?</Example>

The comma matters. *Let's eat**,** Grandma!* (you speak to Grandma) is very different from *Let's eat Grandma!*

At the start of an email, the careful form is **Hi, Zahra,** but many people leave out the first comma: **Hi Zahra,** In a letter, there is **no comma after Dear**: **Dear Mr. Smith,**

### Which name do you use?

<FormTable caption="Forms of address">

| Who? | What to say |
|---|---|
| friends, family, people your age | first name: *Hi, Zahra.* |
| teachers, older people, formal situations | **Mr. / Ms. / Mrs. / Dr.** + **family name**: *Good morning, Ms. Rahimi.* |
| a stranger (to get attention) | **Excuse me**: *Excuse me, is this seat free?* |
| a customer (in a shop, a hotel) | **sir** / **ma'am** (American English): *Can I help you, sir?* |

</FormTable>

- **Mr., Ms., Mrs.** go with the **family name**, not the first name alone: *Mr. **Karimi***, not ~~Mr. Karim~~.
- **Ms.** works for any woman, married or not. It is a safe choice.
- Don't call your teacher ~~Teacher~~. Use their name: *Mr. Karimi, Ms. Rahimi* (or the first name, if your teacher asks you to).
- **Mr., Ms.** and **Mrs.** are not used alone, without a name: not ~~Excuse me, Mr.~~ But you can say **Doctor** alone to your doctor: *Thank you, Doctor.*

**کاما در فهرست:** وقتی سه چیز یا بیشتر را نام می‌بریم، بینشان **کاما (,)** می‌گذاریم و فقط **قبل از آخرین مورد** and (یا در سؤال، or) می‌آوریم: I bought eggs, milk and bread. (تخم‌مرغ، شیر و نان خریدم.) برای دو مورد کاما لازم نیست: tea and coffee.

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟** در فارسی گاهی بین همه‌ی موردها «و» می‌گوییم: «هرات و مزار شریف و یزد را دیدیم». در انگلیسی and فقط یک بار می‌آید: Herat, Mazar-i-Sharif and Yazd. همچنین کامای فارسی (،) برعکس است؛ در متن انگلیسی همیشه کامای انگلیسی (,) بنویسید.

- روزها و ماه‌ها: Monday، June
- تعطیلات و جشن‌ها: Nowruz، Ramadan
- زبان‌ها: Persian، English
- ملیت‌ها، حتی وقتی صفت‌اند: Afghan food (غذای افغان)، an English teacher
- عنوان قبل از اسم: Mr. Karimi، Dr. Ahmadi

**صدا زدن کسی (خطاب):** وقتی کسی را با اسم یا عنوانش صدا می‌زنیم، اسم را با **کاما** از بقیه‌ی جمله جدا می‌کنیم: John, come here. / Come here, John. / Thank you, Mr. Smith.

<MemoryHook title="Commas for lists and names, capitals for names of things">
A **comma** separates: items in a **list** (A, B and C) and the **name** of the person you're talking to (*Come here, John*). A **capital** marks a **name**: a person, a day, a month, a holiday, a language or a nationality. Seasons and ordinary nouns stay small.
</MemoryHook>
