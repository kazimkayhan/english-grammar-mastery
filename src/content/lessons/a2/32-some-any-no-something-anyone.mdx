## Quick review: some and any

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 15 (Countable & uncountable nouns; some, any, how much, how many)</LessonRef> you learned the basic rule:

<FormTable caption="some and any: the basic rule">

| Sentence | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive (+) | **some** | There are **some** eggs. |
| Negative (−) | **any** | There aren't **any** eggs. |
| Question (?) | **any** | Are there **any** eggs? |

</FormTable>

This lesson adds four things: the questions that take **some**, the positive sentences that take **any**, the word **no**, and the words made from them (*something, anyone, nowhere…*).

## some in offers and requests

Most questions take *any*. But when you **offer** something or **ask for** something, use **some**. Why? Because you expect the answer **yes**.

<FormTable caption="Questions with some">

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Offer | **Would you like some** tea? · Do you want **some** help? |
| Request | **Can I have some** water, please? · Could you lend me **some** money? |
| You expect yes | Did you buy **some** bread? (I think you did.) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="کیک میل دارید؟ امروز صبح پختمش.">Would you like <mark>some</mark> cake? I made it this morning.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="می‌شود کمی آب به من بدهید؟">Can I have <mark>some</mark> water, please?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="کمک لازم داری؟">Do you need <mark>some</mark> help with that box?</Example>

Compare a real question, where you don't know the answer: *Are there **any** eggs?* (yes or no, I don't know) and an offer: *Would you like **some** eggs?* (I hope you say yes). The same is true for the *-thing* words: *Would you like **something** to drink?*

## any = it doesn't matter which

**any** can also go in a **positive** sentence. Then it means "**it doesn't matter which**": you can choose. With this meaning, *any* often goes with a **singular** noun.

<Example context="home" fa="هر وقت خواستی به من زنگ بزن.">Call me <mark>any</mark> time.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هر اتوبوسی را سوار شوید. همه به مرکز شهر می‌روند.">Take <mark>any</mark> bus. They all go downtown.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="هر کدام را که دوست داری انتخاب کن.">Choose <mark>any</mark> color you like.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هر کسی می‌تواند آشپزی یاد بگیرد."><mark>Anyone</mark> can learn to cook.</Example>

**any or every?** Both can be «هر» in Persian, but they are different:

- **any** = one, and it doesn't matter which: *Take **any** bus.* (choose one)
- **every** = all of them, one by one: ***Every** bus goes downtown.* (all the buses)

*every* comes in <LessonRef>lesson 36 (all, both, each, every)</LessonRef>.

**any after if.** We often use *any* (and *anything, anyone*) after **if**: *If you have **any** questions, ask me. If you need **anything**, call me.*

**any after never and without.** These words are already negative, so use *any*: *I **never** have **any** free time. She left **without any** money.*

## no and none

**no + noun** means **not any** (or **not a**). The verb stays **positive**. *no* works with all nouns: singular, plural and uncountable.

<FormTable caption="no = not any">

| with no (positive verb) | with not … any (negative verb) |
|---|---|
| There's **no** milk. | There **isn't any** milk. |
| There are **no** buses on Sundays. | There **aren't any** buses on Sundays. |
| I have **no** idea. | I **don't have any** idea. |

</FormTable>

Both columns are correct. **no** sounds a little stronger. Fixed phrases use *no*: ***No** problem. **No** smoking. **No** parking.*

<Example context="home" fa="شیر نداریم. باید بخرم.">There's <mark>no</mark> milk. I need to buy some.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="یکشنبه‌ها اتوبوس نیست.">There are <mark>no</mark> buses on Sundays.</Example>

**none** is the pronoun: it stands **alone**, with **no noun after it**.

<Example context="shopping" fa="«چند تا بلیت مانده؟» «هیچ. همه فروخته شده.»">“How many tickets are left?” “<mark>None</mark>. They're all sold.”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="می‌خواستم کیک تعارف کنم، ولی هیچ نمانده.">I wanted to offer you some cake, but there's <mark>none</mark> left.</Example>

So: **no** + noun (*no tickets*), **none** without a noun (*none*). *None of* + *the / my / them* (*none of my friends*) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 40 (each, every, either, neither, both, all, whole, none)</LessonRef>.

**One negative only.** *no* and *none* are already negative, so don't add *not*: ~~There isn't no milk~~. Say *There isn't **any** milk* or *There's **no** milk*. More in <LessonRef>lesson 2 (Negatives & answering negative questions)</LessonRef>.

## something, anyone, nowhere…

Put **some-, any-, no-** or **every-** in front of **-one / -body** (people), **-thing** (things) and **-where** (places).

<FormTable caption="The compound words">

| | People | Things | Places |
|---|---|---|---|
| **some-** | someone / somebody | something | somewhere |
| **any-** | anyone / anybody | anything | anywhere |
| **no-** | no one / nobody | nothing | nowhere |
| **every-** | everyone / everybody | everything | everywhere |

</FormTable>

- **-one** and **-body** mean the same: *someone = somebody, no one = nobody*.
- **no one** is written as two words (you may also see *no-one*). The others are one word.
- **every-** means "all": *everyone* = all the people, *everywhere* = in all places.

They follow the **same rules** as *some, any* and *no*:

<FormTable caption="Which word?">

| Sentence | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | **some-** | **Someone** is at the door. |
| Offer / request | **some-** | Would you like **something** to eat? |
| Negative | **any-** | I didn't see **anybody**. |
| Question | **any-** | Is there **anything** in the fridge? |
| It doesn't matter which | **any-** | Sit **anywhere**. You can eat **anything**. |
| Zero (positive verb) | **no-** | **Nobody** called. There's **nothing** here. |
| All | **every-** | **Everyone** knows her. I looked **everywhere**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="یک نفر پشت در است. می‌شود در را باز کنی؟"><mark>Someone</mark> is at the door. Can you open it?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="کلیدهایم را جایی نمی‌بینم. همه‌جا را گشتم!">I can't find my keys <mark>anywhere</mark>. I looked <mark>everywhere</mark>!</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="هیچ‌کس زنگ نزد.">No messages? No, <mark>nobody</mark> called.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="کسی در آتش‌سوزی آسیب ندید."><mark>No one</mark> was hurt in the fire.</Example>

**One negative only** works here too: *I **didn't** see **anybody*** or *I saw **nobody***, never ~~I didn't see nobody~~.

**everybody, not all.** To mean "all the people", say **everybody** or **everyone**, not *all* alone: ***Everybody** stood up* (not ~~All stood up~~). More on *all* in <LessonRef>lesson 36 (all, both, each, every)</LessonRef>.

## A singular verb

All these words take a **singular** verb (*is, was, has, knows*), even when they mean many people or things.

<Example context="work" fa="همه اینجا هستند. جلسه را شروع کنیم."><mark>Everyone is</mark> here. Let's start the meeting.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هیچ‌کس جواب را نمی‌داند."><mark>Nobody knows</mark> the answer.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="یک چیزی بوی خوبی می‌دهد! چی می‌پزی؟"><mark>Something smells</mark> good! What are you cooking?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="همه‌چیز عالی بود."><mark>Everything was</mark> perfect.</Example>

In questions too: ***Is** anybody there? **Does** anyone have a pen?*

After these words, we often use **they / their** to talk about the person: *Someone left **their** phone here.* That is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 43 (Generic you, one, they; each other)</LessonRef>.

## something to eat, nothing interesting

### + to + verb

Put **to + verb** after these words to say what the thing, person or place is **for**.

<Example context="home" fa="گرسنه‌ام. چیزی برای خوردن هست؟">I'm hungry. Is there <mark>anything to eat</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="حوصله‌ام سر رفته. اینجا کاری برای انجام دادن نیست.">I'm bored. There's <mark>nothing to do</mark> here.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="دنبال جایی برای اقامت هستیم.">We're looking for <mark>somewhere to stay</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="به کسی نیاز دارم که با او حرف بزنم.">I need <mark>someone to talk</mark> to.</Example>

Use **to**, not *for*: *something **to** eat*, not ~~something for eat~~.

### + adjective

An **adjective** comes **after** these words, not before.

<Example context="news" fa="امروز چیز جالبی در روزنامه نیست.">There's <mark>nothing interesting</mark> in the news today.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="بیا برای شام برویم یک جای ساکت.">Let's go <mark>somewhere quiet</mark> for dinner.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="یک چیز ارزان‌تر دارید؟">Do you have <mark>anything cheaper</mark>?</Example>

Not ~~interesting something~~ or ~~quiet somewhere~~. Other nouns don't work like this (*an interesting book*). Adjectives after nouns come again in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 38 (Adjective order & compound adjectives)</LessonRef>.

**else** works the same way: *something **else*** (a different thing), *someone **else*** (a different person), *nowhere **else***. In a shop: *Anything **else**?*

<Persian>

**some در تعارف و درخواست:** معمولاً در سؤال any می‌آید، اما وقتی چیزی **تعارف** می‌کنیم یا **درخواست** می‌کنیم، some می‌گوییم، چون انتظار جواب «بله» داریم:
- Would you like some tea? (چای میل دارید؟)
- Can I have some water, please? (می‌شود کمی آب بدهید؟)
- Would you like something to drink? (چیزی میل دارید؟)

**any = «هر … که بخواهی»:** در جمله‌ی مثبت، any یعنی «فرقی نمی‌کند کدام»: Call me any time. (هر وقت خواستی زنگ بزن.) / Take any bus. (هر اتوبوسی را سوار شو.) دقت کنید: در فارسی «هر» هم برای any است و هم برای every، ولی در انگلیسی فرق دارند: any یعنی «یکی، هر کدام که خواستی» و every یعنی «همه، تک‌تک». بعد از if هم any می‌آید: If you have any questions, ask me.

**no و none:** no + اسم یعنی «هیچ … نیست» و فعل جمله **مثبت** می‌ماند: There's no milk. (شیر نداریم.) این همان There isn't any milk است. none تنها می‌آید و بعدش اسم نمی‌آید: “How many are left?” “None.” (هیچی.)

**مهم‌ترین تفاوت با فارسی:** در فارسی دو منفی با هم می‌آیند: «هیچ‌کس **نیامد**»، «هیچ شیری **نداریم**». در انگلیسی فقط **یک** منفی: Nobody came. (نه Nobody didn't come) / There's no milk یا There isn't any milk (نه There isn't no milk).

**کلمه‌های ترکیبی:**
- some + one / body / thing / where ← someone (کسی)، something (چیزی)، somewhere (جایی)
- any ← anyone، anything، anywhere (در منفی و سؤالی، یا «هر کسی / هر چیزی / هر جایی»)
- no ← no one / nobody (هیچ‌کس)، nothing (هیچ‌چیز)، nowhere (هیچ‌جا)
- every ← everyone / everybody (همه)، everything (همه‌چیز)، everywhere (همه‌جا)

دو پسوند one و body معنی یکسانی دارند: someone = somebody.

**فعل مفرد:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «همه **آمدند**» یا «همه اینجا **هستند**» (فعل جمع). اما در انگلیسی everyone و everybody با فعل **مفرد** می‌آیند: Everyone **is** here. / Everybody **knows** him. / Nobody **likes** it. برای «همه» به معنی «همه‌ی آدم‌ها» از everybody استفاده کنید، نه all به‌تنهایی.

**چیزی برای خوردن:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «چیزی **برای** خوردن»، ولی در انگلیسی for نمی‌آید؛ to + فعل: something **to** eat، nothing **to** do، somewhere **to** stay.

**صفت بعد از کلمه:** خبر خوب! اینجا انگلیسی شبیه فارسی است: «یه چیز **جالب**» ← something interesting، «یه جای **ساکت**» ← somewhere quiet. صفت **بعد از** something و somewhere می‌آید (نه interesting something).

</Persian>

## Common mistakes

<MemoryHook title="Some says yes">
**some** expects **yes**: positive sentences, offers, requests. **any** is open: negatives, real questions, and "it doesn't matter which". **no** is zero, with a positive verb. And everyone, something, nobody? Just **one** thing each: a **singular** verb.
</MemoryHook>
