## The five forms of a verb

Most English verbs have **five forms**. The dictionary shows the first one, the **base form**.

<FormTable caption="The five verb forms">

| Form | Regular: work | Irregular: go | Irregular: write | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **base** | work | go | write | after *to*, after *do / does / did* and modals, orders: *I want to **go**. Did you **go**? **Go**!* |
| **-s form** | works | goes | writes | present simple with he / she / it: *She **goes** to work by bus.* |
| **-ing form** | working | going | writing | continuous tenses: *She is **going** home.* |
| **past** | worked | went | wrote | past simple (positive): *She **went** home.* |
| **past participle** | worked | gone | written | perfect tenses and the passive: *She has **gone**. It was **written** in 1990.* |

</FormTable>

- The **-s** and **-ing** forms follow simple spelling rules (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 17</LessonRef>, Present simple: positive, and <LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>, Present continuous). The only irregular -s forms are **has** and **is**.
- Some books count **four** forms, because the -s form is just the base + -s. It is the same system.
- **be** has more forms than any other verb: *be, am, is, are, was, were, being, been*.
- **Modal verbs** (*can, must, will, should*) have only one form: no -s, no -ing, no past participle. See <LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>, can / can't (and how modal verbs work).

<Example context="work" fa="او هر روز با اتوبوس سر کار می‌رود.">She <mark>goes</mark> to work by bus every day.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="الان دارد سر کار می‌رود.">She<mark>'s going</mark> to work now.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دیروز با تاکسی رفت.">Yesterday she <mark>went</mark> by taxi.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او همین الان رفته است؛ اینجا نیست.">She<mark>'s</mark> just <mark>gone</mark>. She isn't here.</Example>

## The past participle (third form)

The **past participle** is the third form in lists like *go – went – gone*. You need it for the present perfect (<LessonRef>lesson 15</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 16</LessonRef>), so learn it now.

**Regular verbs:** the past participle is the same as the past: **-ed**. *work – worked – worked · play – played – played · study – studied – studied · stop – stopped – stopped.* (Spelling: <LessonRef>A1 lesson 26</LessonRef>, Past simple: regular verbs.)

**Irregular verbs:** learn the three forms together. They fall into four groups.

<FormTable caption="Irregular verbs: four groups (base – past – past participle)">

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| all three the same | put – put – put · cut – cut – cut · cost – cost – cost · let – let – let |
| past = past participle | buy – bought – bought · make – made – made · have – had – had · find – found – found · leave – left – left |
| base = past participle | come – came – **come** · become – became – **become** · run – ran – **run** |
| all three different | go – went – **gone** · see – saw – **seen** · do – did – **done** · be – was / were – **been** · eat – ate – **eaten** · take – took – **taken** · write – wrote – **written** · speak – spoke – **spoken** |

</FormTable>

Two patterns help you remember:

- Many past participles in the last group end in **-n** or **-en**: *seen, gone, done, eaten, taken, written, spoken, broken, given, known, flown*.
- Some verbs change **i – a – u**: *drink – drank – **drunk** · swim – swam – **swum** · begin – began – **begun***.

Watch the spelling: *write → written* and *forget → forgotten* (double t), *eat → eaten*. All the verbs, with their Persian meanings, are in the [irregular verbs list](/reference#irregular). More irregular verbs come in <LessonRef>lesson 7</LessonRef>, Past simple: more irregular verbs.

<UkNote>The past participle of **get** is **got** in British English (*I've got a cold*) and usually **gotten** in American English (*She's gotten better*).</UkNote>

**The past participle never works alone as a verb.** It needs a helper:

- **have + past participle** → present perfect: *I**'ve lost** my keys.* (<LessonRef>lesson 15</LessonRef>, Present perfect: experience)
- **had + past participle** → past perfect: *The film **had started**.* (<LessonRef>B1 lesson 9</LessonRef>, Past perfect)
- **be + past participle** → passive: *This rug **was made** in Kashan.* (<LessonRef>B1 lesson 21</LessonRef>, Passive 1: present & past simple)

It can also be an **adjective**: *a **broken** phone, a **closed** shop, **boiled** eggs*. Feelings like *bored* and *tired* come in <LessonRef>lesson 42</LessonRef>, -ed or -ing adjectives.

<Example context="home" fa="کلیدهایم را گم کرده‌ام!">I<mark>'ve lost</mark> my keys!</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این فرش در کاشان بافته شد.">This rug <mark>was made</mark> in Kashan.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="گوشی‌ام شکسته است.">My phone is <mark>broken</mark>.</Example>

## Helpers and main verbs

A sentence has one **main verb**. It carries the meaning: *work, eat, go, see*. Before it there can be a **helper** (an **auxiliary verb**). The helper adds grammar: the tense, a question, a negative. English has three helpers, **be**, **have** and **do**, plus the **modal verbs** (*can, will, must, should*).

Each helper has a **partner form** after it:

<FormTable caption="Helper + the form that follows it">

| Helper | Form after it | Makes | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **be** (am, is, are, was, were) | **-ing** | continuous tenses | She **is working**. They **were sleeping**. |
| **be** | **past participle** | the passive | It **was made** in Afghanistan. |
| **have** (have, has, had) | **past participle** | perfect tenses | I **have finished**. She**'s gone** home. |
| **do** (do, does, did) | **base** | present and past simple questions and negatives | **Do** you **like** it? He **didn't call**. |
| modals (can, will, must…) | **base** | ability, future, obligation… | I **can swim**. It **will rain**. |

</FormTable>

### What the helper does

The helper does the grammar work. The main verb doesn't move.

- **Negatives:** put **not** (**n't**) after the helper: *She **isn't** working. I **haven't** finished. He **didn't** call.*
- **Questions:** put the helper **before** the subject: ***Is** she working? **Have** you finished? **Did** he call? Where **are** you going?*
- **Short answers:** repeat the helper: *Yes, I **have**. / No, she **isn't**. / Yes, he **did**.*
- **Short forms:** the helper joins the subject or *not*: *I'm, she's, we've, didn't, haven't*.

**No helper in the sentence?** In a positive present simple or past simple sentence there is no helper (*She works. He called.*). For a question or negative, add **do / does / did**, and the main verb goes back to the **base**:

<Example context="work" fa="او در بانک کار می‌کند. ← آیا او در بانک کار می‌کند؟">She works in a bank. → <mark>Does</mark> she <mark>work</mark> in a bank?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="دیشب زنگ زد. ← دیشب زنگ نزد.">He called last night. → He <mark>didn't call</mark> last night.</Example>

**Two helpers?** Sometimes a verb has two helpers: *She **has been** working.* Then only the **first** helper moves or takes *not*: ***Has** she been working? She **hasn't** been working.* (That tense is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 8</LessonRef>, Present perfect continuous.)

## be, have and do as main verbs

The same three verbs can also be **main verbs**, with their own meaning.

<FormTable caption="Helper or main verb?">

| Verb | As a helper | As a main verb |
|---|---|---|
| **be** | She **is** sleeping. | She **is** a doctor. |
| **have** | I **have** seen it. | I **have** two sisters. · We **have** lunch at one. |
| **do** | **Do** you like tea? | I **do** yoga on Fridays. · What **do** you **do**? |

</FormTable>

- **be** as a main verb makes its questions and negatives by itself, with no *do*: *Are you tired? I wasn't at home.* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 5</LessonRef>, be: am, is, are)
- **have** and **do** as main verbs need the helper **do** in questions and negatives: ***Do** you **have** a car? I **don't have** time. **Did** you **do** your homework?* (In British English you can also say *Have you got a car?* See <LessonRef>A1 lesson 16</LessonRef>, have / have got.)
- One sentence can have the same verb twice, once as a helper and once as a main verb: *I**'ve had** lunch. What **are** you **doing**? **Did** you **do** it?*

<Example context="school" fa="«تکلیفت را انجام دادی؟» «بله، انجام دادم.»">“<mark>Did</mark> you <mark>do</mark> your homework?” “Yes, I <mark>did</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«چه کاره‌ای؟» «پرستارم.»">“What <mark>do</mark> you <mark>do</mark>?” “I'm a nurse.”</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="ناهار خورده‌ام. گرسنه نیستم.">I<mark>'ve had</mark> lunch. I'm not hungry.</Example>

Remember: **'s** can be *is* or *has*. *She's working* = she **is** working. *She's finished* = she **has** finished. The next word tells you: -ing → *is*; past participle → *has*. More in <LessonRef>lesson 6</LessonRef>, Contractions & apostrophes.

Helpers have more jobs later: *So do I* and *Me neither* in <LessonRef>lesson 3</LessonRef>, Short responses; *I can't, but she can* in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 44</LessonRef>, Ellipsis & substitution 1; and *I **do** like it!* in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 45</LessonRef>, Emphasis 1. *make* or *do* is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 16</LessonRef>, make, do, have, take, give + noun.
