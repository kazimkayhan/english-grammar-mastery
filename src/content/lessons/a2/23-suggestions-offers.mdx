## Suggestions for you and me

All these patterns mean "I think **we** should do this". After each one, use the **base form** of the verb.

<FormTable caption="Suggestions with us">

| Pattern | Example | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Let's** + verb | **Let's** go to the park. | direct, friendly |
| **Shall we** + verb? | **Shall we** go to the park? | asks the other person; common in British English |
| **Why don't we** + verb? | **Why don't we** go to the park? | friendly, a little softer |
| **We could** + verb | **We could** go to the park. | soft: just an idea |
| **How about** / **What about** + **-ing**? | **How about going** to the park? | friendly; see below |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="بیا امشب بیرون غذا بخوریم!"><mark>Let's</mark> eat out tonight!</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ده دقیقه استراحت کنیم؟"><mark>Shall we</mark> take a ten-minute break?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="چرا با قطار نرویم؟ ارزان‌تر است."><mark>Why don't we</mark> go by train? It's cheaper.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="می‌توانیم این آخر هفته آشپزخانه را رنگ کنیم."><mark>We could</mark> paint the kitchen this weekend.</Example>

- **Let's** and **Let's not** came in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 20</LessonRef>: *Let's go. Let's not wait.* Never ~~Let's we go~~ or ~~Let's to go~~.
- **Why don't we…?** looks like a question about a reason, but it is a **suggestion**. The answer is not *because…*. It is *Good idea!* or *I'm not sure.*
- **Shall we…?** is common in British English. In American English it can sound formal, and people often say **Should we…?** or **Do you want to…?**: *Should we get a pizza? Do you want to get a pizza?* (More about **shall** in <LessonRef>lesson 12</LessonRef>, will & shall.)

## How about…? What about…?

After **How about** and **What about**, use a verb in **-ing** form or a **noun**. These are the only suggestion phrases that don't take the base form.

<Example context="social" fa="چطور است این شنبه برویم کوه؟"><mark>How about going</mark> hiking on Saturday?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«کی همدیگر را ببینیم؟» «سه‌شنبه ساعت ده چطور است؟»">“When can we meet?” “<mark>How about</mark> Tuesday at ten?”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این کیف آبی چطور است؟"><mark>What about</mark> this blue bag?</Example>

In informal American speech you will also hear **How about we** + base form: *How about we order pizza?* But **How about** + base form with no subject is wrong: ~~How about go~~. *What about + -ing* is less common in American English than *How about + -ing*.

## Suggestions for another person

To suggest something for **someone else**, use **Why don't you…?**, **You could…** or **Why not…?** + base form.

<Example context="school" fa="چرا از معلمت نمی‌پرسی؟"><mark>Why don't you</mark> ask your teacher?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="می‌توانی به او ایمیل بزنی.">You <mark>could</mark> send her an email.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="چرا تاکسی نگیری؟ اتوبوس خیلی کند است."><mark>Why not</mark> take a taxi? The bus is very slow.</Example>

- **Why not** + base form has **no subject** and **no to**: *Why not take a taxi?* Not ~~Why not to take~~. It is also used for general suggestions to anyone: *Why not treat yourself to a holiday?*
- For stronger advice, use **should** (<LessonRef>lesson 20</LessonRef>): *You should take a taxi.*

## Answering a suggestion

<FormTable caption="Replying to a suggestion">

| Yes | Not sure / no |
|---|---|
| **Good idea!** / **Great idea!** | **I'm not sure.** |
| **That sounds great.** / **Sounds good.** | **Maybe another time.** |
| **Sure, why not?** | **I'd prefer to stay home.** |
| **Yes, let's.** (to *Let's…*) | **No, let's not.** (to *Let's…*) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«بیا شام ماهی بخوریم.» «فکر خوبی است!»">“<mark>Let's</mark> have fish for dinner.” “<mark>Good idea!</mark>”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«چرا نرویم سینما؟» «مطمئن نیستم. خیلی خسته‌ام.»">“<mark>Why don't we</mark> go to the cinema?” “<mark>I'm not sure.</mark> I'm really tired.”</Example>

## Offers: Shall I…? I'll… Let me…

To **offer to do something** for someone, use these patterns + base form:

<FormTable caption="Offering help">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Shall I** + verb? | **Shall I** open the window? |
| **Can I** + verb? | **Can I** help you? |
| **I'll** + verb | **I'll** carry that for you. |
| **Let me** + verb | **Let me** help you with those bags. |
| **Do you want me to** + verb? | **Do you want me to** call a taxi? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="«سرد است.» «پنجره را ببندم؟»">“It's cold in here.” “<mark>Shall I</mark> close the window?”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="بگذار آن کیسه‌ها را برایت بیاورم.">Those bags look heavy. <mark>Let me</mark> carry one.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«تلفن زنگ می‌زند!» «من جواب می‌دهم.»">“The phone's ringing!” “<mark>I'll</mark> get it.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="می‌خواهی برایت تاکسی بگیرم؟"><mark>Do you want me to</mark> call you a taxi?</Example>

- **Shall I…?** asks the other person to decide. In American English, **Should I…?** and **Do you want me to…?** are more usual.
- **I'll** for an offer you decide right now is in <LessonRef>lesson 12</LessonRef>.
- **Let me** + base form, no *to*: *Let me **help***, not ~~Let me to help~~.

### Offering things: Would you like…?

To offer **something** (food, a drink, a seat), use **Would you like** + noun. To invite someone, use **Would you like to** + verb (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 24</LessonRef>).

<Example context="social" fa="کمی کیک میل دارید؟"><mark>Would you like</mark> some cake?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="دوست داری جمعه برای ناهار بیایی؟"><mark>Would you like to</mark> come for lunch on Friday?</Example>

## Answering an offer

<FormTable caption="Replying to an offer">

| Yes | No |
|---|---|
| **Yes, please.** | **No, thanks. I'm fine.** |
| **That would be great, thanks.** | **No, don't worry. I can do it.** |
| **That's very kind of you.** | **That's OK, thanks.** |

</FormTable>

Don't answer an offer with ~~Good idea!~~ That is for suggestions. Say **Yes, please** or **Thanks, that would be great.**

**پیشنهاد دادن (برای خودمان و دیگران):** همه‌ی این‌ها یعنی «بیا… / چطور است…؟» و بعدشان فعل ساده بدون to می‌آید:

- Let's go. (بیا برویم.)
- Shall we go? (برویم؟)
- Why don't we go? (چرا نرویم؟ = بیا برویم.)
- We could go. (می‌توانیم برویم.)

**How about / What about** (چطور است…؟): بعدشان فعل ing یا اسم می‌آید: How about **going** to the beach? (چطور است برویم ساحل؟) How about Friday? (جمعه چطور است؟)

**پیشنهاد برای کس دیگر:** Why don't you ask her? (چرا از او نمی‌پرسی؟) You could try again. (می‌توانی دوباره امتحان کنی.) Why not take a taxi? (چرا تاکسی نگیری؟)

**پیشنهاد کمک (offer):** Shall I open the window? (پنجره را باز کنم؟) Can I help? I'll do it. (من انجامش می‌دهم.) Let me help you. (بگذار کمکت کنم.) Do you want me to call her? (می‌خواهی به او زنگ بزنم؟) و برای تعارف چیزی: Would you like some tea? (چای میل دارید؟)

**جواب دادن:** به پیشنهاد کاری: Good idea! / That sounds great. به تعارف یا کمک: Yes, please. / No, thanks. I'm fine.

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟**

- «چطور است برویم؟» را کلمه‌به‌کلمه نسازید (How about go?). بعد از How about فعل ing می‌آید: How about **going**?
- «چرا نرویم؟» در فارسی هم پیشنهاد است و هم سؤال. در انگلیسی Why don't we go? پیشنهاد است؛ جوابش Good idea! است، نه Because… .
- «بیا برویم» = **Let's go**، نه Let's we go و نه Let's to go.
- «می‌خواهی کمکت کنم؟» را با فعل صرف‌شده نسازید (Do you want I help you?). بگویید **Do you want me to** help you? یا **Shall I** help you?
- «پنجره را باز کنم؟» را با do نسازید (Do I open the window?). برای پیشنهاد کمک بگویید **Shall I** open the window? یا **Can I**…?
- **تعارف:** در فرهنگ افغان گاهی اول «نه» می‌گوییم و منتظر اصرار می‌مانیم. در انگلیسی معمولاً میزبان این‌قدر اصرار نمی‌کند و «نه» را جدی می‌گیرد. اگر چیزی را می‌خواهید، همان اول بگویید **Yes, please**.

**Shall** در انگلیسی بریتانیایی رایج است. در انگلیسی آمریکایی رسمی به نظر می‌رسد و بیشتر می‌گویند Should we…? یا Do you want me to…?

<MemoryHook title="The -ing pair">
Every suggestion takes the plain verb (**Let's go, Shall we go, Why don't we go, We could go, Why not go**) except one **pair**: **How about** and **What about**, which take **-ing**. *How about going?*
</MemoryHook>
