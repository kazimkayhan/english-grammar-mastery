## by: how you travel or do something

Use **by** + a noun to say **how** you travel or **how** you do something. Don't put *a*, *the* or *my* after **by**.

<FormTable caption="by + means">

| | Examples |
|---|---|
| **transport** | by bus · by car · by train · by plane · by taxi · by bike · by boat |
| **communication** | by email · by phone · by mail |
| **walking** | **on foot** (not ~~by foot~~) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="هر روز با تکسی سر کار می‌روم.">I go to work <mark>by</mark> taxi every day.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً گزارش را با ایمیل بفرستید.">Please send the report <mark>by</mark> email.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل نزدیک است. پیاده پنج دقیقه است.">The hotel is close. It's five minutes <mark>on foot</mark>.</Example>

**by** also tells you **who made** a book, a song or a painting: *a poem **by** Rumi* · *a song **by** Googoosh*. (In passive sentences, *by* shows who does the action. That is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 21 (Passive 1: present & past simple)</LessonRef>.)

<UkNote>Americans say **subway** for an underground train; British people say **the underground** or **the tube** (in London). American **mail** is British **post**: *by mail* / *by post*.</UkNote>

## in a car, on a bus

**by** is for the **way** you travel in general. When you talk about one **particular** car, bus or train (with *my, the, a, Ahmad's*…), use **in** or **on**, like for position in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 31 (Prepositions of place)</LessonRef>.

<FormTable caption="by, in or on?">

| General (how?) | A particular vehicle |
|---|---|
| by car · by taxi | **in** my car · **in** a taxi · **in** Ahmad's car |
| by bus · by train · by plane | **on** the bus · **on** the 8:00 train · **on** a plane |
| by bike | **on** my bike |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="با موتر من رفتیم هرات.">We went to Herat <mark>in</mark> my car.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در هواپیما فیلم دیدم.">I watched a movie <mark>on</mark> the plane.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«چطور آمدی؟» «با بس.»">“How did you come?” “<mark>By</mark> bus.”</Example>

Remember the simple guide: if you can stand up and walk around inside it (a bus, a train, a plane), use **on**. A car or a taxi → **in**. A bike → **on**.

## with: tools and people

**with** has three common uses.

**1. with + a tool.** What do you use to do it?

<Example context="home" fa="پیازها را با چاقوی تیز خرد کن.">Chop the onions <mark>with</mark> a sharp knife.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="لطفاً با خودکار بنویسید، نه مداد.">Please write <mark>with</mark> a pen, not a pencil.</Example>

**2. with + a person.** Who is together with you?

<Example context="social" fa="آخر هفته با خواهرم رفتم سینما.">I went to the movies <mark>with</mark> my sister on the weekend.</Example>

**3. with / without for description.** Put **with** or **without** + a noun **after** the person or thing.

<Example context="school" fa="آن دختری که موهای بلند دارد، خواهر من است.">The girl <mark>with</mark> long hair is my sister.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="یک اتاق با منظره‌ی دریا می‌خواهیم.">We'd like a room <mark>with</mark> a sea view.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="یک قهوه بدون شکر، لطفاً.">A coffee <mark>without</mark> sugar, please.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="مرد ریش‌دار مدیر جدید ماست.">The man <mark>with</mark> the beard is our new manager.</Example>

### Persian «با»: by or with?

Persian uses **«با»** for both transport and tools. English needs two words.

<FormTable caption="«با» in English">

| Persian | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| با اتوبوس | **by** bus | transport, a way of travelling |
| با ایمیل | **by** email | a method |
| با چاقو | **with** a knife | a tool in your hand |
| با دوستم | **with** my friend | a person, together |

</FormTable>

**with** is for a thing you hold and use; **by** is for a system or a way of doing something: *He paid **by** credit card* but *I opened the door **with** a key*.

## about

**about** says what the **subject** is: what a book, a film or a talk is on.

<Example context="school" fa="دارم کتابی درباره‌ی تاریخ افغانستان می‌خوانم.">I'm reading a book <mark>about</mark> the history of Afghanistan.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="داشتیم درباره‌ی تعطیلات حرف می‌زدیم.">We were talking <mark>about</mark> our vacation.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«این فیلم درباره‌ی چیست؟» «درباره‌ی یک خانواده در بامیان.»">“What's the film <mark>about</mark>?” “It's <mark>about</mark> a family in Bamyan.”</Example>

- In a question, **about** goes at the **end**: *What's it **about**?* Not ~~About what is it?~~
- **about** + a number means *more or less* (approximately): *There were **about** 50 people at the party.*
- Some verbs need **no** *about*: *We **discussed** the plan* (not ~~discussed about~~). That is in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 42 (Prepositional phrases & zero-preposition traps)</LessonRef>.

## between and among

In Persian, «بین» works for both. English has two words.

- **between**: two people or things, one on each side. Use **between** … **and** …
- **among**: in the middle of a **group** of more than two.

<Example context="shopping" fa="داروخانه بین بانک و نانوایی است.">The pharmacy is <mark>between</mark> the bank and the bakery.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="کیفم را بین پاهایم گذاشتم.">I put my bag down <mark>between</mark> my feet.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="خانه‌ی کوچکی میان درخت‌ها پیدا کردیم.">We found a small house <mark>among</mark> the trees.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او در میان دوستانش خیلی محبوب است.">She's very popular <mark>among</mark> her friends.</Example>

After **difference** and **relationship**, always use **between**, even for more than two: *the difference **between** these three phones*.

## above, over, below, under

<FormTable caption="Higher and lower">

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **above** | higher than (often not directly above) | There's a shelf **above** the desk. · The plane flew **above** the clouds. |
| **over** | directly above, or covering | a lamp **over** the table · Put a blanket **over** the baby. |
| **below** | lower than (often not directly below) | Our apartment is **below** theirs. · The sun went **below** the horizon. |
| **under** | directly below, or covered by | The cat is **under** the bed. · I'm wearing a T-shirt **under** my sweater. |

</FormTable>

Often both words are possible: *a lamp **above / over** the table*. But only **over** can mean *covering*, and **under** is the usual word when something is covered or hidden.

**With numbers:**

- **above / below** for levels and temperatures: *ten degrees **below** zero* · ***above** average*.
- **over / under** for ages, prices and amounts: *You must be **over** 18.* · *It costs **under** $10.* · *children **under** five*.

<Example context="news" fa="امشب دما به ده درجه زیر صفر می‌رسد.">Tonight it will fall to ten degrees <mark>below</mark> zero.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="این دارو برای کودکان زیر ۱۲ سال مناسب نیست.">This medicine is not for children <mark>under</mark> 12.</Example>

## before and after + a noun

**before** and **after** can go straight before a noun. Don't add *of* or *from*.

<Example context="home" fa="قبل از شام دست‌هایت را بشور.">Wash your hands <mark>before</mark> dinner.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="بعد از کلاس با هم قهوه می‌خوریم.">We have coffee together <mark>after</mark> class.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً قبل از ساعت ۵ به من زنگ بزنید.">Please call me <mark>before</mark> 5 o'clock.</Example>

- Persian says «قبل **از** ناهار», but English says **before lunch**, not ~~before of lunch~~.
- For the future from now, say **in**: *I'll be back **in** ten minutes* (not ~~after ten minutes~~). See <LessonRef>A1 lesson 32 (Prepositions of time: at, on, in)</LessonRef>.
- **before** and **after** can also start a clause with a verb: *before I leave, after we ate*. That is in <LessonRef>lesson 29 (Time & reason clauses: when, before, because)</LessonRef>. **after that** for telling a story is in <LessonRef>lesson 46 (Sequencing: first, then, after that, finally)</LessonRef>.

## in, on, at: other uses

Some phrases with **in, on** and **at** don't follow the place rules. Learn them as fixed phrases.

<FormTable caption="Fixed phrases with in, on, at">

| Preposition | Phrases | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **on** | on TV · on the radio · on the phone · on the internet · on the left / right · on page 10 · on vacation · on business | What's **on** TV tonight? · She's **on** the phone. |
| **in** | in the newspaper · in a book · in a photo · in the rain · in the sun · in English · in pen | I read it **in** the newspaper. · Don't stand **in** the rain! |
| **at** | at the age of 20 · at the top / at the bottom (of the page) | He retired **at the age of** 60. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="news" fa="آیا دیشب اخبار را از رادیو شنیدی؟">Did you hear the news <mark>on</mark> the radio last night?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="یک ساعت زیر باران منتظر بس بودیم.">We waited for the bus <mark>in</mark> the rain for an hour.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="لطفاً جواب‌ها را به انگلیسی بنویسید.">Please write your answers <mark>in</mark> English.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="او در سن پنج‌سالگی خواندن را یاد گرفت.">She learned to read <mark>at the age of</mark> five.</Example>

Notice: you watch something **on** TV, not ~~in TV~~. But you work **in** television (the industry).

<UkNote>Americans go **on vacation**; British people usually go **on holiday**. Both mean a trip for rest and fun.</UkNote>

Can you **hold** it in your hand? → **with** (with a knife, with a pen). Do you **ride** in it or use a **system**? → **by** (by bus, by email). Walking? Your feet → **on foot**.
</MemoryHook>
