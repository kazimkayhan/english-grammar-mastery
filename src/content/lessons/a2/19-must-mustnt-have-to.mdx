## must: the form

**must** is a modal verb, like *can* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>). It is the same for every subject, and the next verb is the **base form** with no *to*.

<FormTable caption="must: all subjects">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | subject + **must** + verb | You **must** be careful. |
| **−** | subject + **mustn't** / **must not** + verb | You **mustn't** be late. |
| **?** | **Must** + subject + verb? (formal) | **Must** I sign here? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="باید این دارو را بعد از غذا بخوری.">You <mark>must</mark> take this medicine after meals.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="باید امشب به مادرم زنگ بزنم.">I <mark>must</mark> call my mother tonight.</Example>

Questions with *Must I…?* are correct, but they sound formal. In everyday English, people ask **Do I have to…?**

## have to: the form

**have to** is **not** a modal verb. It works like the normal verb *have*: **has** with he / she / it, and **do / does / did** in questions and negatives.

<FormTable caption="have to: present">

| | I / you / we / they | he / she / it |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | I **have to** work. | She **has to** work. |
| **−** | I **don't have to** work. | She **doesn't have to** work. |
| **?** | **Do** you **have to** work? | **Does** she **have to** work? |
| **short answers** | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. | Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**. |
| **wh- question** | What time **do** you **have to** leave? | When **does** he **have to** finish? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="خواهرم باید یونیفرم بپوشد.">My sister <mark>has to</mark> wear a uniform.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«باید ویزا بگیرم؟» «بله، باید.»">“<mark>Do I have to</mark> get a visa?” “Yes, you <mark>do</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="ساعت چند باید آنجا باشیم؟">What time <mark>do we have to</mark> be there?</Example>

## must or have to?

In the positive, **must** and **have to** are very close. Both say that something is necessary. Often you can use either. But there is a small difference:

<FormTable caption="must or have to?">

| | must | have to |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides? | **the speaker**: I think it's necessary | **a rule, a law or the situation** |
| Example | I **must** study more. (my decision) | I **have to** study. The exam is on Monday. |
| Example | You **must** see this film! (strong advice) | You **have to** be 18 to vote. (a law) |
| Where? | written rules, notices, formal speech | everyday speech |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="باید ورزش بیشتری بکنم. (تصمیم خودم)">I'm so unfit. I <mark>must</mark> do more exercise.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="باید ساعت هشت سر کار باشم. (قانون شرکت)">I <mark>have to</mark> be at work at eight. My boss is very strict.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مسافران باید کارت شناسایی همراه داشته باشند.">All passengers <mark>must</mark> carry photo ID. (a written notice)</Example>

Three more things:

- **In American English, have to is the usual form in speech.** *must* can sound very strong or formal. Americans often use *must* for something that is very likely (*She must be tired.*). That use comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 17</LessonRef>, Deduction now: must, might, can't.
- **must for a strong recommendation:** *You <mark>must</mark> try the kebab here! You <mark>must</mark> come and visit us.* This is friendly, not an order.
- **must** can talk about now or the near future: *I <mark>must</mark> call her tomorrow.* But it has **no past** and **no future form**. See below.

## mustn't or don't have to?

This is the big point of the lesson. In the positive, *must* and *have to* are similar. In the **negative**, they are very **different**.

<FormTable caption="mustn't vs don't have to">

| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **mustn't** | It's **not allowed**. Don't do it! | You **mustn't** smoke in the hospital. |
| **don't have to** | It's **not necessary**. You can choose. | You **don't have to** bring food. There's a lot here. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="نباید در امتحان از تلفن استفاده کنید. (ممنوع است)">You <mark>mustn't</mark> use your phone during the exam.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="لازم نیست پول نقد بدهید. می‌توانید با کارت پرداخت کنید.">You <mark>don't have to</mark> pay cash. You can pay by card.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="او لازم نیست فردا کار کند. تعطیل است.">She <mark>doesn't have to</mark> work tomorrow. It's a holiday.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="نباید به کسی بگویی. راز است!">You <mark>mustn't</mark> tell anyone. It's a secret!</Example>

Compare: *You **mustn't** come* = don't come, I don't want you here. *You **don't have to** come* = come if you like, but it's not necessary. Very different!

In American speech, *mustn't* is not very common. People often say **can't** for "not allowed" (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>): *You <mark>can't</mark> park here.* The meaning is the same.

<UkNote>**mustn't** is common in British speech: *You mustn't worry.* Its first *t* is silent: “mussn't”.</UkNote>

## have got to (informal)

**have got to** means the same as **have to**. It is informal and mainly **British**. We usually use the short form: *I've got to, she's got to*.

<Example context="social" fa="ببخشید، باید بروم. بسم ده دقیقه دیگر می‌رسد.">Sorry, I<mark>'ve got to</mark> go. My bus leaves in ten minutes.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او باید این را تا ساعت پنج تمام کند.">She<mark>'s got to</mark> finish this by five.</Example>

- **have got to** is for the **present** (and plans for the near future). For the past, use **had to**, not ~~had got to~~.
- In questions and negatives, most learners can simply use **have to**: *Do you have to go? I don't have to go.*
- In very informal American speech you will hear **gotta**: *I gotta go.* Say it, but don't write it in a formal text.

<UkNote>British speakers often say **have got to** (*I've got to go*), just as they say *have got* for things they own (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 16</LessonRef>). Americans usually say **have to**.</UkNote>

## had to: the past

**must** has **no past form**. For something necessary in the past, use **had to** for every subject. Questions and negatives use **did**.

<FormTable caption="had to: past">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | subject + **had to** + verb | We **had to** wait an hour. |
| **−** | subject + **didn't have to** + verb | I **didn't have to** pay. It was free. |
| **?** | **Did** + subject + **have to** + verb? | **Did** you **have to** wait long? |
| **short answers** | Yes, + subject + **did**. / No, + subject + **didn't**. | Yes, I **did**. / No, I **didn't**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="پروازمان تأخیر داشت، پس مجبور شدیم سه ساعت صبر کنیم.">Our flight was late, so we <mark>had to</mark> wait for three hours.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="«دیروز مجبور شدی بروی دکتر؟» «نه، لازم نبود.»">“<mark>Did</mark> you <mark>have to</mark> see a doctor yesterday?” “No, I <mark>didn't</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="لازم نبود در کلاس انگلیسی صحبت کنیم.">We <mark>didn't have to</mark> speak English in class.</Example>

After *didn't* and *did*, use **have** (not *had*): *Did you **have** to…?* not ~~Did you had to…?~~

## The future: will have to

**must** has no future form either. There is no ~~will must~~. For something necessary in the future, use **will have to**.

<Example context="travel" fa="باید در مرز گذرنامه‌ات را نشان بدهی.">You<mark>'ll have to</mark> show your passport at the border.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="اگر بیشتر از این حقوق بگیری، باید مالیات بدهی.">If you earn more, you<mark>'ll have to</mark> pay more tax.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="لازم نیست فردا زود بیدار شوی.">You <mark>won't have to</mark> get up early tomorrow.</Example>

For a plan or a decision about the near future, *must* and *have to* in the present are fine: *I **have to** work on Saturday. I **must** call him tomorrow.*

<MemoryHook title="NOT allowed or NOT necessary?">
**mustn't** is a **red light**: stop, don't do it. **don't have to** is a **green light**: you're free, do it only if you want. And remember, **must** has no past and no future: they belong to **had to** and **will have to**.
</MemoryHook>
