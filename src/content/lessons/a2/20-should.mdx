## The form

**should** is a modal verb, like *can* and *must*. So it follows the three modal rules from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>: **no -s**, **no to**, **no do**.

<FormTable caption="should: all subjects">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | subject + **should** + verb | You **should** rest. |
| **−** | subject + **shouldn't** / **should not** + verb | You **shouldn't** work so hard. |
| **?** | **Should** + subject + verb? | **Should** I call her? |
| **short answers** | Yes, + subject + **should**. / No, + subject + **shouldn't**. | Yes, you **should**. / No, you **shouldn't**. |
| **wh- question** | question word + **should** + subject + verb? | What **should** I do? |

</FormTable>

- **shouldn't** is the short form of *should not*. We use it in speech and in most writing.
- The **l** in *should* is silent: it sounds like “shood”.

## Giving advice

Use **should** to say that something is a **good idea**. Use **shouldn't** to say that something is a **bad idea**.

<Example context="health" fa="باید بروی دکتر. آن سرفه خوب نیست.">You <mark>should</mark> see a doctor. That cough sounds bad.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="شب نباید قهوه بخوری.">You <mark>shouldn't</mark> drink coffee at night.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="باید زودتر بلیت قطار بخری. خیلی زود تمام می‌شوند.">You <mark>should</mark> book your train tickets early. They sell out fast.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او نباید این‌قدر کار کند. خیلی خسته است.">She <mark>shouldn't</mark> work so hard. She's exhausted.</Example>

You can give advice about yourself too: *I <mark>should</mark> go to bed. It's midnight.* *I <mark>shouldn't</mark> spend so much money on clothes.*

## Asking for advice

Put **should** before the subject. Use **Should I…?** or a question word + **should**.

<Example context="social" fa="«باید به او زنگ بزنم؟» «بله، باید.»">“<mark>Should I</mark> call her?” “Yes, you <mark>should</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="برای مصاحبه چه بپوشم؟">What <mark>should I</mark> wear to the interview?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در استانبول کجا بمانیم؟">Where <mark>should we</mark> stay in Istanbul?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کدام گوشی را بخرم؟">Which phone <mark>should I</mark> buy?</Example>

To ask someone's opinion, you can also say: ***Do you think** I should…?* *Do you think I should take the job?*

## Opinions: what's right

We also use **should** to say what we think is **right** or **wrong**, in general.

<Example context="news" fa="شهر باید مسیرهای دوچرخه‌ی بیشتری بسازد.">The city <mark>should</mark> build more bike lanes.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="بچه‌ها نباید بیش از حد تلویزیون تماشا کنند.">Children <mark>shouldn't</mark> watch too much TV.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="به نظر من مردم باید بیشتر بازیافت کنند.">I think people <mark>should</mark> recycle more.</Example>

## Softer or stronger

Advice can sound too direct. These phrases make it softer:

- **I think** you should…: *I think you should talk to your boss.*
- **I don't think** you should…: *I don't think you should lend him money.* For negative advice, we often put the *not* in *think*. It sounds softer than *I think you shouldn't…*
- **Maybe** you should…: *Maybe you should take a break.*

To make advice stronger, add **really**: *You <mark>really should</mark> try that restaurant.*

## should or must?

**should** is **weaker** than *must*. With **should**, the other person can choose. With **must** or **have to**, there is no choice (<LessonRef>lesson 19</LessonRef>).

<FormTable caption="should vs must">

| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **should** | advice, a good idea | You **should** wear a coat. It's cold. |
| **must** / **have to** | necessary, no choice | You **have to** wear a seatbelt. It's the law. |
| **shouldn't** | advice, a bad idea | You **shouldn't** eat so fast. |
| **mustn't** | not allowed | You **mustn't** smoke here. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="بهتر است شکر کمتری بخوری. (نصیحت) · باید این دارو را روزی دو بار بخوری. (دستور دکتر)">You <mark>should</mark> eat less sugar. (advice) · You <mark>must</mark> take this medicine twice a day. (the doctor's order)</Example>

**ساختار:** should فعل وجهی است، پس s نمی‌گیرد، بعدش to نمی‌آید و در سؤال و منفی do نمی‌خواهد:

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟**

- در فارسی «باید» هم برای اجبار است و هم برای نصیحت. برای همین فارسی‌زبان برای نصیحت از must استفاده می‌کند: You must sleep more. این جمله در انگلیسی مثل دستور است و تند به نظر می‌رسد. برای نصیحت بگویید **You should** sleep more.
- در فارسی می‌گوییم «بهتر است بروی». ترجمه‌ی کلمه‌به‌کلمه‌ی آن (It's better you go) راه معمول نصیحت در انگلیسی نیست. ساده بگویید **You should go**.
- در فارسی بعد از «باید» فعل صرف می‌شود («باید **بروی**»)، پس فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند You should goes یا should to go. درست آن **should go** است.
- «آیا باید زنگ بزنم؟» را با do نسازید (Do I should…?). خود should اول جمله می‌آید: **Should I** call?

**should و must:** should ضعیف‌تر است و طرف مقابل حق انتخاب دارد. must و have to یعنی چاره‌ای نیست. shouldn't یعنی «کار درستی نیست» اما mustn't یعنی «ممنوع است».

<MemoryHook title="should = a friendly push">
**should** is a **friendly push**: “It's a good idea.” The other person can still say no. **must** is a **wall**: there is no other way. When you help a friend, push, don't build walls.
</MemoryHook>
