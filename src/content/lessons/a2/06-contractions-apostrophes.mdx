## Job 1: missing letters

A **contraction** (short form) joins two words into one. Some letters drop out, and the **apostrophe (')** goes exactly where they were.

*I am → I'm* (the *a* is missing) · *do not → don't* (the *o* is missing)

You already know the short forms of **be** from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 5 (be: am, is, are)</LessonRef>. Here is the full set with the other helping verbs.

<FormTable caption="Positive short forms">

| Full | Short | Example |
|---|---|---|
| am | **'m** | **I'm** at work. |
| is | **'s** | **She's** a nurse. · **It's** cold. |
| are | **'re** | **You're** right. · **They're** late. |
| have | **'ve** | **We've** finished. · **I've** got a cold. |
| has | **'s** | **He's** gone home. |
| will | **'ll** | **I'll** call you. · **It'll** rain. |
| would | **'d** | **I'd** like a coffee. |
| had | **'d** | **She'd** already left. |
| us (only in *let us*) | **'s** | **Let's** go. |

</FormTable>

Short forms also come after question words and after *there, here, that*:

<Example context="travel" fa="ایستگاه کجاست؟"><mark>Where's</mark> the station?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="قیمتش چند است؟ / این چیست؟"><mark>What's</mark> this? <mark>That's</mark> too expensive.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="یک نامه برایت آمده.">There<mark>'s</mark> a letter for you.</Example>

In **speech**, *'s* and *'ll* often follow a noun too: *My **car's** outside. The **bus'll** be late.* In writing, the full form is more usual after a noun.

## Negative short forms: n't

Most negatives add **n't** to the helping verb. The apostrophe replaces the *o* of *not*.

<FormTable caption="Negative short forms">

| Full | Short | Full | Short |
|---|---|---|---|
| is not | **isn't** | do not | **don't** |
| are not | **aren't** | does not | **doesn't** |
| was not | **wasn't** | did not | **didn't** |
| were not | **weren't** | have not | **haven't** |
| could not | **couldn't** | has not | **hasn't** |
| should not | **shouldn't** | had not | **hadn't** |
| would not | **wouldn't** | must not | **mustn't** |

</FormTable>

Three special forms:

- **will not → won't** (not ~~willn't~~): *I **won't** be late, I promise.*
- **cannot → can't** (one *n*): *I **can't** find my keys.* Note that the full form *cannot* is usually written as **one word**.
- **am not** has **no** short form *amn't*. Say **I'm not**: *I'm not hungry.* (In questions people say *Aren't I…?*, as in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 6 (be: questions & short answers)</LessonRef>.)

### Two ways to make some negatives

With **be**, both negatives are correct and common:

- *She **isn't** here.* = ***She's not** here.*
- *They **aren't** ready.* = ***They're not** ready.*

<UkNote>**shan't** = shall not, used especially in British English: *I **shan't** be long.* British speakers also say ***I've not** seen it* as well as *I **haven't** seen it.*</UkNote>

You may hear **ain't** in songs and films (*I ain't got time*). Many people think it is incorrect, so don't use it.

<Example context="work" fa="امروز نمی‌توانم بیایم. بیمارم."><mark>I can't</mark> come in today. I'm sick.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="او تکالیفش را انجام نداده.">He <mark>hasn't</mark> done his homework.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="نگران نباش. دیر نمی‌کنیم."><mark>Don't</mark> worry. We <mark>won't</mark> be late.</Example>

## 's: is or has? 'd: had or would?

Two short forms have **two meanings**. The **next word** tells you which one.

<FormTable caption="Read the next word">

| Short form | Next word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **'s** | -ing, adjective, noun, place | **is** | She's **working**. · She's **tired**. · She's **a doctor**. |
| **'s** | past participle (been, gone, got, finished) | **has** | She's **been** to Paris. · She's **got** two kids. |
| **'d** | base verb (like, go, love) | **would** | I'd **like** some water. · I'd **love** to come. |
| **'d** | past participle (finished, gone, eaten) | **had** | I'd **finished** before six. |
| **'d** | **better** | **had** | You'd **better** hurry. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="او سه روز است که بیمار است.">She<mark>'s</mark> been sick for three days. (has)</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او امروز بیمار است.">She<mark>'s</mark> sick today. (is)</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="یک کیلو سیب می‌خواهم، لطفاً.">I<mark>'d</mark> like a kilo of apples, please. (would)</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="وقتی زنگ زدم، آن‌ها به خانه رفته بودند.">When I called, <mark>they'd</mark> gone home. (had)</Example>

*I'd like* is the polite way to ask for things. *I'd finished* is the past perfect, which comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 9 (Past perfect)</LessonRef>. *You'd better* comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 18 (had better, ought to, be supposed to)</LessonRef>.

## When not to use a short form

**1. Not at the end.** A **positive** short form can't end a sentence or a short answer. Use the full form, because it is stressed:

- *“Are you tired?” “Yes, I **am**.”* (not ~~Yes, I'm.~~)
- *“Is she at home?” “I think she **is**.”* (not ~~I think she's.~~)
- *“Have you finished?” “Yes, we **have**.”* (not ~~Yes, we've.~~)

**Negative** short forms are fine at the end: *“No, **I'm not**.” “No, she **isn't**.” “No, we **haven't**.”* More short answers in <LessonRef>lesson 3 (Short responses: So do I, Me neither, I think so)</LessonRef>.

**2. Not for have = own.** Shorten **have / has** only when it is a **helping verb** (*I've finished, she's got*). When it is the **main verb** (= own, eat, take), use the full form:

- *He **has** a big house.* or ***He's got** a big house.* (not ~~He's a big house~~)
- *I **have** breakfast at seven.* (not ~~I've breakfast~~)

See <LessonRef>A1 lesson 16 (have / have got)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 14 (Verb forms & auxiliary verbs)</LessonRef>.

**3. Speaking or writing?** Short forms are normal in speech, text messages and friendly emails. In **formal writing** (reports, essays, official letters) people usually write the full forms: *We **do not** accept cash.* More on this in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 36 (Register: formal & informal grammar)</LessonRef>.

<Example context="work" fa="ایمیل رسمی: متأسفیم که نمی‌توانیم در جلسه شرکت کنیم.">(formal email) We regret that we <mark>cannot</mark> attend the meeting.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="پیام به دوست: نمی‌توانم بیایم، ببخشید!">(text to a friend) <mark>Can't</mark> come, sorry!</Example>

## Job 2: possession

After a noun, **'s** shows that something **belongs** to someone. You met this in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 12 (my, your, his, her… and 's)</LessonRef>.

<Example context="home" fa="موتر برادرم قرمز است.">My <mark>brother's</mark> car is red.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دفتر مدیر طبقه‌ی دوم است.">The <mark>manager's</mark> office is on the second floor.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="رئیس‌جمهور جدید کشور">the <mark>country's</mark> new president</Example>

Possession with plural nouns (*my parents' house, the children's room*) comes in <LessonRef>lesson 33 (mine, yours… and whose)</LessonRef>.

### Never for a plain plural

A plural **s** has **no apostrophe**. Only add *'s* when something **belongs** to the noun.

<FormTable caption="Plural or possessive?">

| Plural (more than one) | Possessive (belongs to) |
|---|---|
| two **apples** | the **apple's** skin |
| three **brothers** | my **brother's** car |
| new **phones** for sale | my **phone's** battery |

</FormTable>

One small exception: we can add **'s** to make single **letters** plural, so they are easy to read: *Your **r's** look like **v's**.*

### Possessive words never take an apostrophe

The possessive words **its, whose, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs** already mean “belonging to”. They **never** have an apostrophe. The forms with an apostrophe are always **contractions**:

<FormTable caption="Sound the same, mean different things">

| No apostrophe: possession | Apostrophe: contraction |
|---|---|
| **its** = belonging to it: *The dog hurt **its** leg.* | **it's** = it is / it has: ***It's** late. **It's** been a long day.* |
| **whose** = belonging to who: ***Whose** bag is this?* | **who's** = who is / who has: ***Who's** that? **Who's** seen my keys?* |
| **your** = belonging to you: *Is this **your** coat?* | **you're** = you are: ***You're** right.* |
| **their** = belonging to them: *That's **their** house.* | **they're** = they are: ***They're** at home.* |

</FormTable>

A quick test: say the full form. If *it is* or *who is* makes sense, write the apostrophe. If not, don't.

<Example context="school" fa="مکتب زمین ورزش خودش را دارد.">The school has <mark>its</mark> own sports field. (it is own? ✗ → its)</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="وقت دکترت است.">Come in. <mark>It's</mark> time for your appointment. (it is ✓ → it's)</Example>

**آپاستروف (') در انگلیسی دو کار دارد:**

**۱. نشان دادن حروفِ حذف‌شده (contraction):** دو کلمه به هم می‌چسبند و آپاستروف جای حروف حذف‌شده را نشان می‌دهد: I am ← **I'm**، do not ← **don't**، we have ← **we've**. این شبیه فارسی محاوره است که «می‌خواهم» را «می‌خوام» و «خوب است» را «خوبه» می‌گوییم. در گفتار، پیام و ایمیل دوستانه شکل کوتاه طبیعی است؛ در نوشته‌ی رسمی معمولاً شکل کامل را می‌نویسیم.

**منفی‌ها:** معمولاً n't اضافه می‌شود: isn't، don't، didn't، haven't. سه مورد خاص: will not ← **won't** (نه willn't)، cannot ← **can't**، و برای am not شکل کوتاه نداریم: **I'm not**.

**'s و 'd دو معنی دارند؛ کلمه‌ی بعدی معنی را مشخص می‌کند:**

- **'s** + صفت / ‎-ing / اسم ← **is**: She's tired. (او خسته است.)
- **'s** + قسمت سوم فعل (been، gone، got) ← **has**: She's gone. (او رفته است.)
- **'d** + شکل ساده‌ی فعل ← **would**: I'd like tea. (چای می‌خواهم.)
- **'d** + قسمت سوم فعل ← **had**: I'd finished. (تمام کرده بودم.)

**اشتباه‌های رایج فارسی‌زبان‌ها:**

- **«دارد» به معنی مالکیت:** در فارسی «او یک ماشین دارد» یک فعل ساده است، و بعضی زبان‌آموزان آن را He's a car می‌گویند. این غلط است، چون He's a car یعنی «او یک ماشین است»! وقتی has فعل اصلی است (به معنی داشتن)، آن را کوتاه نکنید: **He has a car** یا **He's got a car**.
- **پایان جمله:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «آره، هستم». در انگلیسی شکل کوتاهِ مثبت آخر جمله نمی‌آید: **Yes, I am** (نه Yes, I'm). اما منفی آخر جمله اشکالی ندارد: No, I'm not.

**۲. نشان دادن مالکیت:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «کیفِ زهرا» (اول چیز، بعد صاحب، با کسره). در انگلیسی برعکس است: **Zahra's bag** (اول صاحب + 's، بعد چیز).

**جمع هیچ‌وقت آپاستروف نمی‌گیرد:** در فارسی «ها» را گاهی با نیم‌فاصله جدا می‌نویسیم (سیب‌ها)، اما در انگلیسی s جمع مستقیم به کلمه می‌چسبد: **two apples** (نه two apple's). آپاستروف فقط وقتی می‌آید که چیزی «مالِ» آن اسم باشد: my brother's car (ماشینِ برادرم).

<MemoryHook title="Missing letters or an owner">
An apostrophe needs a reason. Either **letters are missing** (*don't, it's, I'd*) or **someone owns something** (*Zahra's bag*). Just “more than one” is **not** a reason: *two apples*, *three phones*. Not sure about *it's* or *who's*? Say ***it is*** or ***who is***. If that works, keep the apostrophe.
</MemoryHook>
