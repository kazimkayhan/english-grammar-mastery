## Telling things in order

When you tell a story, give instructions or explain a plan, small words show the **order**: what happens first, next and last.

<FormTable caption="Sequencing words">

| Stage | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **start** | First · First of all | **First**, boil some water. |
| **middle** | Then · Next · After that · Afterward(s) · Later | **Then** add the tea. **After that**, wait three minutes. |
| **end** | Finally | **Finally**, pour the tea into cups. |

</FormTable>

Most of these words go at the **start** of a sentence. You saw *and* for joining ideas in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 33 (and, but, or, because, so)</LessonRef>; these words go one step further and show the **time order**.

## Instructions: a recipe

<Example context="home" fa="اول برنج را بشور. بعد آن را در قابلمه با آب بگذار. بعد از آن بیست دقیقه صبر کن. در آخر، آن را با ماست سرو کن."><mark>First</mark>, wash the rice. <mark>Then</mark> put it in a pot with water. <mark>After that</mark>, wait twenty minutes. <mark>Finally</mark>, serve it with yogurt.</Example>

<Example context="work" fa="اول وارد سیستم شوید. سپس روی «گزارش جدید» کلیک کنید.">To send a report, <mark>first</mark> log in. <mark>Next</mark>, click “New report”.</Example>

## A story in the past

The same words work for a story about the past. They are very common with the past simple.

<Example context="travel" fa="اول پروازمان تأخیر داشت. بعد کیفی را گم کردیم. بعد از آن همه چیز عالی بود.">“How was your trip?” “<mark>First</mark>, our flight was late. <mark>Then</mark> we lost a bag. <mark>After that</mark>, everything was perfect.”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="شام خوردیم و بعدش رفتیم سینما. بعداً یک بستنی هم خوردیم.">We had dinner, and <mark>then</mark> we went to the movies. <mark>Later</mark>, we had ice cream.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="آتش‌نشان‌ها ساعت‌ها کار کردند. سرانجام آتش خاموش شد.">Firefighters worked for hours. <mark>Eventually</mark>, the fire went out.</Example>

**Later** can also follow a time: *Two hours **later**, the phone rang.* · *Three days **later**, she called me.*

## then, next, after that, afterward(s)

These four words all mean **after the last thing I said**.

- **then** is the most common. It can join two parts with *and*: *We ate, **and then** we left.* It can also start a sentence: ***Then** we left.*
- **next** is common in instructions: ***Next**, add the eggs.* In a question: *What happened **next**?*
- **after that** and **afterward(s)** sound a little stronger. They often start a sentence: ***After that**, we went home.* **Afterward(s)** can also go at the end: *We went home **afterward**.*

<Example context="school" fa="کلاس ساعت ۴ تمام می‌شود. بعدش با هم قهوه می‌خوریم.">Class ends at four. <mark>Afterward</mark>, we can have coffee together.</Example>

### Don't use after alone

**after** usually needs something after it: a **noun** (*after lunch*, <LessonRef>lesson 45 (by, with, about; between, among; above, over)</LessonRef>) or a **clause** (*after we ate*, <LessonRef>lesson 29 (Time & reason clauses: when, before, because)</LessonRef>). To mean *after that*, use **then**, **after that** or **afterward(s)**.

<Example context="home" fa="اول ناهار خوردیم، بعد رفتیم پارک.">First we had lunch, <mark>then</mark> we went to the park. (not ~~after we went to the park~~)</Example>

The opposite of *afterward(s)* is **beforehand** (= before that): *If you're going to be late, call me **beforehand**.*

<UkNote>Americans often say **afterward**; British people usually say **afterwards**. Both are correct.</UkNote>

## first or at first?

These look the same, but they mean different things.

- **first** = step one, before anything else: ***First**, open the box.* · *Who arrived **first**?*
- **at first** = **in the beginning**, when the situation **changed later**. There is usually a *but* or *now* in the next part.

<Example context="work" fa="اولش از کار جدیدم خوشم نمی‌آمد، اما حالا عاشقش هستم."><mark>At first</mark> I didn't like my new job, but now I love it.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="اولش خجالتی بود، اما بعد خیلی حرف زد."><mark>At first</mark> she was shy, but then she talked a lot.</Example>

Don't use *at first* for step one of instructions: ~~At first, cut the onions~~ → ***First**, cut the onions.*

## finally, at last, eventually, in the end

All four can talk about the **end** of a long process. But each has its own job.

<FormTable caption="The end">

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **finally** | (1) the last step in a list; (2) after a long time | **Finally**, pour the tea. · After two delays, we **finally** took off. |
| **at last** | after a long wait, and you are **glad** | You're here **at last**! · **At last**, the exams are over! |
| **eventually** | after a long time or after many things happened | He **eventually** found a job. |
| **in the end** | after a long time or after many changes; common in speech | **In the end**, we decided to stay at home. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="بعد از سه ساعت تأخیر، بالاخره هواپیما پرواز کرد.">After a three-hour delay, the plane <mark>finally</mark> took off.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بالاخره رسیدی! خیلی وقت است منتظریم.">You're home <mark>at last</mark>! We've waited for ages.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کلی فکر کردیم و آخرش موتر آبی را خریدیم.">We thought about it for weeks, and <mark>in the end</mark> we bought the blue car.</Example>

Two things to watch:

- **finally** goes **before** the main verb in the middle of a sentence (*it **finally** arrived*), or at the start with a comma (***Finally**, it arrived*).
- **in the end** (= finally) is not the same as **at the end of** + noun (= at the time something finishes): *There's a surprise **at the end of** the film.* More phrases like this are in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 42 (Prepositional phrases & zero-preposition traps)</LessonRef>.

## Lists of points: firstly, secondly, finally

In a talk, an email or an essay, you can list your **points** (not steps in time) with **first** or **firstly**, **second** or **secondly**, and **finally** or **lastly**. **First of all** is common in speech.

<Example context="work" fa="اول از همه، از همه‌ی شما برای آمدن تشکر می‌کنم. در آخر، از تیم فروش تشکر می‌کنم."><mark>First of all</mark>, thank you all for coming. … <mark>Finally</mark>, I'd like to thank the sales team.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="من دو دلیل دارم. اول، ارزان است. دوم، نزدیک است.">I have two reasons. <mark>Firstly</mark>, it's cheap. <mark>Secondly</mark>, it's close.</Example>

For the last point, say **finally** or **lastly**, never ~~at last~~: *at last* is only for a long wait.

More linking words for writing (*in addition, however, as a result*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 48 (Linking words for writing)</LessonRef>.

## Commas

In writing, we often put a **comma** after these words when they start a sentence:

- **First,** wash the rice. · **Next,** add the salt. · **After that,** wait. · **Finally,** serve it.
- After **then**, a comma is optional and often left out: **Then** add the salt.
- In short sentences, many writers leave the comma out: **First** we ate.

<Persian>

**ترتیب در انگلیسی:** برای گفتن مراحل یک کار یا تعریف کردن یک داستان، این کلمه‌ها را به کار می‌بریم:

- **شروع:** First (اول) · First of all (اول از همه)
- **وسط:** Then (بعد، سپس) · Next (بعد، مرحله‌ی بعد) · After that (بعد از آن) · Afterward / Afterwards (بعداً، بعدش) · Later (بعداً، بعدتر)
- **پایان:** Finally (در آخر، در نهایت)

مثال: First, wash the rice. Then put it in a pot. After that, wait twenty minutes. Finally, serve it. (اول برنج را بشور. بعد آن را در قابلمه بگذار. بعد از آن بیست دقیقه صبر کن. در آخر آن را سرو کن.)

**مهم‌ترین اشتباه فارسی‌زبان‌ها: «بعد» = after؟** در فارسی «بعد» به تنهایی می‌آید: «اول ناهار خوردیم، بعد رفتیم پارک». اما در انگلیسی after معمولاً به تنهایی نمی‌آید و بعد از آن یک اسم (after lunch) یا یک جمله (after we ate) لازم است. پس:

- ✗ First we had lunch, after we went to the park.
- ✓ First we had lunch, **then** we went to the park.
- ✓ First we had lunch. **After that**, we went to the park.

**first یا at first؟**

- **first** = «اول» یعنی مرحله‌ی اول: First, open the box. (اول جعبه را باز کن.)
- **at first** = «اولش، در ابتدا» یعنی اول این‌طور بود، ولی بعد عوض شد: At first I didn't like it, but now I love it. (اولش خوشم نمی‌آمد، اما حالا عاشقش هستم.)

**«بالاخره» در انگلیسی:** فارسی برای همه یک کلمه دارد، اما انگلیسی چند کلمه:

- **finally** = بالاخره، سرانجام (بعد از مدت طولانی) و همچنین «در آخر» (آخرین مرحله یا آخرین نکته)
- **at last** = بالاخره! (بعد از انتظار طولانی، با خوشحالی): You're here at last! (بالاخره آمدی!)
- **eventually** = سرانجام، بعد از مدتی طولانی یا بعد از اتفاق‌های زیاد
- **in the end** = آخرش، در نهایت (بعد از فکر کردن یا تغییرهای زیاد)

**«در آخر» برای آخرین نکته:** در سخنرانی یا نامه، برای آخرین نکته بگویید Finally یا Lastly، نه At last. چون at last یعنی «بالاخره!».

**in the end یا at the end؟** in the end = «آخرش، در نهایت». at the end of the film = «در پایان فیلم» (لحظه‌ای که فیلم تمام می‌شود).

**ویرگول:** در نوشتن، بعد از First، Next، After that و Finally در اول جمله معمولاً ویرگول می‌گذاریم. بعد از Then ویرگول اختیاری است.
