## A quick review

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 27 (Past simple: irregular verbs & did)</LessonRef> you learned the most common irregular verbs: *go → went, see → saw, buy → bought, take → took*. Most English verbs are **regular** (*work → worked*). The irregular verbs are a small group, but they are some of the **most common** verbs in the language. Even the three helper verbs are irregular: *be → was / were*, *have → had*, *do → did*.

This lesson adds about forty more A2 verbs. The rules from A1 don't change:

- The past form is **the same for every subject**: *I caught, she caught, they caught.*
- Negatives and questions use **didn't / Did + base form**: *I didn't catch it. Did you catch it?*

## Learn them in sound groups

Irregular verbs look random, but many of them change in the same way. Learn them **in groups**, and say them out loud. Each group below starts with the new A2 verbs, then reminds you of A1 verbs that work the same way.

<FormTable caption="A2 irregular verbs in groups">

| Group | New A2 verbs | Works like (A1) |
|---|---|---|
| **No change** | cut → **cut** · hit → **hit** · hurt → **hurt** · let → **let** · set → **set** · shut → **shut** | put → put · cost → cost |
| **Short vowel, ends in -t or -d** | keep → **kept** · feed → **fed** · mean → **meant** · lend → **lent** · build → **built** · hold → **held** | sleep → slept · meet → met · send → sent |
| **-ought / -aught** | fight → **fought** · catch → **caught** | buy → bought · teach → taught |
| **-ew** | throw → **threw** · grow → **grew** · blow → **blew** · draw → **drew** | know → knew · fly → flew |
| **-o- or -oo-** | break → **broke** · choose → **chose** · steal → **stole** · freeze → **froze** · ride → **rode** · rise → **rose** · shake → **shook** | speak → spoke · drive → drove · take → took |
| **i → a** | ring → **rang** | drink → drank · swim → swam |
| **Other changes** | fall → **fell** · hide → **hid** · hang → **hung** · shoot → **shot** · shine → **shone** · light → **lit** · become → **became** · forgive → **forgave** | come → came · give → gave |

</FormTable>

Two small notes on sound. **mean → meant** keeps the same letters but changes the sound: *meant* rhymes with *sent*. And *caught, fought, bought, taught, thought* all end with the same sound, so they are easy to learn together.

<Example context="travel" fa="بس ساعت هفت و نیم را گرفتم.">I <mark>caught</mark> the 7:30 bus.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="پدرکلانم این خانه را در سال ۱۳۵۴ ساخت.">My grandfather <mark>built</mark> this house in 1975.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="باد شدیدی وزید و درخت‌ها را انداخت.">A strong wind <mark>blew</mark> all night, and two trees <mark>fell</mark> on the road.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کدام را انتخاب کردی؟ من آبی را انتخاب کردم.">I <mark>chose</mark> the blue one. It <mark>cost</mark> less.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="دیروز انگشتم را بریدم. خیلی درد گرفت.">I <mark>cut</mark> my finger yesterday. It <mark>hurt</mark> a lot.</Example>

**No-change verbs:** with *cut, hit, hurt, let, set, shut*, the present and the past look the same. A **time phrase** or the **context** tells you which one it is: *I cut my finger **yesterday*** (past). For **he / she / it**, the present has **-s** but the past does not: *She cuts the bread every morning* (present) / *She cut the bread this morning* (past).

You can find all the A1 and A2 verbs, with their Persian meanings, in the [irregular verbs list](/reference#irregular).

## Negatives and questions: nothing new

Irregular verbs use **did** exactly like regular verbs. After *did* or *didn't*, the main verb goes back to the **base form**.

<FormTable caption="Irregular verbs with did">

| Positive | Negative | Question | Short answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| She **broke** it. | She **didn't break** it. | **Did** she **break** it? | Yes, she did. / No, she didn't. |
| They **caught** the train. | They **didn't catch** the train. | **Did** they **catch** the train? | Yes, they did. / No, they didn't. |
| I **chose** this one. | I **didn't choose** this one. | **Which** one **did** you **choose**? | |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="امروز صبح بس را از دست دادم. نتوانستم سوارش شوم."><mark>I didn't catch</mark> the bus this morning.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کی این را کشید؟ تو کشیدی؟">Who <mark>drew</mark> this? <mark>Did</mark> you <mark>draw</mark> it?</Example>

Look at *Who drew this?* When **who** is the subject, there is no *did*. That pattern is taught in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 1 (Subject & object questions)</LessonRef>.

## Look-alike verbs

Some pairs look or sound similar, but they are different verbs. These are common mistakes.

<FormTable caption="Don't mix them up">

| Verb | Past | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fall** | **fell** | drop to the ground | He **fell** off his bike. |
| **feel** | **felt** | have a feeling | I **felt** tired after work. |
| **leave** | **left** | go away from a place | She **left** at six. |
| **live** | **lived** (regular) | have your home | We **lived** in Herat. |
| **lie** (= rest flat) | **lay** | be in a flat position | I **lay** on the sofa. |
| **lie** (= say something untrue) | **lied** (regular) | not tell the truth | He **lied** to his boss. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="از پله‌ها افتاد و بعد احساس درد کرد.">She <mark>fell</mark> down the stairs, and then she <mark>felt</mark> a pain in her leg.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بعد از ناهار روی تخت دراز کشیدم.">After lunch I <mark>lay</mark> on the bed for an hour.</Example>

The verb **lay** (= put something down: *lay the table*) is another verb, with the past *laid*. Don't worry about it now. Just remember: **lie down → lay**.

## Telling a sequence of actions

To tell what happened, one thing after another, use **past simple** verbs **in time order**. The first verb is the first action, the second verb is the next action, and so on.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "point", at: -0.85, label: "got up" }, { type: "point", at: -0.6, label: "had a shower" }, { type: "point", at: -0.35, label: "left" }]} caption="Past simple, past simple, past simple: finished actions, one after another." />

<Example context="work" fa="ساعت شش بیدار شدم، دوش گرفتم و ساعت هفت رفتم.">I <mark>got up</mark> at six, <mark>had</mark> a shower and <mark>left</mark> at seven.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="به فرودگاه رسیدیم، بارمان را تحویل دادیم و بعد قهوه خوردیم.">We <mark>arrived</mark> at the airport, <mark>checked in</mark> our bags, and then we <mark>had</mark> a coffee.</Example>

The order matters. Compare:

- *She **read** the letter and **cried**.* (first she read it, then she cried)
- *She **cried** and **read** the letter.* (first she cried, then she read it)

We often join the verbs with **and**, **then** or **and then**. Words like *first, after that* and *finally* come in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 46 (Sequencing: first, then, after that, finally)</LessonRef>.

### when + past simple

**When** + past simple, + past simple means **one action, then the next** (very quickly after it). The *when* part happens first.

<Example context="home" fa="وقتی به خانه رسیدم، شام درست کردم.">When I <mark>got</mark> home, I <mark>made</mark> dinner.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="وقتی تلفن زنگ زد، جواب دادم.">When the phone <mark>rang</mark>, I <mark>answered</mark> it.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="وقتی آتش‌نشان‌ها رسیدند، آتش را خاموش کردند.">When the firefighters <mark>arrived</mark>, they <mark>put out</mark> the fire.</Example>

The *when* part can also go second. Then there is no comma: *I made dinner **when** I got home.* The meaning is the same: first home, then dinner.

With **when** + past **continuous**, the meaning changes: *When I got home, my wife **was cooking*** (she started before I arrived). That comes in <LessonRef>lesson 8 (Past continuous; when & while)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 9 (Past simple or past continuous?)</LessonRef>. To tell an event **out of order** (something that happened earlier), English uses the past perfect, in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 9 (Past perfect)</LessonRef>.

## US and UK spelling

This course uses **American English**. A few verbs have a different past form in British English. Both forms are correct, so you will see both.

<FormTable caption="Past forms: American and British">

| Verb | American English | British English |
|---|---|---|
| travel | **traveled** | travelled |
| cancel | **canceled** | cancelled |
| learn | **learned** | learnt or learned |
| dream | **dreamed** | dreamt or dreamed |
| burn | **burned** | burnt or burned |
| spell | **spelled** | spelt or spelled |
| smell | **smelled** | smelt or smelled |
| get | **got** | got |

</FormTable>

- **One l or two?** American English doubles a final **l** only when the stress is on the last syllable (*con**TROL** → controlled*). So *TRAvel → traveled*, *CANcel → canceled*. British English always doubles it after one vowel: *travelled, cancelled* (see also <LessonRef>A1 lesson 26</LessonRef>).
- **-ed or -t?** American English usually uses the regular **-ed** form: *learned, dreamed, burned*. British speakers use both, and the **-t** forms are more common in Britain than in the US. Careful with the sound: *dreamt* is said /dremt/, with the vowel of *red*.
- **got:** the past simple of *get* is **got** everywhere. American English also has **gotten**, but only after *have* (*I've gotten*). That is a different verb form, the past participle, in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 14 (Verb forms & auxiliary verbs)</LessonRef>.

<Example context="travel" fa="پارسال به ژاپن سفر کردیم.">We <mark>traveled</mark> to Japan last year.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="در کلاس خیلی چیزها یاد گرفتم.">I <mark>learned</mark> a lot in that class.</Example>

<UkNote>British English: *We **travelled** to Japan. The flight was **cancelled**. I **learnt** a lot. I **dreamt** about you.* The forms *learned, dreamed, burned* are also correct in Britain. As an adjective, **burnt** is very common: *burnt toast*. More differences between American and British grammar come in <LessonRef>C2 lesson 25 (US vs UK grammar & other Englishes)</LessonRef>.</UkNote>
