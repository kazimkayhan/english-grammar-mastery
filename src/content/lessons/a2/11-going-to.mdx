## The form

**going to** has three pieces: **am / is / are** + **going to** + the **base form** of a verb. The *be* part changes with the subject, like in the present continuous (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>).

<FormTable caption="going to">

| | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | I**'m going to** call. | I**'m not going to** call. | **Am** I **going to**…? |
| he / she / it | She**'s going to** call. | She **isn't going to** call. / She**'s not**… | **Is** she **going to** call? |
| you / we / they | They**'re going to** call. | They **aren't going to** call. / They**'re not**… | **Are** they **going to** call? |

</FormTable>

- Short answers use only **be**: *Yes, I **am**. / No, I**'m not**. Yes, she **is**. / No, they **aren't**.*
- Wh- questions: question word + **am / is / are** + subject + **going to** + base form: ***What are** you **going to do**? **Where is** she **going to live**?*
- After **going to**, always use the **base form**: *going to **buy***, not ~~going to buys~~ or ~~going to buying~~.

<Example context="work" fa="«می‌خواهی امشب به او زنگ بزنی؟» «بله، می‌زنم.»">“<mark>Are</mark> you <mark>going to call</mark> him tonight?” “Yes, I <mark>am</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="بعد از دانشگاه می‌خواهی چه کار کنی؟"><mark>What are</mark> you <mark>going to do</mark> after university?</Example>

## Use 1: plans and intentions

Use **going to** when you **have already decided** to do something. The decision was made **before** you speak.

<Example context="school" fa="قرار است در دانشگاه پزشکی بخوانم.">I<mark>'m going to study</mark> medicine at university.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="آخر هفته‌ی بعد می‌خواهیم آشپزخانه را رنگ کنیم.">We<mark>'re going to paint</mark> the kitchen next weekend.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="ماه بعد لپ‌تاپ جدید می‌خرم. الان پولش را دارم.">I<mark>'m going to buy</mark> a new laptop next month. I have the money now.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="دیگر قند نمی‌خورم. تصمیمم را گرفتم.">I<mark>'m not going to eat</mark> sugar anymore. That's my decision.</Example>

Common time words: *tomorrow, tonight, this weekend, next week / month / year, soon, in two weeks*.

**going to go:** *I'm going to go to Herat next week* is correct. With **go** and **come**, people also often use the present continuous: *I'm going to Herat next week.* That way of talking about plans comes in <LessonRef>lesson 13 (Present continuous for the future)</LessonRef>.

## Use 2: predictions from evidence

Use **going to** when you can **see** that something will happen. There is **evidence** now: something you see, hear or know.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "point", at: 0, label: "now: dark clouds" }, { type: "arrow", from: 0, to: 0.45, label: "it's going to rain" }]} caption="The evidence is here now, so we can see the future coming." />

<Example context="travel" fa="آن ابرها را ببین! الان باران می‌آید.">Look at those clouds! It<mark>'s going to rain</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مواظب باش! الان می‌افتی!">Watch out! You<mark>'re going to fall</mark>!</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او بچه‌دار می‌شود. ماه بعد به دنیا می‌آید.">She<mark>'s going to have</mark> a baby. It's due next month.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="امتیاز ۳ بر صفر است. افغانستان می‌برد!">It's 3–0. Afghanistan<mark>'s going to win</mark>!</Example>

People also use **going to** for general predictions: *I think it's going to be a great year.* For predictions that are only your **opinion**, with no evidence, English often uses **will**: *I think you'll love Herat.* That is <LessonRef>lesson 12 (will & shall)</LessonRef>.

## in + a period of time

To say **how long from now** something will happen, use **in** + a period of time. *in ten minutes* = ten minutes from now.

<FormTable caption="Counting time: past and future">

| From now, into the future | From now, back into the past | In a story about the past |
|---|---|---|
| **in** ten minutes | ten minutes **ago** | ten minutes **later** |
| **in** two weeks | two weeks **ago** | two weeks **later** |
| **in** a few days | a few days **ago** | a few days **later** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="ده دقیقه‌ی دیگر جلسه داریم.">The meeting starts <mark>in ten minutes</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="دو هفته‌ی دیگر خواهرم عروسی می‌کند.">My sister is going to get married <mark>in two weeks</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="یک ساعت دیگر خانه هستم.">I'm going to be home <mark>in an hour</mark>.</Example>

- **ago** goes **after** the time and looks back from now (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 28 (Past time phrases: yesterday, last week, ago)</LessonRef>). **in** goes **before** the time and looks forward.
- To count forward from now, say **in**, not *after*: *I'm going to be back **in** ten minutes.* We use **after** with an event: *after lunch, after the meeting* (<LessonRef>A2 lesson 45 (by, with, about; between, among; above, over)</LessonRef>).
- **in** can also tell you **how long something takes**: *I finished the test **in** twenty minutes.* The context tells you which meaning it is.

<UkNote>British speakers often add **time**: *in two weeks' time, in a month's time.* American speakers usually just say *in two weeks*.</UkNote>

## going to: future or movement?

Don't mix up two different things:

- **going to + verb** = future: *I'm going to **call** the bank.* (a plan)
- **going to + place** = movement now or soon. This is the present continuous of **go**: *I'm going **to the bank**.* (I'm on my way.)

<Example context="shopping" fa="دارم به بانک می‌روم. چیزی لازم داری؟">I'm going <mark>to the bank</mark>. Do you need anything?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="فردا به بانک زنگ می‌زنم.">I'm going to <mark>call</mark> the bank tomorrow.</Example>

## gonna

In fast, informal speech, **going to** before a verb often sounds like **gonna**: *I'm gonna call you.* You will hear it in songs and films. But don't **write** *gonna* in emails, essays or exams; write **going to**. And *gonna* only works for the future: you can't say ~~I'm gonna the bank~~.

<MemoryHook title="Already on the road">
**going to** is a future that is **already on its way**. The decision is made (*I'm going to study medicine*), or the signs are here (*Look at those clouds!*). And for time on the road ahead, count with **in**: *in ten minutes*.
</MemoryHook>
