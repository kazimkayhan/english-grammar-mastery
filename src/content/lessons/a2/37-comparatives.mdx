## Comparing two things

A **comparative** adjective compares two people or things: *This phone is **cheaper**. That one is **more expensive**.* There are three ways to make it.

<FormTable caption="How to make a comparative">

| Adjective | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Short (one syllable) | add **-er** | cheap → **cheaper** · old → **older** |
| Two syllables, ending in **-y** | y → **-ier** | happy → **happier** · easy → **easier** |
| Most other two-syllable and all long adjectives | **more** + adjective | modern → **more modern** · expensive → **more expensive** |
| Irregular | a new word | good → **better** · bad → **worse** · far → **farther / further** |

</FormTable>

Use **than** before the second thing: *The bus is cheaper **than** the taxi.* If the second thing is clear, you can stop after the adjective: *Do you have a bigger size? This one is too small.*

## Short adjectives: -er

Most one-syllable adjectives just add **-er**. Some need a small spelling change.

<FormTable caption="Spelling: short adjectives">

| Adjective ends in… | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add **-er** | cheap → cheaper · tall → taller · fast → faster |
| **-e** | add **-r** | nice → nicer · safe → safer · large → larger |
| one vowel + one consonant | **double** the consonant + -er | big → bigger · hot → hotter · thin → thinner · sad → sadder |
| consonant + **-y** | y → **-ier** | happy → happier · easy → easier · busy → busier · heavy → heavier |

</FormTable>

- **Don't double** after two vowels or two consonants: *cheap → cheaper* (ea), *fast → faster* (st), *cold → colder* (ld).
- **Don't double w**: *new → newer, slow → slower*.

<Example context="shopping" fa="این کفش‌ها از آن‌ها ارزان‌تر است.">These shoes are <mark>cheaper</mark> than those.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="آپارتمان جدیدمان از قبلی بزرگ‌تر است.">Our new apartment is <mark>bigger</mark> than the old one.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="امروز از دیروز گرم‌تر است.">Today is <mark>hotter</mark> than yesterday.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="امتحان از آنچه فکر می‌کردم آسان‌تر بود.">The test was <mark>easier</mark> than I thought.</Example>

## Two-syllable adjectives

Two-syllable adjectives are the tricky group.

- Ending in **-y**: use **-ier**: *happy → happier, funny → funnier, early → earlier, busy → busier*.
- **Most others** take **more**: *modern → more modern, famous → more famous, careful → more careful, boring → more boring, crowded → more crowded*.
- A few common ones take **-er**: *quiet → quieter, simple → simpler, narrow → narrower, clever → cleverer*. Some can take both: *common → commoner* or *more common*.

Not sure? Use **more**. It is the safe choice for any adjective of two syllables or more, except the **-y** group.

<Example context="work" fa="کار جدیدم از قبلی جالب‌تر و شلوغ‌تر است.">My new job is <mark>more interesting</mark> and <mark>busier</mark> than my old one.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="قطار شب‌ها خلوت‌تر است.">The train is <mark>quieter</mark> at night.</Example>

## Long adjectives: more

Adjectives with **three or more syllables** always take **more**. Never add -er to them.

<Example context="shopping" fa="هتل‌های مرکز شهر گران‌ترند.">Hotels in the city center are <mark>more expensive</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="این خیابان از خیابان اصلی خطرناک‌تر است.">This road is <mark>more dangerous</mark> than the highway.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="این مبل راحت‌تر است.">This sofa is <mark>more comfortable</mark>.</Example>

Use **one** way, not two. Say *cheaper* or *more expensive*, but never ~~more cheaper~~ or ~~expensiver~~.

## Irregular: better, worse, farther / further

Three common adjectives have a completely new comparative.

<FormTable caption="Irregular comparatives">

| Adjective | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| good | **better** | This café is **better than** that one. |
| bad | **worse** | The traffic is **worse** today. |
| far | **farther** or **further** | The airport is **farther / further** than the station. |

</FormTable>

- **better** and **worse** are already comparatives. Don't put *more* before them: ~~more better~~, ~~more worse~~.
- **well** (= healthy) also becomes **better**: *I was sick yesterday, but I feel **better** today.*
- **farther** and **further** both work for distance. **further** has one more meaning: *more, extra*. *For **further** information, call this number.* (Not *farther* here.)
- **more** and **less** are also the comparatives of *much / many* and *little*: *more time, less money*. See <LessonRef>lesson 31 (much, many, a lot of, a few, a little)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 38 (Superlatives)</LessonRef>.

<Example context="health" fa="دیروز مریض بودم، ولی امروز حالم بهتر است.">I was sick yesterday, but today I feel <mark>better</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="آلودگی هوا امسال از پارسال بدتر است.">Air pollution is <mark>worse</mark> this year than last year.</Example>

## than + pronoun or clause

After **than**, you can use a noun, a pronoun or a short clause.

<FormTable caption="What comes after than">

| After than | Example | Style |
|---|---|---|
| a noun | Ahmad is taller **than his father**. | all styles |
| object pronoun | Ahmad is taller **than me**. | everyday, very common |
| subject pronoun + verb | Ahmad is taller **than I am**. | careful, always correct |
| a clause | The trip was longer **than I expected**. | all styles |

</FormTable>

- In everyday English, people say **than me, than him, than us**.
- In careful writing, use a subject pronoun + verb: **than I am, than he is, than we do**. Repeat **be** if the first part has *be*. For other verbs, use **do / does / did**: *He'**s** older than I **am**. She **works** harder than I **do**.*
- *than I* alone (no verb) is correct but sounds very formal. As a learner, choose **than me** or **than I am**.

<Example context="home" fa="خواهرم از من بلندتر است.">My sister is taller <mark>than me</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او همیشه از من شلوغ‌تر است.">She's always busier <mark>than I am</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="بلیت‌ها از آنچه فکر می‌کردیم گران‌تر بود.">The tickets were more expensive <mark>than we thought</mark>.</Example>

## older or elder?

Both come from **old**, but they work differently.

- **older** works **everywhere**, and it is the one you need with **than**: *My brother is **older than** me.*
- **elder** only goes **before a family noun** (*brother, sister, son, daughter*): *my **elder** sister*. It also appears in *the elder (of the two)*. It sounds a little formal and is more common in British English.
- **Never** say ~~elder than~~.
- *my **big** brother / sister* also means *my older brother / sister*. But don't say ~~my bigger brother~~.

<Example context="home" fa="خواهر بزرگ‌ترم در هرات زندگی می‌کند.">My <mark>older</mark> sister lives in Herat. (or: My <mark>elder</mark> sister…)</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او دو سال از من بزرگ‌تر است.">He's two years <mark>older</mark> than me.</Example>

The superlative **eldest** (*my eldest son*) and **oldest** are in <LessonRef>lesson 38 (Superlatives)</LessonRef>.

## Questions and negatives

Comparatives work in questions and negatives like any adjective after **be**.

<FormTable caption="Questions and negatives">

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Yes/no question | **Is** Kabul **bigger than** Mazar-i-Sharif? |
| Which…, A or B? | **Which is cheaper**, the bus or the train? |
| Who…? | **Who is older**, you or your sister? |
| Negative | The train **isn't faster than** the plane. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="کدام ارزان‌تر است، اتوبوس یا قطار؟"><mark>Which is cheaper</mark>, the bus or the train?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این مدل از قبلی بهتر نیست.">This model <mark>isn't better</mark> than the old one.</Example>

A negative comparative can also be said with **not as … as**: *The train isn't as fast as the plane.* That is <LessonRef>lesson 39 (as … as, the same as, different from)</LessonRef>.

**صفت تفضیلی (comparative)** برای مقایسه‌ی دو چیز است. در فارسی همه‌ی صفت‌ها با «تر» تفضیلی می‌شوند: ارزان‌تر، گران‌تر، زیباتر. اما در انگلیسی **دو راه** داریم و باید بر اساس طول صفت انتخاب کنیم:

- **صفت کوتاه (یک‌هجایی) ← er-:** cheap ← cheaper (ارزان‌تر)، old ← older، nice ← nicer.
- **حرف آخر تکراری:** اگر صفت با یک حرف صدادار کوتاه + یک حرف بی‌صدا تمام شود، حرف آخر دو تا می‌شود: big ← bigger، hot ← hotter، thin ← thinner.
- **صفت دوهجایی که به y- ختم می‌شود ← ier-:** happy ← happier، easy ← easier، busy ← busier.
- **بیشتر صفت‌های دوهجایی دیگر و همه‌ی صفت‌های بلند ← more:** more modern، more expensive (گران‌تر)، more interesting (جالب‌تر). اگر مطمئن نیستید، more امن‌تر است.

**هرگز هر دو با هم نه:** چون در فارسی «تر» همیشه هست، بعضی‌ها هم more می‌گذارند و هم er-: ~~more cheaper~~ غلط است. یا cheaper یا more expensive.

**بی‌قاعده‌ها:** good ← better (بهتر)، bad ← worse (بدتر)، far ← farther / further (دورتر). در فارسی «بهتر» شکل عادیِ «به» است، ولی better خودش تفضیلی است؛ پس **more better** غلط است. برای تأکید بگویید much better (خیلی بهتر). well به معنی «سالم» هم better می‌شود: I feel better today. (امروز حالم بهتر است.) further معنای «بیشتر، اضافی» هم دارد: further information (اطلاعات بیشتر).

**«از» = than، نه from:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «او **از** من بلندتر است». فارسی‌زبان‌ها «از» را from ترجمه می‌کنند و می‌گویند bigger from. در انگلیسی بعد از صفت تفضیلی همیشه **than** می‌آید. ترتیب هم فرق دارد: در فارسی «از من» **قبل** از صفت است، در انگلیسی than me **بعد** از صفت: She is taller than me.

**than me یا than I am؟** در گفتار روزمره: than me، than him. در نوشتار دقیق: ضمیر فاعلی + فعل: than I am، than he is، than we do. اگر در بخش اول be باشد، be را تکرار کنید (He's older than I am)؛ برای فعل‌های دیگر از do / does / did استفاده کنید: She works harder than I do.

**older یا elder؟** در فارسی «برادر بزرگ‌تر» می‌گوییم، پس بعضی‌ها می‌گویند my bigger brother که درست نیست. بگویید my older brother یا my elder brother یا my big brother. elder فقط **قبل از اسمِ خانوادگی** می‌آید (my elder sister) و کمی رسمی است. با than هرگز elder نمی‌آید: She is older than me، نه elder than me.

**سؤالی و منفی:** مثل هر صفت دیگری بعد از be: Is Kabul bigger than Mazar-i-Sharif? (کابل از مزار شریف بزرگ‌تر است؟) / Which is cheaper, the bus or the train? (کدام ارزان‌تر است، اتوبوس یا قطار؟) / The train isn't faster than the plane. (قطار از هواپیما سریع‌تر نیست.)
