## Two verbs together

Often one verb comes right after another verb: *I **want to leave**. I **enjoy cooking**.*

The **first** verb decides the form of the **second** verb:

- Some verbs take **to + verb** (the base form): *I want **to leave**.*
- Some verbs take **-ing**: *I enjoy **cooking**.*

There is no simple rule that tells you which verb takes which. You need to learn the common verbs in two groups. This lesson gives you the most useful ones. You already know some from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 24</LessonRef> (*like, love, hate + -ing; would like + to*).

## Group 1: verb + to + verb

These verbs take **to + the base form** of the next verb.

<FormTable caption="Verbs + to + verb">

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| **want** | I **want to** learn Spanish. |
| **need** | We **need to** leave at seven. |
| **decide** | She **decided to** quit her job. |
| **hope** | I **hope to** see you soon. |
| **plan** | They're **planning to** move to Herat. |
| **agree** | He **agreed to** help us. |
| **refuse** | The driver **refused to** stop. |
| **promise** | I **promise to** call you tonight. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="می‌خواهم سال دیگر اسپانیایی یاد بگیرم.">I <mark>want to learn</mark> Spanish next year.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="تصمیم گرفت کارش را ترک کند.">She <mark>decided to quit</mark> her job.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="امیدواریم تابستان به ترکیه برویم.">We <mark>hope to go</mark> to Turkey in the summer.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="کارگران حاضر نشدند به سر کار برگردند.">The workers <mark>refused to go</mark> back to work.</Example>

Many of these verbs look **forward**: they are about plans, wishes and decisions (*want, hope, plan, decide, promise*). That can help you remember them. Other common verbs in this group: **learn** (*I'm learning to drive*), **offer** (*He offered to carry my bag*) and **would like** (*I'd like to book a table*).

### After to, always the base form

The verb after **to** is the **base form**: *to go, to see, to buy*. Never ~~to going~~ or ~~to went~~.

<Example context="home" fa="دیروز قول داد ظرف‌ها را بشوید.">Yesterday he <mark>promised to wash</mark> the dishes. (not ~~to washed~~)</Example>

### want to, not want that

After **want**, use **to + verb**. Don't use *that* and a second subject: *I **want to go** home*, not ~~I want that I go home~~. (For *I want **you** to come*, with another person, see <LessonRef>B1 lesson 34</LessonRef>, want sb to; make, let, help sb do.)

### hope to and agree to

- **hope to + verb** when the subject is the same: *I hope **to pass** the exam.* For another subject, use **hope (that)**: *I hope (that) **you** pass the exam.*
- **agree to + verb** = say yes to doing something: *She agreed **to lend** me her car.* But **agree with** + person = have the same opinion: *I agree **with** you.*

### Negatives: not to

To make the **second** verb negative, put **not** before **to**.

<FormTable caption="not to">

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| I decided **to go**. | I decided **not to go**. |
| He promised **to tell** her. | He promised **not to tell** her. |
| We plan **to stay** long. | We plan **not to stay** long. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="تصمیم گرفتم امشب بیرون نروم. خسته‌ام.">I <mark>decided not to go</mark> out tonight. I'm tired.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="قول بده به کسی نگویی!"><mark>Promise not to tell</mark> anyone!</Example>

Be careful: the meaning changes when *not* moves. *I **didn't decide** to go.* (Someone else decided.) That is different from *I decided **not to go**.* (I decided: no.)

## Group 2: verb + -ing

These verbs take the **-ing** form of the next verb. (For -ing spelling, like *making, running*, see <LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>.)

<FormTable caption="Verbs + -ing">

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **enjoy** | like doing it | She **enjoys** cooking for friends. |
| **finish** | come to the end of | Have you **finished** eating? |
| **mind** | not like, be unhappy about | I don't **mind** waiting. |
| **avoid** | try not to do it | I **avoid** driving in rush hour. |
| **practice** | do it again to get better | He **practices** speaking every day. |
| **keep** | continue, or again and again | It **kept** raining all day. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="ساعت ده تمیز کردن آشپزخانه را تمام کردم.">I <mark>finished cleaning</mark> the kitchen at ten.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="از خوردن غذای شور پرهیز می‌کنم. برای قلبم بد است.">I <mark>avoid eating</mark> salty food. It's bad for my heart.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هر روز صحبت کردن به انگلیسی را تمرین می‌کنیم.">We <mark>practice speaking</mark> English every day.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="کامپیوترم مدام خاموش می‌شود!">My computer <mark>keeps crashing</mark>!</Example>

### keep and keep on

**keep + -ing** has two meanings:

- **continue**: *Keep walking. The station is near.*
- **again and again** (often when it's annoying): *I **keep forgetting** my password!*

**keep on + -ing** means the same: *She kept on talking.*

### mind: questions and negatives

We use **mind + -ing** mostly in **questions** and **negatives**. *I don't mind* = it's OK for me, no problem.

<Example context="travel" fa="«اشکالی ندارد کمی صبر کنید؟» «نه، اصلاً.»">“Do you <mark>mind waiting</mark> a few minutes?” “No, not at all.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="برایم مهم نیست آخر هفته کار کنم.">I <mark>don't mind working</mark> on weekends.</Example>

Careful with the answer: *Do you mind…?* means *Is it a problem for you?* So **No** (= no problem) is the friendly answer. Polite requests like *Would you mind opening the window?* come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 20</LessonRef>, Polite requests: Would you mind…?

<UkNote>In British English the verb is spelled **practise**: *He practises speaking every day.* The noun is **practice** in both: *football practice*.</UkNote>

## Questions and negatives

The question or the negative goes on the **first** verb. The second verb (*to + verb* or *-ing*) does not change.

<FormTable caption="Questions and negatives">

| | verb + to | verb + -ing |
|---|---|---|
| **−** | I **don't want** to go. | She **didn't finish** reading it. |
| **?** | **Do you need** to leave now? | **Did you enjoy** dancing? |
| **wh-** | **What do you plan** to do? | **When did they finish** building it? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="فردا می‌خواهید چه کار کنید؟">What <mark>do you want to do</mark> tomorrow?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هنوز نوشتن انشایش را تمام نکرده.">He <mark>hasn't finished writing</mark> his essay yet.</Example>

## Not to after modal verbs

Modal verbs (**can, must, should, will, could, might**) take the base form with **no to** (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>). Compare:

- I **can swim**. · I **want to swim**.
- You **must go**. · You **need to go**.
- She **should stop**. · She **decided to stop**.

<Example context="health" fa="باید ورزش کنم. می‌خواهم ورزش کنم.">I <mark>should exercise</mark>. I <mark>want to exercise</mark>.</Example>

## go + -ing for activities

For many **free-time activities**, especially sports and shopping, use **go + -ing**.

<FormTable caption="go + -ing">

| Sports | Free time | Outdoors |
|---|---|---|
| go **swimming** | go **shopping** | go **fishing** |
| go **skiing** | go **dancing** | go **hiking** |
| go **running** | go **sightseeing** | go **camping** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="شنبه‌ها با دوستانم شنا می‌روم.">I <mark>go swimming</mark> with my friends on Saturdays.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="دیروز با مادرم خرید رفتیم.">Yesterday I <mark>went shopping</mark> with my mom.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در رم به گشت‌وگذار رفتیم.">In Rome we <mark>went sightseeing</mark> every day.</Example>

- **No to and no for**: *go shopping*, not ~~go to shopping~~ or ~~go for shopping~~.
- **go** changes with the tense, the -ing word stays the same: *I **go** swimming · I **went** swimming · I'm **going** swimming tomorrow · She's **gone** shopping* (she is at the shops now).
- To say where, use **in, at** (place), not **to**: *We went swimming **in** the sea* (not ~~to the sea~~). *She went shopping **at** the mall.*
- **come** can work the same way: *Come shopping with us!*
- It can follow another verb, too: *Do you **want to go** fishing? I **enjoy going** camping.*

### go swimming or go for a swim?

Both are correct. **go + -ing** is the activity in general. **go for a + noun** is one short time: *Let's **go for a swim** before lunch.* (*for* + noun is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 25</LessonRef>, to for purpose: I came to study.)

## The two groups together

<FormTable caption="to or -ing?">

| verb + to + verb | verb + -ing |
|---|---|
| want, need, decide, hope | enjoy, finish, mind, avoid |
| plan, agree, refuse, promise | practice, keep (on) |
| learn, offer, would like | (and go + -ing) |
| I **decided to stay**. | I **enjoyed staying**. |
| ~~I decided staying.~~ | ~~I enjoyed to stay.~~ |

</FormTable>

<MemoryHook title="Plans take to, doing takes -ing">
Verbs about **plans and wishes** look forward, so they take **to**: *want to, hope to, plan to, decide to, promise to.* Verbs about the **activity itself** (how you feel about it, how it goes on or ends) take **-ing**: *enjoy doing, keep doing, finish doing, avoid doing.* It's a helpful guide, not a perfect rule, so learn the lists too.
</MemoryHook>
