## Same for me: So … I and Neither … I

When someone says something and it is **true for you too**, you can reply with **So** or **Neither** + a helper verb + the subject. Notice the order: the helper comes **before** the subject.

<FormTable caption="So and Neither">

| The first sentence is… | Reply | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **positive** | **So** + helper + subject | “I'm hungry.” “**So am I.**” |
| **negative** | **Neither** + helper + subject | “I'm not hungry.” “**Neither am I.**” |
| **negative** | **Nor** + helper + subject | “I'm not hungry.” “**Nor am I.**” |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«من غذای افغان را دوست دارم.» «من هم همین‌طور.»">“I love Persian food.” “<mark>So do I</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«امروز صبح دیر رسیدم.» «من هم.»">“I was late this morning.” “<mark>So was I</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="«دیشب خوب نخوابیدم.» «من هم نخوابیدم.»">“I didn't sleep well last night.” “<mark>Neither did I</mark>.”</Example>

**Neither** and **Nor** mean the same here. After *Neither / Nor*, the helper is **positive**: *Neither **do** I* (not ~~Neither don't I~~). The word *neither* is already negative. Remember: one negative only (<LessonRef>lesson 2 (Negatives & answering negative questions)</LessonRef>).

The subject can be any person: *“Ahmad works on Fridays.” “So **does Karim**.”* · *“We can't come.” “Neither **can they**.”*

### Which helper?

The helper **copies the verb** in the first sentence. The same helpers make negatives and short answers (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 18 (Present simple: negatives & questions)</LessonRef>).

<FormTable caption="Choosing the helper">

| First sentence | Verb | Agree |
|---|---|---|
| I'm tired. | be (present) | So **am** I. |
| My parents were at home. | be (past) | So **was** I. |
| I like tea. | present simple | So **do** I. |
| She doesn't eat meat. | present simple | Neither **does** her husband. |
| I went to the party. | past simple | So **did** I. |
| I can't drive. | can | Neither **can** I. |
| I'll be there at six. | will | So **will** I. |
| I'm learning Spanish. | present continuous | So **am** I. |

</FormTable>

The most common mistake is the wrong helper. *I'm tired* has **am**, so the reply is *So **am** I*, not ~~So do I~~. *I like it* has no helper, so use **do**: *So **do** I*, not ~~So am I~~.

<Example context="work" fa="«من می‌توانم فردا زود بیایم.» «من هم می‌توانم.»">“I can come in early tomorrow.” “<mark>So can I</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«من در قطار اول نبودم.» «ما هم نبودیم.»">“I wasn't on the first train.” “<mark>Neither were we</mark>.”</Example>

**So I do** (with the normal order) is something different. It shows surprise when you see that something is true: *“You have paint on your shirt!” “Oh, **so I do**!”* That comes in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 5 (Ellipsis & substitution 2)</LessonRef>. To say “me too”, always put the helper first: ***So do I***.

## Me too and Me neither

In everyday conversation, people often say **Me too** and **Me neither**. They are short and informal, and they work with every tense. You don't need to choose a helper.

- After a **positive** sentence: **Me too.** *“I'm cold.” “Me too.”* · *“I loved the movie.” “Me too.”*
- After a **negative** sentence: **Me neither.** *“I don't understand this.” “Me neither.”* · *“I can't hear anything.” “Me neither.”*

<Example context="social" fa="«خیلی خسته‌ام.» «من هم.»">“I'm so tired.” “<mark>Me too</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«این تمرین را نمی‌فهمم.» «من هم نمی‌فهمم.»">“I don't get this exercise.” “<mark>Me neither</mark>.”</Example>

Don't say ~~Me too~~ after a negative sentence. *“I don't like it.” “Me too.”* sounds strange. Say **Me neither** (or *Neither do I*).

In informal American English you may also hear **Me either** with the same meaning. **Me neither** is the safe choice.

Other ways to say the same: *I am too. I do too. I'm not either. I don't either.* Those use **too** and **either**, which are in <LessonRef>lesson 43 (already, yet, still, just; also, too, either)</LessonRef>.

## When you are different

If the sentence is **not** true for you, reply with **subject + helper**. Say the helper strongly. Words like *Oh* or *Really?* make it sound friendly.

<FormTable caption="Agree or disagree">

| They say | You agree | You are different |
|---|---|---|
| I love coffee. | So do I. / Me too. | Oh, **I don't**. |
| I'm not tired. | Neither am I. / Me neither. | **I am!** |
| I didn't like the hotel. | Neither did I. | Really? **I did.** |
| I can speak French. | So can I. | **I can't.** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«من بسکتبال دوست ندارم.» «واقعاً؟ من دوست دارم!»">“I don't like basketball.” “Really? <mark>I do</mark>!”</Example>

## I think so, I hope not

To answer a yes/no question without saying the full sentence, use **so** for *yes* and **not** for *no* after verbs like *think, hope* and *be afraid*.

<FormTable caption="Answers with so and not">

| Verb | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| think | **I think so.** | **I don't think so.** |
| hope | **I hope so.** | **I hope not.** |
| be afraid (= sorry, bad news) | **I'm afraid so.** | **I'm afraid not.** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="«قطار سر وقت است؟» «فکر کنم آره.»">“Is the train on time?” “<mark>I think so</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«جلسه طولانی است؟» «فکر نکنم.»">“Is the meeting long?” “<mark>I don't think so</mark>. About 30 minutes.”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«فردا آفتابی است؟» «امیدوارم!»">“Will it be sunny tomorrow?” “<mark>I hope so</mark>!”</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="«جدی است؟» «امیدوارم نه.»">“Is it serious?” “<mark>I hope not</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«پرواز لغو شده؟» «متأسفانه بله.»">“Is the flight canceled?” “<mark>I'm afraid so</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«این را سایز بزرگ دارید؟» «متأسفانه نه.»">“Do you have this in large?” “<mark>I'm afraid not</mark>.”</Example>

Four rules:

- Say **I think so**, not ~~I think yes~~ or ~~I think it~~.
- The usual negative of *think* is **I don't think so**. (*I think not* is possible but much less common.)
- The negative of *hope* is always **I hope not**. Never ~~I don't hope so~~ or ~~I hope no~~.
- **I'm afraid so / not** is polite. It means “yes / no, and I'm sorry about it.”

Don't use *so* after **know**: *“Is Ahmad here?” “**I know.**” / “**I don't know.**”* (not ~~I know so~~).

In speech you can also say *I guess so / I guess not* and *I suppose so*. More verbs like these come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 44 (Ellipsis & substitution 1)</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**«من هم همین‌طور»:** در فارسی برای موافقت فقط می‌گوییم «من هم» یا «من هم همین‌طور»، چه جمله مثبت باشد چه منفی، و فعل را تکرار نمی‌کنیم. در انگلیسی باید **فعل کمکی** را تکرار کنیم:

- جمله‌ی **مثبت** ← **So** + فعل کمکی + فاعل: I'm tired. ← **So am I.** (من هم خسته‌ام.)
- جمله‌ی **منفی** ← **Neither** (یا **Nor**) + فعل کمکی + فاعل: I can't swim. ← **Neither can I.** (من هم نمی‌توانم.)
- ترتیب مهم است: اول فعل کمکی، بعد فاعل: So **do I**، نه So I do.
- بعد از Neither فعل کمکی **مثبت** می‌آید: Neither do I، نه Neither don't I. چون neither خودش منفی است و در انگلیسی فقط یک منفی در جمله می‌آید.

**کدام فعل کمکی؟** همان فعلی که گوینده‌ی اول به کار برده:

- با am / is / are ← So am I
- با زمان حال ساده (like، live) ← So do I
- با گذشته‌ی ساده (went، liked) ← So did I
- با can ← So can I · با will ← So will I

اشتباه رایج فارسی‌زبان‌ها این است که همیشه So do I می‌گویند: I'm tired ← So do I غلط است؛ درستش So am I است.

**Me too و Me neither:** شکل خودمانی و کوتاه هستند. بعد از جمله‌ی مثبت **Me too** و بعد از جمله‌ی منفی **Me neither**. چون در فارسی برای هر دو «من هم» می‌گوییم، فارسی‌زبان‌ها بعد از جمله‌ی منفی هم Me too می‌گویند: I don't like it ← Me too غلط است؛ بگویید **Me neither**.

**وقتی نظرت فرق دارد:** فاعل + فعل کمکی: I love coffee ← Oh, I don't. (من دوست ندارم.) · I'm not tired ← I am! (من خسته‌ام!)

**I think so و I hope not:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «فکر کنم آره»، «فکر نکنم»، «امیدوارم»، «امیدوارم نه». فارسی‌زبان‌ها اغلب کلمه‌به‌کلمه ترجمه می‌کنند و می‌گویند I think yes یا I hope no. در انگلیسی:

- فکر کنم آره ← **I think so.** · فکر نکنم ← **I don't think so.**
- امیدوارم (آره) ← **I hope so.** · امیدوارم نه ← **I hope not.** (هرگز I don't hope so)
- متأسفانه آره ← **I'm afraid so.** · متأسفانه نه ← **I'm afraid not.** (این afraid به معنی ترسیدن نیست؛ یعنی «متأسفانه».)
- بعد از know کلمه‌ی so نمی‌آید: I know. / I don't know. (نه I know so)

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="Copy the helper, then flip">
To agree, **copy** the first speaker's helper and **flip** it in front: *I **am** → So **am** I. I **can't** → Neither **can** I.* No helper? Use **do / did**. To answer a question, **so** = yes and **not** = no: *I think so. I hope not.*
</MemoryHook>
