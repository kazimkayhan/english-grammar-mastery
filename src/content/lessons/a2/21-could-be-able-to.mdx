## could: ability in the past

**could** is the past of **can** for ability (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 21</LessonRef>). It is a modal verb: the same for every subject, no *to*, no *do*.

<FormTable caption="could: past ability">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | subject + **could** + verb | I **could** read when I was four. |
| **−** | subject + **couldn't** / **could not** + verb | She **couldn't** swim until she was ten. |
| **?** | **Could** + subject + verb? | **Could** you speak English as a child? |
| **short answers** | Yes, + subject + **could**. / No, + subject + **couldn't**. | Yes, I **could**. / No, I **couldn't**. |

</FormTable>

Use **could** for a **general** ability: something you were able to do at any time in the past.

<Example context="home" fa="پدرکلانم وقتی جوان بود می‌توانست ده کیلومتر بدود.">My grandfather <mark>could</mark> run ten kilometers when he was young.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="وقتی به کانادا رفتم، نمی‌توانستم انگلیسی صحبت کنم.">When I moved to Canada, I <mark>couldn't</mark> speak English.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="دیشب نتوانستم بخوابم. سرم خیلی درد می‌کرد.">I <mark>couldn't</mark> sleep last night. I had a terrible headache.</Example>

**could** can also mean "was allowed to" in general: *When I was a child, I <mark>could</mark> stay up late on Fridays.*

### One success: was able to

For one **success** on **one occasion** in the past, don't use *could*. Use **was / were able to**:

<Example context="travel" fa="همه‌ی پروازها پر بود، اما توانستیم آخرین دو بلیت را بگیریم.">All the flights were full, but we <mark>were able to</mark> get the last two seats. (not “we could get”)</Example>

For one **failure**, **couldn't** is fine: *I <mark>couldn't</mark> open the door. The key was wrong.* (More about this, and about **managed to**, in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 19</LessonRef>, need, be allowed to, managed to.)

## be able to

**can** has only two forms: *can* and *could*. There is no *will can*, *to can* or *have could*. When you need another form, use **be able to**. It means the same as *can*.

<FormTable caption="be able to: all the forms">

| Time | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present | **am / is / are able to** | She **is able to** work from home. |
| past | **was / were able to** | We **were able to** find a hotel. |
| future | **will be able to** | You**'ll be able to** drive next year. |
| present perfect | **have / has been able to** | I **haven't been able to** sleep this week. |
| negative | **not able to** / **unable to** | He's **not able to** come. He's **unable to** come. (formal) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="بعد از عمل، چند هفته نمی‌توانی ورزش کنی.">After the operation, you <mark>won't be able to</mark> play sports for a few weeks.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="این هفته نتوانسته‌ام به ایمیل‌هایم جواب بدهم.">I <mark>haven't been able to</mark> answer my emails this week.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="سال دیگر می‌توانی فیلم‌ها را بدون زیرنویس ببینی.">Next year you<mark>'ll be able to</mark> watch films without subtitles.</Example>

- Questions: *<mark>Will</mark> you <mark>be able to</mark> come on Friday?* *<mark>Were</mark> you <mark>able to</mark> fix it?*
- In the present, **can** is more usual: *I can swim* is more natural than *I am able to swim*.
- For a decision about the future, **can** is also fine: *I can help you tomorrow.* For a new ability in the future, use **will be able to**.

## Asking people to do things: Could you…?

To ask someone to do something, use **Can you…?** or **Could you…?** + base form. **Could** is more polite. Add **please**.

<Example context="shopping" fa="می‌شود این را برایم کادو کنید، لطفاً؟"><mark>Could you</mark> wrap this for me, please?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="ببخشید، می‌توانید راه ایستگاه را به من نشان بدهید؟">Excuse me, <mark>could you</mark> tell me the way to the station?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="می‌توانی آهسته‌تر صحبت کنی، لطفاً؟"><mark>Could you</mark> speak more slowly, please?</Example>

Answers: *Sure. / Of course. / No problem.* To say no politely: *Sorry, I can't. I'm busy right now.*

Use **Can you…?** with friends and family. Use **Could you…?** with strangers, at work, or when you ask for something big. (*Would you…?* and *Would you mind…?* come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 20</LessonRef>, Polite requests.)

## Asking for permission: Can I, Could I, May I

<FormTable caption="Asking for permission">

| | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Can I**…? | friendly, normal | **Can I** sit here? |
| **Could I**…? | more polite | **Could I** leave a little early today? |
| **May I**…? | formal, very polite | **May I** come in? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="ممکن است امروز کمی زودتر بروم؟"><mark>Could I</mark> leave a little early today? I have a dentist appointment.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="اجازه هست بیایم داخل؟"><mark>May I</mark> come in?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«می‌توانم از دستشویی‌تان استفاده کنم؟» «بله، حتماً.»">“<mark>Can I</mark> use your bathroom?” “Yes, of course.”</Example>

**Answering:** to say yes, use **can** (or **may** in formal English), **not could**. *Could* is only for asking.

- Yes: *Yes, of course. / Sure. / Go ahead. / Yes, you **can**. / Yes, you **may**.* (formal)
- No: *Sorry, you **can't**. / I'm afraid not.* (formal: *No, you **may not**.*)

## Asking for things: Could I have…?

To ask for a thing, use **Can I have…? / Could I have…? / May I have…?** It is much more polite than an imperative (*Give me…*).

<Example context="shopping" fa="«یک قهوه‌ی کوچک لطفاً.» «حتماً. چیز دیگری هم می‌خواهید؟»">“<mark>Could I have</mark> a small coffee, please?” “Sure. Anything else?”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="ممکن است صورت‌حساب را بیاورید؟"><mark>Could we have</mark> the bill, please?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="می‌شود نمک را بدهی؟"><mark>Could you</mark> pass the salt, please?</Example>

You can also say **I'd like…, please** (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 24</LessonRef>): *I'd like a small coffee, please.*

## Senses: I can see, I could hear

With **see, hear, smell, taste** and **feel**, we often use **can**. It means "I'm seeing / hearing it now". Don't use the present continuous for this.

<Example context="travel" fa="از پنجره‌ی هتل دریا را می‌بینم!">I <mark>can see</mark> the sea from my hotel window! (not “I'm seeing the sea”)</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بوی سوختگی می‌آید. چیزی روی اجاق است؟">I <mark>can smell</mark> something burning. Is something on the stove?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="صدایم را می‌شنوی؟ صدایت قطع و وصل می‌شود."><mark>Can</mark> you <mark>hear</mark> me? You're breaking up.</Example>

For the past, use **could / couldn't**. With these verbs, *could* is fine even for **one moment** in the past:

<Example context="news" fa="همسایه‌ها از خیابان صدای فریاد می‌شنیدند.">The neighbors <mark>could hear</mark> shouts from the street.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مه زیاد بود و نمی‌توانستیم جاده را ببینیم.">It was very foggy. We <mark>couldn't see</mark> the road.</Example>

We use **can / could** in the same way with **understand** and **remember**: *I <mark>can't remember</mark> his name. I <mark>couldn't understand</mark> the announcement.*

**could = می‌توانستم (گذشته‌ی can).** برای توانایی کلی در گذشته: I could swim when I was six. (شش‌ساله که بودم می‌توانستم شنا کنم.) منفی: couldn't. سؤالی: Could you…? مثل can، بعدش to نمی‌آید و do نمی‌خواهد.

**یک موفقیت در گذشته:** فارسی خودش این تفاوت را دارد: «می‌توانستم» (توانایی کلی) با «توانستم» (یک بار موفق شدم) فرق دارد. در انگلیسی «می‌توانستم» = could، اما «توانستم» (یک بار) = **was / were able to**: We were able to get the last tickets. (توانستیم آخرین بلیت‌ها را بگیریم.) برای منفی (نتوانستم) couldn't هم درست است.

**be able to = توانستن در همه‌ی زمان‌ها.** can فقط دو شکل دارد (can و could). برای بقیه از be able to استفاده می‌کنیم:

- آینده: «خواهم توانست» = **will be able to** (نه will can).
- ماضی نقلی: «نتوانسته‌ام» = **haven't been able to**: I haven't been able to sleep. (نتوانسته‌ام بخوابم.)

**درخواست مؤدبانه:** «می‌شود…؟» یا «ممکن است…؟» در انگلیسی معمولاً **Could you…?** است: Could you help me? (می‌شود کمکم کنید؟) با دوستان Can you…? کافی است.

**اجازه گرفتن:** Can I…? (خودمانی) ← Could I…? (مؤدبانه) ← May I…? (رسمی): May I come in? (اجازه هست بیایم داخل؟) برای جواب مثبت از can یا may استفاده کنید، **نه could**: Yes, of course you can.

**چیزی خواستن:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «یک قهوه بدهید». ترجمه‌ی مستقیم آن (Give me a coffee) در انگلیسی بی‌ادبانه است. بگویید **Could I have** a coffee, please?

**حواس:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «دریا را می‌بینم». در انگلیسی معمولاً می‌گوییم **I can see** the sea، نه I'm seeing the sea. برای گذشته: I could hear voices. (صداهایی می‌شنیدم.) با remember و understand هم همین‌طور: I can't remember his name. (اسمش یادم نیست.)

<MemoryHook title="Could asks softly">
**Could** is the **soft voice** of *can*. Use it to **ask** (*Could you…? Could I…?*) and to look **back** (*I could swim at six*). But when someone asks you, answer with **can**. And when *can* has no form, call **be able to**.
</MemoryHook>
