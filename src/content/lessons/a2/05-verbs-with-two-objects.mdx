## Two objects

Some verbs have **two objects**: a **thing** (what you give) and a **person** (who gets it). The person is called the *indirect object*.

There are two patterns. They mean the same thing.

<FormTable caption="Two patterns">

| Pattern | Verb | Object 1 | Object 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · person first | gave | **Zahra** | the keys. |
| 2 · thing first | gave | the keys | **to Zahra**. |
| 1 · person first | bought | **my mom** | some flowers. |
| 2 · thing first | bought | some flowers | **for my mom**. |

</FormTable>

In pattern 1, there is **no** *to* or *for*. In pattern 2, the person comes after **to** or **for**.

<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً آن پرونده را به من بده.">Can you give <mark>me</mark> that file, please?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="معلم گزارش را به مدیر نشان داد.">The teacher showed the report <mark>to the principal</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مادرکلانم برایم یک شال گردن بافت.">My grandmother made <mark>me</mark> a scarf.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="برای همسرم یک ساعت خریدم.">I bought a watch <mark>for my wife</mark>.</Example>

### Which pattern?

Both are correct. Usually the **new** or **most important** information goes **last**.

- “What did you give your mom?” “I gave her **flowers**.” (the new part is *flowers*)
- “Who did you give the flowers to?” “I gave them **to my mom**.” (the new part is *my mom*)

When the person is a long phrase, pattern 2 is easier: *He showed his ticket **to the man at the door***.

## to or for?

<FormTable caption="Verbs with to and verbs with for">

| to: the thing goes to the person | for: you do it to help or please the person |
|---|---|
| give, send, show, lend, sell, pass, offer, pay, teach, take, bring, write | buy, make, cook, get, find, book, save, keep |
| I sent a message **to** Ahmad. | I made a cake **for** Ahmad. |
| She lent her car **to** me. | She found a good hotel **for** me. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="هتل برای ما یک تکسی رزرو کرد.">The hotel booked a taxi <mark>for us</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="او موتر قدیمی‌اش را به همسایه فروخت.">He sold his old car <mark>to his neighbor</mark>.</Example>

**bring** and **take** usually go with **to** (the thing moves to the person): *Bring the book **to** me.* With **for**, they mean “as a present or a favor”: *I brought these flowers **for** you.*

## it and them

When the thing is a pronoun (**it, them**), put it **right after the verb** and use **to / for** for the person.

<FormTable caption="it and them come first">

| ✓ Natural | ✗ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Give **it** to me. | ~~Give me it.~~ |
| I sent **them** to my boss. | ~~I sent my boss them.~~ |
| I'll buy **it** for you. | ~~I'll buy you it.~~ |

</FormTable>

You may hear *Give me it* in very informal speech, but many speakers find it unnatural. **Give it to me** is always correct.

<Example context="home" fa="«کلیدها کجاست؟» «به پدرت دادمشان.»">“Where are the keys?” “I gave <mark>them to</mark> your dad.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="گزارش آماده است. فعلا برایت می‌فرستمش.">The report is ready. I'll email <mark>it to</mark> you now.</Example>

If only the **person** is a pronoun, pattern 1 is very common: *Give **me** the keys. Send **her** a message.*

## Verbs that never take the person first

Some verbs look like *give*, but they **can't** put the person first. Always use **to** + person.

<FormTable caption="Always to + person">

| Verb | ✓ Correct | ✗ Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **explain** | Can you explain the rule **to me**? | ~~explain me the rule~~ |
| **say** | She said hello **to me**. | ~~she said me hello~~ |
| **describe** | Describe the man **to the police**. | ~~describe the police the man~~ |
| **suggest** | Can you suggest a good dentist **to me**? | ~~suggest me a dentist~~ |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="ببخشید، می‌توانید این تمرین را دوباره برایم توضیح بدهید؟">Sorry, can you explain this exercise <mark>to me</mark> again?</Example>

### tell and ask

**tell** and **ask** usually put the **person** right after the verb, with no *to*:

- *Tell **me** the truth.* · *Can you tell **us** the way to the station?*
- *Can I ask **you** a question?* (not ~~ask a question to you~~)

**say** or **tell**? *Say* something **to** someone, but *tell* **someone** something: *She **said** goodbye **to** me. She **told me** a story.* More in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 26 (Reported statements: say or tell?)</LessonRef>.

### lend or borrow?

- **lend** = give something for a short time. It has two objects: *Can you **lend me** your pen?*
- **borrow** = take something for a short time. The person comes after **from**: *Can I **borrow** your pen?* · *I borrowed some money **from** my brother.*

Never say ~~borrow me~~.

## Questions

In a question, the preposition often goes at the end (<LessonRef>lesson 1 (Wh- questions: which, whose, how long, what … like)</LessonRef>):

<Example context="social" fa="آن کادو را برای چه کسی خریدی؟">Who did you buy that present <mark>for</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ایمیل را برای چه کسی فرستادی؟">Who did you send the email <mark>to</mark>?</Example>

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="I gave to Ahmad the book." right="I gave Ahmad the book. / I gave the book to Ahmad." why="to + person never comes before the thing." />
<WrongRight wrong="Give me it." right="Give it to me." why="With it or them, put the pronoun first and use to." />
<WrongRight wrong="Can you explain me the problem?" right="Can you explain the problem to me?" why="explain + thing + to + person." />
<WrongRight wrong="She said me the news." right="She told me the news. / She said the news to me." why="say + to + person; tell + person." />
<WrongRight wrong="Can you borrow me some money?" right="Can you lend me some money?" why="lend = give; borrow = take." />
<WrongRight wrong="I bought a present to my sister." right="I bought a present for my sister." why="buy takes for." />

<MemoryHook title="Person in the middle, or to at the end">
Put the **person in the middle** with no little word (*give **Zahra** the keys*), or the **thing in the middle** and **to / for** at the end (*give the keys **to Zahra***). If the thing is **it**, it always wins the middle: *give **it** to her*.
</MemoryHook>
