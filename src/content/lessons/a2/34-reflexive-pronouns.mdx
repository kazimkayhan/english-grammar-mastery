## The forms

A **reflexive pronoun** ends in **-self** (one person) or **-selves** (more than one).

<FormTable caption="Subject, object and reflexive pronouns">

| Subject | Object | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | **myself** |
| you (one person) | you | **yourself** |
| he | him | **himself** |
| she | her | **herself** |
| it | it | **itself** |
| we | us | **ourselves** |
| you (more than one) | you | **yourselves** |
| they | them | **themselves** |

</FormTable>

- *you* has two reflexives: **yourself** for one person, **yourselves** for a group.
- **himself** and **themselves** are built on *him* and *them*. Forms like ~~hisself~~ and ~~theirselves~~ are not standard English.

## When the subject and the object are the same

Use a reflexive pronoun when the person **does something to himself or herself**: the subject and the object are the **same person**.

<Example context="home" fa="موقع آشپزی دستم را بریدم.">I cut <mark>myself</mark> while I was cooking.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="او در آینه به خودش نگاه کرد.">She looked at <mark>herself</mark> in the mirror.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="او خودش به خودش انگلیسی یاد داد.">He taught <mark>himself</mark> English with videos.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="کامپیوتر شب‌ها خودش خاموش می‌شود.">The computer turns <mark>itself</mark> off at night.</Example>

Compare:

- *Zahra hurt **herself**.* (Zahra hurt Zahra.)
- *Zahra hurt **her**.* (Zahra hurt another woman.)

After a **preposition**, use a reflexive when it means the same person: *He's talking **to himself**. Take care **of yourself**. She's proud **of herself**.* But after place words, English usually uses the normal pronoun when the meaning is clear: *She took her dog **with her*** (not *with herself*).

**themselves or each other?** *They looked at **themselves*** = each person looked at himself or herself. *They looked at **each other*** = each person looked at the other. *each other* is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 43 (Generic you, one, they; each other)</LessonRef>.

## Fixed phrases

Some very common phrases use a reflexive. You hear them at parties, at work and at school.

<FormTable caption="Everyday phrases with -self">

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **enjoy yourself** | have a good time | Did you **enjoy yourself** at the wedding? |
| **help yourself (to…)** | take some (food, drink) | **Help yourself to** some cake! |
| **make yourself at home** | relax, act as if it's your home | Come in! **Make yourself at home**. |
| **introduce yourself** | say your name | Please **introduce yourselves**. |
| **take care of / look after yourself** | stay safe and well | **Take care of yourself**! |
| **behave yourself** | be good | **Behave yourselves**, kids! |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="بفرمایید، از غذا بردارید!">Please <mark>help yourselves</mark> to some food!</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="در مهمانی خوش گذشت؟">Did you <mark>enjoy yourself</mark> at the party?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="لطفاً خودتان را معرفی کنید.">Please <mark>introduce yourselves</mark>. Tell us your name and where you're from.</Example>

**enjoy needs an object.** Say *I **enjoyed myself*** or *I **enjoyed the party***, never ~~I enjoyed~~ alone.

## by myself = alone or without help

**by + reflexive** has two meanings:

- **alone**: *My grandmother lives **by herself**.*
- **without help**: *Did you make this cake **by yourself**?*

<Example context="travel" fa="او تنهایی به ژاپن سفر کرد.">She traveled to Japan <mark>by herself</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="من خودم تنهایی میز را درست کردم.">I fixed the table <mark>by myself</mark>. Nobody helped me.</Example>

Add **all** to make it stronger: *He was **all by himself** in the house.* *on my own* means the same and comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 43 (Generic you, one, they; each other)</LessonRef>.

## Emphasis: I did it myself

A reflexive can also add **emphasis**. It means "this person, and **nobody else**".

<Example context="home" fa="«کی این کیک را پخت؟» «خودم پختمش!»">“Who made this cake?” “I made it <mark>myself</mark>!”</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="رئیس‌جمهور خودش بیمارستان جدید را افتتاح کرد.">The president <mark>himself</mark> opened the new hospital.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="مدیر خودش به من زنگ زد.">The manager called me <mark>herself</mark>.</Example>

Put the emphatic pronoun **right after the noun** (*The president himself…*) or **at the end** (*I made it myself*).

How is this different from the first use? Here you **can remove** the reflexive and the sentence still works: *I made it.* In *I cut myself*, you can't remove it: ~~I cut~~ is not complete.

## Verbs without a reflexive

Some verbs take a reflexive in other languages but **not usually in English**.

- **feel**: *I **feel** tired.* (not ~~I feel myself tired~~)
- **concentrate**, **hurry**: *I can't **concentrate**. **Hurry** up!*
- **wash, dress, shave** usually have no reflexive: *I got up, **washed** and **got dressed**.* Use the reflexive only when it's important to say who did it: *She's old enough to **dress herself** now.*

More verbs like this come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 8 (Ergative, reflexive & reciprocal verbs)</LessonRef>.

**Don't use myself for me** when the subject is someone else: *She called **me**.* (not ~~She called myself~~). Other uses of *myself* in place of *me* are in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 29 (Pronouns: advanced)</LessonRef>.

<WrongRight wrong="They did it theirselves." right="They did it themselves." why="themselves is the standard form, never theirselves." />
<WrongRight wrong="Did you enjoy at the party?" right="Did you enjoy yourself at the party?" why="enjoy needs an object: enjoy yourself, or enjoy the party." />

<MemoryHook title="The mirror test">
Imagine a **mirror**: if the action comes **back** to the same person (*I cut myself, she looked at herself*), use **-self**. More than one person in the mirror? **-selves**.
</MemoryHook>
