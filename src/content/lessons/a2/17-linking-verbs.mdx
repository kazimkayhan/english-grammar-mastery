## Verbs that work like be

Most verbs are **actions**: *run, eat, write*. A **linking verb** is different. It **links** the subject to a word that **describes** it, just like **be** (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 29</LessonRef>, Adjectives: before nouns and after be).

*The soup **is** hot.* → *The soup **smells** good.* → *The soup **tastes** salty.*

<FormTable caption="Linking verb + adjective">

| Verb | How you know | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **be** | (it is a fact) | The soup **is** hot. |
| **look** | with your eyes | You **look** tired. |
| **sound** | with your ears, or from what you hear or read | Your plan **sounds** great. |
| **smell** | with your nose | The bread **smells** wonderful. |
| **taste** | with your mouth | This coffee **tastes** bitter. |
| **feel** | by touch, or inside your body and mind | The towel **feels** soft. · I **feel** happy. |
| **seem** | your general impression | Your new boss **seems** nice. |
| **get / become** | a change | It's **getting** cold. · She **became** famous. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="خسته به نظر می‌رسی. دیشب خوابیدی؟">You <mark>look tired</mark>. Did you sleep last night?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این ژاکت نرم است. قیمتش چند است؟">This sweater <mark>feels soft</mark>. How much is it?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="آشپزخانه بوی خیلی خوبی می‌دهد! چه می‌پزی؟">The kitchen <mark>smells amazing</mark>! What are you cooking?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«بیا بعد از کار شام بیرون بخوریم.» «فکر خوبی است!»">“Let's have dinner out after work.” “That <mark>sounds great</mark>!”</Example>

### seem

**seem** gives your **impression**: what you think after seeing, hearing or meeting something. Use it with an adjective, or with **to be**:

<Example context="work" fa="همکار جدیدمان آدم مهربانی به نظر می‌رسد.">Our new colleague <mark>seems</mark> very kind.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="امروز به نظر می‌رسد سرش خیلی شلوغ است.">She <mark>seems to be</mark> very busy today.</Example>

*seem* is never used in the continuous: ~~She is seeming happy~~. More patterns (*It seems that…, There seems to be…*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 4</LessonRef>, it or there?

### get and become: a change

**get** + adjective means *become*. It is very common in everyday English, often in the continuous for a change in progress (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 23</LessonRef>, Present simple or present continuous?). **become** means the same but is more **formal**.

<Example context="home" fa="دیر وقت است. دارم خسته می‌شوم.">It's late. I<mark>'m getting tired</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="امیدوارم زود خوب شوی.">I hope you <mark>get better</mark> soon.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="بعد از آن فیلم مشهور شد.">She <mark>became famous</mark> after that film.</Example>

Before a **noun**, use **become**, not *get*: *She **became** a doctor*, not ~~She got a doctor~~. The other meanings of *get* come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 15</LessonRef>, The many uses of get.

## Adjective, not adverb

After a linking verb, use an **adjective** (*happy, good, soft*), not an **adverb** (*happily, well, softly*). The word describes the **subject**, not the action.

<Example context="home" fa="این کیک خوشمزه است!">This cake <mark>tastes delicious</mark>!</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="بابت دیروز احساس بدی دارم.">I <mark>feel bad</mark> about yesterday.</Example>

Not ~~tastes deliciously~~, not ~~feel badly~~.

But the same verbs can also be **actions**. Then they take an **adverb**, like any action verb:

- *He **looks angry**.* (description: his face is angry) / *He **looked angrily** at me.* (action: how did he look at me?)
- *The soup **tastes good**.* (description) / *She **tasted** the soup **carefully**.* (action: she tried it)

**well** has two jobs. As an adverb it means *in a good way* (*She sings well*). As an adjective it means **healthy**: *I don't **feel well**. You **look well**!* So *I feel good* and *I feel well* are both correct. The full story of adjectives and adverbs is in <LessonRef>lesson 41</LessonRef>, Adverbs of manner: quickly, well.

**Continuous or not?** With the meaning *have a smell / taste*, **smell** and **taste** are not used in the continuous: *This cake **tastes** delicious*, not ~~is tasting~~. **look** and **feel** about health can be: *How **are** you **feeling**? You**'re looking** well.* (Verbs with two meanings come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 10</LessonRef>, State verbs & action verbs.)

## Questions and negatives

These are normal verbs, so the present and past simple need **do / does / did**.

<Example context="shopping" fa="این لباس به من می‌آید؟">Does this dress <mark>look</mark> OK on me?</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="حالت خوب به نظر نمی‌رسد. دکتر رفتی؟">You <mark>don't look</mark> well. Have you seen a doctor?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="«ماهی چه مزه‌ای دارد؟» «عالی است!»">“How <mark>does</mark> the fish <mark>taste</mark>?” “Wonderful!”</Example>

- **How does it look / taste / sound?** asks for your opinion: *“How does my hair look?” “It looks great.”*
- **How do you feel?** or **How are you feeling?** asks about health or mood.

## look like, sound like + noun

Before a **noun**, add **like**. It means *similar to*.

<FormTable caption="Three patterns">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + **adjective** | She **looks happy**. That **sounds** interesting. |
| verb + **like** + noun | She **looks like** a film star. That **sounds like** a good idea. |
| verb + **as if / as though** + clause | She **looks as if** she's won the lottery. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="اینجا بوی قهوه می‌آید.">It <mark>smells like</mark> coffee in here.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این پارچه مثل ابریشم است.">This scarf <mark>feels like</mark> silk.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="آن صدا شبیه صدای کریم است. او اینجاست؟">That <mark>sounds like</mark> Karim's voice. Is he here?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="این مزه‌ی مرغ می‌دهد.">This <mark>tastes like</mark> chicken.</Example>

- No **like** before an adjective: *She looks **tired***, not ~~She looks like tired~~.
- **What does he look like?** asks about **appearance**: *He's tall, with dark hair.* **What's he like?** asks about **character**: *He's friendly.* (See <LessonRef>lesson 1</LessonRef>, Wh- questions: which, whose, how long, what … like.)
- For a flavour, British English also says **taste of**: *This tea **tastes of** lemon.*
- **feel like** + noun or -ing can also mean **want**: *I feel like a coffee.* That meaning comes in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 30</LessonRef>, Fixed expressions + -ing.

<UkNote>Especially in British English, you can sometimes leave out **like** before *a / an* + adjective + noun: *That **sounds a good idea**. It **seems a** strange **decision**.* With **like** it is correct everywhere, so **like** is the safe choice.</UkNote>

## look as if, sound as if + clause

Before a whole **clause** (subject + verb), use **as if** or **as though**. They mean the same.

<Example context="travel" fa="انگار قرار است باران بیاید. چتر ببر.">It <mark>looks as if</mark> it's going to rain. Take an umbrella.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="صدایت طوری است که انگار سرما خورده‌ای.">You <mark>sound as though</mark> you've got a cold.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="به نظر می‌رسد دیشب نخوابیده است.">He <mark>looks as if</mark> he hasn't slept.</Example>

In **informal** English, people often use **like** instead of *as if*: *It **looks like** it's going to rain.* In careful writing, *as if* is safer. (*as if* with unreal meanings, *He talks as if he were the boss*, comes in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 16</LessonRef>, as if, would rather, it's high time, suppose.)

**فعل‌های ربطی (linking verbs):** این فعل‌ها کار نیستند؛ مثل be فاعل را به یک صفت وصل می‌کنند و آن را توصیف می‌کنند. معمولاً در فارسی با «به نظر رسیدن»، «بو دادن»، «مزه دادن» یا «احساس کردن» ترجمه می‌شوند.

- **look** (با چشم): You look tired. (خسته به نظر می‌رسی.)
- **sound** (با گوش یا از چیزی که می‌شنویم): That sounds great. (عالی به نظر می‌رسد.)
- **smell** (با بینی): The bread smells good. (نان بوی خوبی می‌دهد.)
- **taste** (با دهان): The soup tastes salty. (سوپ شور است / مزه‌ی شوری می‌دهد.)
- **feel** (با لمس یا در درون): The towel feels soft. / I feel happy. (احساس خوشحالی می‌کنم.)
- **seem** (برداشت کلی): He seems nice. (آدم خوبی به نظر می‌رسد.)
- **get / become** (تغییر، «شدن»): It's getting dark. (دارد تاریک می‌شود.) get محاوره‌ای و رایج است؛ become رسمی‌تر است. قبل از اسم فقط become: She became a doctor.

**بعد از فعل ربطی صفت می‌آید، نه قید:** It tastes good (نه well)، I feel bad (نه badly)، You look happy (نه happily). اما well وقتی به معنی «سالم» است صفت است: I don't feel well. (حالم خوب نیست.)

**like + اسم:** قبل از اسم باید like بگذاریم: She looks like her mother. (شبیه مادرش است.) This tastes like chicken. (مزه‌ی مرغ می‌دهد.) اما قبل از صفت like نمی‌آید: She looks tired (نه looks like tired).

**as if + جمله:** «انگار / مثل اینکه»: It looks as if it's going to rain. (انگار می‌خواهد باران بیاید.) در محاوره like هم می‌گویند: It looks like it's going to rain.

**سؤال و منفی:** این‌ها فعل معمولی‌اند و do / does / did می‌خواهند: Does it taste OK? / You don't look well. برای ظاهر کسی می‌پرسیم: What does he look like? (چه شکلی است؟)

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟**

- در فارسی «خوب» هم صفت است و هم قید («غذای خوب» و «خوب می‌خواند»). پس فارسی‌زبان فرق good و well را حس نمی‌کند و می‌گوید It tastes well. بعد از فعل ربطی صفت بیاورید: It tastes **good**.
- در فارسی می‌گوییم «بوی قهوه می‌دهد» یا «مزه‌ی مرغ می‌دهد» و حرف اضافه‌ای لازم نیست. فارسی‌زبان می‌گوید It smells coffee. در انگلیسی قبل از اسم like لازم است: It smells **like** coffee.
- چون «به نظر می‌رسد» را گاهی «مثل… به نظر می‌رسد» می‌گوییم، بعضی‌ها like را قبل از صفت هم می‌آورند: She looks like tired. قبل از صفت like نمی‌آید: She looks **tired**.
- در فارسی برای «دکتر شد» و «خسته شد» یک فعل داریم: «شدن». در انگلیسی get فقط با صفت می‌آید (get tired)؛ برای شغل و نقش از become استفاده کنید: She **became** a doctor.

<MemoryHook title="Can you put is there?">
If you can change the verb to **is** and the sentence still makes sense (*You look tired* → *You **are** tired*), it is a **linking verb**: use an **adjective**. A **noun** after it? Add **like**. A whole **sentence** after it? Use **as if**.
</MemoryHook>
