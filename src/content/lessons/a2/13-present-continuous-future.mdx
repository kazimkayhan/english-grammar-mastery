## The same form, a future meaning

You know the present continuous from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>, Present continuous: **am / is / are + -ing** for things happening now. We use the **same form** for the **future** when the plan is already **arranged**.

<FormTable caption="Present continuous for the future">

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | I**'m meeting** Ahmad tomorrow. | I**'m not meeting** him today. | **Am** I **meeting** him at six? |
| he / she / it | She**'s flying** to Istanbul on Monday. | She **isn't flying** on Sunday. | **Is** she **flying** on Monday? |
| you / we / they | We**'re having** a party on Friday. | We **aren't having** it on Saturday. | **Are** you **having** a party? |

</FormTable>

The forms, the short forms and the **-ing spelling** are exactly the same as for now. Only the **time** is different.

<Example context="social" fa="شنبه یک مهمانی داریم. می‌آیی؟">We<mark>'re having</mark> a party on Saturday. Can you come?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مینا دوشنبه به استانبول پرواز می‌کند. بلیتش را دیروز خرید.">Mina<mark>'s flying</mark> to Istanbul on Monday. She bought her ticket yesterday.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ماه بعد کار جدیدی را شروع می‌کنم.">I<mark>'m starting</mark> a new job next month.</Example>

## Arrangements: plans in your calendar

An **arrangement** is a plan you have already **fixed**. Usually another person knows about it too: you agreed a day and a time, you booked a table, or you bought a ticket. A good test: if you can write it in your **calendar** or **diary**, the present continuous is a natural choice.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "span", from: -0.45, to: -0.05, label: "we arranged it" }, { type: "point", at: 0.55, label: "Friday: I'm meeting Ahmad" }]} caption="The plan was fixed before now. The action happens in the future." />

<Example context="work" fa="فردا ساعت ده با مدیر جلسه دارم.">I<mark>'m having</mark> a meeting with the manager at ten tomorrow.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پنجشنبه بعدازظهر پیش دکتر می‌روم. وقت گرفته‌ام.">I<mark>'m seeing</mark> the doctor on Thursday afternoon. I have an appointment.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="زهرا و کریم بهار سال بعد عروسی می‌کنند.">Zahra and Karim <mark>are getting</mark> married next spring.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="پدر و مادرم یکشنبه برای شام می‌آیند.">My parents <mark>are coming</mark> for dinner on Sunday.</Example>

Verbs we often use this way: **meet, see** (a person), **have** (a party, a meeting, dinner), **go, come, leave, arrive, fly, visit, start, play** (a match).

## The time shows the future

Without a future time, the present continuous usually means **now**. So add a future time, or make sure the conversation shows it.

<Example context="social" fa="دارم فوتبال بازی می‌کنم. (فعلا)">I<mark>'m playing</mark> football. (now, at this moment)</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="یکشنبه فوتبال بازی می‌کنم. (برنامه‌ی آینده)">I<mark>'m playing</mark> football <mark>on Sunday</mark>. (a future arrangement)</Example>

Common future times: **tonight, this evening, tomorrow (morning), this weekend, on Friday, next week, next month, at six, in June, after class**.

Sometimes the time is already in the question: *“What are you doing **after work**?” “I'm meeting some friends.”* The answer needs no time word, because the question gave it.

## Negatives and questions

**Negative:** put **not** after *am / is / are*, as usual.

<Example context="work" fa="فردا کار نمی‌کنم. روز تعطیلم است.">I<mark>'m not working</mark> tomorrow. It's my day off.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="احمد به مهمانی نمی‌آید. سرما خورده است.">Ahmad <mark>isn't coming</mark> to the party. He has a cold.</Example>

**Questions:** this is the normal way to ask about someone's plans.

<FormTable caption="Asking about plans">

| Type | Example | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| yes/no | **Are** you **doing** anything tonight? | Yes, I **am**. / No, I**'m not**. |
| yes/no | **Is** your brother **coming** with us? | Yes, he **is**. / No, he **isn't**. |
| wh- | **What are** you **doing** this weekend? | I'm visiting my grandparents. |
| wh- | **When are** you **leaving**? · **What time is** she **arriving**? | On Sunday. · At 9:30. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«این آخر هفته چه کار می‌کنی؟» «به دیدن پدرکلان و مادرکلانم می‌روم.»">“<mark>What are</mark> you <mark>doing</mark> this weekend?” “I'm visiting my grandparents.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="ساعت چند می‌رسید؟">What time <mark>are</mark> you <mark>arriving</mark>?</Example>

Watch the difference: *What **are** you **doing** tonight?* asks about your plans. *What **do** you **do**?* asks about your job.

## What it is not for

**1. Not for your plans in the present simple.** We don't normally use the present simple for personal arrangements. Say *I'm meeting Ahmad tomorrow*, not ~~I meet Ahmad tomorrow~~, and *What time **are** you **meeting** him?*, not ~~What time do you meet him?~~ The present simple does have one future use, for **timetables** (*The train leaves at 8:15*). That comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 11</LessonRef>, All the futures compared.

**2. Not for the weather or predictions.** Nobody arranges the weather or the result of a match. Use **going to** (you can see it coming) or **will** (your opinion).

<Example context="travel" fa="به آن ابرها نگاه کن! باران می‌آید.">Look at those clouds! It<mark>'s going to rain</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="فکر می‌کنم تیم ما فردا می‌برد.">I think our team <mark>will win</mark> tomorrow.</Example>

## will, going to or present continuous? (first look)

You now know three ways to talk about the future. Each one has a main job.

<FormTable caption="Three futures: first look">

| Form | Main use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** | a decision you make **now**, while you speak; offers and promises; your opinion about the future | It's cold. I**'ll close** the window. · I think you**'ll like** it. |
| **going to** | an **intention**: you decided **before** now, but maybe nothing is booked; a prediction from what you can **see** now | I**'m going to learn** Spanish this year. · Look! It**'s going to rain**. |
| **present continuous** | an **arrangement**: decided before **and** fixed with other people or booked, usually with a time | I**'m meeting** Ahmad at six. · We**'re flying** to Band-e-Amir on Friday. |

</FormTable>

One conversation, three futures:

<Example context="social" fa="«امشب برنامه‌ای داری؟» «بلی، با پدر و مادرم شام می‌خورم.»">“Are you doing anything tonight?” “Yes, I<mark>'m having</mark> dinner with my parents.” (arrangement)</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="این آخر هفته می‌خواهم اتاقم را رنگ کنم.">I<mark>'m going to paint</mark> my room this weekend. (my intention; nothing to arrange)</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«خودکار ندارم.» «اشکالی ندارد. یکی به تو قرض می‌دهم.»">“I haven't got a pen.” “No problem. I<mark>'ll lend</mark> you one.” (decision now)</Example>

**Present continuous or going to?** For an arrangement, both are often possible, with little change in meaning: *I'm seeing Zahra tomorrow* ≈ *I'm going to see Zahra tomorrow.* The present continuous puts the weight on the **arrangement**; *going to* puts it on your **intention**. For a plan in your head that involves nobody else, *going to* is the safer choice.

**going + to a place.** *I'm going to the cinema tonight* is the present continuous of *go*. *I'm going to go to the cinema tonight* is also correct, but people often prefer the shorter sentence.

**Not will for a plan you made before.** *I'll visit my grandparents on Sunday* sounds as if you are deciding right now. If you decided last week, say *I'm visiting* (arranged) or *I'm going to visit* (intention).

<MemoryHook title="The calendar test">
If you can write it in your **calendar** (a time, a place, another person), use the **present continuous**: *I'm meeting Ahmad at six.* Only in your **head**? Use **going to**. Deciding **right now**? Use **will**.
</MemoryHook>
