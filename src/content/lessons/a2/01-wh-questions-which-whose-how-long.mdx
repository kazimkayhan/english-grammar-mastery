## which or what?

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 7 (Question words: what, where, who, when, how)</LessonRef> you learned **what**. **which** also asks about a thing, but it is a little different.

- **what** = an **open** choice. There are many possible answers.
- **which** = a choice from a **small group** that you can see or that you both know.

<FormTable caption="what or which?">

| Question | Why? |
|---|---|
| **What** music do you like? | open: there are thousands of kinds of music |
| **Which** bag is yours, the black one or the brown one? | only two bags |
| **What** languages do you speak? | open |
| We have white or brown bread. **Which** do you want? | two kinds of bread |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="کدام بس به فرودگاه می‌رود، ۱۲ یا ۱۵؟"><mark>Which</mark> bus goes to the airport, the 12 or the 15?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کدام رنگ را ترجیح می‌دهی، قرمز یا آبی؟"><mark>Which</mark> color do you prefer, red or blue?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="آخر هفته مکاکالاً چه کار می‌کنی؟"><mark>What</mark> do you usually do on weekends?</Example>

Often both words are possible and the meaning is almost the same. Choose **which** when you have a few choices in mind.

### which of, which one

Before **the, my, your, these, those** or a pronoun (**us, you, them**), say **which of**. “What of” is not normal English.

<Example context="school" fa="کدام‌یک از شما فارسی صحبت می‌کند؟"><mark>Which of</mark> you speaks Persian?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کدام‌یک از این کت‌ها مال توست؟"><mark>Which of</mark> these coats is yours?</Example>

You can also say **which one** (for one thing) and **which ones** (for two or more) instead of repeating the noun: *“I like those shoes.” “**Which ones**?”*

### People: who, not which

For people, when there is no noun after the question word, use **who**: ***Who** is older, you or your sister?* (not ~~Which is older…?~~). With a noun, **which** is fine: ***Which** teacher do you like best?*

## Whose…?

**whose** asks about the **owner**. It can go before a noun, or alone.

<Example context="work" fa="این موبایلِ کیست؟ مال احمد است.">“<mark>Whose</mark> phone is this?” “It's Ahmad's.”</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="این‌ها مال کیست؟">“<mark>Whose</mark> are these?” “They're mine.”</Example>

Don't mix up **whose** (the owner) and **who's** (= who is): *Who's that man?* Words like *mine, yours, hers* and more practice with *whose* are in <LessonRef>lesson 33 (mine, yours… and whose)</LessonRef>.

## How long, how often, how far

**how** joins with another word to ask for a measure. You know *how old, how much, how many* from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 7</LessonRef>. Here are three more.

<FormTable caption="how long, how often, how far">

| Question | Asks about | Example | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How long** | an amount of time | How long does the trip take? | About three hours. |
| **How long** | length | How long is the movie? | Two hours. |
| **How often** | how many times | How often do you go to the gym? | Twice a week. |
| **How far** | distance | How far is it to the station? | About 500 meters. |

</FormTable>

### How long

**How long** asks about time. It often goes with **take**: *How long does it take to get to work?* · *It takes 20 minutes.*

<Example context="travel" fa="پرواز چقدر طول می‌کشد؟"><mark>How long</mark> does the flight take?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="در هرات چقدر ماندید؟"><mark>How long</mark> did you stay in Herat?</Example>

**how long** already means “how much time”, so don't add *time*: ~~How long time…?~~ (*How much time do we have?* is also correct.)

*How long have you lived here?* uses the present perfect. That is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 7 (for, since, ago, during; by or until?)</LessonRef>.

### How often

**How often** asks how many times something happens. Answer with a frequency word from <LessonRef>A1 lesson 19 (Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, never)</LessonRef>, or with a phrase: **every day**, **once a week**, **twice a month**, **three times a year**.

<Example context="health" fa="«هر چند وقت یک‌بار ورزش می‌کنی؟» «سه بار در هفته.»">“<mark>How often</mark> do you exercise?” “Three times a week.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="«بس هر چند وقت یک‌بار می‌آید؟» «هر بیست دقیقه.»">“<mark>How often</mark> does the bus come?” “Every 20 minutes.”</Example>

*How many times a week do you exercise?* is also correct, but **how often** is shorter and more common.

### How far

**How far** asks about distance. Use **it** as the subject: *How far is **it** from Kabul to Mazar-i-Sharif?*

<Example context="travel" fa="«تا هتل چقدر راه است؟» «حدود دو کیلومتر.»">“<mark>How far</mark> is it to the hotel?” “About two kilometers.”</Example>

## What … like?

**What is X like?** asks someone to **describe** a person, a place or a thing. Here **like** is not the verb *like*. It means “similar to”, so the question means “Tell me about it.”

<Example context="work" fa="«رئیس جدیدت چطور آدمی است؟» «خیلی مهربان است.»">“<mark>What's</mark> your new boss <mark>like</mark>?” “She's really nice.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هوای آنجا چطور است؟">“<mark>What's</mark> the weather <mark>like</mark> there?” “Hot and sunny.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل چطور بود؟"><mark>What was</mark> the hotel <mark>like</mark>?</Example>

Four questions look similar but ask different things:

<FormTable caption="Four questions about Zahra">

| Question | Asks about | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **What's** Zahra **like**? | her character, a description | She's funny and kind. |
| **What does** Zahra **like**? | her likes (*like* = verb) | She likes tennis and jazz. |
| **What does** Zahra **look like**? | her appearance | She's tall, with long hair. |
| **How is** Zahra? | her health or mood now | She's fine. She's a bit tired. |

</FormTable>

With **look like** the question word is **what**: *What does he look like?* Never ~~How does he look like?~~ (Without *like*, *How does he look?* asks if he looks well or tired.)

## What kind of…?

**What kind of**, **what sort of** and **what type of** ask about the **type** of something. The three mean the same. *kind* and *sort* are the most common in speech.

<Example context="social" fa="چه جور موسیقی‌ای دوست داری؟"><mark>What kind of</mark> music do you like?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="چه نوع کاری می‌کنی؟"><mark>What sort of</mark> work do you do?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«چه جور موتری است؟» «یک موتر کوچک شهری.»">“<mark>What kind of</mark> car is it?” “A small city car.”</Example>

After *kind of / sort of*, we usually use a **singular** or **uncountable** noun: *What kind of **car**? What kind of **music**?* A plural noun is also common in speech: *What kind of **movies** do you like?* And never drop **of**: ~~What kind music…?~~

## Prepositions at the end

Some verbs go with a preposition: *talk **to**, look **at**, wait **for**, think **about**, come **from**, stay **with***. In a question, the question word goes first, but the preposition stays **at the end**.

<FormTable caption="Question word … preposition">

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| She's talking **to** her boss. | **Who** is she talking **to**? |
| I'm looking **at** the map. | **What** are you looking **at**? |
| He comes **from** Bamyan. | **Where** does he come **from**? |
| This present is **for** my mom. | **Who** is this present **for**? |
| They're staying **at** the Park Hotel. | **Which** hotel are they staying **at**? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="داری به چی فکر می‌کنی؟">What are you thinking <mark>about</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="با کی درس می‌خوانی؟">Who do you study <mark>with</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="چه جور فیلم‌هایی دوست داری؟ (به چه فیلم‌هایی علاقه داری؟)">What kind of movies are you interested <mark>in</mark>?</Example>

This is normal, correct English. Don't drop the preposition: *Who are you waiting **for**?* (not ~~Who are you waiting?~~).

In very formal English, the preposition can go first, with **whom**: *To whom…? With whom…?* This sounds very formal. Who and whom are in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 29 (Pronouns: advanced)</LessonRef>. In speech, we often use a short question: *“I'm going to the movies tonight.” “**Who with?**”* · *“I bought a present.” “**Who for?**”*

## Questions with or

A question with **or** gives two (or more) choices. It is not a yes/no question, and it is not an open question. Answer with **the choice**.

<Example context="social" fa="«چای می‌خوری یا قهوه؟» «قهوه، لطفاً.»">“Do you want tea <mark>or</mark> coffee?” “Coffee, please.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="داخل می‌نشینید یا بیرون؟">“Would you like to sit inside <mark>or</mark> outside?” “Outside, please.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="جلسه امروز است یا فردا؟">Is the meeting today <mark>or</mark> tomorrow?</Example>

You can also start with **which** and put the choices at the end: ***Which** is faster, the bus **or** the metro?*

When you say the choices, your voice goes **up** on the first one and **down** on the last one: *tea ↗ or coffee ↘?* That tells the listener to choose. If your voice goes up at the end (*tea or coffee ↗?*), you are asking “Do you want a drink?”, and the answer can be *Yes, please.*
