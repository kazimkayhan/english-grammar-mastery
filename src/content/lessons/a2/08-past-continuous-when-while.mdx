## The form

The past continuous is the past of the present continuous (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>). It has **two pieces**: **was / were** (the past of *be*, <LessonRef>A1 lesson 25</LessonRef>) + the verb with **-ing**.

<FormTable caption="Past continuous">

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | She **was** work**ing**. | She **wasn't** working. | **Was** she working? |
| you / we / they | They **were** work**ing**. | They **weren't** working. | **Were** they working? |

</FormTable>

- **was** goes with *I, he, she, it* and any singular noun. **were** goes with *you, we, they* and plural nouns.
- **wasn't** = *was not*, **weren't** = *were not*. The full forms are more formal.
- The **-ing** spelling is the same as in the present: *make → making, run → running, lie → lying* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 22</LessonRef>).

<Example context="home" fa="ساعت هشت داشتم شام می‌پختم.">At eight o'clock I <mark>was cooking</mark> dinner.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="داشتیم منتظر اتوبوس می‌ماندیم.">We <mark>were waiting</mark> for the bus.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دیروز بعدازظهر کار نمی‌کرد. مرخصی بود.">She <mark>wasn't working</mark> yesterday afternoon. She was on vacation.</Example>

## Questions and short answers

Put **was / were** before the subject. Short answers use only **was / were**, never *did*.

<FormTable caption="Past continuous: questions">

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| yes/no | **Was / Were** + subject + -ing? | **Were** you sleeping? |
| short answers | Yes, + subject + **was / were**. No, + subject + **wasn't / weren't**. | Yes, I **was**. / No, I **wasn't**. |
| wh- | question word + **was / were** + subject + -ing? | **What were** you **doing**? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«وقتی زنگ زدم خواب بودی؟» «نه، نبودم.»">“<mark>Were</mark> you <mark>sleeping</mark> when I called?” “No, I <mark>wasn't</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="دیشب ساعت نه چه کار می‌کردید؟"><mark>What were</mark> you <mark>doing</mark> at nine o'clock last night?</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="وقتی زمین خورد، داشت می‌دوید؟"><mark>Was</mark> he <mark>running</mark> when he fell?</Example>

## Use 1: in progress at a moment in the past

Use the past continuous for an action that was **in progress** at a past moment. The action **started before** that moment and **went on after** it. We don't know (or care) when it finished.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "wave", from: -0.8, to: -0.25, label: "was cooking" }]} marker={{ at: -0.5, label: "8 p.m." }} caption="At 8 p.m. the cooking was in progress: it started before and went on after." />

Compare:

- *At 8 p.m. I **was cooking**.* (I was in the middle of it.)
- *I **cooked** dinner at 8 p.m.* (the whole action: I started at about 8 and finished.)

We also use it for an activity that went on **through a period**: *all morning, all day, all evening*.

<Example context="work" fa="کل صبح داشتم به ایمیل‌ها جواب می‌دادم.">I <mark>was answering</mark> emails all morning.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="دیروز تمام روز در کابل باران می‌بارید.">It <mark>was raining</mark> in Kabul all day yesterday.</Example>

It can also show a **temporary** or **changing** situation in the past:

<Example context="home" fa="آن سال پیش خاله‌ام زندگی می‌کردم.">That year I <mark>was living</mark> with my aunt. (for a short time)</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="داشت تاریک می‌شد، پس به هتل برگشتیم.">It <mark>was getting</mark> dark, so we went back to the hotel.</Example>

## Use 2: two actions at the same time

When two **long** actions were in progress at the same time, use the past continuous for both. **while** joins them.

<Timeline items={[{ type: "wave", from: -0.8, to: -0.3, label: "I was cooking" }, { type: "span", from: -0.8, to: -0.3, label: "he was watching TV" }]} caption="Two actions in progress at the same time." />

<Example context="home" fa="وقتی من آشپزی می‌کردم، شوهرم تلویزیون تماشا می‌کرد.">While I <mark>was cooking</mark>, my husband <mark>was watching</mark> TV.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="معلم داشت حرف می‌زد و دانش‌آموزها یادداشت برمی‌داشتند.">The teacher <mark>was talking</mark>, and the students <mark>were taking</mark> notes.</Example>

## Use 3: background and events in a story

In a story, the **past continuous** paints the **background**: the scene, what was already happening. The **past simple** tells the **events**: what happened next.

<Example context="news" fa="شبی سرد در ماه دی بود. برف می‌بارید و مردم با عجله به خانه می‌رفتند. من منتظر اتوبوس بودم. ناگهان ماشینی کنارم ایستاد و زنی پیاده شد.">It was a cold night in December. Snow <mark>was falling</mark>, and people <mark>were hurrying</mark> home. I <mark>was waiting</mark> for the bus. Suddenly a car <mark>stopped</mark> next to me, and a woman <mark>got</mark> out.</Example>

- **Background** (past continuous): *Snow was falling. People were hurrying home. I was waiting.*
- **Events** (past simple): *A car stopped. A woman got out.*

Words like **suddenly** and **then** often start the events. Telling a whole story, and choosing between the two tenses, is the topic of <LessonRef>lesson 9 (Past simple or past continuous?)</LessonRef>.

## when and while

Both words join two parts of a sentence about time. But they usually go with different kinds of actions.

<FormTable caption="when and while">

| Word | Usually with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **while** | a **longer** action in progress (past continuous) | **While** I **was driving**, … |
| **when** | a **short** event (past simple) | … **when** the phone **rang**. |
| **when** | a period of life or a state | **When** I **was** a child, … |
| **when** + simple, simple | one action, then the next | **When** I **got** home, I **made** dinner. |

</FormTable>

Put them together and you get the most common pattern: a long action (past continuous) and a short event (past simple).

<Timeline items={[{ type: "wave", from: -0.8, to: -0.25, label: "I was driving" }, { type: "point", at: -0.5, label: "the phone rang" }]} caption="The long action was in progress when the short event happened." />

<Example context="travel" fa="داشتم رانندگی می‌کردم که تلفنم زنگ زد.">I <mark>was driving</mark> to work <mark>when</mark> my phone <mark>rang</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="وقتی داشتم آشپزی می‌کردم، برق رفت."><mark>While</mark> I <mark>was cooking</mark>, the lights <mark>went</mark> out.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="وقتی بچه بودم، در یک روستای کوچک زندگی می‌کردیم."><mark>When</mark> I <mark>was</mark> a child, we <mark>lived</mark> in a small village.</Example>

A few more things to know:

- **The order can change.** *While I was cooking, the lights went out* = *The lights went out while I was cooking.* When the *while / when* part comes **first**, put a **comma** after it.
- **when** can also go with the past continuous: *When I was walking home, I saw Zahra.* Both *when* and *while* are correct here. But **while** with a short past simple event is unusual: say *when the phone rang*, not *while the phone rang*.
- In stories you will also see **as** with the meaning of *while*: *As I was leaving, the phone rang.* More time words (*as soon as, until*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 32 (Time & place clauses: as soon as, until, wherever)</LessonRef>.

### while or during?

**while** is followed by a **subject + verb**. **during** is followed by a **noun**. They mean the same thing.

- ***while*** *we were watching the film* · ***during*** *the film*
- ***while*** *I was on vacation* · ***during*** *my vacation*

<Example context="work" fa="ببخشید، در طول جلسه گوشی‌ام خاموش بود.">Sorry, my phone was off <mark>during</mark> the meeting.</Example>

Never say ~~during I was sleeping~~. Say *while I was sleeping* or *during the night*. <LessonRef>B2 lesson 43 (Preposition or conjunction? during/while, despite/although)</LessonRef> looks at this pair again, and <LessonRef>B1 lesson 7 (for, since, ago, during; by or until?)</LessonRef> compares *during* and *for*. Short forms like *while waiting* come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 25 (Participle clauses)</LessonRef>.

## When NOT to use the past continuous

- **State verbs** (*know, like, want, need, understand, believe*) don't usually take **-ing**, in the past or the present: *I **knew** the answer*, not ~~I was knowing~~. Some verbs can be both (*I was having lunch* is fine, because *have lunch* is an action). That is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 10 (State verbs & action verbs)</LessonRef>.
- **Habits and repeated actions** use the **past simple** (or *used to*): *When I was a child, I **walked** to school every day*, not ~~I was walking to school every day~~. *used to* is in <LessonRef>lesson 10 (used to)</LessonRef>.
- **A finished action from start to end** uses the past simple: *I **cooked** dinner last night* (the whole action).

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="I watching TV when you called." right="I was watching TV when you called." why="The past continuous needs was / were." />
<WrongRight wrong="They was playing football." right="They were playing football." why="they → were." />
<WrongRight wrong="Did you sleeping at midnight?" right="Were you sleeping at midnight?" why="Questions use was / were, not did." />
<WrongRight wrong="During I was sleeping, someone called." right="While I was sleeping, someone called." why="during + noun; while + subject + verb." />
<WrongRight wrong="I was cooking while the phone rang." right="I was cooking when the phone rang." why="when + the short event; while + the longer action." />

<WrongRight wrong="She was wanting a new phone." right="She wanted a new phone." why="want is a state verb: no -ing." />

<MemoryHook title="While is long, when is quick">
Say **while** slowly: it sounds long, like a **long** action: *while I was cooking*. **when** is quick, like a **short** event: *when the phone rang*. The long action is the **background**; the quick one is the **event**.
</MemoryHook>
