## How do you do it? Adverbs of manner

An **adverb of manner** tells us **how** someone does something. It describes a **verb**.

- *How does she drive?* She drives **carefully**.
- *How did they play?* They played **badly**.

Most adverbs of manner are an **adjective + -ly**.

<FormTable caption="Adjective → adverb">

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quick | **quickly** | She answered **quickly**. |
| careful | **carefully** | Drive **carefully**! |
| slow | **slowly** | Can you speak **slowly**, please? |
| bad | **badly** | We played **badly** yesterday. |
| polite | **politely** | He asked **politely**. |
| quiet | **quietly** | The kids are playing **quietly**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="او همیشه سریع به ایمیل‌ها جواب می‌دهد.">She always answers emails <mark>quickly</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="جاده خیس است. با احتیاط رانندگی کن.">The road is wet. Drive <mark>carefully</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او مؤدبانه از ما تشکر کرد.">He thanked us <mark>politely</mark>.</Example>

Adverbs work in negatives and questions too: *He **doesn't** drive **carefully**. **How** does she sing? **How well** do you speak English?*

## Spelling: -ly

<FormTable caption="Spelling rules for -ly">

| Adjective ends in… | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add **-ly** | slow → slowly · nice → nicely · safe → safely |
| **-l** | add **-ly** (so you get -lly) | careful → carefully · real → really · usual → usually |
| consonant + **-y** | y → **-ily** | happy → happily · easy → easily · angry → angrily · lucky → luckily |
| consonant + **-le** | drop e, add **-y** | gentle → gently · simple → simply · terrible → terribly · comfortable → comfortably |
| **-ic** | add **-ally** | basic → basically · automatic → automatically · dramatic → dramatically |
| **-ll** | add **-y** | full → fully |
| **-ue** | drop e, add **-ly** | true → truly |

</FormTable>

- Most adjectives ending in **-e** keep the e: *nice → nicely, polite → politely, safe → safely*. **true → truly** is the exception to learn.
- **public → publicly** is the one common **-ic** word that doesn't take *-ally*.

<Example context="home" fa="بچه‌ها با خوشحالی در باغ بازی می‌کردند.">The kids were playing <mark>happily</mark> in the garden.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پرستار به آرامی دستم را گرفت.">The nurse held my hand <mark>gently</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="در اصل، مشکل پول است.">The problem is <mark>basically</mark> money.</Example>

## Irregular: well, fast, hard, late, early

Some adverbs are not adjective + -ly.

<FormTable caption="Irregular adverbs">

| Adjective | Adverb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| good | **well** | She's a **good** singer. She sings **well**. |
| fast | **fast** | It's a **fast** car. It goes **fast**. |
| hard | **hard** | He's a **hard** worker. He works **hard**. |
| late | **late** | We had a **late** dinner. We ate **late**. |
| early | **early** | An **early** flight. I got up **early**. |

</FormTable>

- There is no word ~~fastly~~.
- **hardly** and **lately** are different words, with different meanings. **hardly** = *almost not* (*I can **hardly** hear you*). **lately** = *recently* (*I've been busy **lately***). So: *He works **hard*** (not ~~hardly~~). These are taught in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 40 (Adverbs 2: hard or hardly? late or lately?)</LessonRef>.
- **well** can also be an **adjective** that means *healthy*: *I don't feel **well** today.*
- Adjectives that already end in **-ly** (*friendly, lovely, lonely, silly*) have no adverb. Say ***in a friendly way***: *She smiled at me **in a friendly way**.*

<Example context="school" fa="او انگلیسی را خیلی خوب صحبت می‌کند.">He speaks English very <mark>well</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="این هفته خیلی سخت کار کردیم.">We worked really <mark>hard</mark> this week.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="بس دیر رسید.">The bus arrived <mark>late</mark>.</Example>

## Adjective or adverb?

Ask: **what does the word describe?**

- A **noun** → **adjective**: *He's a **careful** driver.* (careful describes *driver*)
- A **verb** → **adverb**: *He drives **carefully**.* (carefully describes *drives*)

<FormTable caption="Adjective or adverb?">

| Adjective + noun | Verb + adverb |
|---|---|
| a **careful** driver | drives **carefully** |
| a **good** singer | sings **well** |
| a **quick** answer | answers **quickly** |
| a **hard** worker | works **hard** |
| a **bad** player | plays **badly** |

</FormTable>

After **be** and the linking verbs **look, feel, seem, sound, taste, smell**, use an **adjective**, because the word describes the **subject**, not the action:

- *You **look tired**.* (not ~~tiredly~~) · *This soup **tastes good**.* (not ~~well~~) · *I **feel bad** about it.*

These verbs are in <LessonRef>lesson 17 (Linking verbs: look, feel, sound, seem + adjective)</LessonRef>.

<Example context="home" fa="این کیک خوشمزه است. مادرت خوب آشپزی می‌کند.">This cake tastes <mark>good</mark>. Your mother cooks <mark>well</mark>.</Example>

An adverb can also describe an **adjective**: *really good, very quickly, terribly sorry*. Words like **very** and **really** are in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 30 (very, really, too)</LessonRef>.

## Where the adverb goes

An adverb of manner usually goes **after the verb**. If the verb has an **object**, the adverb goes **after the object**.

<FormTable caption="Position">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + **adverb** | She sings **beautifully**. |
| verb + object + **adverb** | She speaks **English well**. · He closed **the door quietly**. |

</FormTable>

Don't put the adverb **between** the verb and its object: *She speaks English **well***, not ~~She speaks well English~~. More about adverb position is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 46 (Where adverbs go)</LessonRef>.

## Comparing adverbs: faster, more carefully, the best

Adverbs have comparatives and superlatives, like adjectives (<LessonRef>lesson 37 (Comparatives)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>lesson 38 (Superlatives)</LessonRef>).

<FormTable caption="Comparative and superlative adverbs">

| Adverb | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| fast · hard · early · late · soon | **faster** · **harder** · **earlier** · **later** · **sooner** | **(the) fastest** · **(the) hardest** · **(the) earliest** |
| carefully · slowly · quietly | **more carefully** · **more slowly** · **more quietly** | **(the) most carefully** |
| well | **better** | **(the) best** |
| badly | **worse** | **(the) worst** |
| far | **farther / further** | **(the) farthest / furthest** |

</FormTable>

- Adverbs with the same form as the adjective take **-er / -est**: *faster, harder, earlier*.
- **-ly** adverbs take **more / most**: *more carefully*, not ~~carefullier~~.
- In informal speech, people also say *slower, quicker, louder*: *Can you talk **louder**?* In careful English, use *more slowly, more quickly, more loudly*.
- With superlative adverbs, **the** is often left out: *Who runs **(the) fastest**? I sing **worst** of all.*

<Example context="school" fa="لطفاً آهسته‌تر صحبت کنید.">Could you speak <mark>more slowly</mark>, please?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او از همه‌ی ما سخت‌تر کار می‌کند.">She works <mark>harder</mark> than all of us.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="کدامتان بهتر آشپزی می‌کنید، تو یا برادرت؟">Who cooks <mark>better</mark>, you or your brother?</Example>

<Persian>

**قید حالت (adverb of manner)** می‌گوید کاری **چطور** انجام می‌شود و **فعل** را توصیف می‌کند: She drives carefully. (او با احتیاط رانندگی می‌کند.) بیشتر قیدها = صفت + **ly-**: quick ← quickly، careful ← carefully، slow ← slowly.

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟** در فارسی اغلب صفت و قید **یک کلمه** هستند: «خوب» هم good است هم well؛ «سریع» هم fast است هم quickly؛ «آرام» هم quiet است هم quietly. یعنی می‌گوییم «غذای خوب» و «خوب آشپزی می‌کند». برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند He speaks English very good. در انگلیسی باید بپرسید: این کلمه **اسم** را توصیف می‌کند یا **فعل** را؟

- **اسم ← صفت:** a careful driver (رانندهٔ بااحتیاط)، a good singer.
- **فعل ← قید:** drives carefully، sings well.
- بعد از **be** و فعل‌هایی مثل look، feel، taste، smell، sound، seem **صفت** می‌آید، چون فاعل را توصیف می‌کند: This soup tastes good (نه well). You look tired.

**املا:**

- y ← ily: happy ← happily، easy ← easily
- le ← ly: gentle ← gently، simple ← simply
- ic ← ically: basic ← basically (ولی public ← publicly)
- l ← lly: careful ← carefully، real ← really
- true ← truly

**بی‌قاعده‌ها:** good ← **well**. کلمه‌های fast، hard، late و early تغییر نمی‌کنند: She runs fast (نه fastly). مراقب باشید: **hardly** یعنی «به‌سختی / تقریباً نه» و **lately** یعنی «اخیراً». پس «سخت کار می‌کند» = He works hard، نه hardly. صفت‌هایی که خودشان به ly- ختم می‌شوند (friendly، lovely) قید ندارند: in a friendly way (با رفتاری دوستانه).

**جای قید:** در فارسی قید معمولاً **قبل از فعل** می‌آید («انگلیسی را خوب صحبت می‌کند»). در انگلیسی قید حالت **بعد از فعل** یا **بعد از مفعول** می‌آید: She speaks English well. قید را بین فعل و مفعول نگذارید: نه She speaks well English.

**مقایسه:** قیدهای کوتاه (fast، hard، early) ← faster، harder، earlier. قیدهای ly-دار ← more carefully، more slowly (آهسته‌تر). بی‌قاعده: well ← better ← best، badly ← worse ← worst. Could you speak more slowly? (می‌شود آهسته‌تر صحبت کنید؟)

**سؤالی و منفی:** How does she drive? (چطور رانندگی می‌کند؟) / How well do you speak English? (انگلیسی‌ات چقدر خوب است؟) / He doesn't drive carefully. (با احتیاط رانندگی نمی‌کند.)
