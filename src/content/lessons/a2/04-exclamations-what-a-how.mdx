## What a…!

An **exclamation** shows a strong feeling: surprise, joy, anger, admiration. It ends with an exclamation point (**!**).

**What** goes before a **noun**. Choose the pattern by the kind of noun.

<FormTable caption="Exclamations with what">

| Noun | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| singular countable | **What a / an** (+ adjective) + noun | What a surprise! · What a beautiful day! · What an idea! |
| plural | **What** (+ adjective) + noun | What lovely flowers! · What nice people! |
| uncountable | **What** (+ adjective) + noun | What awful weather! · What good news! |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="چه منظره‌ی زیبایی!"><mark>What a</mark> beautiful view!</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="چه سورپرایزی! نمی‌دانستم داری می‌آیی."><mark>What a</mark> surprise! I didn't know you were coming.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="چه کفش‌های قشنگی!"><mark>What</mark> lovely shoes!</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="چه خبر خوبی!"><mark>What</mark> good news!</Example>

The **a / an** rule is the same as in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 9 (a or an?)</LessonRef>. Use **an** before a vowel sound: *What **an** amazing story!* And remember: **weather, news, advice, music, information** are uncountable, so they never take **a**: *What awful weather!* (not ~~What an awful weather!~~).

The adjective is optional. Without it, the noun itself shows the feeling: *What a day! What a mess! What a game!*

## How…!

**How** goes before an **adjective** (or an adverb), with no noun.

<Example context="social" fa="«برایت شام درست کردم.» «چقدر لطف کردی!»">“I made you dinner.” “<mark>How kind</mark> of you!”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="چه جالب!"><mark>How interesting</mark>!</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="«پایم شکست.» «وای، چه بد!»">“I broke my leg.” “Oh, <mark>how awful</mark>!”</Example>

Don't put a noun after *How*. If there is a noun, use *What a*: ***What a** beautiful day!* (not ~~How beautiful day!~~). Without the noun: ***How** beautiful!*

### Word order: like a statement

After *What a… / How…* you can add the subject and the verb. Use **statement order** (subject + verb), not question order.

<FormTable caption="Statement order in exclamations">

| ✓ Exclamation | ✗ Not (question order) |
|---|---|
| What a nice house **you have**! | ~~What a nice house do you have!~~ |
| How cold **it is**! | ~~How cold is it!~~ |
| How well **you speak** English! | ~~How well do you speak English!~~ |

</FormTable>

*How cold is it?* and *How well do you speak English?* are correct **questions**, but they ask for information. They are not exclamations.

<Example context="home" fa="چه خانه‌ی قشنگی داری!">What a nice house <mark>you have</mark>!</Example>

In conversation, people often use just the short forms: *What a day! How nice! How sweet!*

## so and such

You can also show a strong feeling inside a normal sentence with **so** and **such**. They mean “very”, but they are stronger and show more feeling.

<FormTable caption="so or such?">

| Word | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **so** | so + adjective (no noun) | It's **so** cold! · You're **so** lucky! |
| **such a / an** | such a + adjective + singular noun | She's **such a** kind person! · It was **such a** long day! |
| **such** | such + adjective + plural or uncountable noun | They're **such** nice people! · It's **such** awful weather! |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="امروز خیلی گرم است!">It's <mark>so</mark> hot today!</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="رئیس جدیدمان آدم خیلی خوبی است!">Our new boss is <mark>such a</mark> nice person!</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="چه بچه‌های مؤدبی داری!">You have <mark>such</mark> polite children!</Example>

Two rules:

- **so** + adjective **alone**. **such** + adjective + **noun**: *The movie was **so** good!* / *It was **such a** good movie!* (not ~~so good movie~~)
- The order is **such a**, not ~~a such~~: *It was **such a** long day!*

*so … that* and *such … that* (*It was so cold that we stayed home.*) are in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 44 (so & such; too & enough + to)</LessonRef>.

## Other short exclamations

- **One adjective**: *Great! Lovely! Terrible! Perfect!*
- **Interjections** (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 1 (Parts of speech & sentence parts)</LessonRef>): *Wow! Oh no! Ouch!*
- **Negative questions** (<LessonRef>lesson 2 (Negatives & answering negative questions)</LessonRef>): *Isn't it beautiful! Isn't she clever!* The speaker doesn't want an answer, only agreement.

<Persian>

**جمله‌های تعجبی (exclamations):** برای نشان دادن احساس قوی (تعجب، خوشحالی، تحسین) به کار می‌روند و با علامت **!** تمام می‌شوند.

**What a / an + اسم مفرد:** «چه روز قشنگی!» ← **What a** beautiful day! (چه + اسم + ی)

**What + اسم جمع یا غیرقابل‌شمارش (بدون a):**

- چه گل‌های قشنگی! ← **What** lovely flowers!
- چه هوای بدی! ← **What** awful weather! (نه What an awful weather؛ چون weather غیرقابل‌شمارش است.)
- چه خبر خوبی! ← **What** good news! (news هم غیرقابل‌شمارش است.)

**How + صفت (بدون اسم):** «چه قشنگ!» ← **How** beautiful! · «چه لطفی کردی!» ← **How** kind of you!

**مهم‌ترین نکته برای فارسی‌زبان‌ها:** در فارسی برای همه‌ی این‌ها فقط یک کلمه داریم: «چه». «چه روز قشنگی!» و «چه قشنگ!» هر دو با «چه» شروع می‌شوند. اما در انگلیسی:

- اگر **اسم** داریم ← **What (a)**: What a beautiful day!
- اگر فقط **صفت** داریم ← **How**: How beautiful!
- غلط: How beautiful day! و What beautiful!

**a را جا نیندازید:** چون فارسی حرف تعریف a ندارد، فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند What beautiful day! درستش **What a** beautiful day! است (با اسم مفرد قابل‌شمارش).

**ترتیب کلمات:** بعد از What a… یا How… ترتیب جمله‌ی خبری می‌آید، نه سؤالی: What a nice house **you have**! (چه خانه‌ی قشنگی داری!) نه What a nice house do you have!

**so و such** (خیلی، با احساس بیشتر):

- **so** + صفت تنها: It's **so** cold! (خیلی سرد است!)
- **such a** + صفت + اسم مفرد: She's **such a** kind person! (خیلی آدم مهربانی است!)
- **such** + صفت + اسم جمع یا غیرقابل‌شمارش: They're **such** nice people!
- غلط: She's so kind person. و a such kind person.

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="Noun? What. No noun? How.">
See a **noun**? Start with **What** (and add **a** if it's one countable thing): *What a day! What weather!* Only an **adjective**? Start with **How**: *How nice!* Inside a sentence, **so** stands alone, **such** brings a noun: *so cold · such a cold day*.
</MemoryHook>
