## my or mine?

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 12 (my, your, his, her… and 's)</LessonRef> you learned **my, your, his, her, our, their**. They always come **before a noun**: *my bag, their house*.

English has a second set for when there is **no noun** after the word: **mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs**. We call them **possessive pronouns**. They stand alone.

<FormTable caption="Before a noun or alone?">

| Subject | + noun | Alone (no noun) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **my** bag | **mine** | The bag is **mine**. |
| you | **your** bag | **yours** | Is this **yours**? |
| he | **his** bag | **his** | It's **his**. |
| she | **her** bag | **hers** | The red one is **hers**. |
| it | **its** bowl | not used | |
| we | **our** table | **ours** | That table is **ours**. |
| they | **their** car | **theirs** | **Theirs** is bigger. |

</FormTable>

**Why use them?** So we don't repeat the noun:

<Example context="home" fa="ماشین تو قرمز است. مال من آبی است.">Your car is red. <mark>Mine</mark> is blue. (= my car)</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="خودکار ندارم. می‌توانم مال تو را قرض بگیرم؟">I don't have a pen. Can I borrow <mark>yours</mark>? (= your pen)</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="این چمدان‌ها مال ما نیستند. مال ما سیاه است.">These suitcases aren't <mark>ours</mark>. <mark>Ours</mark> are black.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="اتاق او کوچک است، ولی مال آن‌ها خیلی بزرگ است.">Her office is small, but <mark>theirs</mark> is huge.</Example>

A possessive pronoun can be the **subject** (*Mine is blue*), the **object** (*Can I borrow yours?*) or come **after be** (*It's mine*).

Three small rules:

- **No apostrophe**: *yours, hers, ours, theirs*, never ~~your's~~, ~~her's~~.
- **No a or the**: *It's **mine***, not ~~It's the mine~~.
- **his** is the same in both sets: *It's **his** bag. It's **his**.* But **its** is not used alone.

### mine or for me?

In Persian, «این مال منه» shows the owner. In English, say **It's mine**. *It's for me* means something different: it is a present for me, or it is meant for me.

<Example context="shopping" fa="این کیف مال من است. (صاحبش منم)">This bag is <mark>mine</mark>. I bought it last year.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="این کادو برای من است؟ ممنون!">Is this present <mark>for me</mark>? Thank you! (a gift)</Example>

## Whose…?

**Whose** asks **who owns** something. There are two patterns:

<FormTable caption="Questions with whose">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Whose** + noun + be + this / that…? | **Whose bag** is this? · **Whose keys** are these? |
| **Whose** + be + this / that / noun? | **Whose** is this? · **Whose** is that car? |
| **Whose** + noun + other verb | **Whose car** did you borrow? · **Whose idea** was it? |

</FormTable>

Answer with a possessive pronoun or a name + **'s**:

<Example context="school" fa="«این چتر مال کیه؟» «مال زهرا است.»">“<mark>Whose</mark> umbrella is this?” “It's <mark>Zahra's</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="«این کفش‌ها مال کیه؟» «مال من است.»">“<mark>Whose</mark> are these shoes?” “They're <mark>mine</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ایده‌ی چه کسی بود؟">That was a great plan. <mark>Whose</mark> idea was it?</Example>

**whose or who's?** They sound the same, but they are different words:

- **whose** = belonging to which person: ***Whose** phone is ringing?*
- **who's** = **who is** or **who has**: ***Who's** your teacher? (who is) **Who's** got a pen? (who has)*

A test: can you say *who is* or *who has*? Then write **who's**. If not, write **whose**.

*whose* also joins two parts of a sentence (*the man **whose** car was stolen*). That is <LessonRef>B1 lesson 29 (Defining & non-defining relative clauses)</LessonRef>.

## a friend of mine

You can't put **a** and **my** together: ~~a my friend~~ and ~~my a friend~~ are wrong. To say "one of my friends", English uses **a + noun + of + mine / yours / hers…**

<Example context="social" fa="او یکی از دوستان من است.">She's <mark>a friend of mine</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="یکی از همکارانش این را به من گفت.">A <mark>colleague of his</mark> told me about it.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="یکی از پسرعموهای ما در ونکوور زندگی می‌کند.">A <mark>cousin of ours</mark> lives in Vancouver.</Example>

With a **name** or a **noun**, add **'s**: *a friend of **Zahra's**, a colleague of **my father's***. (You will also hear *a friend of my father*, without 's.)

<Example context="social" fa="کریم یکی از دوستان برادرم است.">Karim is <mark>a friend of my brother's</mark>.</Example>

Compare:

- ***my friend** Ahmad* = a particular friend; you know who.
- ***a friend of mine*** = one of my friends; I don't say which one.

You can also say **this / that + noun + of + yours** in friendly speech: *How's **that brother of yours**?* *my own* (*my own car*) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 43 (Generic you, one, they; each other)</LessonRef>.

## 's or s'?

In <LessonRef>A1 lesson 12 (my, your, his, her… and 's)</LessonRef> you added **'s** to one owner: *Zahra's phone*. When the owner is **plural**, look at the end of the word:

<FormTable caption="Where does the apostrophe go?">

| Owner | Add | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| one person (singular) | **'s** | the boy**'s** room · my sister**'s** car · James**'s** phone |
| plural that ends in -s | **'** only | the boys**'** room · my parents**'** house · the students**'** books |
| plural that doesn't end in -s | **'s** | the children**'s** room · men**'s** shoes · women**'s** rights · people**'s** ideas |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="خانه‌ی پدر و مادرم در بامیان است.">My <mark>parents'</mark> house is in Bamyan.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کتاب‌های دانشجوها روی میز است.">The <mark>students'</mark> books are on the desk.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="لباس بچه‌ها طبقه‌ی دوم است."><mark>Children's</mark> clothes are on the second floor.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="امشب در خانه‌ی خانواده‌ی احمدی شام می‌خوریم.">We're having dinner at the <mark>Ahmadis'</mark> house tonight. (the Ahmadi family)</Example>

The apostrophe tells the reader how many owners there are:

- *the **boy's** room* = the room of **one** boy
- *the **boys'** room* = the room of **two or more** boys

In speech they sound the same. Only the writing shows the difference.

**Names that end in -s** usually take **'s**: *James's phone, Mr. Davis's office*. You will sometimes see only an apostrophe (*James' phone*).

**An apostrophe never makes a plural**: *two apples*, not ~~two apple's~~. That is in <LessonRef>lesson 6 (Contractions & apostrophes)</LessonRef>. More uses of 's (*at the doctor's, today's news, Zahra and Ahmad's house*) come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 35 (Possession 3: 's, of or noun + noun?)</LessonRef>.

**ضمیرهای ملکی (mine, yours…):** در فارسی برای «مالِ من» یک عبارت داریم. در انگلیسی وقتی بعد از کلمه **اسمی نمی‌آید**، از این ضمیرها استفاده می‌کنیم:

- my book ← mine (مالِ من)
- your book ← yours (مالِ تو / شما)
- his book ← his (مالِ او، مرد)
- her book ← hers (مالِ او، زن)
- our book ← ours (مالِ ما)
- their book ← theirs (مالِ آن‌ها)

مثال: Your car is red. Mine is blue. (ماشین تو قرمز است. مال من آبی است.) این کلمه‌ها آپاستروف ندارند (yours، نه your's) و the هم قبلشان نمی‌آید (نه the mine).

**«مال منه» یا «برای منه»؟** فارسی‌زبان‌ها گاهی «این مال منه» را This is for me ترجمه می‌کنند. اما for me یعنی «برای من» (مثلاً هدیه است یا برای استفاده‌ی من است). برای مالکیت بگویید: This is **mine**.

**whose (مالِ کی؟):** برای پرسیدن صاحب چیزی: Whose bag is this? یا Whose is this? (این مال کیه؟) جواب: It's mine. / It's Zahra's. نگویید This is for who? دقت کنید whose با who's (یعنی who is یا who has) هم‌صداست ولی معنی‌اش فرق دارد.

**a friend of mine (یکی از دوستانم):** در انگلیسی a و my کنار هم نمی‌آیند (نه a my friend). به جایش می‌گوییم: a friend of **mine** (نه a friend of me). با اسم: a friend of Zahra's (یکی از دوستان زهرا).

**'s یا s'؟** در فارسی «اتاقِ پسر» و «اتاقِ پسرها» را با «ها» نشان می‌دهیم. در انگلیسی جای آپاستروف نشان می‌دهد صاحب یکی است یا چندتا:
- یک صاحب: the boy**'s** room (اتاقِ پسر)
- صاحب جمع با s: the boys**'** room (اتاقِ پسرها)، my parents**'** house (خانه‌ی پدر و مادرم)
- جمع بی‌قاعده بدون s: the children**'s** room (اتاقِ بچه‌ها)، women**'s** clothes

در گفتار boy's و boys' یکسان تلفظ می‌شوند؛ فقط در نوشتن فرق دارند.

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="“Is this your pen?” “Yes, it's my.”" right="“Yes, it's mine.”" why="No noun after it → mine." />
<WrongRight wrong="This phone is for me. (= I own it)" right="This phone is mine." why="For the owner, use mine. for me = a present for me, or meant for me." />
<WrongRight wrong="The blue car is your's." right="The blue car is yours." why="Possessive pronouns never have an apostrophe." />
<WrongRight wrong="Who's bag is this?" right="Whose bag is this?" why="Whose = of which person. Who's = who is / who has." />
<WrongRight wrong="She's a my friend." right="She's a friend of mine. / She's my friend." why="a and my can't go together." />
<WrongRight wrong="He's a friend of me." right="He's a friend of mine." why="a friend of + mine, yours, his, hers…" />
<WrongRight wrong="the childrens' toys" right="the children's toys" why="children is already plural (no -s) → add 's." />
<WrongRight wrong="my parent's house (= mother and father)" right="my parents' house" why="Plural with -s → put the apostrophe after the s." />

<MemoryHook title="Mine stands alone">
**my** needs a friend: a noun (*my bag*). **mine** stands alone (*It's mine*). And the apostrophe? Write the owner first (*boy*, *boys*, *children*), then add **'s**, or just **'** if the word already ends in a plural **s**.
</MemoryHook>
