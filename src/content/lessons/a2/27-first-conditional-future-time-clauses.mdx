## A real possibility: if + present, will + verb

Use the **first conditional** for a real situation in the **future**. Maybe it will happen, maybe not. If it happens, you know the result.

<FormTable caption="First conditional">

| if-part (present simple) | main part (will + verb) |
|---|---|
| **If** it **rains** tomorrow, | I**'ll stay** home. |
| **If** you **study** hard, | you**'ll pass** the exam. |
| **If** we **leave** now, | we **won't be** late. |
| **If** she **doesn't call** me, | I**'ll call** her. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="اگر فردا باران بیاید، خانه می‌مانم.">If it <mark>rains</mark> tomorrow, I<mark>'ll stay</mark> home.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="اگر سخت درس بخوانی، در امتحان قبول می‌شوی.">If you <mark>study</mark> hard, you<mark>'ll pass</mark> the exam.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="اگر فعلا راه بیفتیم، دیر نمی‌رسیم.">If we <mark>leave</mark> now, we <mark>won't be</mark> late.</Example>

**Why no will after if?** The if-part talks about the future, but English uses the **present simple** there. The **will** goes in the other part. Say *If it rains*, not ~~If it will rain~~.

### Zero or first?

Compare the two:

- **Zero conditional** (<LessonRef>A2 lesson 26, *Zero conditional*</LessonRef>): always true, every time. *If you heat ice, it melts.*
- **First conditional**: one real situation in the future. *If it's hot tomorrow, we'll go to the beach.*

## What we use it for

<FormTable caption="Uses of the first conditional">

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| a possible future result | If the price goes down, I'll buy a new phone. |
| a warning | If you touch that, you'll burn yourself! |
| a promise | If you pass your exams, I'll buy you a bike. |
| an offer | If you like, I'll drive you to the station. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="به آن دست نزن! اگر دست بزنی، می‌سوزی.">Don't touch it! If you touch it, <mark>you'll burn</mark> yourself.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="اگر بخواهی، تو را تا ایستگاه می‌رسانم.">If you like, <mark>I'll drive</mark> you to the station.</Example>

## Order and comma

The if-part can come first or second. When it comes **first**, put a **comma** after it. When it comes second, we usually don't use a comma.

<Example context="health" fa="اگر فردا حالت بهتر نشد، تو را پیش دکتر می‌برم.">If you don't feel better tomorrow<mark>,</mark> I'll take you to the doctor.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="اگر فردا حالت بهتر نشد، تو را پیش دکتر می‌برم.">I'll take you to the doctor <mark>if</mark> you don't feel better tomorrow.</Example>

## Negatives and questions

**Negatives.** Use **don't / doesn't** in the if-part, and **won't** (= will not) in the main part.

<Example context="travel" fa="اگر عجله نکنی، به بس نمی‌رسی.">If you <mark>don't hurry</mark>, you<mark>'ll miss</mark> the bus.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="اگر فعلا بلیت بخری، گران نخواهد بود.">If you buy the tickets now, they <mark>won't be</mark> expensive.</Example>

**Questions.** The question is usually in the **main part**: *will* goes before the subject. The if-part stays the same.

<Example context="work" fa="اگر کار را نگیری، چه کار می‌کنی؟"><mark>What will you do</mark> if you don't get the job?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="اگر خبری شد، به من زنگ می‌زنی؟"><mark>Will you call</mark> me if you hear any news? Yes, I will.</Example>

## Other verbs in the main part

The main part is often **will**, but other forms are possible too:

- **Imperative** (an instruction): *If you see Ahmad, **tell** him to call me.*
- **can** (possibility, permission): *If you finish early, you **can** go home.*
- **might / may** (less sure than *will*): *If it rains, we **might** stay home.* See <LessonRef>A2 lesson 22, *may & might*</LessonRef>.

<Example context="work" fa="اگر احمد را دیدی، بگو به من زنگ بزند.">If you see Ahmad, <mark>tell</mark> him to call me.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="اگر زود تمام کنی، می‌توانی بروی خانه.">If you finish early, you <mark>can</mark> go home.</Example>

More modals in the main part (*could, should*) and imaginary situations (*If I had more money, I'd…*) come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 23, *Second conditional*</LessonRef>.

## Future time clauses: when, as soon as, before, after, until

The same rule works with **time words**. When they talk about the future, the verb after them is in the **present simple**, not *will*. The other part has *will* (or an imperative).

<FormTable caption="Time word + present for the future">

| Time word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | at the time | I'll call you **when** I **arrive**. |
| **as soon as** | immediately when | I'll send it **as soon as** I **get** home. |
| **before** | earlier than | Turn off the lights **before** you **leave**. |
| **after** | later than | We'll have dinner **after** the film **finishes**. |
| **until** | up to that time | I'll wait here **until** you **come** back. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="وقتی به هتل رسیدم، به تو زنگ می‌زنم.">I'll call you <mark>when I arrive</mark> at the hotel.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="به محض اینکه به خانه برسم، ایمیل را می‌فرستم.">I'll send you the email <mark>as soon as I get</mark> home.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="قبل از اینکه بروی، چراغ‌ها را خاموش کن.">Turn off the lights <mark>before you leave</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="بعد از اینکه فیلم تمام شد، شام می‌خوریم.">We'll have dinner <mark>after the film finishes</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="تا برنگشته‌ام همین‌جا منتظر بمان.">Wait here <mark>until I come</mark> back.</Example>

- Never put **will** after these words: *when I arrive*, not ~~when I will arrive~~.
- The time part can come first. Then put a comma after it: *As soon as I get home, I'll send it.*
- Sometimes you will see the present perfect after these words: *I'll call you when I've finished.* It stresses that the first action is complete. That is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 12, *Future continuous & future time clauses*</LessonRef>.

### until: a positive verb

**until** means *up to that time*. The verb after it is **positive**: *Wait here **until I come** back*, not ~~until I don't come back~~. Negative main verb + until is fine: *I **won't** leave **until** you call.*

## when or if for the future?

Both take the present simple, but the meaning is different:

<FormTable caption="when or if?">

| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **when** | I'm sure it will happen. | **When** I see Ahmad tomorrow, I'll tell him. (I'll see him at work.) |
| **if** | Maybe it will happen, maybe not. | **If** I see Ahmad, I'll tell him. (Maybe I won't see him.) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="وقتی احمد را دیدم (حتماً می‌بینمش)، به او می‌گویم."><mark>When</mark> I see Ahmad tomorrow, I'll tell him.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="اگر احمد را دیدم (شاید نبینمش)، به او می‌گویم."><mark>If</mark> I see Ahmad, I'll tell him.</Example>

More about *when* and *if* (and *unless, in case*) comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 24, *unless, as long as, in case*</LessonRef>.

<MemoryHook title="Will stays out of the little door">
Think of **if, when, as soon as, before, after, until** as a little door. **Will** is too big to go through it. It stays in the **other** part of the sentence: *If it **rains**, I**'ll** stay home. I**'ll** call you when I **arrive**.*
</MemoryHook>
