## The form: have / has + past participle

The present perfect has two pieces: the helper **have / has** + the **past participle** (the third form, <LessonRef>lesson 14</LessonRef>, Verb forms & auxiliary verbs).

<FormTable caption="Present perfect">

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | I **have seen** / I**'ve seen** | I **haven't seen** | **Have** you **seen**…? |
| he / she / it | she **has been** / she**'s been** | she **hasn't been** | **Has** she **been**…? |

</FormTable>

- Short forms: **'ve** = have, **'s** = has. *She's been* = she **has** been. (*She's going* = she **is** going: the next word tells you.)
- Short answers use the helper only: *“Have you seen it?” “Yes, I **have**.” / “No, I **haven't**.”* Not ~~Yes, I've~~.
- Wh- questions: *Where **have** you **been**? How many times **has** she **seen** it?*

Regular verbs end in **-ed** (*I've worked, she's visited*). Irregular verbs have their own past participle (*seen, been, eaten, written, ridden, flown*). Check the [irregular verbs list](/reference#irregular).

<Example context="travel" fa="دو بار به ترکیه رفته‌ام.">I<mark>'ve been</mark> to Turkey twice.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او هیچ‌وقت در عمرش شفاخانه بستری نشده است.">She <mark>hasn't</mark> ever <mark>stayed</mark> in hospital.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«تا حالا شتر سوار شده‌ای؟» «بله، سوار شده‌ام.»">“<mark>Have</mark> you ever <mark>ridden</mark> a camel?” “Yes, I <mark>have</mark>.”</Example>

## Experience: at some time in your life

Use the present perfect to talk about **experiences**: things that happened at **some time** between your birth and **now**. We don't say **when**, because the time is not important. The important thing is: *it is part of my life* (or *not yet*).

<Timeline items={[{ type: "span", from: -0.95, to: 0, label: "my life up to now" }, { type: "point", at: -0.75 }, { type: "point", at: -0.4 }, { type: "point", at: -0.15 }]} caption="Some time in the past, up to now. When? It doesn't matter." />

<Example context="travel" fa="به چند کشور سفر کرده‌ام، اما هیچ‌وقت به آمریکا نرفته‌ام.">I<mark>'ve visited</mark> a few countries, but I<mark>'ve never been</mark> to America.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او قبلاً در یک بانک کار کرده است، پس این کار را خوب بلد است.">She<mark>'s worked</mark> in a bank, so she knows this job well.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="هیچ‌وقت در امتحان رانندگی رد نشده‌ایم.">We<mark>'ve</mark> never <mark>failed</mark> a driving test.</Example>

### ever in questions

**ever** means *at any time in your life*. It goes between the subject and the past participle.

<Example context="social" fa="تا حالا غذای هندی خورده‌ای؟">Have you <mark>ever</mark> eaten Indian food?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او تا حالا جایزه برده است؟">Has she <mark>ever</mark> won a prize?</Example>

Answers: *Yes, I have.* / *No, I haven't.* / *No, never.* We don't usually put *ever* in a positive sentence: say *I've been to Paris*, not ~~I've ever been to Paris~~. (*ever* after a superlative, *the best film I've ever seen*, comes in <LessonRef>lesson 38</LessonRef>, Superlatives.)

### never

**never** means *not ever*. It is already negative, so the verb stays **positive**: *I've **never** been there.* Two negatives together are wrong: ~~I haven't never been there~~. You can say *I've never been there* or *I haven't ever been there*.

<Example context="travel" fa="هیچ‌وقت سوار هواپیما نشده‌ام.">I<mark>'ve never flown</mark> in a plane.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پدرم هیچ‌وقت سیگار نکشیده است.">My father <mark>has never smoked</mark>.</Example>

### How many times? before? the first time?

- Say how often with **once, twice, three times, many times, a few times**: *I've seen this film **three times**.*
- **before** = at some time before now: *I've **never** been here **before**. Have you tried this **before**?*
- After **It's the first time** (or the second time…), use the present perfect: *It's the first time I**'ve been** to a football match.* More practice with this pattern comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 6</LessonRef>, Present perfect or past simple?

<Example context="school" fa="این کتاب را دو بار خوانده‌ام.">I<mark>'ve read</mark> this book <mark>twice</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="اولین بار است که به هرات می‌آیم.">It's the first time I<mark>'ve been</mark> to Herat.</Example>

## been or gone?

*go* has two past participles in the present perfect: **been** and **gone**.

<FormTable caption="been or gone?">

| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **has / have been to** | went **and came back** (a visit, an experience) | *Ahmad **has been** to Bamyan.* He visited it; now he is home. |
| **has / have gone to** | went and **is still there** (or on the way) | *Ahmad **has gone** to Bamyan.* He is not here; he is in Bamyan now. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="«مریم کجاست؟» «رفته ناهار بخورد. ساعت دو برمی‌گردد.»">“Where's Maryam?” “She<mark>'s gone</mark> to lunch. She'll be back at two.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مریم سه بار به چین رفته است (و برگشته).">Maryam <mark>has been</mark> to China three times.</Example>

- For **experiences**, and in questions with **ever**, use **been**: *Have you ever **been** to Band-e-Amir?*
- **been to** a place = visited it. **been in** a place = stayed or lived there: *How long have you been in Kabul?* (how long with *for* and *since* comes in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 7</LessonRef>, for, since, ago, during; by or until?)
- You may also hear **be gone** = not here any more: *I turned round and my bag **was gone**!*

## Then give the details: past simple

The present perfect is good for the **first** question or answer. When you add **when**, **where exactly** or **how**, change to the **past simple**, because now you are talking about a **finished** time.

<Example context="social" fa="«تا حالا به هرات رفته‌ای؟» «بله. پارسال رفتم. هوا عالی بود.»">“<mark>Have</mark> you ever <mark>been</mark> to Herat?” “Yes, I <mark>have</mark>. I <mark>went</mark> there last year. The weather <mark>was</mark> great.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«قبلاً با این برنامه کار کرده‌ای؟» «بله، در کار قبلی‌ام از آن استفاده می‌کردم.»">“<mark>Have</mark> you <mark>used</mark> this program before?” “Yes, I <mark>used</mark> it in my last job.”</Example>

So: ~~I have been to Herat last year~~ → *I **went** to Herat last year.* With a finished time (*yesterday, last week, in 2019, when I was a child*) use the past simple. The full comparison is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 6</LessonRef>, Present perfect or past simple?

<MemoryHook title="Been = there and back">
**been**: you went **there and back**, like a round-trip ticket. **gone**: a **one-way** ticket, still away. And **ever** questions always take **been**: *Have you ever **been** to…?*
</MemoryHook>
