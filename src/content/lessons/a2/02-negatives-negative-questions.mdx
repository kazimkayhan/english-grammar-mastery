## Negatives: a quick review

You already make negatives with **not**:

- after **be**: *I'm not tired. She isn't here.* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 5 (be: am, is, are)</LessonRef>)
- with **don't / doesn't / didn't** + base verb: *I don't drive. He didn't call.* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 18 (Present simple: negatives & questions)</LessonRef> and <LessonRef>A1 lesson 27 (Past simple: irregular verbs & did)</LessonRef>)
- after **can, will, should** and other helpers: *I can't swim. It won't rain.*

This lesson adds two things: **negative words** like *nothing* and *nobody*, and **negative questions** like *Don't you…?*

## Negative words

Some words are negative by themselves. They already mean “not”.

<FormTable caption="Negative words">

| Negative word | Means | Example | Same meaning with not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no** + noun | not any, not a | There's **no** milk. | There isn't any milk. |
| **never** | not ever | I **never** drink coffee. | I don't ever drink coffee. |
| **nothing** | not anything | I bought **nothing**. | I didn't buy anything. |
| **nobody / no one** | not anybody | I saw **nobody**. | I didn't see anybody. |
| **nowhere** | not anywhere | There's **nowhere** to sit. | There isn't anywhere to sit. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="یخچال خالی است. هیچ شیری نداریم.">The fridge is empty. There's <mark>no</mark> milk.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="من هیچ وقت گوشت نمی‌خورم.">I <mark>never</mark> eat meat.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="به همه‌ی مغازه‌ها سر زدم ولی هیچ چیز نخریدم.">I went to every store, but I bought <mark>nothing</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="هیچ‌کس به تلفن جواب نداد.">I called the office, but <mark>nobody</mark> answered.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="قطار شلوغ بود و جایی برای نشستن نبود.">The train was full. There was <mark>nowhere</mark> to sit.</Example>

A few details:

- **no one** is two words. **nobody** is one word. They mean the same.
- After **nobody, no one** and **nothing**, the verb is **singular**: *Nobody **knows**. Nothing **is** open.*
- **never** goes **before** the main verb (*I never eat*) but **after** be and helpers (*She's never late. I can never remember.*).
- **none** (*None of my friends came.*) and words like *something, anyone, everywhere* are in <LessonRef>lesson 32 (some, any, no; something, anyone, nowhere)</LessonRef>.

### no or not?

- **no** goes before a **noun**: *no time, no money, no problem*.
- **not** goes with a **verb**, an **adjective** and most other words: *I'm **not** hungry. It's **not** far. **Not** today.*

So say *I'm **not** hungry* (not ~~I'm no hungry~~), and *There's **no** bread* or *There **isn't any** bread*.

Both *There's no bread* and *There isn't any bread* are correct. *no* sounds a little stronger. In everyday speech, *not … any* is very common.

## One negative only

In standard English, **one negative word is enough** in a clause. If the verb is negative (*didn't, don't, can't*), use **any-words**: *anybody, anyone, anything, anywhere, ever*. If you use a negative word (*nobody, nothing, never*), the verb is **positive**.

<FormTable caption="One negative: two correct ways">

| ✓ Negative verb + any-word | ✓ Positive verb + negative word | ✗ Two negatives |
|---|---|---|
| I **didn't** see **anybody**. | I saw **nobody**. | ~~I didn't see nobody.~~ |
| She **doesn't** know **anything**. | She knows **nothing**. | ~~She doesn't know nothing.~~ |
| We **didn't** go **anywhere**. | We went **nowhere**. | ~~We didn't go nowhere.~~ |
| He **doesn't ever** call. | He **never** calls. | ~~He doesn't never call.~~ |

</FormTable>

The two correct sentences mean the same thing. In conversation, the *not … any* form is more common: *I didn't see anybody.*

<Example context="school" fa="من هیچ چیزی درباره‌ی امتحان نمی‌دانم.">I <mark>don't</mark> know <mark>anything</mark> about the exam.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="امروز صبح هیچ‌کس را در دفتر ندیدم.">I <mark>didn't</mark> see <mark>anyone</mark> in the office this morning.</Example>

### Negative word at the start

When the subject is negative, use **nobody, no one** or **nothing**. An *any*-word can't start a negative sentence.

<Example context="work" fa="هیچ‌کس به جلسه نیامد."><mark>Nobody</mark> came to the meeting. (not ~~Anybody didn't come~~)</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="هیچ اتفاقی نیفتاد."><mark>Nothing</mark> happened. (not ~~Nothing didn't happen~~)</Example>

You may hear double negatives in songs and in some dialects (*I can't get no…*). They are not standard English, so don't use them in your speaking or writing. More about this in <LessonRef>C2 lesson 26 (Prescriptive rules vs real usage)</LessonRef>.

## Negative questions

A **negative question** starts with a helper verb + **n't**: *Don't, Doesn't, Didn't, Isn't, Aren't, Wasn't, Can't, Won't…*

<FormTable caption="Negative questions">

| Helper + n't | Subject | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| **Don't** | you | like it? |
| **Doesn't** | she | live here? |
| **Didn't** | you | hear the alarm? |
| **Isn't** | that | your brother? |
| **Aren't** | you | hungry? |
| **Can't** | you | swim? |
| **Why didn't** | you | call me? |

</FormTable>

The full form has a different word order: *Do you **not** like it?* It sounds slow and formal, so in speech we almost always use the short form with **n't**.

### Why do we ask negative questions?

**1. Surprise.** You expected something different.

<Example context="social" fa="مگر شکلات دوست نداری؟ همه دوست دارند!"><mark>Don't you</mark> like chocolate? Everybody likes chocolate!</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مگر صدای زنگ را نشنیدی؟"><mark>Didn't you</mark> hear the doorbell?</Example>

**2. Checking.** You think something is true and you want the other person to agree.

<Example context="social" fa="آن آقا برادرت نیست؟"><mark>Isn't</mark> that your brother over there?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ما پارسال هم را ندیدیم؟ در کنفرانس؟"><mark>Didn't</mark> we meet last year, at the conference?</Example>

**3. Exclamations.** You don't want an answer. You want the other person to agree with your feeling: *Isn't it a beautiful day!* (Exclamations are in <LessonRef>lesson 4 (Exclamations: What a…! How…!)</LessonRef>.)

**4. Warm invitations and suggestions.** *Won't you come in? Why don't you stay for dinner?* (More suggestions in <LessonRef>lesson 23 (Suggestions & offers: Let's, Shall we, How about)</LessonRef>.)

**Careful:** for a normal polite request, don't use a negative question. ***Can't** you help me?* sounds like a complaint. Say ***Can** you help me?*

**Why + negative** asks for a reason or complains: *Why didn't you call me? Why aren't you at school?*

## Answering negative questions

In English, **yes** and **no** are about the **facts**, not about the question. Answer a negative question **exactly like a positive question**.

- **Yes** = the positive fact: *Yes, I do. Yes, I am. Yes, I did.*
- **No** = the negative fact: *No, I don't. No, I'm not. No, I didn't.*

<FormTable caption="Do you like tea? / Don't you like tea?">

| The fact | Answer to Do you like tea? | Answer to Don't you like tea? |
|---|---|---|
| You like tea. | **Yes, I do.** | **Yes, I do.** |
| You don't like tea. | **No, I don't.** | **No, I don't.** |

</FormTable>

The question changes. The answer doesn't.

<Example context="social" fa="«مگر نمی‌آیی؟» «نه، نمی‌آیم. خیلی خسته‌ام.»">“<mark>Aren't</mark> you coming?” “<mark>No, I'm not.</mark> I'm too tired.”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="«مگر نمی‌آیی؟» «چرا، می‌آیم! پنج دقیقه صبر کن.»">“<mark>Aren't</mark> you coming?” “<mark>Yes, I am!</mark> Give me five minutes.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«مگر تکلیفت را انجام ندادی؟» «چرا، انجام دادم. اینجاست.»">“<mark>Didn't</mark> you do your homework?” “<mark>Yes, I did.</mark> Here it is.”</Example>

Don't answer with only *Yes* or *No*. Add the short answer (*Yes, I do. / No, I don't.*), so the meaning is clear. Never mix them: ~~Yes, I don't~~ and ~~No, I do~~ are always wrong.

**کلمه‌های منفی:** این کلمه‌ها خودشان معنی منفی دارند:

- **no** + اسم = هیچ: There's no milk. (هیچ شیری نیست.)
- **never** = هیچ وقت: I never drink coffee. (هیچ وقت قهوه نمی‌خورم.)
- **nothing** = هیچ چیز: I bought nothing. (هیچ چیز نخریدم.)
- **nobody / no one** = هیچ‌کس: Nobody came. (هیچ‌کس نیامد.)
- **nowhere** = هیچ جا: There's nowhere to sit. (هیچ جایی برای نشستن نیست.)

**no یا not؟** no قبل از اسم می‌آید (no time، no money). not با فعل و صفت می‌آید: I'm not hungry. (نه I'm no hungry.)

**فقط یک منفی، مهم‌ترین نکته برای فارسی‌زبان‌ها:** در فارسی «هیچ» و فعل منفی با هم می‌آیند: «هیچ‌کس را **ن**دیدم»، «هیچ وقت گوشت **نمی**‌خورم». در انگلیسی معیار **فقط یک کلمه‌ی منفی** در جمله می‌آید. دو راه درست داریم:

- فعل منفی + any: I **didn't** see **anybody**.
- فعل مثبت + کلمه‌ی منفی: I **saw nobody**.
- غلط: I didn't see nobody. (دو منفی)

همین‌طور: «هیچ وقت گوشت نمی‌خورم» ← I never eat meat. (نه I don't never eat meat.)

**اول جمله:** «هیچ‌کس نیامد» ← **Nobody came.** فعل مثبت است. نه Nobody didn't come و نه Anybody didn't come.

**سؤال منفی:** فعل کمکی + n't + فاعل: Don't you like it؟ Isn't she here؟ Didn't you hear؟ این سؤال‌ها شبیه «مگر…؟» در فارسی هستند. برای تعجب (مگر شکلات دوست نداری؟ ← Don't you like chocolate?)، برای اطمینان از چیزی که فکر می‌کنیم درست است (آن آقا برادرت نیست؟ ← Isn't that your brother?) و برای دعوت گرم (Won't you come in?) به کار می‌روند. برای درخواست مؤدبانه از سؤال منفی استفاده نکنید: Can't you help me? حالت گله دارد. بگویید Can you help me?

**جواب سؤال منفی، تله‌ی بزرگ:** در فارسی به سؤال منفی این‌طور جواب می‌دهیم:

- «مگر چای دوست نداری؟» ← «**نه**» یعنی «درست است، دوست ندارم».
- «مگر چای دوست نداری؟» ← «**چرا**» یعنی «دوست دارم».

انگلیسی کلمه‌ای مثل «چرا» ندارد. در انگلیسی Yes و No درباره‌ی **واقعیت** هستند، نه درباره‌ی سؤال:

- اگر چای دوست **داری**: Yes, I do.
- اگر چای دوست **نداری**: No, I don't.

یعنی جواب دقیقاً مثل جوابِ سؤال مثبت (Do you like tea?) است. Yes, I don't یا No, I do همیشه غلط است. همیشه بعد از Yes یا No جواب کوتاه را هم بگویید تا معنی روشن باشد.

<MemoryHook title="One no, and answer the facts">
English has room for **one** “no” in a clause: *didn't + any* or *nothing / nobody / never* alone. And when someone asks *Don't you…?*, forget the question. Just say the facts: **Yes, I do** or **No, I don't**.
