## too: more than is good

You met **too** in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 30 (very, really, too)</LessonRef>. **too** + adjective or adverb means *more than is good, possible or needed*. It usually means there is a **problem**.

<Example context="shopping" fa="این کت خیلی گران است؛ نمی‌توانم بخرمش.">This coat is <mark>too expensive</mark>. I can't buy it.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="خیلی تند می‌رانی! آهسته‌تر!">You're driving <mark>too fast</mark>! Slow down!</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="بیرون برای بچه‌ها خیلی سرد است.">It's <mark>too cold</mark> for the kids outside.</Example>

- Add **for** + a person to say who has the problem: *This jacket is **too big for me**.*
- **too** + adjective normally comes after a verb like *be*: *The car is too expensive*, not ~~a too expensive car~~.
- **too much** and **too many** go before **nouns**: *too much sugar, too many cars*. That is in <LessonRef>lesson 31 (much, many, a lot of, a few, a little)</LessonRef>. Don't say ~~too much expensive~~.

## enough: after an adjective, before a noun

**enough** means *as much as you need*. Its position changes.

<FormTable caption="Where enough goes">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| adjective + **enough** | The room is **big enough**. |
| adverb + **enough** | You aren't speaking **loudly enough**. |
| **enough** + noun (plural or uncountable) | We have **enough chairs**. · Do you have **enough money**? |
| **enough** alone (no noun) | Is that **enough**? · I've had **enough**, thanks. |
| … **enough for** + person | Is the water **warm enough for you**? |

</FormTable>

- With adjectives and adverbs, **enough** comes **last**: *big enough*, never ~~enough big~~.
- With nouns, **enough** comes **first**: *enough time*, not ~~time enough~~.
- Negatives and questions: *It **isn't** warm **enough**. **Do** we have **enough** chairs?*

<Example context="home" fa="آپارتمان برای ما به اندازه‌ی کافی بزرگ است.">The apartment is <mark>big enough</mark> for us.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="وقت کافی نداریم.">We don't have <mark>enough time</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="بلند صحبت نمی‌کنی. نمی‌شنوم.">You're not speaking <mark>loudly enough</mark>. I can't hear you.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="برای همه صندلی کافی هست؟">Are there <mark>enough chairs</mark> for everyone?</Example>

## too or not enough? The same problem, two ways

You can often say the same problem with **too** or with **not … enough**. Use the **opposite** adjective.

<FormTable caption="too = not … enough">

| too | not … enough |
|---|---|
| The shoes are **too small**. | The shoes **aren't big enough**. |
| He's **too young** to drive. | He **isn't old enough** to drive. |
| The bus is **too slow**. | The bus **isn't fast enough**. |

</FormTable>

*too young to drive* and *old enough to vote* (too / enough + **to** + verb) are taught fully in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 44 (so & such; too & enough + to)</LessonRef>.

## very or too?

**very** makes an adjective stronger, but there is **no problem**. **too** means *more than is good*: there **is** a problem.

<FormTable caption="very or too?">

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| The soup is **very** hot. | It's hotter than usual. Eat it carefully. (OK) |
| The soup is **too** hot. | You can't eat it now. (a problem) |
| She's **very** young. | She's young. (a fact) |
| She's **too** young to work. | Her age is a problem: she can't work. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="هتل خیلی گران بود، ولی عالی بود.">The hotel was <mark>very</mark> expensive, but it was great.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل خیلی گران بود، برای همین جای دیگری ماندیم.">The hotel was <mark>too</mark> expensive, so we stayed somewhere else.</Example>

Three more things about **very**:

- **very** can't go alone before a **comparative**. Say ***much** better* or ***a lot** better*, not ~~very better~~. (More in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 45 (Comparatives 2: much better, more and more, the … the)</LessonRef>.)
- **very** can go between **the** and a **superlative** for extra force: *We use **the very best** rice.*
- In everyday speech, **not too** often means *not very*: *"How was the exam?" "**Not too** bad."*

## a lot, a bit, much, very much after verbs

**very** can't describe a verb (~~I very like it~~). After a verb, use these words:

<FormTable caption="After a verb">

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **a lot** | very much, often | I like this song **a lot**. · He travels **a lot**. |
| **very much** | a lot (a little more formal) | Thank you **very much**. · We enjoyed it **very much**. |
| **a bit** / **a little** | to a small degree | It helps **a bit**. · Can you wait **a little**? |
| **much** (negatives and questions) | a lot | I **don't** like it **much**. · **Does** it hurt **much**? |
| **too much** | more than is good | You work **too much**. |

</FormTable>

- **Position:** these words go **after the verb**, and **after the object** if there is one: *I miss **my family a lot***, not ~~I miss a lot my family~~.
- **much** alone sounds wrong or very formal in **positive** sentences. Say *I like it **a lot*** or *I like it **very much***, not ~~I like it much~~. In negatives and questions, *much* is normal.
- **a bit** is informal and very common in speech. **a little** means the same: *I'm **a bit** tired. It helps **a little**.*

<Example context="social" fa="دلم برای خانواده‌ام خیلی تنگ شده.">I miss my family <mark>a lot</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="این دارو کمی کمک می‌کند.">This medicine helps <mark>a bit</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="زیاد تلویزیون نگاه نمی‌کنم.">I don't watch TV <mark>much</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="خیلی کار می‌کنی. به مرخصی احتیاج داری.">You work <mark>too much</mark>. You need a vacation.</Example>

<Persian>

**«خیلی» فارسی چهار ترجمه دارد.** بسته به جمله، «خیلی» می‌تواند very، too، a lot یا very much باشد:

- **very + صفت (بدون مشکل):** The food is very good. (غذا خیلی خوب است.)
- **too + صفت (با مشکل):** The tea is too hot. (چای خیلی داغ است ← نمی‌توانم بخورم.) از خودتان بپرسید: «آیا مشکلی هست؟» اگر بله ← too.
- **بعد از فعل ← a lot یا very much:** I like it a lot. (خیلی دوستش دارم.) نه I very like it و نه I like much it.

**enough = کافی / به اندازه‌ی کافی:** جای enough مهم است:

- **بعد از صفت یا قید:** big enough (به اندازه‌ی کافی بزرگ)، fast enough. در فارسی «به اندازه‌ی کافی» **قبل** از صفت می‌آید، برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند enough big که غلط است. در انگلیسی enough **بعد** از صفت است.
- **قبل از اسم:** enough money (پول کافی). در فارسی «کافی» **بعد** از اسم است («پولِ کافی»)، پس می‌گویند money enough. در انگلیسی enough **قبل** از اسم است.
- یعنی ترتیب فارسی را در هر دو حالت **برعکس** کنید.

**یک مشکل، دو راه:** too small = not big enough (خیلی کوچک است = به اندازه‌ی کافی بزرگ نیست). صفت مخالف را به کار ببرید.

**very قبل از تفضیلی نمی‌آید:** «خیلی بهتر» = much better یا a lot better، نه very better.

**بعد از فعل:**

- a lot = خیلی / زیاد: I miss my family a lot. (دلم برای خانواده‌ام خیلی تنگ شده.)
- very much = خیلی (کمی رسمی‌تر): Thank you very much.
- a bit / a little = کمی / یه کم: It helps a bit. (کمی کمک می‌کند.)
- much تنها فقط در جمله‌های **منفی و سؤالی**: I don't like it much. (زیاد دوستش ندارم.) / Does it hurt much? (خیلی درد می‌کند؟) در جمله‌ی مثبت نگویید I like it much.
- too much = بیش از حد: You work too much. (بیش از حد کار می‌کنی.)
- این کلمه‌ها بعد از فعل و **بعد از مفعول** می‌آیند: I like this song a lot، نه I like a lot this song.

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="Enough comes last, except with nouns">
Adjectives go first, then **enough**: *big **enough***. Nouns wait for **enough** to arrive first: ***enough** money*. And remember **too = trouble**: no trouble, say **very**.
</MemoryHook>
