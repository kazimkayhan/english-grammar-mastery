## What is a phrasal verb?

A **phrasal verb** is a **verb + a particle**. The particle is a small word like *up, down, on, off, in, out, away, back, over, around*. Together, the two words work like **one verb**.

<Example context="home" fa="هر روز ساعت هفت بیدار می‌شوم و از جا بلند می‌شوم.">I <mark>wake up</mark> at seven and <mark>get up</mark> at quarter past.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="لطفاً کامپیوترت را خاموش کن.">Please <mark>turn off</mark> your computer before you leave.</Example>

### Easy meanings and new meanings

Some phrasal verbs mean exactly what the words say. The particle shows **direction**:

- *come in* (enter) · *go out* (leave the house) · *come back* (return) · *sit down* · *stand up* · *run away* · *fall off*

Others have a **new meaning**. You can't guess it from the two words, so learn it as one unit:

- *give up* = stop trying, or stop a habit: *I **gave up** smoking last year.*
- *find out* = learn a fact: *I **found out** that the shop closes at six.*
- *break down* = stop working (a car or a machine): *Our car **broke down** on the highway.*
- *grow up* = become an adult: *I **grew up** in Bamyan.*

<FormTable caption="Common particles and their basic ideas">

| Particle | Basic idea | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **up** | upward; more; completely | get up, stand up, turn up (louder), pick up, clean up |
| **down** | downward; less; on paper | sit down, turn down (quieter), write down, slow down |
| **on** | connected; on the body | turn on, switch on, put on, try on |
| **off** | not connected; away from | turn off, switch off, take off, get off |
| **in / out** | into / out of a place | come in, go out, eat out, check in, check out |
| **away** | to another place; gone | go away, throw away, put away, run away |
| **back** | return | come back, give back, call back |

</FormTable>

These ideas help, but they are not rules. Many phrasal verbs simply have to be learned.

One verb can have more than one meaning: *Take off your shoes* (remove clothes) · *The plane **took off** at six* (left the ground).

## Phrasal verbs with no object

Many phrasal verbs have **no object**. The verb and particle stay **together**.

<FormTable caption="No object: verb + particle together">

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **get up** | leave your bed | I **get up** at 6:30. |
| **wake up** | stop sleeping | I **woke up** in the night. |
| **sit down** / **stand up** | take a seat / get on your feet | Please **sit down**. |
| **go out** | leave home for fun | We **went out** last night. |
| **come back** | return | When are you **coming back**? |
| **grow up** | become an adult | She **grew up** in Herat. |
| **break down** | stop working | The bus **broke down**. |
| **hurry up** | go faster | **Hurry up**! We're late. |
| **check in** | arrive and register | We **checked in** at the hotel at 3. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="هواپیما دیر بلند شد.">The plane <mark>took off</mark> an hour late.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="آهسته‌تر برو! خیلی سریع رانندگی می‌کنی."><mark>Slow down</mark>! You're driving too fast.</Example>

## Phrasal verbs with an object

Other phrasal verbs have an **object**: you *turn on* **something**, you *pick up* **someone**.

<FormTable caption="Common phrasal verbs with an object">

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **turn on / off** | start / stop a machine or light | Turn off the TV. |
| **turn up / down** | make louder / quieter | Turn down the music. |
| **put on** | start wearing | Put on your coat. |
| **take off** | remove (clothes) | Take off your shoes. |
| **try on** | put on clothes to see if they fit | Can I try on this shirt? |
| **pick up** | lift; collect someone | Pick up your toys. · I'll pick up the kids. |
| **throw away** | put in the trash | Throw away the old milk. |
| **give back** | return to the owner | Give back my pen! |
| **write down** | write on paper | Write down the address. |
| **look up** | find in a dictionary or online | Look up the word. |
| **fill out** (or **fill in**) | complete a form | Fill out this form, please. |

</FormTable>

### A noun object: two positions

With a **noun** object, you can put it **after** the particle or **in the middle**. Both are correct.

<FormTable caption="Noun object: two orders">

| Verb + particle + noun | Verb + noun + particle |
|---|---|
| Turn **on** the light. | Turn the light **on**. |
| I picked **up** the kids. | I picked the kids **up**. |
| She took **off** her coat. | She took her coat **off**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="shopping" fa="می‌توانم این کفش‌ها را امتحان کنم؟">Can I <mark>try on</mark> these shoes? / Can I <mark>try</mark> these shoes <mark>on</mark>?</Example>

When the object is **long**, put it **after** the particle. It is easier to follow:

- *Turn off **all the lights in the house**.* (better than *Turn all the lights in the house off.*)
- *Please fill out **the form on the second page**.*

## Pronouns go in the middle

When the object is a **pronoun** (*it, them, me, you, him, her, us*), it **must** go **between** the verb and the particle. It never goes after the particle.

<FormTable caption="Pronoun object: only in the middle">

| ✓ Correct | ✗ Wrong |
|---|---|
| Turn **it** on. | ~~Turn on it.~~ |
| Take **them** off. | ~~Take off them.~~ |
| I'll pick **you** up. | ~~I'll pick up you.~~ |
| Write **it** down. | ~~Write down it.~~ |
| Can I try **it** on? | ~~Can I try on it?~~ |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="«تلویزیون روشن است.» «خوب، خاموشش می‌کنم.»">“The TV is still on.” “OK, I'll <mark>turn it off</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«این کلمه یعنی چه؟» «در دیکشنری پیدایش کن.»">“What does this word mean?” “<mark>Look it up</mark> in the dictionary.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="ساعت شش در فرودگاه دنبالت می‌آیم.">I'll <mark>pick you up</mark> at the airport at six.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="این قرص‌ها تاریخ‌گذشته‌اند. دورشان بینداز.">These pills are old. <mark>Throw them away</mark>.</Example>

You met the object pronouns in <LessonRef>A1 lesson 13 (Object pronouns: me, him, her, us, them)</LessonRef>.

## Questions, negatives and other tenses

A phrasal verb works like any other verb. The **helping verb** (do, did, have, will…) does the grammar work, and the **particle stays** with the verb.

<FormTable caption="Phrasal verbs in different sentences">

| Sentence type | Example |
|---|---|
| Present simple question | What time **do** you **get up**? |
| Past simple question | **Did** you **turn off** the oven? · **Did** you **turn** it **off**? |
| Negative | I **didn't get up** early. · She **doesn't eat out** much. |
| Present continuous | Hurry! The plane **is taking off**. |
| Present perfect | Have you **found out** the answer? |
| Future | **I'll call** you **back** later. |
| Imperative | **Sit down**, please. · **Don't throw** it **away**. |

</FormTable>

Only the **verb** changes form: *get up → got up*, *grow up → grew up*, *take off → took off*. The particle never changes.

The pronoun rule works in every tense: *Did you turn **it** off? I haven't given **it** back yet.*

<Example context="home" fa="«گاز را خاموش کردی؟» «بلی، خاموشش کردم.»">“<mark>Did</mark> you <mark>turn off</mark> the stove?” “Yes, I <mark>turned it off</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="چند بس امروز صبح خراب شدند.">Several buses <mark>broke down</mark> this morning.</Example>

## put on or wear?

**put on** is the **action** (you start to have clothes on). **wear** is the **state** (you have them on).

<Example context="social" fa="کتت را بپوش. داریم می‌رویم."><mark>Put on</mark> your jacket. We're leaving.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او یک کت آبی پوشیده است.">She's <mark>wearing</mark> a blue jacket.</Example>

The opposite of *put on* is **take off**: *Put on your shoes. · Take off your shoes.*

## Not a phrasal verb: verb + preposition

Some verbs are followed by a **preposition**: *look at, look for, listen to, wait for, get on (the bus), get off (the train)*. These look like phrasal verbs, but the object **always comes after** the small word, even when it is a pronoun.

<FormTable caption="Phrasal verb or verb + preposition?">

| Phrasal verb: pronoun in the middle | Verb + preposition: object after |
|---|---|
| Turn **it** on. | Look at **it**. (not ~~look it at~~) |
| Pick **her** up. | Wait for **her**. (not ~~wait her for~~) |
| Throw **them** away. | Look for **them**. (not ~~look them for~~) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="بیا سوار این بس شویم.">Let's <mark>get on</mark> this bus. (not *get this bus on*)</Example>

A good tip: learn new phrasal verbs from the dictionary with *something* in the right place: **turn something on**, but **look for something**. The full story (and three-word verbs like *look forward to* and *run out of*) is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 14 (Phrasal & prepositional verbs)</LessonRef>.

**سؤالی و منفی:** فعل کمکی (do، did، will…) کار دستوری را انجام می‌دهد و particle سر جایش می‌ماند: Did you turn off the oven؟ (اجاق را خاموش کردی؟) · I didn't get up early. (زود بلند نشدم.)

**اشتباه‌های رایج فارسی‌زبان‌ها:**

- **پوشیدن:** در فارسی «پوشیدن» هم برای عملِ لباس پوشیدن است و هم برای حالتِ به تن داشتن. در انگلیسی دو فعل داریم: **put on** = عمل (Put on your coat. کتت را بپوش.) و **wear** = حالت (She's wearing a coat. کت تنش است.). پس نگویید I wore my coat and went out؛ بگویید **I put on my coat** and went out. عکسِ put on هم **take off** است (درآوردن).

<MemoryHook title="Little words like it sit in the middle">
Think of a phrasal verb as a **sandwich**. A short word like **it**, **them** or **me** is the filling, so it goes **in the middle**: *turn **it** on, take **them** off, pick **me** up*, just like «روشن**ش** کن». A noun can go in the middle or after. But *look for*, *listen to* and *wait for* are not sandwiches: the object always comes **after**.
</MemoryHook>
