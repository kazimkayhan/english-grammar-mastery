## What used to means

**used to + base form** tells us about the past and also about now. It says: *this was true in the past, again and again or for a long time, but it is **not true now**.*

<Timeline items={[{ type: "repeat", from: -0.95, to: -0.35, label: "I used to play football" }, { type: "point", at: 0, label: "now: I don't" }]} caption="A past habit that has stopped." />

<Example context="social" fa="قبلاً فوتبال بازی می‌کردم، ولی دیگر بازی نمی‌کنم.">I <mark>used to play</mark> football, but I don't play anymore.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مادربزرگم قبلاً در این روستا زندگی می‌کرد.">My grandmother <mark>used to live</mark> in this village.</Example>

## Habits and states

We use **used to** for two kinds of things:

- **Habits** (repeated actions): *I **used to walk** to school. We **used to go** to the beach every summer.*
- **States** (situations that lasted for a time): *She **used to have** long hair. I **used to be** very shy. They **used to live** in Bamyan.*

<Example context="school" fa="هر روز پیاده به مدرسه می‌رفتم.">I <mark>used to walk</mark> to school every day.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="قبلاً سیگار می‌کشید، ولی پارسال ترک کرد.">He <mark>used to smoke</mark>, but he stopped last year.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="قبلاً موهایش بلند بود.">She <mark>used to have</mark> long hair.</Example>

**There used to be** is very useful for places that have changed:

<Example context="shopping" fa="قبلاً اینجا یک سینما بود. حالا بانک است.">There <mark>used to be</mark> a cinema here. Now it's a bank.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="قبلاً در این شهر توریست زیادی نبود.">There <mark>didn't use to be</mark> many tourists in this town.</Example>

## The form

**used to** looks like a past verb, and it is: it has **no present form**. It is the same for every subject, and the next verb is always the **base form**.

<FormTable caption="used to">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | subject + **used to** + base form | She **used to work** in a bank. |
| Negative | subject + **didn't use to** + base form | She **didn't use to work** on Saturdays. |
| Question | **Did** + subject + **use to** + base form? | **Did** she **use to work** there? |
| Short answers | Yes, + subject + **did**. / No, + subject + **didn't**. | Yes, she **did**. / No, she **didn't**. |
| Wh- question | question word + **did** + subject + **use to**…? | Where **did** she **use to work**? |

</FormTable>

Two things to notice:

- After **did / didn't**, write **use to**, without *d*: *didn't **use** to, Did you **use** to…?* It works like any other past verb: *didn't work*, not ~~didn't worked~~ (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 27</LessonRef>). You will sometimes see *didn't used to* in informal writing, but *didn't use to* is the safe, standard form.
- **Pronunciation:** *used to* and *use to* sound the same: /ˈjuːst tə/. The **s** sounds like **s**, not **z**, and *to* is short (in fast speech it sounds like “yoosta”). It is different from the verb *use* (/juːz/, *I use a computer*).

## Negatives

The usual negative is **didn't use to**. In speech, people also say **never used to**.

<Example context="home" fa="قبلاً قهوه دوست نداشتم، ولی حالا روزی سه فنجان می‌خورم.">I <mark>didn't use to like</mark> coffee, but now I drink three cups a day.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او هیچ‌وقت آخر هفته‌ها کار نمی‌کرد.">He <mark>never used to work</mark> on the weekend.</Example>

<UkNote>British English also has a formal negative, **used not to**: *She used not to eat meat.* It is rare in conversation. *didn't use to* is fine in both British and American English.</UkNote>

## Questions and short answers

Use **Did** + subject + **use to** + base form. Short answers repeat **did**.

<Example context="social" fa="«قبلاً عینک می‌زدی؟» «بله، می‌زدم.»">“<mark>Did</mark> you <mark>use to wear</mark> glasses?” “Yes, I <mark>did</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="قبل از این شغل کجا کار می‌کردی؟">Where <mark>did</mark> you <mark>use to work</mark> before this job?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="در مدرسه ورزش می‌کردی؟"><mark>Did</mark> you <mark>use to do</mark> any sports at school?</Example>

## used to or the past simple?

For past habits and states, you can also use the **past simple**: *I **walked** to school every day* is correct too. **used to** adds an extra message: *it doesn't happen now*. It also makes the habit sound like a long, repeated pattern.

But **used to** only works for repeated actions or long states. For **one single action** at one time, you must use the past simple:

- ✓ *I **went** to Paris in 2019.* (one trip)
- ✗ ~~I used to go to Paris in 2019.~~
- ✓ *We **used to go** to Paris every spring.* (a habit)

To say **exactly how many times** something happened, the past simple is the normal choice: *I **called** her three times last week.*

## used to or the present simple?

**used to** is only about the past. There is no present *I use to*. For a habit **now**, use the present simple, often with **usually** (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 19 (Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, never)</LessonRef>).

<Example context="work" fa="معمولاً پیاده سر کار می‌روم.">I <mark>usually walk</mark> to work. (now, not ~~I use to walk~~)</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="قبلاً با اتوبوس می‌رفتم، ولی حالا پیاده می‌روم.">I <mark>used to take</mark> the bus, but now I <mark>walk</mark>.</Example>

## Coming later

- **be used to + -ing** and **get used to + -ing** look similar, but they mean something else: *I'm used to getting up early* = it is normal and easy for me. Compare *I used to get up early* (a past habit). This is in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 13 (used to, be used to, get used to)</LessonRef>, together with **would** for past habits (*Every summer we would go to the beach*).
- More ways to talk about habits (*would, will, always + -ing*) come in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 6 (Habits: would, used to, will, always -ing)</LessonRef>.

<MemoryHook title="used to = then, not now">
Think of a photo album. **used to** points at an old photo and says: *“That was me **then**, not **now**.”* I **used to** have long hair. There **used to** be a garden here. And when **did** comes, **used** loses its **d**: *Did you **use** to…?*
</MemoryHook>
