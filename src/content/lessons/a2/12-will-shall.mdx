## The form

**will** is a **modal verb**, like *can* (<LessonRef>A1 lesson 21 (can / can't (and how modal verbs work))</LessonRef>). So it follows the modal rules: the **same form for every subject**, **no -s**, and the **base form** after it, with no *to*.

<FormTable caption="will">

| | Full form | Short form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | I / you / she / we / they **will** + base form | **'ll** | I**'ll** call you. She**'ll** be here soon. |
| Negative | **will not** + base form | **won't** | I **won't** be late. |
| Question | **Will** + subject + base form? | | **Will** you **be** home at six? |
| Short answers | Yes, + subject + **will**. / No, + subject + **won't**. | | Yes, I **will**. / No, I **won't**. |
| Wh- question | question word + **will** + subject + base form? | | What time **will** you **arrive**? |

</FormTable>

- In speech we usually say **'ll**: *I'll, you'll, she'll, it'll, we'll, they'll.* But a short answer must use the full form: *Yes, I **will***, not ~~Yes, I'll~~.
- **won't** is the short form of *will not*. Say it /woʊnt/: it sounds different from *want* /wɑːnt/.
- **will** + **be**: *I'll be home at six. It will be cold tomorrow.*

<Example context="travel" fa="«ساعت شش خانه هستی؟» «بله، هستم.»">“<mark>Will</mark> you <mark>be</mark> home at six?” “Yes, I <mark>will</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="فردا کی می‌رسید؟">What time <mark>will</mark> you <mark>arrive</mark> tomorrow?</Example>

## Use 1: decisions made now

Use **will** when you **decide at the moment of speaking**. Something happens, and you react.

<Example context="home" fa="تلفن زنگ می‌زند. من جواب می‌دهم!">The phone's ringing. I<mark>'ll get</mark> it!</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«چه میل دارید؟» «من کیک شکلاتی می‌خورم.»">“What would you like?” “I<mark>'ll have</mark> the chocolate cake, please.”</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="خسته‌ام. فکر کنم امشب بیرون نمی‌روم.">I'm really tired. I think I <mark>won't go</mark> out tonight.</Example>

Compare **will** and **going to** (<LessonRef>lesson 11 (going to)</LessonRef>):

<FormTable caption="will or going to for decisions?">

| | When did you decide? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** | **now**, as you speak | “There's no milk.” “Oh, I**'ll buy** some on my way home.” |
| **going to** | **before** now (it's a plan) | I**'m going to buy** milk after work. (I decided this morning.) |

</FormTable>

Don't use the present simple for a decision made now: *I'll get it*, not ~~I get it~~.

## Use 2: offers, promises and requests

**will** is also the verb of **helping** and **promising**.

- **Offers** (I'm happy to do it): *That bag looks heavy. I**'ll carry** it for you.*
- **Promises**: *I**'ll call** you tonight, I promise. I **won't tell** anyone.*
- **Requests** (asking someone to do something): ***Will** you close the door, please?*

<Example context="work" fa="نگران نباش. در گزارش کمکت می‌کنم.">Don't worry. I<mark>'ll help</mark> you with the report.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="قول می‌دهم دیر نکنم.">I <mark>won't be</mark> late, I promise.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="لطفاً در را می‌بندی؟"><mark>Will</mark> you <mark>close</mark> the door, please?</Example>

*Will you…?* is a direct request between people who know each other. More polite requests (*Could you…? May I…?*) are in <LessonRef>lesson 21 (could, be able to; asking for things & permission)</LessonRef>. **won't** can also mean *refuse*: *The car **won't** start.* That is in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 12 (dare, need; be able to; won't for refusal)</LessonRef>.

## Use 3: predictions and opinions

Use **will** to say what you **think** or **believe** about the future. It is your **opinion**; you can't see the future coming.

We often use it with:

- **I think** / **I'm sure** / **I expect**: *I think you'**ll** love Herat. I'm sure she'**ll** pass the exam.*
- **I don't think**: *I **don't think** it'll rain.* In negatives, English usually puts *not* on **think**, not on *will*. *I think it won't rain* is possible, but less common.
- **maybe / perhaps** at the start: *Maybe I'll see you there.*

<Example context="travel" fa="فکر می‌کنم هرات را دوست خواهی داشت.">I think you<mark>'ll love</mark> Herat.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="مطمئنم امتحان را قبول می‌شود.">I'm sure she<mark>'ll pass</mark> the exam.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="فکر نمی‌کنم فردا باران ببارد.">I <mark>don't think</mark> it<mark>'ll rain</mark> tomorrow.</Example>

We also use **will** for **future facts** and to **ask about the future**:

<Example context="social" fa="سال بعد سی ساله می‌شوم.">I<mark>'ll be</mark> 30 next year.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="فکر می‌کنی در بامیان هوا سرد باشد؟">Do you think it<mark>'ll be</mark> cold in Bamyan?</Example>

**will or going to for predictions?** With **evidence** you can see now, use **going to**: *Look at those clouds! It's **going to** rain.* With your **opinion** or what you know, **will** is common: *I think it**'ll** rain tomorrow.* (<LessonRef>Lesson 11</LessonRef> has the evidence use.)

## probably and definitely

To say **how sure** you are, add **probably** (quite sure) or **definitely** (100% sure). Their position is important:

<FormTable caption="Position of probably / definitely">

| | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | will + **probably / definitely** + verb | He**'ll probably** come. · I**'ll definitely** be there. |
| Negative | **probably / definitely** + won't + verb | He **probably won't** come. · I **definitely won't** forget. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="احتمالاً امشب دیر می‌رسم.">I<mark>'ll probably be</mark> late tonight.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او احتمالاً به مهمانی نمی‌آید. خیلی سرش شلوغ است.">She <mark>probably won't</mark> come to the party. She's very busy.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="حتماً یک روز به مزار شریف می‌روم.">I<mark>'ll definitely</mark> visit Mazar-i-Sharif one day.</Example>

From most sure to least sure: *will definitely* → *will probably* → *maybe … will* → *probably won't* → *definitely won't*. For less certain predictions, English also has **might** (*It might rain*). That is <LessonRef>lesson 22 (may & might)</LessonRef>.

## Shall I…? Shall we…?

Today **shall** is used mostly in **questions** with **I** and **we**:

<FormTable caption="Questions with shall">

| Pattern | Meaning | Example | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shall I** + base form? | an **offer**: do you want me to do it? | **Shall I** open the window? | Yes, please. / No, thanks. I'm fine. |
| **Shall we** + base form? | a **suggestion**: let's do it together | **Shall we** go for a walk? | Good idea! / Sorry, I can't. |
| **What / Where / When shall** I / we…? | asking for advice or ideas | **What shall we** do tonight? | Let's watch a film. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="هوا گرم است. پنجره را باز کنم؟">It's hot in here. <mark>Shall I</mark> open the window?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="دیر است. تاکسی بگیریم؟">It's late. <mark>Shall we</mark> take a taxi?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="این جعبه‌ها را کجا بگذارم؟">Where <mark>shall I</mark> put these boxes?</Example>

**shall** is especially common in **British English**. In **American English**, *shall* is rare in everyday speech. Americans usually say **Should I…? / Should we…?** or *Do you want me to…?*: *Should I open the window? What should we do tonight?* Both are correct, and you will hear both.

<UkNote>British speakers often use **Shall I…? / Shall we…?** for offers and suggestions: *Shall I make some tea? Shall we meet at six?* In formal British English, *I shall / we shall* can also mean *I will / we will*. You don't need it now; it comes in <LessonRef>C1 lesson 10 (Modality 1: may well, might as well, That'll be John)</LessonRef>.</UkNote>

More ways to make offers and suggestions (*Let's…, How about…?, Would you like…?*) come in <LessonRef>lesson 23 (Suggestions & offers: Let's, Shall we, How about)</LessonRef>.

## One more rule: no will after when and if

After **when**, **if**, **before** and **as soon as**, use the **present**, not *will*, even for the future: *I'll call you **when I get** home*, not ~~when I will get home~~. That is in <LessonRef>lesson 27 (First conditional & future time clauses)</LessonRef>. And for choosing between *will*, *going to* and the present continuous, see <LessonRef>lesson 13 (Present continuous for the future)</LessonRef>.

<MemoryHook title="will is born now">
A **will** decision is **born at the moment you speak**: *I'll get it! I'll have the cake. I'll help you.* A **going to** plan was born **earlier**. And **shall** just asks: ***Shall I*** help? ***Shall we*** go?
</MemoryHook>
