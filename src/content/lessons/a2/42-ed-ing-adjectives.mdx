## Two adjectives from one verb

Many verbs about feelings (*bore, interest, tire, excite, worry, surprise*) make **two** adjectives: one with **-ed** and one with **-ing**. They look alike, but they don't mean the same thing.

<FormTable caption="-ed or -ing?">

| | -ed | -ing |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | how a person **feels** | the thing, situation or person that **causes** the feeling |
| Example | **I'm bored.** | **The film is boring.** |
| Example | **She's interested** in art. | **Art is interesting.** |
| Example | **We were tired** after the trip. | **The trip was tiring.** |

</FormTable>

Think of it like this: *The film is **boring**, so I'm **bored**.* The **-ing** thing makes the person feel **-ed**.

## -ed: how you feel

Use the **-ed** adjective for the person who **has** the feeling.

<Example context="school" fa="کلاس تمام نمی‌شد. حوصله‌ام سر رفته بود.">The class went on and on. I was so <mark>bored</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="بچه‌ها برای سفر خیلی هیجان‌زده‌اند.">The kids are really <mark>excited</mark> about the trip.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="مادرم نگران سلامتی‌ام است.">My mother is <mark>worried</mark> about my health.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="بعد از ده ساعت کار، خیلی خسته بودم.">After ten hours at work, I was very <mark>tired</mark>.</Example>

## -ing: what causes the feeling

Use the **-ing** adjective for the **thing**, **situation** or **person** that makes someone feel that way.

<Example context="news" fa="خبرها امروز نگران‌کننده است.">The news today is <mark>worrying</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="توضیح معلم گیج‌کننده بود.">The teacher's explanation was <mark>confusing</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="پرواز دوازده‌ساعته خیلی خسته‌کننده بود.">The twelve-hour flight was really <mark>tiring</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او آدم جالبی است. ساعت‌ها می‌شود با او حرف زد.">She's a very <mark>interesting</mark> person. You can talk to her for hours.</Example>

Both kinds can go **after be** or **before a noun**: *a **boring** meeting, an **exciting** game, a **tired** child, **worried** parents*.

## Careful with people

People can be **-ed** or **-ing**, but the meaning changes:

- ***I'm bored.*** = I feel bored. (Nothing is interesting for me.)
- ***I'm boring.*** = Other people feel bored when they are with me!
- *He's **interested** in football* (he likes it) ≠ *He's **interesting*** (people like talking to him).

So when you have nothing to do, say **I'm bored**. Things and situations (*a film, the news, a trip*) can't have feelings, so they are almost always **-ing**: *The news was **worrying***, not ~~The news was worried~~.

## Common pairs

<FormTable caption="Common -ed / -ing pairs">

| -ed (the person feels) | -ing (the cause) |
|---|---|
| bored | boring |
| interested | interesting |
| tired | tiring |
| excited | exciting |
| worried | worrying |
| surprised | surprising |
| confused | confusing |
| disappointed | disappointing |
| annoyed | annoying |
| frightened | frightening |
| relaxed | relaxing |
| embarrassed | embarrassing |
| amazed | amazing |

</FormTable>

- Spelling follows the verb: *worry → worried / worrying* (y → i only before -ed), *annoy → annoyed* (vowel + y: no change).
- **scared** has a different partner: something that makes you scared is **scary** (informal) or **frightening**.

## Prepositions after -ed adjectives

Many **-ed** adjectives are followed by a preposition and then the cause:

<FormTable caption="-ed adjective + preposition">

| Adjective | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| interested | **in** | I'm interested **in** photography. |
| worried | **about** | Are you worried **about** the exam? |
| excited | **about** | We're excited **about** the wedding. |
| scared / frightened | **of** | She's scared **of** dogs. |
| tired | **of** (= bored with, annoyed by) | I'm tired **of** this rain! |
| bored | **with** | He got bored **with** the game. |
| surprised | **at / by** | I was surprised **at / by** the price. |

</FormTable>

**interested in** + **-ing** is very common: *Are you interested **in working** abroad?* More adjectives with prepositions are in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 36 (Dependent prepositions 1)</LessonRef>.

## Other participles as adjectives

Other **-ed** (past participle) and **-ing** forms can also work as adjectives, not only feeling words:

- **-ed / past participle** = something happened to it: *a **broken** window, **lost** keys, The shop is **closed**.*
- **-ing** = it is doing something now: *a **sleeping** baby, **boiling** water.*

<Example context="home" fa="مراقب باش! روی زمین شیشه‌ی شکسته هست.">Be careful! There's <mark>broken</mark> glass on the floor.</Example>

Past participles are in <LessonRef>lesson 14 (Verb forms & auxiliary verbs)</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**صفت‌های ed- و ing-:** از بسیاری از فعل‌های احساسی (bore، interest، tire، excite، worry) **دو صفت** ساخته می‌شود:

- **ed- = حسی که شخص دارد:** I'm bored. (حوصله‌ام سر رفته.) / She's interested in art. (به هنر علاقه دارد.) / We were tired. (خسته بودیم.)
- **ing- = چیزی که این حس را ایجاد می‌کند:** The film is boring. (فیلم خسته‌کننده / کسل‌کننده است.) / Art is interesting. (هنر جالب است.) / The trip was tiring. (سفر خسته‌کننده بود.)

**یک راه ساده برای فارسی‌زبان‌ها:** پسوندهای فارسیِ **«-کننده»، «-انگیز» و «-آور»** دقیقاً همان ing- انگلیسی هستند، چون «علت» را نشان می‌دهند:

- خسته‌کننده = tiring / boring · نگران‌کننده = worrying · گیج‌کننده = confusing · ناامیدکننده = disappointing
- هیجان‌انگیز = exciting · شگفت‌انگیز = amazing
- تعجب‌آور = surprising · خجالت‌آور = embarrassing

و صفت‌های فارسی بدون این پسوندها (خسته، نگران، گیج، هیجان‌زده، متعجب، ناامید) همان ed- هستند: tired، worried، confused، excited، surprised، disappointed.

**چرا اشتباه می‌کنیم؟** در فارسی «علاقه‌مند» و «جالب» دو کلمه‌ی کاملاً متفاوت‌اند و قاطی نمی‌شوند. اما در انگلیسی interested و interesting خیلی شبیه‌اند. برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند I'm interesting in music، که یعنی «من آدم جالبی هستم در موسیقی»! «به موسیقی علاقه دارم» = **I'm interested in music**.

**مراقب I'm boring باشید:** وقتی حوصله‌تان سر رفته، بگویید **I'm bored**. جمله‌ی I'm boring یعنی «من آدم کسل‌کننده‌ای هستم» (دیگران با من حوصله‌شان سر می‌رود). خبر، فیلم و سفر احساس ندارند، پس معمولاً ing- می‌گیرند: The news was worrying (نه worried).

**حرف اضافه بعد از ed-:** interested in (علاقه‌مند به)، worried about (نگرانِ)، excited about، scared of (ترسیده از)، tired of (خسته از / به ستوه آمده از)، surprised at / by.

**سؤالی و منفی:** Are you interested in art? (به هنر علاقه داری؟) / The film wasn't very interesting. (فیلم خیلی جالب نبود.) / I'm not tired. (خسته نیستم.)

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="-ed is inside you, -ing is the thing">
The **-ed** word is the feeling **inside** you: *I'm **bored**.* The **-ing** word is the **thing** outside that gives you that feeling: *the **boring** thing*. Bor-ING, th-ING: they end the same way.
</MemoryHook>
