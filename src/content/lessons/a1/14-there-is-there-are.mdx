## Something exists

To say that something exists, or is in a place, English starts with **there** + **be**:

<FormTable caption="there is / there are">

| | One thing (or uncountable) | More than one |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | There **is** (There's) a bank. | There **are** two banks. |
| **−** | There **isn't** a bank. | There **aren't** any banks. |
| **?** | **Is there** a bank? | **Are there** any banks? |
| **Short answers** | Yes, there **is**. / No, there **isn't**. | Yes, there **are**. / No, there **aren't**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="یک آشپزخانه‌ی بزرگ هست و دو اتاق خواب."><mark>There's</mark> a big kitchen, and <mark>there are</mark> two bedrooms.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="ببخشید، این نزدیکی داروخانه هست؟">Excuse me, <mark>is there</mark> a pharmacy near here?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="در کلاس ما بیست دانشجو هست."><mark>There are</mark> twenty students in our class.</Example>

## There is or there are?

Look at the noun after the verb. It decides **is** or **are**.

- **One** thing → **there is**: *There is a bus stop on the corner.*
- An **uncountable** noun (milk, water, bread, money, traffic) → **there is**: *There's some water in the bottle.* (More about uncountable nouns in <LessonRef>lesson 15</LessonRef>.)
- **Two or more** → **there are**: *There are three buses an hour.*

<Example context="shopping" fa="روی میز مقداری پول هست.">There's <mark>some money</mark> on the table.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="امروز ترافیک زیادی در مرکز شهر هست.">There's <mark>a lot of traffic</mark> downtown today.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="در این ساختمان دو آسانسور هست.">There are <mark>two elevators</mark> in this building.</Example>

**Short form:** *there is* → **there's**. This is very common in speech. *There are* has no short form in writing. Write **there are**.

**A list of things?** In everyday speech, people often start with *there's* when the first thing is singular: *There's a café, a supermarket and a bus stop on my street.* More on this in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 5 (Subject–verb agreement 1)</LessonRef>.

## Where does the place go?

The usual order is **there + be + thing + place**:

*There's a cat in the garden.*

You can also put the place first, but you still need **there**: *In the garden, there's a cat.* Never drop **there**: ~~In the garden is a cat.~~

We use *there is* for **new** information: *a, an, some, any, no* or a number come after it. We don't usually say *there is the…*: say ***The** bank is next to the café*, not ~~There is the bank next to the café~~.

## a, some, any

- **There's a / an** + one countable thing: *There's an apple in my bag.*
- **There are some** + plural: *There are some apples on the table.*
- **There's some** + uncountable: *There's some bread in the kitchen.*
- **any** in negatives and questions: *There aren't any apples. Is there any bread?*

<Example context="home" fa="در یخچال چند تا تخم‌مرغ هست، ولی شیر نیست.">There are <mark>some</mark> eggs in the fridge, but there <mark>isn't any</mark> milk.</Example>

<LessonRef>Lesson 15 (Countable & uncountable nouns; some, any, how much, how many)</LessonRef> teaches *some* and *any* in full.

## Negatives

Put **not** after *is* or *are*. Use **a** with one thing and **any** with plural or uncountable nouns.

<FormTable caption="there: negative">

| | Negative | Strong negative (with **no**) |
|---|---|---|
| One thing | There **isn't a** café. | |
| Uncountable | There **isn't any** milk. | There's **no** milk. |
| Plural | There **aren't any** toilets. | There are **no** toilets. |

</FormTable>

**There's no / There are no** means the same as *there isn't any / there aren't any*, but it sounds stronger. Don't use **no** and **not** together: ~~There aren't no toilets.~~

<Example context="travel" fa="در این روستا هیچ هتلی نیست.">There <mark>aren't any</mark> hotels in this village.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="در دفترچه‌ی کمک‌های اولیه هیچ چسب زخمی نیست.">There are <mark>no</mark> bandages in the first-aid kit.</Example>

## Questions and short answers

Swap **there** and **is / are**: *There is a bank.* → ***Is there** a bank?*

<FormTable caption="there: questions">

| Question | Short answers |
|---|---|
| **Is there** a gym in the hotel? | Yes, there **is**. / No, there **isn't**. |
| **Is there any** milk? | Yes, there **is**. / No, there **isn't**. |
| **Are there any** shops near here? | Yes, there **are**. / No, there **aren't**. |
| **How many** rooms **are there**? | There are four. |

</FormTable>

In a positive short answer, don't use the short form: say **Yes, there is.**, not ~~Yes, there's.~~

<Example context="travel" fa="آیا در هتل باشگاه ورزشی هست؟ بله، هست.">“<mark>Is there</mark> a gym in the hotel?” “Yes, there <mark>is</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="در مکتب‌ات چند کلاس هست؟"><mark>How many</mark> classrooms <mark>are there</mark> in your school?</Example>

## there or it?

Use **there** the first time you talk about something. After that, use **it** (one thing) or **they** (more than one).

<Example context="social" fa="یک کافه‌ی جدید در خیابانم هست. خیلی خوب است.">There's a new café on my street. <mark>It's</mark> really good.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="در بازار دو نانوایی هست. هر دو ارزان‌اند.">There are two bakeries in the market. <mark>They're</mark> both cheap.</Example>

More about *it* and *there* in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 4 (it or there?)</LessonRef>.

<MemoryHook title="Start with there, then count">
When something **exists**, start with **there**, then count: one (or uncountable) → **is**, many → **are**. For a question, jump the verb to the front: **Is there…? Are there…?**
</MemoryHook>
