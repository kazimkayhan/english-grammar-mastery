## Subject + Verb + Object

English uses a fixed order. The **verb** comes right after the **subject**, and the **object** comes after the verb. Place and time come at the end.

<FormTable caption="The basic order">

| Subject | Verb | Object | Place | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zahra | buys | bread | at the bakery | every morning. |
| I | study | English | at home | in the evening. |
| My father | drinks | tea | in the garden | after lunch. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="مادربزرگم هر شب کتاب می‌خواند."><mark>My grandmother reads</mark> a book every night.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او در یک بیمارستان کار می‌کند."><mark>She works</mark> in a hospital.</Example>

### The order shows who does what

In Persian, the little word **را** shows the object, so you can move words around: «احمد زهرا را دید» and «زهرا را احمد دید» mean the same thing. English has no *را*. The **order** tells us who does what:

<Example context="home" fa="گربه سگ را می‌بیند." note="the cat = subject (it sees)">The cat sees the dog.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="سگ گربه را می‌بیند." note="the dog = subject (it sees)">The dog sees the cat.</Example>

Same words, different order, different meaning. The subject is always **before** the verb.

### Keep the verb and the object together

Don't put words between the verb and its object. Words like *very much*, *well* and *every day* go **after** the object.

<Example context="shopping" fa="من چای را خیلی دوست دارم.">I <mark>like tea</mark> very much. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: I like very much tea.)</span></Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او انگلیسی را خیلی خوب صحبت می‌کند.">She <mark>speaks English</mark> very well. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: She speaks very well English.)</span></Example>

### Four basic patterns

Every statement starts with a subject and a verb. The **verb** decides what comes next.

<FormTable caption="Basic sentence patterns">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| subject + verb | Zahra **smiles**. The baby **sleeps** a lot. |
| subject + verb + object | Zahra **buys** *bread*. |
| subject + verb + complement | Zahra **is** *a doctor*. Zahra **is** *tired*. |
| subject + verb + place | Zahra **lives** *in Bamyan*. |

</FormTable>

Some verbs need a place after them. *She lives.* is not complete. Say *She lives **in Bamyan**.*

Two more patterns come later: *She gave **me** **a book**.* (level <LessonRef>A2, lesson 5, “Verbs with two objects: give me it / give it to me”</LessonRef>) and *They painted **the door** **red**.* (level <LessonRef>C1, lesson 23, “Advanced complementation: painted it red”</LessonRef>).

## Every sentence needs a subject

In Persian the verb ending shows the subject («می‌روم» = I go), so we often drop *من*. An English verb doesn't show this, so the **subject is always there**.

<Example context="health" fa="خسته‌ام."><mark>I'm</mark> tired. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: Am tired.)</span></Example>
<Example context="social" fa="در بانک کار می‌کنم."><mark>I</mark> work in a bank. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: Work in a bank.)</span></Example>

### it: a subject with no meaning

For weather and time there is no real subject, so English uses **it**. This *it* means nothing. It just fills the subject place.

<Example context="travel" fa="هوا گرم است."><mark>It's</mark> hot. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: Is hot.)</span></Example>
<Example context="news" fa="باران می‌بارد."><mark>It's</mark> raining.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ساعت پنج است. وقت رفتن است."><mark>It's</mark> five o'clock. Time to go home!</Example>

More uses of this *it* (distance, dates) are in <LessonRef>lesson 34 (it for time, weather & distance)</LessonRef>. For *There is a bank near here*, see <LessonRef>lesson 14 (there is / there are)</LessonRef>.

### One subject, not two

Say the subject **once**. Don't add a pronoun after a noun subject.

<Example context="home" fa="برادرم در کانادا زندگی می‌کند."><mark>My brother</mark> lives in Canada. <span class="text-muted-foreground">(not: My brother he lives in Canada.)</span></Example>

When two verbs share one subject and you join them with **and**, you don't need to say the subject again: *I get up **and** have breakfast.*

### Orders have no subject

An order or instruction (an **imperative**) has no subject: *Sit down. Open your books.* That is <LessonRef>lesson 20 (Imperatives & let's)</LessonRef>. All other sentences need a subject.

## Place before time

When you have both, the usual order is **place → time**:

<Example context="school" fa="ما ساعت هشت به مدرسه می‌رویم.">We go <mark>to school</mark> <mark>at eight</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="ما شب‌ها در خانه تلویزیون تماشا می‌کنیم.">We watch TV <mark>at home</mark> <mark>in the evening</mark>.</Example>

A time phrase can also open the sentence: *<mark>On Fridays</mark> we visit my grandparents.*

In Persian the time usually comes first («ساعت هشت به مدرسه می‌رویم»), so this order feels strange at first. Practice it until it sounds natural.

Where to put words like *always* and *never* is <LessonRef>lesson 19 (Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, never)</LessonRef>. The full order of *how, where* and *when* is level <LessonRef>B1, lesson 46 (Where adverbs go)</LessonRef>.

## Questions change the order

This lesson is about **statements**. Questions use a different order: *You are ready.* → *Are you ready?* That is <LessonRef>lesson 6 (be: questions & short answers)</LessonRef>, and questions with *do* are <LessonRef>lesson 18 (Present simple: negatives & questions)</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**ترتیب کلمات در انگلیسی:** فاعل + فعل + مفعول (Subject + Verb + Object). بعد از آن **مکان** و بعد **زمان** می‌آید.

- فارسی: «من هر روز صبح در نانوایی نان می‌خرم.» ← فعل آخر است.
- انگلیسی: I buy bread at the bakery every morning. ← فعل (buy) درست بعد از فاعل (I) آمده است.

**ترتیب، معنی را نشان می‌دهد:** در فارسی «را» مفعول را نشان می‌دهد، پس می‌توانیم کلمه‌ها را جابه‌جا کنیم. انگلیسی «را» ندارد و فقط **جای کلمه** نشان می‌دهد چه کسی کار را انجام می‌دهد: The cat sees the dog (گربه سگ را می‌بیند) با The dog sees the cat (سگ گربه را می‌بیند) فرق دارد.

**فعل و مفعول کنار هم:** در فارسی قید را قبل از فعل می‌گذاریم: «چای را **خیلی** دوست دارم». در انگلیسی بین فعل و مفعول چیزی نمی‌گذاریم: I like tea very much (نه I like very much tea و نه I very like tea).

**فاعل را حذف نکنید:** در فارسی شناسه‌ی فعل («می‌روم»، «خسته‌ام») فاعل را نشان می‌دهد، پس اغلب «من» را نمی‌گوییم. در انگلیسی فعل این اطلاعات را ندارد، پس **فاعل همیشه لازم است**: I'm tired. فقط جمله‌های امری فاعل ندارند: Sit down.

**it برای هوا و زمان:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «هوا سرد است» یا «ساعت پنج است». در انگلیسی به جای «هوا» و «ساعت» از **it** استفاده می‌کنیم: It's cold. / It's five o'clock. این it معنایی ندارد و فقط جای فاعل را پر می‌کند.

**یک فاعل، نه دو تا:** نگویید My brother he lives in Canada. فقط یک فاعل: My brother lives in Canada.

**مکان قبل از زمان:** در فارسی معمولاً زمان اول می‌آید («ساعت هشت سر کار می‌روم»)، اما در انگلیسی مکان قبل از زمان است: I go to work at 8 (نه I go at 8 to work). زمان را می‌توانیم اول جمله هم بگذاریم: At 8 I go to work.

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="My brother every day football plays." right="My brother plays football every day." why="Verb right after the subject; time at the end." />
<WrongRight wrong="I very like tea." right="I like tea very much." why="very much goes after the object, not before the verb." />
<WrongRight wrong="Is very hot today." right="It is very hot today." why="Weather sentences need the subject it." />
<WrongRight wrong="Air is cold today." right="It's cold today." why="For weather, English uses it, not air (Persian هوا)." />
<WrongRight wrong="My sister she is a nurse." right="My sister is a nurse." why="One subject only." />
<WrongRight wrong="I go at 8 to work." right="I go to work at 8." why="Place before time." />

<MemoryHook title="S-V-O, then P before T">
**S**ubject, **V**erb, **O**bject, then **P**lace before **T**ime (P comes before T in the alphabet, too). And never leave the S out: if there's no real subject, use **it**.
</MemoryHook>
