## One or more than one?

A **singular** noun is one thing: *a book*. A **plural** noun is two or more: *books*. In English the noun itself changes. Most nouns just add **-s**.

<Example context="school" fa="یک کتاب، سه کتاب">one <mark>book</mark>, three <mark>books</mark></Example>

A plural noun takes **are** (<LessonRef>lesson 5</LessonRef>): *The books **are** on the desk.*

## Spelling the plural

<FormTable caption="Spelling the plural">

| Ending | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most nouns | + **s** | book → book**s** · car → car**s** · pen → pen**s** |
| -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x | + **es** | bus → bus**es** · class → class**es** · dish → dish**es** · watch → watch**es** · box → box**es** |
| consonant + **y** | y → **ies** | city → cit**ies** · baby → bab**ies** · party → part**ies** |
| vowel + **y** | + **s** | key → key**s** · boy → boy**s** · day → day**s** |

</FormTable>

Why **-es**? After *s, sh, ch* and *x*, the plural adds an extra sound: *bus-es, box-es, watch-es*. The **e** helps us say it. (One common word ends in *z*: quiz → **quizzes**, with a double z.)

<Example context="travel" fa="بس‌ها هر ده دقیقه می‌آیند.">The <mark>buses</mark> come every ten minutes.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بشقاب‌ها داخل آشپزخانه‌اند.">The <mark>dishes</mark> are in the kitchen.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="کابل و مزار شریف شهرهای بزرگی هستند.">Kabul and Mazar-i-Sharif are big <mark>cities</mark>.</Example>

## Nouns ending in -f or -fe

Many nouns ending in **-f** or **-fe** change to **-ves**:

- knife → kni**ves** · wife → wi**ves** · leaf → lea**ves**

But some just add **-s**: roof → roof**s**, chief → chief**s**. If you're not sure, check a dictionary.

<Example context="home" fa="چاقوها داخل کشو هستند.">The <mark>knives</mark> are in the drawer.</Example>

## Nouns ending in -o

Most nouns ending in **-o** just add **-s**: photo → photo**s**, kilo → kilo**s**. A few common ones add **-es**:

- potato → potato**es** · tomato → tomato**es** · hero → hero**es**

<Example context="shopping" fa="دو کیلو گوجه و سه کیلو کچالو، لطفاً.">Two <mark>kilos</mark> of <mark>tomatoes</mark> and three <mark>kilos</mark> of <mark>potatoes</mark>, please.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="عکس‌ها مال سفرمان است.">The <mark>photos</mark> are from our trip.</Example>

## Irregular plurals

A few very common nouns don't add -s. Learn them as pairs:

<FormTable caption="Irregular plurals">

| One | More than one |
|---|---|
| man / woman | **men** / **women** |
| child | **children** |
| person | **people** |
| foot / tooth | **feet** / **teeth** |
| mouse (the animal) | **mice** |

</FormTable>

- **people** is already plural. It takes **are**: *The people here **are** very friendly.* Don't add -s. (*peoples* is a different word: it means nations.)
- **persons** is only for very formal notices. In everyday English, say **people**.

<Example context="social" fa="آن‌ها سه بچه دارند.">They have three <mark>children</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="بیست نفر در این شرکت کار می‌کنند.">Twenty <mark>people</mark> work in this company.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پاهایم درد می‌کند.">My <mark>feet</mark> hurt.</Example>

## Same form for one and many

A few nouns are the **same** in the singular and the plural:

- one **sheep**, two **sheep** · one **deer**, ten **deer** · one **aircraft**, five **aircraft**
- one **fish**, three **fish** (*fishes* exists, but *fish* is the usual plural)

<Example context="travel" fa="ماهی‌های این دریاچه خیلی بزرگ‌اند.">The <mark>fish</mark> in this lake are very big.</Example>

## After a number: always plural

After **two, three, four…**, the noun is **always plural**. After **one**, it is singular.

<Example context="home" fa="من دو برادر و یک خواهر دارم.">I have two <mark>brothers</mark> and one <mark>sister</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="سه بلیت، لطفاً.">Three <mark>tickets</mark>, please.</Example>

The same is true after **many** and **How many…?**: *How **many** brothers do you have?* (<LessonRef>lesson 7</LessonRef>).

If you **count** more than one, **add** the plural, every time. Your Persian ear says «دو کتاب»; your English mouth says **two books**.
