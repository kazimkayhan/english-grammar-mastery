## very and really: stronger

**very** and **really** make an adjective or an adverb **stronger**. They go **before** it.

<FormTable caption="very and really">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **very / really** + adjective | It's **very cold**. · She's **really nice**. |
| **very / really** + adverb | He drives **very slowly**. · You speak English **really well**. |
| a / an + **very** + adjective + noun | She's **a very good** teacher. |
| **not very** + adjective (weaker) | The hotel **wasn't very** clean. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="او خیلی مهربان است.">She's <mark>very</mark> kind.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مزار شریف واقعاً زیباست.">Mazar-i-Sharif is <mark>really</mark> beautiful.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او خیلی سریع کار می‌کند.">He works <mark>very</mark> quickly.</Example>

- **really** is very common in **speech**. **very** is good everywhere, and in **writing** it is the safer choice.
- **not very** makes the meaning **weaker**, and it sounds polite: *The film wasn't very good* is softer than *The film was bad*.
- Some adjectives already mean "very" (*wonderful, terrible*). With these, use **really**, not *very*: *It was really terrible.* <LessonRef>B2 lesson 37 (Gradable & non-gradable adjectives)</LessonRef> teaches these strong adjectives.

## With verbs: really or very much

**very** can't go before a verb. Use **really** before the verb, or **very much** after the verb and its object.

- *I **really like** this song.* ✓
- *I like this song **very much**.* ✓
- ~~I very like this song.~~ ✗ ~~I like very much this song.~~ ✗

<Example context="home" fa="من این آهنگ را خیلی دوست دارم.">I <mark>really like</mark> this song.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="از مهمانی خیلی لذت بردیم.">We enjoyed the party <mark>very much</mark>.</Example>

## too: more than is good

**too** means *more than is good, possible or needed*. It usually brings a **problem**: something is wrong, or you can't do something.

<Example context="shopping" fa="این موتر خیلی گران است؛ نمی‌توانم بخرمش.">This car is <mark>too</mark> expensive. I can't buy it.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="چای خیلی داغ است؛ صبر کن.">The tea is <mark>too</mark> hot. Wait a minute.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="آهسته‌تر! خیلی تند رانندگی می‌کنی.">Slow down! You're driving <mark>too</mark> fast.</Example>

Three things to remember:

- **too** + adjective usually comes after **be**: *The house is too expensive*, not ~~a too expensive house~~.
- Don't add *much*: *too expensive*, not ~~too much expensive~~.
- *I like it very much* = I like it a lot. But *I like it too much* means more than is good: it sounds like a problem.

More patterns come later: *too young to drive* and *big enough* in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 40 (too, enough, very)</LessonRef>; **too much / too many** + noun (*too many cars*) in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 31 (much, many, a lot of, a few, a little)</LessonRef>. And **too** at the **end** of a sentence means *also*: *I'm hungry **too**.* That is <LessonRef>A2 lesson 43 (already, yet, still, just; also, too, either)</LessonRef>.

## very or too?

<FormTable caption="very or too?">

| | Meaning | Result |
|---|---|---|
| The tea is **very** hot. | hotter than usual | Drink it carefully. (OK) |
| The tea is **too** hot. | more than is good | You can't drink it. (a problem) |
| The bag is **very** big. | bigger than usual | Good, everything fits. (OK) |
| The bag is **too** big. | bigger than I want | I can't carry it. (a problem) |

</FormTable>

<Persian>

**very / really = خیلی (معمولاً بدون مشکل):** قبل از صفت یا قید می‌آیند و آن را قوی‌تر می‌کنند:

- It's very cold. (خیلی سرد است.) / She's really nice. (واقعاً مهربان است.)
- He drives very slowly. (خیلی آهسته رانندگی می‌کند.)
- a very good teacher (یک معلمِ خیلی خوب). دقت کنید: در فارسی «خیلی خوب» بعد از اسم می‌آید، ولی در انگلیسی very و صفت هر دو **قبل از اسم** هستند.
- really بیشتر در **گفتار** می‌آید. در نوشتن، very بهتر است.
- **not very** معنی را ضعیف‌تر و مؤدبانه‌تر می‌کند: The film wasn't very good. (فیلم خیلی خوب نبود.)

**خیلی + فعل:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «خیلی دوستش دارم» و «خیلی» را قبل از فعل می‌گذاریم. در انگلیسی very قبل از فعل **نمی‌آید**:

- I **really** like it. یا I like it **very much**. (نه I very like it)
- very much بعد از مفعول می‌آید: I like this song very much. (نه I like very much this song)
- I like it too much یعنی «بیش از حد دوستش دارم» و مشکل دارد. برای «خیلی دوست دارم» بگویید very much.

**too = بیش از حد:** وقتی «خیلی» یک **مشکل** ایجاد می‌کند، از too استفاده کنید:

- It's too expensive. (خیلی گران است ← نمی‌توانم بخرم.)
- She's too young to drive. (برای رانندگی خیلی جوان است.)
- You're driving too fast. (خیلی تند می‌رانی ← خطرناک است.)
- بگویید too expensive، نه too much expensive.

**نکته‌ی مهم:** «خیلی» فارسی هم very است و هم too. از خودتان بپرسید: «آیا مشکلی هست؟» اگر بله ← too. اگر نه ← very. مثلاً «اینجا خیلی قشنگ است!» = It's very beautiful here (نه too beautiful، چون مشکلی نیست).

**too در آخر جمله** معنی دیگری دارد: «هم». I'm hungry too. (من هم گرسنه‌ام.)

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="Too = trouble">
**Too** and **trouble** both start with **t**: if there's trouble, say **too**. No trouble? Say **very** (or **really**). And for verbs: **really like**, or like it **very much**.
</MemoryHook>
