## in, on, at

These three small words answer the question **Where?** Each one shows the place in a different way.

<FormTable caption="in, on, at for place">

| | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | inside a space with limits | in the box · in the kitchen · in my bag · in Kabul · in Afghanistan |
| **on** | on a surface or a line | on the table · on the wall · on the floor · on the second floor · on Chicken Street |
| **at** | at a point, or a place for an activity | at the door · at the bus stop · at work · at school · at home |

</FormTable>

<DecisionTree id="in-on-at-place" />

## in: inside a space

Use **in** when something is inside a space: a box, a bag, a room, a building, a city or a country.

<Example context="home" fa="شکر در کابینت است.">The sugar is <mark>in</mark> the cupboard.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مادرم در آشپزخانه است.">My mother is <mark>in</mark> the kitchen.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="خواهرم در کانادا زندگی می‌کند.">My sister lives <mark>in</mark> Canada.</Example>

Some common phrases with **in**:

- **in bed**: *She's still in bed.* (no *the*)
- **in a photo, in a book, in the newspaper**: *Who's that man in the photo?*
- **in the car, in a taxi**: *The kids are in the car.*

## on: a surface or a line

Use **on** when something touches a surface: a table, a wall, the floor, a door. We also use **on** for a line, like a street or a river, and for the floors of a building.

<Example context="home" fa="«کلیدهایم کجاست؟» «روی میز هستند.»">“Where are my keys?” “They're <mark>on</mark> the table.”</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دفتر ما در طبقه‌ی سوم است.">Our office is <mark>on</mark> the third floor.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="مکتب را سمت چپتان می‌بینید.">You'll see the school <mark>on</mark> your left.</Example>

Some common phrases with **on**:

- **on the left / on the right**: *The bank is on the right.*
- **on page 25**: *There's a map on page 25.*
- **on the wall**: a picture touches the wall, so it is *on* the wall, not *in* it.

## at: a point, or a place where you do something

Use **at** for a point, a place you see as one spot: the door, the bus stop, the traffic lights, your desk. Use **at** also for a place where you do an activity: work, school, a party, a meeting.

<Example context="travel" fa="در ایستگاه بس منتظر بودم.">I was waiting <mark>at</mark> the bus stop.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او فعلا سر کار است.">She's <mark>at</mark> work right now.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="با همسرم در یک مهمانی آشنا شدم.">I met my wife <mark>at</mark> a party.</Example>

<FormTable caption="Common phrases with at (no the)">

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| **at home** | I'm at home this evening. |
| **at work** | Dad's at work. |
| **at school** | The children are at school. |

</FormTable>

Say **at home**, not ~~in home~~ or ~~at the home~~. (After *go* and *come* we say **go home**, with no preposition. That is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 44, *Prepositions of movement*</LessonRef>.)

**at or in?** Sometimes both are possible. **at** sees the place as a point on your day or your trip: *I'm at the station.* **in** means inside the building: *It's raining, so we're waiting in the station.*

## Transport: on or in?

<FormTable caption="Prepositions with transport">

| on | in |
|---|---|
| on the bus · on the train | in the car |
| on the plane · on a ship | in a taxi |
| on a bike · on a motorbike | |

</FormTable>

A simple guide: if you can stand up and walk around inside it, use **on**. For a car or a taxi, use **in**. For a bike, you sit on top of it, so use **on**.

<Example context="travel" fa="ما شش ساعت در بس بودیم.">We were <mark>on</mark> the bus for six hours.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="توانستی در هواپیما بخوابی؟">Did you sleep <mark>on</mark> the plane?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بچه‌ها داخل موتر هستند.">The kids are <mark>in</mark> the car.</Example>

You can also say how you travel with **by**: *by bus, by car*. That is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 45, *by, with, about; between, among; above, over*</LessonRef>.

## Other positions

<FormTable caption="Prepositions of place">

| Preposition | Persian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **under** | زیر | The cat is **under** the bed. |
| **next to** (= **beside**) | کنار | The bank is **next to** the bakery. |
| **between** A **and** B | بین … و … | The pharmacy is **between** the bank **and** the bakery. |
| **behind** | پشت | The garden is **behind** the house. |
| **in front of** | جلوی | There's a tree **in front of** my window. |
| **near** | نزدیکِ | We live **near** the station. |
| **opposite** | روبه‌روی | The hotel is **opposite** the park. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="shopping" fa="داروخانه بین بانک و نانوایی است.">The pharmacy is <mark>between</mark> the bank <mark>and</mark> the bakery.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل ما روبه‌روی پارک است.">Our hotel is <mark>opposite</mark> the park.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="یک دختر کوچک کنار او نشسته بود.">A little girl was sitting <mark>next to</mark> him.</Example>

Watch the small words:

- **next to** needs **to**. **in front of** needs **of**. **between** goes with **and**.
- **opposite** and **near** need nothing after them: *opposite the park*, *near the station*.
- **next to** = very close, with nothing in between. **near** = not far.
- **in front of** = close to the front of something, on the same side. **opposite** = facing it, on the other side of a street, a table or a room.
- **beside** means *next to*. Don't mix it up with **besides** (= also, as well).

After a preposition, use an object pronoun: *next to **me***, *behind **him***, *between **us*** (<LessonRef>lesson 13, *Object pronouns: me, him, her, us, them*</LessonRef>).

## Asking and answering: Where…?

Ask with **Where + be**. Answer with **be + preposition + place**.

<FormTable caption="Where questions">

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Where's** the bank? | **It's next to** the hotel. |
| **Where are** my glasses? | **They're on** the table. |
| **Is** the cat **under** the bed? | No, it **isn't**. It's **on** the sofa. |
| **Are** the kids **at** school? | Yes, they **are**. |

</FormTable>

For a negative, put **not** after *be*: *Your keys **aren't** in your bag. They're on the table.* To say what a place has, use **there is / there are**: *There's a café **next to** the hotel* (<LessonRef>lesson 14, *there is / there are*</LessonRef>).

<Example context="home" fa="«کیفم کجاست؟» «پشت در است.»">“Where's my bag?” “It's <mark>behind</mark> the door.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«ببخشید، داروخانه کجاست؟» «نزدیک ایستگاه است.»">“Excuse me, where's the pharmacy?” “It's <mark>near</mark> the station.”</Example>

## Addresses: in → on → at

Zoom in on the map: the city, then the street, then the house.

<FormTable caption="Prepositions in addresses">

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **in** + city / country | I live **in** Kabul. |
| **on** + street | I live **on** Chicken Street. |
| **at** + number + street | I live **at** 12 Chicken Street. |
| **on** + floor | My apartment is **on** the fourth floor. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="شرکت ما در خیابان ولیعصر است.">Our company is <mark>on</mark> Valiasr Street.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="آن‌ها در جاده‌ی دارالامان، پلاک ۲۴ زندگی می‌کنند.">They live <mark>at</mark> 24 Darulaman Road.</Example>

<UkNote>British speakers say **in** or **on** a street (*in Oxford Street*, *on the High Street*); Americans say **on**. **opposite** is very common in British English; Americans also say **across from**. In British English the **first floor** is one floor above the ground floor; in American English the first floor is at street level.</UkNote>

<Persian>

**فارسی برای بیشترِ جاها «در» یا «تو» می‌گوید؛ انگلیسی سه کلمه‌ی اصلی دارد:**

- **in** = داخلِ یک فضای بسته یا محدود: in the kitchen (در آشپزخانه)، in my bag (توی کیفم)، in Kabul، in Afghanistan، in bed (در رختخواب)
- **on** = روی یک سطح یا یک خط: on the table (روی میز)، on the wall (روی دیوار)، on the third floor (در طبقه‌ی سوم)، on the left (سمت چپ)
- **at** = در یک نقطه، یا جایی که در آن کاری انجام می‌دهیم: at the door (دمِ در)، at the bus stop (در ایستگاه اتوبوس)، at work (سرِ کار)، at school (در مدرسه)، at home (در خانه)

**چرا اشتباه می‌کنیم؟** چون در فارسی برای همه‌ی این جاها «در» یا «تو» می‌گوییم، فارسی‌زبان‌ها معمولاً همه‌جا in به کار می‌برند و می‌گویند I'm in home یا I'm in work. اما در انگلیسی برای خانه، کار و مدرسه **at** می‌آید: at home، at work، at school (بدون the).

**دیوار:** دیوار یک سطح است، پس تابلو on the wall است، نه in the wall.

**وسیله‌ی نقلیه:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «توی اتوبوس»، اما در انگلیسی: on the bus، on the train، on the plane، on a bike. برای ماشین و تاکسی: in the car، in a taxi. یک راه ساده برای به خاطر سپردن: اگر بتوانید داخلش بایستید و راه بروید ← on.

**کلمه‌های دیگر:**

- under = زیر
- next to (یا beside) = کنار
- between … and … = بین … و …
- behind = پشت
- in front of = جلوی
- near = نزدیکِ
- opposite = روبه‌روی

**کلمه‌های کوچک را جا نیندازید:** در فارسی با کسره‌ی اضافه می‌گوییم «کنارِ خانه» و «جلوی خانه». در انگلیسی به جای این کسره یک کلمه می‌آید: next **to** the house، in front **of** the house. «بین بانک **و** نانوایی» = between the bank **and** the bakery (نه with). اما «روبه‌روی پارک» = opposite the park (بدون to). near هم معمولاً بدون to می‌آید: near the station.

**جلوی یا روبه‌روی؟** in front of = جلوی چیزی، نزدیک به قسمتِ جلوی آن. opposite = روبه‌روی آن، در طرف دیگرِ خیابان یا میز، رو در رو.

**سؤال با Where:** Where's the bank? (بانک کجاست؟) ← It's next to the hotel. (کنار هتل است.) منفی: Your keys aren't in your bag. (کلیدهایت توی کیفت نیستند.)

**آدرس:** از بزرگ به کوچک: شهر با in، خیابان با on، پلاک و خیابان با at:
I live in Kabul. · I live on Chicken Street. · I live at 12 Chicken Street.

</Persian>
