## Five small linking words

<FormTable caption="and, but, or, because, so">

| Word | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | adds | I speak Persian **and** English. |
| **but** | contrasts | The flat is small **but** comfortable. |
| **or** | gives a choice | Do you want tea **or** coffee? |
| **because** | gives a reason (answers *Why?*) | I stayed home **because** I was sick. |
| **so** | gives a result | I was sick, **so** I stayed home. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="خسته بودم، پس زود خوابیدم.">I was tired, <mark>so</mark> I went to bed early.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دیر رسید چون ترافیک بود.">He was late <mark>because</mark> the traffic was bad.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل قدیمی ولی خیلی تمیز بود.">The hotel was old <mark>but</mark> very clean.</Example>

### or in negatives

In a negative sentence, use **or** to join two things: *I don't eat meat **or** fish.*

### Punctuation

We usually put a comma before **so** when it joins two sentences: *It was late, so I took a taxi.* When **because** comes first, put a comma after the reason: *Because it was late, I took a taxi.*

## also

**also** goes **before the main verb** but **after be**:

<Example context="school" fa="او فرانسوی هم صحبت می‌کند.">She <mark>also</mark> speaks French.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او دکتر هم هست.">He's <mark>also</mark> a doctor.</Example>

<Persian>

**پنج کلمه‌ی ربط:**

- **and** = و
- **but** = ولی، اما
- **or** = یا (در جمله‌ی منفی هم or: I don't eat meat or fish = گوشت و ماهی نمی‌خورم.)
- **because** = چون، زیرا (دلیل)
- **so** = پس، برای همین (نتیجه)

**اشتباه بسیار رایج:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «**چون** خسته بودم، **پس** رفتم خانه». در انگلیسی فقط **یکی** از آن‌ها:

- Because I was tired, I went home.
- I was tired, so I went home.

**also** (هم، همچنین) قبل از فعل اصلی می‌آید ولی بعد از be: She also speaks French. / He's also a doctor.

</Persian>

## Common mistakes

<WrongRight wrong="Although it was cold, but we went out." right="Although it was cold, we went out." why="The same idea: one linking word is enough. (You'll meet although in B1.)" />
<WrongRight wrong="I like also football." right="I also like football." why="also before the main verb." />

<MemoryHook title="One bridge, one river">
A linking word is a **bridge** between two ideas. One river needs **one bridge**: because **or** so, never both.
</MemoryHook>
