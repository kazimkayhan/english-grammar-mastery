## Five small linking words

Linking words join two words or two sentences. Each one has its own job.

<FormTable caption="and, but, or, because, so">

| Word | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | adds | I speak Persian **and** English. |
| **but** | shows a contrast | The flat is small **but** comfortable. |
| **or** | gives a choice | Do you want tea **or** coffee? |
| **because** | gives a reason (answers *Why?*) | I stayed home **because** I was sick. |
| **so** | gives a result | I was sick, **so** I stayed home. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="خسته بودم، برای همین زود خوابیدم.">I was tired, <mark>so</mark> I went to bed early.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دیر رسید چون ترافیک سنگین بود.">He was late <mark>because</mark> the traffic was bad.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل قدیمی ولی خیلی تمیز بود.">The hotel was old <mark>but</mark> very clean.</Example>

## and: adding

**and** joins two things of the same kind: two nouns, two adjectives, two verbs, or two sentences.

<Example context="shopping" fa="نان و شیر خریدم.">I bought bread <mark>and</mark> milk.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="او قدبلند و مهربان است.">She's tall <mark>and</mark> friendly.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بیدار شدم و دوش گرفتم.">I got up <mark>and</mark> had a shower.</Example>

- **Same subject?** You don't need to say it again after *and*: *I got up and had a shower.* (*I got up and I had a shower* is also correct.) The same is true for *but* and *or*: *She's tired but happy.*
- **Lists:** put commas between the items and **and** before the last one: *We need eggs, rice **and** oil.* (More about commas in lists in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 47, *Commas in lists & capital letters*</LessonRef>.)

## but: a contrast

**but** joins two ideas that are different, or a second idea that is a surprise after the first.

<Example context="shopping" fa="موتر قدیمی است، ولی خیلی مطمئن است.">It's an old car, <mark>but</mark> it's very reliable.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="چای دوست دارم، ولی قهوه دوست ندارم.">I like tea, <mark>but</mark> I don't like coffee.</Example>

We often put a comma before **but** when it joins two sentences. With two adjectives there is usually no comma: *small but nice*.

## or: a choice

**or** gives two (or more) possible things. We use it a lot in questions.

<Example context="shopping" fa="پیراهن قرمز را می‌خواهی یا آبی را؟">Do you want the red shirt <mark>or</mark> the blue shirt?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="می‌توانیم پیاده برویم یا با بس.">We can walk <mark>or</mark> take a bus.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="او دانشجوست یا معلم؟">Is he a student <mark>or</mark> a teacher?</Example>

### or after a negative

After a negative verb (*don't, doesn't, isn't, can't*…), use **or** to mean *not this and not that*:

<Example context="health" fa="گوشت و ماهی نمی‌خورم.">I don't eat meat <mark>or</mark> fish.</Example>

(*neither … nor* and *either … or* come in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 41, *Paired conjunctions: both … and, either … or*</LessonRef>.)

## because: the reason

**because** gives the reason. It answers the question **Why?** After *because*, use a full sentence: **subject + verb**.

<Example context="work" fa="تکسی گرفتم چون دیر شده بود.">I took a taxi <mark>because</mark> it was late.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«چرا دیر کردی؟» «چون بس را از دست دادم.»">“Why are you late?” “<mark>Because</mark> I missed the bus.”</Example>

- In speech, a short answer to *Why?* can start with *Because*: *Because I missed the bus.*
- **because** usually comes in the middle. It can also come first; then put a comma after the reason: *Because it was late, I took a taxi.*
- Don't put a noun alone after *because*: ~~because the traffic~~. Say *because the traffic was bad*. (*because of the traffic* comes later, in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 43, *Preposition or conjunction? during/while, despite/although*</LessonRef>.)

## so: the result

**so** gives the result. The reason comes **first**, then *so* + the result. We usually put a comma before **so**.

<Example context="health" fa="سرما خورده بودم، برای همین خانه ماندم.">I had a cold, <mark>so</mark> I stayed home.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="فروشگاه بسته بود، پس به خانه برگشتیم.">The shop was closed, <mark>so</mark> we went back home.</Example>

## because or so: never both

The same story can be told two ways. Choose **one** linking word.

<FormTable caption="Same story, two ways">

| | Order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | result + **because** + reason | I went home **because** I was tired. |
| **so** | reason + **so** + result | I was tired, **so** I went home. |
| ✗ both | — | ~~Because I was tired, so I went home.~~ |

</FormTable>

More ways to give reasons and results (*as, since, so that*) come in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 29, *Time & reason clauses: when, before, because*</LessonRef>, and <LessonRef>B1 lesson 30, *Purpose, reason & result*</LessonRef>.

## also

**also** means *as well*. It adds one more fact. It has a fixed place in the sentence:

<FormTable caption="Where also goes">

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| **before** the main verb | She **also** speaks French. |
| **after** be | He's **also** a doctor. |
| **after** can (and other helping verbs) | I can **also** cook. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="school" fa="او فرانسوی هم صحبت می‌کند.">She <mark>also</mark> speaks French.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او پزشک هم هست.">He's <mark>also</mark> a doctor.</Example>

- In writing, don't start a sentence with *Also*. Put it before the verb: *It **also** costs more.*
- At the end of a sentence, people say **too**: *She speaks French **too**.* In negative sentences, use **either**: *I don't like it **either**.* Both are in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 43, *already, yet, still, just; also, too, either*</LessonRef>.

<Persian>

**پنج کلمه‌ی ربط:**

- **and** = و
- **but** = ولی، اما
- **or** = یا
- **because** = چون، زیرا (دلیل)
- **so** = پس، برای همین (نتیجه)

**اشتباه بسیار رایج:** در فارسی طبیعی است بگوییم «**چون** خسته بودم، **برای همین** رفتم خانه». اما در انگلیسی فقط **یکی** از این دو کلمه می‌آید:

- Because I was tired, I went home. (یا: I went home because I was tired.)
- I was tired, so I went home.

**ترتیب:** بعد از because دلیل می‌آید؛ بعد از so نتیجه. پس با so اول دلیل را می‌گوییم و بعد نتیجه را.

**بعد از because یک جمله‌ی کامل لازم است:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «به خاطر ترافیک دیر کردم». در انگلیسی نمی‌شود گفت because the traffic؛ باید فاعل و فعل بیاید: I was late because the traffic was bad.

**«یا» در جمله‌ی منفی:** در فارسی می‌گوییم «چای **و** قهوه نمی‌خورم». در انگلیسی بعد از فعل منفی **or** می‌آید: I don't drink tea **or** coffee.

**تکرار نکردن فاعل:** اگر فاعل هر دو قسمت یکی است، لازم نیست آن را تکرار کنیم: I got up and had a shower. (بیدار شدم و دوش گرفتم.)

**also (هم، همچنین):** در فارسی «هم» بعد از کلمه می‌آید («فرانسوی هم صحبت می‌کند»)، برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها also را بعد از فعل می‌گذارند: She speaks also French ✗. در انگلیسی also **قبل از فعل اصلی** می‌آید، اما **بعد از be و can**:

- She also speaks French. (او فرانسوی هم صحبت می‌کند.)
- He's also a doctor. (او پزشک هم هست.)
- I can also cook. (آشپزی هم بلدم.)

در جمله‌ی منفی به جای also از either استفاده می‌کنیم (درس ۴۳ سطح A2).

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="One bridge, one river">
A linking word is a **bridge** between two ideas. One river needs **one bridge**: because **or** so, never both.
</MemoryHook>
