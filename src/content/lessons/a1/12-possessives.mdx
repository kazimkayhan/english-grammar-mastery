## Possessive words

Each subject pronoun (<LessonRef>lesson 4</LessonRef>) has a **possessive** word. It goes **before a noun** and shows who something belongs to.

<FormTable caption="Subject → possessive">

| Subject | Possessive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **my** | This is **my** bag. |
| you | **your** | Is this **your** phone? |
| he | **his** | Karim and **his** wife |
| she | **her** | Maryam and **her** husband |
| it | **its** | The cat and **its** toy |
| we | **our** | **Our** house is small. |
| they | **their** | **Their** children are at school. |

</FormTable>

- The possessive word never changes for a plural noun: *my book*, *my books*.
- **your** is for one person or many people, like *you* (<LessonRef>lesson 4</LessonRef>).

<Example context="social" fa="این برادرم و همسرش هستند.">This is my brother and <mark>his</mark> wife.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="این خانم مدیر ماست. دفترش طبقه‌ی دوم است.">This is <mark>our</mark> manager. <mark>Her</mark> office is on the second floor.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="بلیت‌ها و پاسپورت‌هایتان، لطفاً.">Your tickets and <mark>your</mark> passports, please.</Example>

## his or her? Look at the owner

The choice depends on the **owner**, not on the thing:

- *Karim and **his** mother* (Karim is a man → **his**)
- *Maryam and **her** husband* (Maryam is a woman → **her**)
- *The company and **its** workers* (a company is a thing → **its**)

<Example context="home" fa="زهرا با پدرش زندگی می‌کند.">Zahra lives with <mark>her</mark> father.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="کابل به‌خاطر بازارش معروف است.">Kabul is famous for <mark>its</mark> bazaar.</Example>

## No a or the with my, your…

**my, your, his…** already tell us *which one*. So we don't add **a** or **the**:

- **my** car (not ~~the my car~~) · **her** brother (not ~~a her brother~~)

To say *one of my friends*, English says *a friend of mine*. That is in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 33 (mine, yours… and whose)</LessonRef>.

## Family, body parts and clothes

We use **my, your, his…** for people in our family, for parts of the **body** and for **clothes**. English doesn't use *the* here.

<Example context="health" fa="دست‌هایم را می‌شویم.">I'm washing <mark>my</mark> hands.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مادرم دکتر است."><mark>My</mark> mother is a doctor.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="کتت را بپوش. سرد است.">Put on <mark>your</mark> coat. It's cold.</Example>

## 's for people

To say something belongs to a **person** (or an animal), add **'s** to the owner. The **owner comes first**, then the thing:

<FormTable caption="Owner + 's + thing">

| Owner | + 's | Thing |
|---|---|---|
| Zahra | **'s** | phone |
| my brother | **'s** | car |
| Mr. Karimi | **'s** | office |
| the dog | **'s** | bed |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="این گوشیِ زهرا است.">This is <mark>Zahra's</mark> phone.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="ماشینِ پدرم در پارکینگ است."><mark>My father's</mark> car is in the parking lot.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="دفترِ آقای کریمی طبقه‌ی سوم است."><mark>Mr. Karimi's</mark> office is on the third floor.</Example>

Remember from <LessonRef>lesson 5</LessonRef>: **'s** can mean three things. Context tells you which:

- **'s = is**: *She's a nurse.*
- **'s = has**: *She's got a car.* (<LessonRef>lesson 16</LessonRef>)
- **'s = possessive**: *Zahra's car.* (this lesson)

For a plural noun that ends in -s, add only an apostrophe: *my parents' house*. That and words like *children's* are in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 33</LessonRef>. For things, English often uses **of** instead: *the end **of** the movie*. That is in <LessonRef>B2 lesson 35 (Possession 3: 's, of or noun + noun?)</LessonRef>.

## its or it's?

These two sound the same, but they are different words:

- **its** = belonging to *it*: *The company changed **its** name.* (no apostrophe)
- **it's** = *it is*: ***It's** a good company.*

<Example context="shopping" fa="این کافه عالی است. قهوه‌اش بهترین است.">I love this café. <mark>Its</mark> coffee is the best. <mark>It's</mark> very cheap, too.</Example>

A test: can you say **it is**? Then write **it's**. If not, write **its**.

The same thing happens with **their** and **there**. They sound the same:

- **their** = belonging to them: *They love **their** jobs.*
- **there** = in that place: *Put it **there**.*
- **they're** = they are (<LessonRef>lesson 5</LessonRef>): ***They're** at home.*

<Persian>

**صفت‌های ملکی:** در فارسی مالکیت را بیشتر با ضمیرهای چسبیده نشان می‌دهیم که **بعد** از اسم می‌آیند: «کتاب**م**»، «خانه‌**مان**». در انگلیسی کلمه‌ی ملکی **قبل** از اسم می‌آید:

- my (ـَم) ← my book (کتابم)
- your (ـَت / ـِتان) ← your phone (گوشی‌ات / گوشی‌تان)
- his (ـَش، برای مرد) ← his car (ماشینش)
- her (ـَش، برای زن) ← her bag (کیفش)
- its (ـَش، برای چیز یا حیوان) ← its name (اسمش)
- our (ـِمان) ← our house (خانه‌مان)
- their (ـِشان) ← their children (بچه‌هایشان)

**his یا her؟** در فارسی «ـَش» برای مرد و زن یکی است، برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها his و her را قاطی می‌کنند. به **صاحب** نگاه کنید، نه به چیز: «مریم و شوهرش» = Maryam and **her** husband (چون مریم زن است). «کریم و مادرش» = Karim and **his** mother (چون کریم مرد است).

**a و the با my نمی‌آیند:** my car (نه the my car).

**اعضای بدن و لباس:** در انگلیسی با my, your… می‌آیند: I'm washing my hands (دست‌هایم را می‌شویم).

**'s برای آدم‌ها:** «ماشینِ زهرا» در انگلیسی برعکس است: **Zahra's car**. اول صاحب، بعد s'، بعد چیز. «ماشینِ برادرم» = my brother's car. نگویید the car of Zahra.

**its و it's:** its یعنی «مالِ آن» و آپاستروف ندارد. it's یعنی it is. اگر بتوانید جایش it is بگذارید، it's بنویسید.

**their و there:** هم‌صدا هستند. their = مالِ آن‌ها، there = آنجا.

</Persian>

<MemoryHook title="Owner first, then the thing">
English puts the **owner first**: **Zahra's** car, **my** car. Persian puts it last (ماشینِ **زهرا**). Flip it!
</MemoryHook>
