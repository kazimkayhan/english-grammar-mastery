## Making a question

With **be**, a question is easy: move **am / is / are** to the front, before the subject. We **don't** use *do*.

<FormTable caption="be: statement → question">

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| I **am** late. | **Am** I late? |
| She **is** a nurse. | **Is** she a nurse? |
| It **is** expensive. | **Is** it expensive? |
| You **are** ready. | **Are** you ready? |
| They **are** at home. | **Are** they at home? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="ببخشید، این بسِ فرودگاه است؟"><mark>Is</mark> this the bus to the airport?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="آماده‌ای؟ جلسه دارد شروع می‌شود."><mark>Are</mark> you ready? The meeting is starting.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این کفش‌ها نو هستند؟"><mark>Are</mark> these shoes new?</Example>

### Long subjects

The subject can be more than one word. Keep it together, after *am / is / are*:

<FormTable caption="Questions with a longer subject">

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| **Your sister** is a student. | Is **your sister** a student? |
| **Ahmad and Zahra** are at home. | Are **Ahmad and Zahra** at home? |
| **The new teacher** is from Canada. | Is **the new teacher** from Canada? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="health" fa="دکتر امروز در مطب است؟">Is <mark>the doctor</mark> in today?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="بچه‌ها خواب هستند؟">Are <mark>the children</mark> asleep?</Example>

### Am I…? and Aren't I…?

*Am I…?* is the normal question with *I*: *Am I late? Am I in the right room?*

For a **negative** question with *I*, people say **Aren't I…?** There is no “amn't”. (*Am I not…?* is correct but very formal.)

<Example context="work" fa="مگر اسم من در فهرست نیست؟"><mark>Aren't I</mark> on the list?</Example>

Other negative questions (*Isn't it beautiful? Aren't you hungry?*) and how to answer them come at level <LessonRef>A2, lesson 2 (Negatives & answering negative questions)</LessonRef>.

## Short answers

In conversation we rarely answer with the whole sentence. We use a **short answer**: **Yes / No + pronoun + be**.

<FormTable caption="Short answers">

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Am I late? | Yes, you **are**. | No, you **aren't**. / No, you'**re not**. |
| Are you a student? | Yes, I **am**. | No, I'**m not**. |
| Is he busy? | Yes, he **is**. | No, he **isn't**. / No, he'**s not**. |
| Is it far? | Yes, it **is**. | No, it **isn't**. / No, it'**s not**. |
| Are they here? | Yes, they **are**. | No, they **aren't**. / No, they'**re not**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«تشنه‌ای؟» «نه، نیستم.»">“Are you thirsty?” “<mark>No, I'm not.</mark>”</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="«زهرا مریض است؟» «بله، هست.»">“Is Zahra sick?” “<mark>Yes, she is.</mark>”</Example>

Four rules:

- A **yes** short answer is never shortened: *Yes, I am* (not ~~Yes, I'm~~), *Yes, she is* (not ~~Yes, she's~~).
- A **no** answer is usually shortened: *No, she isn't* or *No, she's not*. With *I* there is only one: *No, I'm not.*
- **Yes** goes with a positive verb, **No** with a negative verb: *Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.* Never ~~Yes, I'm not~~.
- Just *Yes.* or *No.* is correct, but a short answer sounds friendlier. You can add more: *Yes, I am. I'm from Herat.*

### Who is in the answer?

The short answer uses a **pronoun**, not the noun. Think about who answers:

<FormTable caption="The pronoun in the short answer">

| The question is about… | The answer uses… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| you (one person) | **I** | “Are you OK?” “Yes, **I** am.” |
| you (more than one) | **we** | “Are you students?” “Yes, **we** are.” |
| I | **you** | “Am I late?” “No, **you**'re not.” |
| your brother · your mother | **he** · **she** | “Is your brother here?” “Yes, **he** is.” |
| the hotel · this · that | **it** | “Is this your bag?” “Yes, **it** is.” |
| the children · these · those | **they** | “Are these your keys?” “No, **they** aren't.” |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="«این چمدان شماست؟» «بله، هست. ممنون!»">“Is this your suitcase?” “Yes, <mark>it is</mark>. Thanks!”</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«بچه‌ها، آماده‌اید؟» «بله، آماده‌ایم!»">“Are you ready, class?” “Yes, <mark>we are</mark>!”</Example>

## Questions in speech

In conversation, people sometimes keep the statement order and only raise their voice: *You're from Afghanistan?* They do this to check something or to show surprise. That comes at level <LessonRef>B1, lesson 1 (Subject & object questions)</LessonRef>. For a normal question, and always in writing, change the order: ***Are you** from Afghanistan?*

**سؤال با be:** در فارسی برای سؤال فقط لحن صدا را عوض می‌کنیم («خسته‌ای؟») یا «آیا» را اول جمله می‌گذاریم. در انگلیسی **جای فعل عوض می‌شود**: am / is / are به اول جمله، قبل از فاعل، می‌رود.

- You are tired. ← Are you tired?
- Your sister is at home. ← Is your sister at home?

اگر فاعل چند کلمه است، همه‌ی آن را با هم بعد از فعل بگذارید: Is **the new teacher** from Canada?

**do نگذارید:** در سؤال با be از do استفاده نمی‌کنیم: Are you hungry? (نه Do you hungry? و نه Do you are hungry?).

**Aren't I:** برای «مگر من … نیستم؟» می‌گوییم Aren't I…? شکل amn't وجود ندارد.

**جواب کوتاه (short answer):** به جای تکرار کل جمله، می‌گوییم: Yes / No + ضمیر + am / is / are. در فارسی می‌گوییم «بله، هست» یا «نه، نیستم»، اما در انگلیسی ضمیر را هم می‌آوریم:

- Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
- Yes, he is. / No, he isn't. (یا No, he's not.)
- Yes, they are. / No, they aren't. (یا No, they're not.)

فقط Yes یا No هم درست است، اما جواب کوتاه دوستانه‌تر است.

**نکته‌ها:**

- جواب مثبت کوتاه را هرگز خلاصه نکنید: Yes, I am (نه Yes, I'm).
- در جواب از **ضمیر** استفاده کنید، نه اسم: Is your brother here? ← Yes, he is (نه Yes, my brother is). برای this و that از it، و برای these و those از they استفاده کنید.
- you در سؤال ← I (یک نفر) یا we (چند نفر) در جواب.
- Yes با فعل مثبت و No با فعل منفی می‌آید: No, I'm not (نه Yes, I'm not).

**سؤال با لحن:** در گفتار، انگلیسی‌زبان‌ها هم گاهی فقط با لحن می‌پرسند (You're from Afghanistan?)، برای تعجب یا اطمینان. اما سؤال معمولی، و همیشه در نوشتن، با جابه‌جایی فعل است: Are you from Afghanistan?

In a question, **be jumps to the front**: You are → **Are** you? In the answer, it **comes back** after a pronoun: Yes, I **am**. No, I**'m not**.
