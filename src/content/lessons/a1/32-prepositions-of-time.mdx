## at, on, in

These three small words answer the question **When?** Each one goes with a different kind of time.

<FormTable caption="at, on, in for time">

| | Use it with | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | clock times, short moments | at 5:30 · at noon · at midnight · at lunchtime · **at night** |
| **on** | days, dates, special days | on Monday · on May 5 · on my birthday · **on Friday morning** |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, parts of the day | in May · in 2024 · in (the) summer · **in the morning / afternoon / evening** |

</FormTable>

<DecisionTree id="in-on-at-time" />

<Example context="work" fa="جلسه دوشنبه ساعت ۱۰ صبح است.">The meeting is <mark>on</mark> Monday <mark>at</mark> 10 <mark>in</mark> the morning.</Example>

## at: clock times and short moments

Use **at** for a clock time and for other short points in the day.

- **at** + clock time: *at 7 o'clock, at 5:30, at a quarter past six*
- **at noon, at midnight, at lunchtime**
- **at night**: *I can't sleep at night.*
- **at** + a holiday (the few days around it): *at Christmas, at Easter*

<Example context="health" fa="ساعت ۹ صبح نوبت دکتر دارم.">I have a doctor's appointment <mark>at</mark> 9 a.m.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="شب‌ها سر و صدای ترافیک بیدارم نگه می‌دارد.">The traffic noise keeps me awake <mark>at</mark> night.</Example>

## on: days and dates

Use **on** for a day of the week, a date, or a special day.

- **on** + day: *on Monday, on Saturday*
- **on** + day + **s** for something that happens every week: *I don't work **on Fridays**.*
- **on** + date: *on May 5, on July 1st* (how to say dates is in <LessonRef>lesson 35, *Numbers, dates, times & prices*</LessonRef>)
- **on** + special day: *on my birthday, on New Year's Day, on Christmas Day*
- **on** + day + part of the day: *on Friday morning, on Sunday night*

<Example context="school" fa="کلاس‌ها روز شنبه شروع می‌شوند.">Classes start <mark>on</mark> Saturday.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="روز تولدم با دوستانم بیرون رفتیم.">We went out with friends <mark>on</mark> my birthday.</Example>

Notice: **at** Christmas (the holiday days), but **on** Christmas Day (one day).

## in: longer periods

Use **in** for longer periods: months, years, seasons, and the parts of the day.

- **in** + month: *in May, in October*
- **in** + year: *in 2024, in 1998*
- **in** + season: *in summer* or *in the summer* (both are correct)
- **in the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening**

<Example context="travel" fa="ما تابستان به شیراز می‌رویم.">We're going to Shiraz <mark>in</mark> the summer.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="صبح‌ها چه ساعتی بیدار می‌شوی؟">What time do you get up <mark>in</mark> the morning?</Example>

**Be careful:** *in the morning*, but **at night**. And when you name the day, use **on**: *in the morning* → *on Monday morning*.

### in + a length of time

**in** + a length of time can mean *that much time from now*:

<Example context="travel" fa="قطار ده دقیقه‌ی دیگر حرکت می‌کند.">The train leaves <mark>in</mark> ten minutes.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="نتایج دو هفته‌ی دیگر اعلام می‌شود.">We'll have the results <mark>in</mark> two weeks.</Example>

Don't say ~~after ten minutes~~ for this meaning. Say **in ten minutes**.

## the weekend

This course uses American English: **on the weekend**. For something that happens every week, say **on weekends**.

<Example context="social" fa="آخر هفته رفتم خرید.">I went shopping <mark>on the weekend</mark>.</Example>

<UkNote>British speakers say **at the weekend** and **at weekends**. Americans also say **Monday through Friday** for *from Monday to Friday* (Friday is included).</UkNote>

## No preposition

Use **no** preposition before **next, last, this, every**, and with **today, tomorrow, yesterday, tonight**.

<FormTable caption="Time words with no preposition">

| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right |
|---|---|
| in next week | **next week** |
| in last summer | **last summer** |
| in this morning | **this morning** |
| on every Monday | **every Monday** |
| on tomorrow | **tomorrow** · **tomorrow morning** |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="هفته‌ی بعد با تو تماس می‌گیرم.">I'll call you <mark>next week</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="هر روز پیاده‌روی می‌کنم.">I walk <mark>every day</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="امروز صبح چه کار کردی؟">What did you do <mark>this morning</mark>?</Example>

You will use *yesterday, last week* and *ago* a lot with the past simple. They are in <LessonRef>lesson 28, *Past time phrases: yesterday, last week, ago*</LessonRef>.

## from … to / until

Use **from** for the start and **to** or **until** for the end.

- **from** … **to**: *from 9 to 5, from Monday to Friday*
- **until** = up to that time, and then it stops: *The shop is open until 10 p.m.*
- **till** means the same as *until*. It is more informal.

<Example context="shopping" fa="فروشگاه از ۹ صبح تا ۶ عصر باز است.">The shop is open <mark>from</mark> 9 a.m. <mark>to</mark> 6 p.m.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="تا ساعت ۶ منتظرت می‌مانم.">I'll wait for you <mark>until</mark> six o'clock.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="تا نیمه‌شب در مهمانی ماندیم.">We stayed at the party <mark>until</mark> midnight.</Example>

More time prepositions come later: **before / after** + noun (*before lunch*) in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 45, *by, with, about; between, among; above, over*</LessonRef>; and **for, since, during** and **by or until** in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 7, *for, since, ago, during; by or until?*</LessonRef>

## Questions and negatives

The question word tells you which preposition to use in the answer.

<FormTable caption="Asking about time">

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| **What time** does the film start? | **At** 8 o'clock. |
| **What day** is the party? | **On** Friday. |
| **When** is your birthday? | **On** May 5. / **In** May. |
| **When** do you get up? | **At** 7, **in** the morning. |

</FormTable>

The preposition stays the same in negatives: *I **don't** work **on** Fridays.* · *The bank **isn't** open **on** Sundays.*

Put the time at the end, after the place: *I go to work **at 8**.* (place before time, <LessonRef>lesson 3</LessonRef>)

<WrongRight wrong="I'll see you in this evening." right="I'll see you this evening." why="No preposition before this / next / last / every." />

<MemoryHook title="The time pyramid">
Picture a pyramid. The wide **bottom** is **in** (years, months, seasons: long times). The **middle** is **on** (days and dates). The sharp **top** is **at** (a clock time: one small point).
