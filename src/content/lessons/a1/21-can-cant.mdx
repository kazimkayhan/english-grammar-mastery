## The form of can

**can** is the same for every subject. After it, use the **base form** of the verb (*swim, drive, speak*).

<FormTable caption="can: all subjects (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)">

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | subject + **can** + verb | She **can** swim. |
| **−** | subject + **can't** / **cannot** + verb | She **can't** swim. |
| **?** | **Can** + subject + verb? | **Can** she swim? |
| **short answers** | Yes, + subject + **can**. / No, + subject + **can't**. | Yes, she **can**. / No, she **can't**. |
| **wh- question** | question word + **can** + subject + verb? | What **can** you see? |

</FormTable>

- **can't** is the normal negative in speech. **cannot** is the full form, written as **one word**.
- In a question, **can** moves before the subject. There is no *do*.

<Example context="travel" fa="کجا می‌توانم بلیت بخرم؟">Where <mark>can</mark> I buy a ticket?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="«می‌توانی آشپزی کنی؟» «بله، می‌توانم.»">“<mark>Can</mark> you cook?” “Yes, I <mark>can</mark>.”</Example>

**Saying it:** in a normal sentence, *can* is short and weak: *I can swim* sounds like "I k'n swim". *can't* is strong and clear: /kænt/. In a short answer (*Yes, I can.*), *can* is strong too.

<UkNote>In British English, **can't** is said /kɑːnt/, with the vowel of *car*.</UkNote>

## Ability: what you are able to do

Use **can** for skills and things you are able to do now.

<Example context="work" fa="می‌توانم به سه زبان صحبت کنم.">I <mark>can</mark> speak three languages.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="خواهرم رانندگی بلد نیست.">My sister <mark>can't</mark> drive.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="«می‌توانی شنا کنی؟» «نه، نمی‌توانم.»">“<mark>Can</mark> you swim?” “No, I <mark>can't</mark>.”</Example>

We often use **can** with *see, hear, smell*:

<Example context="travel" fa="از اینجا دریا را می‌بینم!">I <mark>can see</mark> the sea from here!</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="صدایم را می‌شنوی؟"><mark>Can</mark> you <mark>hear</mark> me?</Example>

**can't** is also a polite way to say no to an invitation: *Sorry, I <mark>can't</mark> come on Friday. I'm working.*

## Permission: is it OK?

- **Ask** for permission: **Can I** …?
- **Give** permission: You **can** …
- **Say no**: You **can't** … (= it is not allowed)

<Example context="travel" fa="ببخشید، می‌توانم اینجا بنشینم؟">Excuse me, <mark>can I</mark> sit here?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="می‌توانی از تلفن من استفاده کنی.">You <mark>can</mark> use my phone.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="اینجا نمی‌توانید پارک کنید. پارک ممنوع است.">You <mark>can't</mark> park here. It's a no-parking zone.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="می‌توانم فردا زودتر بروم؟"><mark>Can I</mark> leave early tomorrow?</Example>

Answers: *Yes, of course. / Sure. / Sorry, you can't.*

## Requests and offers

- **Request** (you ask someone to do something): **Can you** …, please?
- **Offer** (you want to help): **Can I** …?

<Example context="shopping" fa="می‌شود کمکم کنید، لطفاً؟"><mark>Can you</mark> help me, please?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="کمکی از دستم برمی‌آید؟ (فروشنده)"><mark>Can I</mark> help you?</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="می‌توانی در را باز کنی، لطفاً؟"><mark>Can you</mark> open the door, please?</Example>

*Can you…?* is friendly and normal. *Could you…?* is a little more polite: that comes in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 21</LessonRef>, could, be able to; asking for things & permission. You can also use **can** to suggest something: *We <mark>can</mark> take a taxi.* (More ways to suggest come in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 23</LessonRef>.)

## How modal verbs work

**can** is a **modal verb**. The modal verbs are *can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must*. They all follow the same three rules:

<FormTable caption="The three rules of modal verbs">

| Rule | Right | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **no -s** with he / she / it | She **can** swim. | ~~She cans swim.~~ |
| 2. **no to** after them, just the base form | I can **swim**. | ~~I can to swim.~~ ~~I can swimming.~~ |
| 3. **no do** in questions and negatives | **Can** you swim? I **can't** swim. | ~~Do you can swim?~~ ~~I don't can swim.~~ |

</FormTable>

One more thing: **can** has no other forms. There is no *to can*, *canning* or *will can*. When you decide now about a plan, people often use *can*: *I can help you tomorrow. Sorry, I can't come on Friday.* For a new skill in the future, we use **will be able to**: *She'll be able to walk soon.* That comes in <LessonRef>A2 lesson 21</LessonRef>, together with the past form *could*.
