## have and has

<FormTable caption="have: present">

| | Positive | Negative | Question | Short answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | I **have** a car. | I **don't have** a car. | **Do** you **have** a car? | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. |
| he / she / it | She **has** a car. | She **doesn't have** a car. | **Does** she **have** a car? | Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**. |

</FormTable>

Two things to remember:

- **he, she, it** → **has**. Everyone else → **have**.
- After **do / does / don't / doesn't**, always use **have**: *Does she **have**…?* *She doesn't **have**…* (never ~~does she has~~).

## What do we use have for?

<FormTable caption="Uses of have">

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Things you own | I **have** a car. We **have** a big apartment. |
| Family and friends | She **has** two brothers. I **have** a lot of friends. |
| Body and looks | He **has** blue eyes. My sister **has** long hair. |
| Illness and pain | I **have** a headache. He **has** a cold. |
| Plans and events | I **have** a meeting at ten. We **have** an English class today. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="دو خواهر و یک برادر دارم.">I <mark>have</mark> two sisters and a brother.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او یک دفتر بزرگ دارد.">She <mark>has</mark> a big office.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="امروز سرم درد می‌کند.">I <mark>have</mark> a headache today.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="ساعت ده امتحان داریم.">We <mark>have</mark> an exam at ten.</Example>

**have or there is?** Both can say what a place has: *My city **has** a big park.* = ***There's** a big park in my city.* (<LessonRef>lesson 14</LessonRef>)

## Negatives and questions

Make the negative with **don't / doesn't + have**. Make questions with **Do / Does + subject + have**.

<Example context="shopping" fa="کیسه داری؟"><mark>Do</mark> you <mark>have</mark> a bag?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او موتر ندارد. با بس می‌آید.">She <mark>doesn't have</mark> a car. She takes the bus.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="اتاق خالی دارید؟ بله، داریم.">“<mark>Do</mark> you <mark>have</mark> a free room?” “Yes, we <mark>do</mark>.”</Example>

With a question word: *How many children **do** they **have**?* · *What **does** she **have** in her bag?*

## have got

**have got** means the same as **have** for things you own, family, looks, illness and plans. It is very common in British speech. In American English, *have* is more common, but you will hear *I've got* too.

<FormTable caption="have got: present">

| | Positive | Negative | Question | Short answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | I**'ve got** a car. | I **haven't got** a car. | **Have** you **got** a car? | Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven't**. |
| he / she / it | She**'s got** a car. | She **hasn't got** a car. | **Has** she **got** a car? | Yes, she **has**. / No, she **hasn't**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="یک گربه‌ی جدید دارم.">I<mark>'ve got</mark> a new cat.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="سرما خورده. امروز نمی‌آید.">He<mark>'s got</mark> a cold. He isn't coming today.</Example>

Rules for **have got**:

- Here **'s = has**: *She's got a car* = *She **has** got a car.* (In *She's a nurse*, 's = is. See <LessonRef>lesson 5</LessonRef>.)
- Questions and negatives **don't use do**: ***Have** you got…?* not ~~Do you have got…?~~
- Short answers have **no got**: *Yes, I **have**.* not ~~Yes, I have got.~~
- **have got** is only for the **present**. For the past, use **had**: *I had a red bike.* (<LessonRef>lesson 27</LessonRef>)
- In everyday English, use the short form with *got*: *I've got a car.* With *have* alone, say the full form: *I have a car.*
- For things that happen often, *have* is more usual: *I often **have** headaches.*

<UkNote>British speakers often say **have got**: *I've got two brothers. Have you got a minute? I haven't got time.* Americans usually say **have**: *Do you have a minute? I don't have time.*</UkNote>

## have for actions

**have** also means *eat, drink, take* or *enjoy* in many fixed expressions. These are **actions**, not things you own.

- **have** breakfast / lunch / dinner / a coffee
- **have** a shower / a bath / a break / a rest
- **have** a party / a meeting / a good time

<Example context="social" fa="بیا با هم ناهار بخوریم.">Let's <mark>have</mark> lunch together.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="مکاکالاً ساعت هفت صبحانه می‌خورم.">I usually <mark>have</mark> breakfast at seven.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ساعت یازده استراحت می‌کنیم و قهوه می‌خوریم.">We <mark>have</mark> a break and a coffee at eleven.</Example>

For actions:

- **Never** use *have got*: *I have breakfast*, not ~~I've got breakfast~~.
- Questions and negatives use **do**: ***Do** you **have** lunch at work?*
- You can say *I'm having lunch* (happening now, <LessonRef>lesson 22</LessonRef>). But for things you own, don't use *-ing*: *I **have** a car*, not ~~I'm having a car~~. (More in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 10</LessonRef>, State verbs & action verbs.)

In American English, people usually **take** a shower. *Have a shower* is more British.
