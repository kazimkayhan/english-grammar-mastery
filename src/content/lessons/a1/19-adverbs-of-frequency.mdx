## From always to never

These adverbs answer the question **How often?** They go from 100% (every time) to 0% (no time).

<FormTable caption="Adverbs of frequency">

| Adverb | How often | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **always** | 100% | I **always** have breakfast. |
| **usually** | about 90% | She **usually** walks to work. |
| **often** | about 70% | We **often** eat out. |
| **sometimes** | about 50% | They **sometimes** watch films. |
| **rarely** / **hardly ever** | about 5–10% | He **rarely** drinks coffee. We **hardly ever** go out. |
| **never** | 0% | I **never** smoke. |

</FormTable>

The numbers are only a guide. **rarely** and **hardly ever** both mean "almost never".

<Example context="home" fa="همیشه قبل از خواب کتاب می‌خوانم.">I <mark>always</mark> read before bed.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او معمولاً ساعت هشت به محل کار می‌رسد.">She <mark>usually</mark> gets to work at eight.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="گاهی آنلاین خرید می‌کنیم.">We <mark>sometimes</mark> shop online.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پدرم به‌ندرت مریض می‌شود.">My father <mark>rarely</mark> gets ill.</Example>

## Where do they go?

The position depends on the verb.

<FormTable caption="Position of frequency adverbs">

| Verb | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a main verb (work, eat, go…) | **before** the verb | I **often** walk to work. |
| be (am / is / are) | **after** be | She is **never** late. |
| a helper (don't, doesn't) | **after** the helper | I don't **usually** drive. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="travel" fa="معمولاً با قطار سفر می‌کنم.">I <mark>usually</mark> travel by train.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کلاس همیشه ساعت نه شروع می‌شود.">The class <mark>always</mark> starts at nine.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="ما هیچ‌وقت دیر نمی‌کنیم.">We are <mark>never</mark> late.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او همیشه سرش شلوغ است.">He's <mark>always</mark> busy.</Example>

Don't put the adverb between the verb and the rest: say *I **often** go to the cinema*, not ~~I go often to the cinema~~. And don't put it before *be*: say *She **is usually** late*, not ~~She usually is late~~.

## Questions

In a question, the adverb goes **after the subject**. For main verbs, use **Do / Does** (lesson 18).

<FormTable caption="Questions with frequency adverbs">

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| main verb | Do / Does + subject + **adverb** + verb | **Do** you **often** eat out? |
| be | Am / Is / Are + subject + **adverb** | **Is** he **always** late? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="اغلب بیرون غذا می‌خوری؟">Do you <mark>often</mark> eat out?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کلاس همیشه شلوغ است؟">Is the class <mark>always</mark> full?</Example>

## Negatives

Put the adverb **after don't / doesn't** or **after isn't / aren't**.

<Example context="travel" fa="معمولاً رانندگی نمی‌کنم. با مترو می‌روم.">I <mark>don't usually</mark> drive. I take the metro.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="او زیاد دیر نمی‌کند.">She <mark>isn't often</mark> late.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="همیشه صبحانه نمی‌خورم.">I <mark>don't always</mark> have breakfast.</Example>

**never** is already negative, so it never goes with *not*:

<Example context="health" fa="هیچ‌وقت سیگار نمی‌کشم.">I <mark>never</mark> smoke.</Example>

Say *I never smoke*, not ~~I don't never smoke~~. If you want *don't*, use **ever**: *I don't ever smoke.* (same meaning)

## At the start of a sentence

**sometimes**, **usually** and **often** can also go at the **start** of a sentence. This gives them a little more weight.

<Example context="work" fa="گاهی پیاده سر کار می‌روم.">I <mark>sometimes</mark> walk to work. / <mark>Sometimes</mark> I walk to work.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="معمولاً او با اتوبوس می‌رود.">She <mark>usually</mark> takes the bus. / <mark>Usually</mark> she takes the bus.</Example>

**always** and **never** don't go at the start of a normal sentence: not ~~Always I travel with my sister~~. (You can start an instruction with them: *Always lock the door. Never open this box.* That is lesson 20, Imperatives & let's.)

**sometimes** and **often** can also go at the **end**: *I see him at the gym sometimes.* For all the positions of adverbs, see B1 lesson 46, Where adverbs go.

## How often?

Ask **How often…?** to find out the frequency. With a main verb, use **do / does**. With be, use **am / is / are**.

<Example context="health" fa="هر چند وقت یک بار ورزش می‌کنی؟"><mark>How often</mark> do you exercise?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="اتوبوس هر چند وقت یک بار می‌آید؟"><mark>How often</mark> does the bus come?</Example>

To answer, use a frequency adverb (*Usually. / Not often. / Never.*) or a **time phrase**:

<FormTable caption="Time phrases for frequency">

| How many times | Phrase | Example |
|---|---|---|
| each time | **every** day / week / Monday | I call my mum **every day**. |
| 1 time | **once** a week / a month | We eat out **once a week**. |
| 2 times | **twice** a month / a year | I see the dentist **twice a year**. |
| 3, 4, 5… times | **three times** a week | She swims **three times a week**. |
| with a gap | **every two** weeks / **every ten** minutes | The bus comes **every ten minutes**. |

</FormTable>

- For one and two, we usually say **once** and **twice**. From three, say **three times**, **four times**…
- The **a** in *once a week* means "each". Don't say ~~once in week~~.
- **every** is followed by a singular noun: *every **day***, not ~~every days~~. And *every day* is two words here.
- These time phrases usually go at the **end** of the sentence: *I go to the gym **three times a week**.*

<Example context="health" fa="هفته‌ای سه بار به باشگاه می‌روم.">I go to the gym <mark>three times a week</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="ماهی یک بار به پدربزرگ و مادربزرگم سر می‌زنم.">I visit my grandparents <mark>once a month</mark>.</Example>

<Persian>

**قیدهای تکرار (adverbs of frequency):** نشان می‌دهند کاری چند وقت یک بار انجام می‌شود: always (همیشه)، usually (معمولاً)، often (اغلب، زیاد)، sometimes (گاهی)، rarely / hardly ever (به‌ندرت، تقریباً هیچ‌وقت)، never (هرگز، هیچ‌وقت).

**جای آن‌ها در جمله:**

- **قبل از فعل اصلی:** I often walk to work. (نه I walk often to work)
- **بعد از am / is / are:** She is never late. (نه She never is late)
- **بعد از فعل کمکی don't / doesn't:** I don't usually drive. (معمولاً رانندگی نمی‌کنم.)
- **در سؤال، بعد از فاعل:** Do you often eat out? / Is he always late?

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟** در فارسی قید را قبل از کل گزاره می‌گذاریم: «من **همیشه** خسته‌ام». چون «ـَم» در «خسته‌ام» همان فعل است، قید قبل از فعل می‌آید و فارسی‌زبان می‌گوید He always is tired. اما در انگلیسی قید **بعد از** be می‌آید: He is always tired.

**never و منفی:** در فارسی «هیچ‌وقت» را با فعل منفی می‌گوییم: «هیچ‌وقت قهوه **نمی‌**خورم». برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند I don't never drink coffee. اما در انگلیسی never خودش منفی است و فعل باید مثبت باشد: **I never drink coffee.** (اگر don't می‌خواهید، ever بگذارید: I don't ever drink coffee.)

**اول جمله:** sometimes، usually و often می‌توانند اول جمله هم بیایند: Sometimes I walk to work. اما always و never در جمله‌ی معمولی اول جمله نمی‌آیند. (در جمله‌های امری می‌آیند: Always lock the door. درس ۲۰)

**How often…?** یعنی «هر چند وقت یک بار؟». جواب با عبارت‌های زمانی:

- every day (هر روز)، every Monday (هر دوشنبه)
- once a week (هفته‌ای یک بار)، twice a month (ماهی دو بار)، three times a year (سالی سه بار)
- every two weeks (هر دو هفته یک بار)

در فارسی می‌گوییم «هفته‌ای دو بار»، پس فارسی‌زبان‌ها گاهی می‌گویند two times in week. در انگلیسی بگویید **twice a week**: برای یک و دو بار معمولاً once و twice می‌گوییم، و a یعنی «هر». این عبارت‌ها معمولاً **آخر جمله** می‌آیند: I go to the gym three times a week.

</Persian>

<WrongRight wrong="Do often you eat out?" right="Do you often eat out?" why="In a question, the adverb goes after the subject." />

<MemoryHook title="Sit before the verb, stand after be">
Frequency adverbs **sit before** the action verb but **stand after** be: I **often walk** · I **am often** tired. And **never** works alone: it never needs *not*.
</MemoryHook>
