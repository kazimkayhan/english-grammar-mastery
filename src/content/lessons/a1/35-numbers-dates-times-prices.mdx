## Cardinal numbers

Cardinal numbers count things: *one, two, twenty-one, a hundred*.

<FormTable caption="Saying and writing numbers">

| Number | Say / write | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 13 / 30 | thir**teen** / **thir**ty | Don't mix them up: stress -TEEN, but THIR-ty |
| 21 | twenty**-**one | a hyphen between tens and units (21–99) |
| 100 | **a** hundred / **one** hundred | |
| 350 | three hundred **and** fifty · three hundred fifty | Americans often leave out **and** |
| 2,000 | two **thousand** | no -s: two thousand (not thousands) |
| 10,000 | ten thousand | a **comma** groups thousands |
| 1,000,000 | a / one **million** | |

</FormTable>

- **hundred, thousand, million** take no -s after a number: *three hundred people, two thousand books*.
- After a number, the noun is plural: *three book**s***, *twenty dollar**s*** (lesson 8, *Plural nouns*).
- A decimal uses a **point**: 2.5 = *two point five*. (Decimals, fractions and *hundreds of* come in B1 lesson 42, *Numbers & quantities: hundreds of, two-thirds, 50%*.)

<Example context="school" fa="مدرسه‌ی ما سیصد دانش‌آموز دارد.">Our school has <mark>three hundred</mark> students.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این ماشین بیست و پنج هزار دلار است.">This car is <mark>twenty-five thousand</mark> dollars.</Example>

### Saying 0 and phone numbers

- **0** is **zero**. In phone numbers and in times like 7:05, people often say **oh** (like the letter O).
- Say phone numbers **one number at a time**: 0912 345 6789 = *oh nine one two, three four five, six seven eight nine*.

<Example context="work" fa="«شماره‌ات چند است؟» «صفر نهصد و دوازده...»">“What's your number?” “It's <mark>oh nine one two</mark>, three four five…”</Example>

## Ordinal numbers

Ordinal numbers show order: *first, second, third*. Most of them are the number + **-th**.

<FormTable caption="Ordinals">

| Number | Ordinal | Number | Ordinal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **first** | 9th | **ninth** (no e) |
| 2nd | **second** | 12th | **twelfth** (ve → f) |
| 3rd | **third** | 13th | thirteenth |
| 4th | fourth | 20th | twent**ieth** (y → ie) |
| 5th | **fifth** (ve → f) | 21st | twenty-**first** |
| 8th | **eighth** (one t) | 100th | hundredth |

</FormTable>

Spelling rules:

- **first, second, third** are special. All the others add **-th**.
- **five → fifth**, **twelve → twelfth**, **eight → eighth**, **nine → ninth**.
- Tens change **-y** to **-ie**: *twenty → twentieth, thirty → thirtieth*.
- In big numbers, only the **last** word changes: *twenty-**first**, thirty-**second**, forty-**third***.
- Short forms: 1**st**, 2**nd**, 3**rd**, 4**th**… but 11**th**, 12**th**, 13**th**, then 21**st**, 22**nd**, 23**rd**.

Use ordinals for dates, floors, birthdays and order. We usually put **the** or **my / your** before them:

<Example context="work" fa="دفتر من در طبقه‌ی دوم است.">My office is on <mark>the second</mark> floor.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="فردا تولد سی سالگی اوست.">Tomorrow is her <mark>thirtieth</mark> birthday.</Example>

## Dates and years

<FormTable caption="Dates">

| | US (this course) | UK |
|---|---|---|
| Write | May 5, 2024 · 5/5/2024 | 5 May 2024 · 5/5/2024 |
| Say | May fifth | the fifth of May |
| Watch out! | 3/4 = **March 4** (month first) | 3/4 = **3 April** (day first) |

</FormTable>

You **write** a date with a number (*May 21*), but you **say** it with an ordinal: *May twenty-**first***, or *the twenty-**first** of May*.

<FormTable caption="Years">

| Year | Say |
|---|---|
| 1998 | nineteen ninety-eight |
| 1900 | nineteen hundred |
| 2000 | two thousand |
| 2005 | two thousand (and) five |
| 2024 | twenty twenty-four (or two thousand (and) twenty-four) |

</FormTable>

Most years are said in **two pairs**: 19 + 98 = *nineteen ninety-eight*.

<Example context="social" fa="تولد من بیست و یکم مه است.">My birthday is on May <mark>twenty-first</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="او در سال ۱۹۹۸ به دنیا آمد.">She was born in <mark>nineteen ninety-eight</mark>.</Example>

**Asking:** *What's the date today?* → *It's May 5.* (lesson 34, *it for time, weather & distance*) · *When's your birthday?* → *It's on May 21.* Remember: **on** + date, **in** + year (lesson 32, *Prepositions of time: at, on, in*).

## Telling the time

There are two ways. The **easy way** says the hour, then the minutes. The **traditional way** uses **past** and **to**.

<FormTable caption="Times">

| Time | Easy way | Traditional way |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00 | seven | seven **o'clock** |
| 7:05 | seven oh five | five **past** seven |
| 7:15 | seven fifteen | a quarter **past** seven |
| 7:30 | seven thirty | **half past** seven |
| 7:45 | seven forty-five | a quarter **to** eight |
| 7:50 | seven fifty | ten **to** eight |

</FormTable>

- **past** = after the hour (minutes 1 to 30). **to** = before the next hour (minutes 31 to 59).
- In American English you also hear **after** for *past* (*a quarter after seven*) and, in speech, **of** for *to* (*a quarter of eight*).
- **o'clock** is only for full hours: *seven o'clock* (not ~~seven thirty o'clock~~).
- The easy way works for every minute: 6:23 = *six twenty-three*.
- **a.m.** = from midnight to noon (morning). **p.m.** = from noon to midnight: *9 a.m., 6 p.m.*

<Example context="travel" fa="قطار ساعت یک ربع به نه حرکت می‌کند.">The train leaves at <mark>a quarter to nine</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="«ساعت چند است؟» «ده و نیم است.»">“What time is it?” “It's <mark>half past ten</mark>.”</Example>

Ask **What time is it?** Answer with **It's** + time. For an event, use **at**: *What time does the bank open? **At** nine.*

## Prices

**Asking:** *How much **is** it?* (one thing) · *How much **are** they?* (two or more) · *How much does it cost?*

<FormTable caption="Saying prices">

| Price | Short way | Long way |
|---|---|---|
| $3.50 | three fifty | three dollars **and** fifty cents |
| $12.99 | twelve ninety-nine | twelve dollars and ninety-nine cents |
| $20 | twenty dollars | |
| 50¢ | fifty cents | |
| 50,000 tomans | fifty thousand tomans | |

</FormTable>

- The short way (*three fifty*) is the most common in shops.
- With an exact amount, say the money word: *twenty dollars*.
- Write the **$** before the number, but **say** *dollars* after it: $5 = *five dollars* (not ~~dollars five~~).

<Example context="shopping" fa="«این چند است؟» «دوازده دلار و پنجاه سنت.»">“How much is it?” “It's <mark>twelve fifty</mark>.” ($12.50)</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«این کفش‌ها چند است؟» «چهل دلار.»">“How much <mark>are</mark> these shoes?” “They're <mark>forty dollars</mark>.”</Example>

<UkNote>British speakers usually say **and** after hundred (*three hundred **and** fifty*). They write the day first (*5 May*) and say *the fifth of May*. They use **past** and **to** (not *after* or *of*). For money: £3.50 = *three fifty* or *three pounds fifty*; 50p = *fifty pence* or *fifty p*.</UkNote>

<MemoryHook title="Point for parts, comma for thousands">
English uses a **point** for small parts (**2.5**, **$3.50**) and a **comma** for big groups (**10,000**). Think: the **point** is small, like a part; the **comma** has a tail, like a long number.
</MemoryHook>
