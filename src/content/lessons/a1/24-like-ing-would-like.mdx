## Feelings in general: like, love, enjoy, hate + -ing

Use these verbs to talk about what you like and don't like **in general**. After them, use the **-ing** form of a verb. (For -ing spelling, see lesson 22.)

<FormTable caption="Verb + -ing">

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **love** | like very much | She **loves** cooking Persian food. |
| **enjoy** | it makes me happy | We **enjoy** traveling. |
| **like** | it's nice for me | I **like** walking in the park. |
| **don't like** | it isn't nice for me | He **doesn't like** getting up early. |
| **hate** | really don't like | They **hate** waiting in traffic. |

</FormTable>

These are present simple verbs, so remember the **-s** with he / she / it: *She **likes** reading.* (lesson 17)

<Example context="home" fa="مادرم عاشق آشپزی است.">My mother <mark>loves cooking</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="از سفر با قطار لذت می‌برم.">I <mark>enjoy traveling</mark> by train.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="از دیر رسیدن متنفرم.">I <mark>hate being</mark> late.</Example>

You can also use a **noun** or a **pronoun**: *I like tea. Do you like jazz? I love it!*

### like, love, hate + to

**like**, **love** and **hate** can also take **to** + verb. The meaning is almost the same:

<Example context="home" fa="دوست دارم شب‌ها کتاب بخوانم.">I like <mark>to read</mark> at night. = I like <mark>reading</mark> at night.</Example>

There are small differences between *like doing* and *like to do*, but you don't need them now. (They come in C1 lesson 24, -ing or to: the subtle cases.)

### enjoy: only -ing

**enjoy** takes only **-ing**, never *to*: *I enjoy cooking*, not ~~I enjoy to cook~~.

**enjoy** also needs an **object** (something after it): *I enjoyed **the party**. I enjoyed **it**.* Not ~~I enjoyed.~~

<Example context="social" fa="مهمانی عالی بود. خیلی لذت بردم.">The party was great. I really <mark>enjoyed it</mark>.</Example>

## Negatives and questions

**like, love, enjoy, hate** are normal verbs, so the negative and the question use **do / does** (lesson 18).

<FormTable caption="Negatives and questions">

| | Example | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| **−** | I **don't like** waiting. She **doesn't enjoy** shopping. | |
| **?** | **Do you like** cooking? | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. |
| **?** | **Does** he **enjoy** his job? | Yes, he **does**. / No, he **doesn't**. |
| **wh-** | **What do you like** doing on weekends? | I like going to the cinema. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«آخر هفته‌ها دوست داری چه کار کنی؟» «دوست دارم کوهنوردی کنم.»">“What <mark>do you like doing</mark> on weekends?” “I <mark>love hiking</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="او از خرید کردن خوشش نمی‌آید.">She <mark>doesn't like shopping</mark>.</Example>

## Wants now: would like

**would like** means *want*, but it is more **polite**. The short form is **'d like**: *I'd like, you'd like, she'd like*.

<FormTable caption="would like">

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| I'd like + **noun** | I'd like **a coffee**, please. |
| I'd like + **to** + verb | I'd like **to book** a table. |
| **Would you like** + noun? (offer) | Would you like **some tea**? |
| **Would you like to** + verb? (invitation) | Would you like **to come** to dinner? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="shopping" fa="یک کیلو سیب می‌خواستم، لطفاً.">I<mark>'d like</mark> a kilo of apples, please.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="می‌خواستم با مدیر صحبت کنم.">I<mark>'d like to speak</mark> to the manager, please.</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="چای میل دارید؟"><mark>Would you like</mark> some tea?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="دوست داری جمعه بیایی شام؟"><mark>Would you like to come</mark> to dinner on Friday?</Example>

- After **would like**, use **to** + verb, not -ing: *I'd like **to go***, not ~~I'd like going~~.
- In a shop or a restaurant, *I want…* can sound too direct. **I'd like…, please** is polite and normal.
- The same pattern works with **love** and **hate**: *I'd love to visit Japan.* (a strong wish) *I'd hate to be late.* (I really don't want to be late.)

### Answering an offer or an invitation

<FormTable caption="Answers to Would you like…?">

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Would you like some tea? | **Yes, please.** | **No, thank you.** / No, thanks. |
| Would you like to come to dinner? | **I'd love to!** / Yes, I'd like that. | **I'd love to, but I can't.** I'm busy. |

</FormTable>

For the negative, you can say **I don't want to…** or **I wouldn't like to…**: *I don't want to go out tonight. I wouldn't like to live in a big city.*

## I like or I'd like?

This is the big difference in this lesson:

<FormTable caption="like vs would like">

| | I like… | I'd like… |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | in general, always | now, a wish, a polite request |
| Example | I **like** coffee. (I drink it often.) | I**'d like** a coffee. (Can I have one now?) |
| Question | **Do you like** coffee? | **Would you like** a coffee? |
| Answer | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. | Yes, **please**. / No, **thank you**. |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="«قهوه دوست داری؟» «بله، دوست دارم.» «یک قهوه میل داری؟» «بله، لطفاً.»">“<mark>Do you like</mark> coffee?” “Yes, I <mark>do</mark>.” “<mark>Would you like</mark> one now?” “Yes, <mark>please</mark>.”</Example>

Don't answer *Would you like a coffee?* with ~~Yes, I like~~. Say **Yes, please**.

**like / love / enjoy / hate + ing:** برای احساس کلی درباره‌ی یک کار:

- I love cooking. (عاشق آشپزی‌ام.) I like reading. (خواندن را دوست دارم.) I hate waiting. (از انتظار متنفرم.)
- like، love و hate با to هم می‌آیند و معنی تقریباً یکی است: I like to read = I like reading.
- **enjoy فقط با ing می‌آید:** I enjoy cooking (نه to cook). enjoy همیشه مفعول می‌خواهد: I enjoyed **it**. (نه فقط I enjoyed.)
- در فارسی می‌گوییم «**از** شنا لذت می‌برم»، اما در انگلیسی بعد از enjoy حرف اضافه نمی‌آید: I enjoy swimming (نه I enjoy from swimming).

**منفی و سؤالی:** با do / does: I don't like waiting. / Do you like cooking? جواب: Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

**would like = خواستن، مؤدبانه.** در مغازه و رستوران به جای I want بگویید I'd like. در فارسی هم برای ادب می‌گوییم «یک قهوه **می‌خواستم**» به جای «می‌خواهم».

- I'd like + اسم: I'd like a coffee. (یک قهوه می‌خواستم.)
- I'd like + to + فعل: I'd like to book a table. (می‌خواستم یک میز رزرو کنم.) نه I'd like booking.

**پیشنهاد و دعوت:** Would you like some tea? (چای میل دارید؟) Would you like to come to dinner? (دوست داری برای شام بیایی؟) جواب: Yes, please. / No, thank you. / I'd love to! (با کمال میل!)

**چرا فارسی‌زبان‌ها اشتباه می‌کنند؟** در فارسی «دوست دارم» هم یعنی I like و هم یعنی I'd like:

- «قهوه دوست دارم» (کلی، همیشه) = **I like** coffee.
- «دوست دارم یک قهوه بخورم» (الان) = **I'd like** a coffee.

پس همیشه بپرسید: کلی است یا الان؟ کلی ← like. الان و مؤدبانه ← would like. همچنین در فارسی بعد از «دوست دارم» فعل صرف‌شده می‌آید («دوست دارم **بروم**»)، برای همین فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند I like go. در انگلیسی بعد از like باید ing یا to بیاید: I like going / I like to go.

<MemoryHook title="The little d is polite">
The little **'d** in **I'd like** turns a wish into a **polite request**. Without it, **I like** is just a feeling in general. Add **please** and you sound perfect.
</MemoryHook>
