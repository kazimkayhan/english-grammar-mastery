## Capital letters

English has two sizes of letter: **capital letters** (A, B, C) and **small letters** (a, b, c). Persian has only one size, so this is new for you. Use a capital letter:

<FormTable caption="When to use a capital letter">

| Use a capital for… | Examples |
|---|---|
| the first word of a sentence | **T**he coffee is hot. **I**s it ready? |
| the pronoun **I** | My friend and **I** study together. |
| names of people | **R**eza, **S**ara **A**hmadi |
| cities and countries | **M**ashhad, **I**ran, **C**anada |
| streets, rivers and mountains | **V**aliasr **S**treet, the **K**arun **R**iver, **M**ount **D**amavand |
| languages | **E**nglish, **P**ersian, **A**rabic |
| days and months | **M**onday, **J**une |
| a title before a name | **M**r. **K**arimi, **D**r. **A**hmadi |

</FormTable>

<Example context="social" fa="اسم من مریم است. من اهل غزنی هستم.">My name is Maryam. I'm from Ghazni.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="ما در دانشگاه انگلیسی و فرانسوی می‌خوانیم.">We study English and French at university.</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="هتل ما در خیابان ولیعصر است.">Our hotel is on Valiasr Street.</Example>

### Only I is always a capital

The pronoun **I** is always a capital letter, also in the short form **I'm**. Other words for *me* are small: *Can you help **me**? This is **my** bag.*

<Example context="home" fa="من و خواهرم با هم آشپزی می‌کنیم.">My sister and <mark>I</mark> cook together.</Example>

### Small letters

Ordinary words are small, also in the middle of a sentence. Use a capital letter only for a name (or for *I*).

- ordinary nouns: *I live in a big **city**. Our **teacher** is nice.*
- seasons: ***spring, summer, fall, winter*** (but days and months take capitals)
- words after a comma: *Hi, **how** are you?*

<Example context="travel" fa="تابستان در هرات گرم است.">Herat is hot in <mark>summer</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="جلسه دوشنبه است، نه سه‌شنبه.">The meeting is on <mark>Monday</mark>, not <mark>Tuesday</mark>.</Example>

## Periods, question marks and exclamation points

Every sentence ends with a mark. The mark tells the reader what kind of sentence it is.

<FormTable caption="End marks">

| Mark | Name | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **.** | period | a statement or an instruction | I'm hungry. Sit down, please. |
| **?** | question mark | a question | Are you hungry? Where is the station? |
| **!** | exclamation point | a strong feeling: surprise, anger, excitement | Great! Stop! What a nice day! |

</FormTable>

- After the end mark, the **next sentence** starts with a capital letter: *I'm tired. **I**s it late?*
- Every question ends with **?**, even a very short one: *Really? Ready?*
- Use **!** only for strong feelings. In formal writing, like an email to your boss, use it very rarely.
- In American English, short titles take a period, too: *Mr. Karimi, Mrs. Hosseini, Dr. Ahmadi.*

<Example context="work" fa="جلسه ساعت ده است. می‌توانی بیایی؟">The meeting is at ten. Can you come?</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="دکتر احمدی امروز اینجاست؟">Is Dr. Ahmadi here today?</Example>
<Example context="social" fa="وای! تولدت مبارک!">Wow! Happy birthday!</Example>

<UkNote>In British English the period is called a **full stop**, and the exclamation point is an **exclamation mark**. British English often writes titles with no period: **Mr** Karimi, **Dr** Ahmadi.</UkNote>

- بعد از علامت پایان جمله، جمله‌ی بعدی با حرف بزرگ شروع می‌شود.

**عنوان‌ها:** در انگلیسی آمریکایی بعد از Mr.، Mrs. و Dr. نقطه می‌گذاریم. در انگلیسی بریتانیایی معمولاً بدون نقطه: Mr، Dr.
