## When did it happen?

Past time phrases answer the question **When…?** We use them with the **past simple** (<LessonRef>lessons 26 and 27</LessonRef>) and with *was / were* (<LessonRef>lesson 25</LessonRef>).

<FormTable caption="Past time phrases">

| Phrase | Examples |
|---|---|
| **yesterday** (+ part of the day) | yesterday · yesterday morning · yesterday afternoon · yesterday evening |
| **the day before yesterday** | two days before today |
| **last** + time | last night · last week · last month · last year · last summer · last Friday |
| time + **ago** | five minutes ago · two days ago · a long time ago |
| **in** + year / month / season | in 2019 · in March · in the summer |
| **on** + day / date | on Monday · on May 5 |
| **at** + clock time | at 6:00 · at noon |
| **this** + part of today (now finished) | this morning (said in the afternoon) |

</FormTable>

<Example context="home" fa="سه سال پیش به غزنی نقل مکان کردم.">I moved to Ghazni <mark>three years ago</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="هفته‌ی گذشته کار جدیدی شروع کردم."><mark>Last week</mark> I started a new job.</Example>
<Example context="news" fa="این پل را در سال ۱۹۷۱ ساختند.">They built this bridge <mark>in 1971</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="پریروز پیش دکتر رفتم.">I went to the doctor <mark>the day before yesterday</mark>.</Example>

## yesterday and last

- **yesterday** = the day before today. Add a part of the day after it: *yesterday morning, yesterday afternoon, yesterday evening*.
- For the night before today, we say **last night**. *Yesterday night* is not usual.
- **last** + a time = the one just before now: *last week, last month, last year, last Friday*.

**No preposition** before *yesterday* or *last*: *I saw her last week*, not ~~in last week~~. <LessonRef>Lesson 32 (Prepositions of time: at, on, in)</LessonRef> teaches the full list of time words with no preposition.

<Example context="social" fa="دیشب مهمانی بزرگی بود.">There was a big party <mark>last night</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="دیروز بعدازظهر امتحان داشتیم.">We had an exam <mark>yesterday afternoon</mark>.</Example>

## ago

**ago** counts **back from now**. It comes **after** the length of time: **two hours ago**, **ten years ago**, **a long time ago**.

- Use **ago** with the **past simple**: *I **started** a new job two weeks ago*, not ~~I have started a new job two weeks ago~~.
- Don't put a preposition before it, and don't use *before*: *They met fifteen years ago*, not ~~in fifteen years ago~~ or ~~before fifteen years~~.
- To ask, use **How long ago…?** *How long ago did you buy your car?*

<Example context="work" fa="او حدود نیم ساعت پیش از دفتر رفت.">She left the office about half an hour <mark>ago</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="«این موتر را چند وقت پیش خریدی؟» «دو سال پیش.»">“<mark>How long ago</mark> did you buy this car?” “Two years <mark>ago</mark>.”</Example>

**ago** tells you **when** (a point in the past). **for** tells you **how long**: *I lived in Herat **for** three years.* (More about *for*, *since* and *ago* in <LessonRef>B1 lesson 7, *for, since, ago, during; by or until?*</LessonRef>)

## in, on, at with past times

The same prepositions work for the past as for any other time: **in** + year or month, **on** + day or date, **at** + clock time.

<Example context="travel" fa="ما دوشنبه ساعت ۶ صبح به استانبول رسیدیم.">We arrived in Istanbul <mark>on Monday at 6:00</mark> in the morning.</Example>

<LessonRef>Lesson 32 (Prepositions of time: at, on, in)</LessonRef> teaches these in full.

## Where do time phrases go?

Time phrases usually go at the **end** of the sentence, **after** the place: *I went to the gym yesterday.* You can also put them at the **start**: *Yesterday I went to the gym.* Don't put them between the subject and the verb: ~~I yesterday went~~.

<Example context="travel" fa="پارسال به ترکیه رفتیم.">We went to Turkey <mark>last year</mark>.</Example>
<Example context="home" fa="دیروز صبح خانه را تمیز کردم."><mark>Yesterday morning</mark> I cleaned the house.</Example>

<Persian>

**عبارت‌های زمان گذشته:**

- **yesterday** = دیروز (yesterday morning = دیروز صبح، yesterday evening = دیروز عصر)
- **the day before yesterday** = پریروز
- **last** + زمان = … گذشته / … پیش: last week (هفته‌ی گذشته)، last year (پارسال)، last Friday (جمعه‌ی گذشته)
- **last night** = دیشب. نمی‌گوییم yesterday night.
- زمان + **ago** = … پیش: two days ago (دو روز پیش)
- **in** + سال یا ماه: in 2019، in March — **on** + روز: on Monday — **at** + ساعت: at 6:00

**چرا اشتباه می‌کنیم؟**

۱. در فارسی می‌گوییم «**در** هفته‌ی گذشته» یا «**در** سال گذشته». به همین دلیل فارسی‌زبان‌ها می‌گویند in last week. اما در انگلیسی قبل از last و yesterday حرف اضافه نمی‌آید: **last week**، نه in last week.

۲. در فارسی می‌گوییم «دو ساعت **قبل**» و «قبل» را before ترجمه می‌کنیم. اما در انگلیسی برای «… پیش / … قبل» از **ago** استفاده می‌کنیم و ago **بعد از** زمان می‌آید: **two hours ago**، نه before two hours.

۳. «دیشب» در انگلیسی **last night** است، نه yesterday night.

۴. «گذشته» در فارسی بعد از اسم می‌آید (هفته‌ی گذشته)، ولی last در انگلیسی **قبل از** اسم می‌آید: last week.

**ago فقط با گذشته‌ی ساده:** I started a new job two weeks ago. (دو هفته پیش کار جدیدی شروع کردم.) نه I have started… . برای سؤال: How long ago did you…? (چند وقت پیش …؟)

**جای عبارت زمان:** در فارسی معمولاً زمان را اول جمله یا بعد از فاعل می‌گوییم («من دیروز به باشگاه رفتم»). در انگلیسی زمان معمولاً **آخر جمله** و بعد از مکان می‌آید: I went to the gym yesterday. اول جمله هم درست است: Yesterday I went to the gym. اما بین فاعل و فعل نمی‌آید: نه I yesterday went.

<MemoryHook title="ago looks back">
**ago** always comes **after** the time, like looking **back** over your shoulder: three days ← **ago**. And **last** and **yesterday** travel alone: no *in*, no *on*.
