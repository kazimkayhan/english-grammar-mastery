## Negatives and questions need do / does

The present simple has no helper verb in positive sentences (*I work*). For negatives and questions, we add one: **do** or **does**.

<FormTable caption="Present simple: negative and question">

| | I / you / we / they | he / she / it |
|---|---|---|
| **+** | I work. | She work**s**. |
| **−** | I **don't** work. | She **doesn't** work. |
| **?** | **Do** you work? | **Does** she work? |
| **Short answers** | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. | Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**. |

</FormTable>

The **-s** moves to **does**, so the main verb goes back to its base form: *She work**s**. → She **does**n't work. → **Does** she work?*

## Negatives

**don't** = do not. **doesn't** = does not. The short forms are normal in speech. The full forms sound more formal or stronger.

<Example context="home" fa="برادرم گوشت نمی‌خورد.">My brother <mark>doesn't eat</mark> meat.</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="ما شنبه‌ها کار نمی‌کنیم.">We <mark>don't work</mark> on Saturdays.</Example>
<Example context="health" fa="او سیگار نمی‌کشد.">She <mark>doesn't smoke</mark>.</Example>

**One negative only.** In English, one negative word is enough. After *don't / doesn't*, use **anything, anyone, any**: *I **don't** know **anything**.* Not ~~I don't know nothing~~. (More in A2 lesson 2, Negatives & answering negative questions.)

## Yes/no questions

Put **Do / Does** before the subject. The main verb stays in its base form.

<Example context="social" fa="انگلیسی صحبت می‌کنی؟ بله.">“<mark>Do</mark> you speak English?” “Yes, I <mark>do</mark>.”</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این مغازه جمعه‌ها باز است؟"><mark>Does</mark> this store <mark>open</mark> on Fridays?</Example>
<Example context="travel" fa="این اتوبوس به فرودگاه می‌رود؟ نه، نمی‌رود.">“<mark>Does</mark> this bus <mark>go</mark> to the airport?” “No, it <mark>doesn't</mark>.”</Example>

**Short answers** use *do / does / don't / doesn't*. Don't use the main verb alone: *Yes, I **do**.* not ~~Yes, I like.~~ (A full answer is fine too: *Yes, I like it.*)

## Wh- questions

Put the question word first, then **do / does**, then the subject and the verb:

<FormTable caption="Question word + do / does + subject + verb">

| Question word | do / does | Subject | Verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | **do** | you | live? |
| What | **does** | he | do? |
| What time | **does** | the class | start? |
| How | **do** | you | get to work? |
| How much | **does** | it | cost? |

</FormTable>

<Example context="work" fa="شغل پدرت چیست؟">What <mark>does</mark> your father <mark>do</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="school" fa="کلاس ساعت چند شروع می‌شود؟">What time <mark>does</mark> the class <mark>start</mark>?</Example>
<Example context="shopping" fa="این چقدر قیمت دارد؟">How much <mark>does</mark> it <mark>cost</mark>?</Example>

**do** can be the helper **and** the main verb: *What **do** you **do**?* = What is your job? *She **doesn't do** her homework.*

**Who** as the subject (the person who does the action) needs no *do*: ***Who lives** here?* not ~~Who does live here?~~ That comes in B1 lesson 1 (Subject & object questions). Questions with a preposition at the end (*Who do you work **with**?*) come in A2 lesson 1 (Wh- questions: which, whose, how long, what … like).

## No do with be (and can)

**be** makes its own negatives and questions (lesson 5 and lesson 6). Don't add *do*:

- ***Are** you tired?* not ~~Do you are tired?~~
- *She **isn't** a doctor.* not ~~She doesn't be a doctor.~~

**can** works the same way: *Can you swim? I can't drive.* (lesson 21). But **have** uses *do*: *Do you have a car?* (lesson 16).

<Example context="health" fa="خسته‌ای؟"><mark>Are</mark> you tired?</Example>
<Example context="work" fa="آیا در اداره کار می‌کنی؟"><mark>Do</mark> you <mark>work</mark> in an office?</Example>

Negative questions (*Don't you like it?*) come in A2 lesson 2 (Negatives & answering negative questions).

<Persian>

**منفی و سؤالی در حال ساده:** به یک فعل کمکی نیاز داریم: **do** (برای I / you / we / they) و **does** (برای he / she / it).

- منفی: I **don't** work. / She **doesn't** work. (don't = do not، doesn't = does not)
- سؤالی: **Do** you work? / **Does** she work?
- جواب کوتاه: Yes, I **do**. / No, she **doesn't**. فعل اصلی را تکرار نمی‌کنیم: نه Yes, I like.

**مهم:** s فعل به does منتقل می‌شود، پس بعد از does / doesn't فعل اصلی **بدون s** است: Does she **work**? (نه works) / She doesn't **work**. (نه works)

**سؤال با کلمه‌ی پرسشی:** کلمه‌ی پرسشی + do / does + فاعل + فعل: **Where do you work?** (کجا کار می‌کنی؟) / **What does he do?** (شغلش چیست؟)

**اشتباه رایج فارسی‌زبان‌ها ۱:** در فارسی فقط با لحن سؤال می‌پرسیم («کجا کار می‌کنی؟») و ترتیب کلمه‌ها عوض نمی‌شود، پس «Where you work?» می‌گوییم. در انگلیسی do لازم است: **Where do you work?**

**اشتباه رایج ۲:** در فارسی دو منفی با هم می‌آیند: «**هیچ** چیز **نمی**‌دانم». در انگلیسی فقط یک منفی: I **don't** know **anything**. (نه I don't know nothing)

**با be از do استفاده نمی‌کنیم:** Are you tired? (خسته‌ای؟)، نه Do you are tired. همین‌طور با can: Can you swim?
